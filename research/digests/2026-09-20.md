### AI Antitrust Suit — Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI: no movement anywhere, and why that's the right answer

Four subscribers filed a federal antitrust suit (N.D. Cal., 19 September 2026) against Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and xAI, alleging the labs "violated antitrust laws when they agreed to coordinate slowdown efforts," reducing the value of paid subscriptions. All three benchmark-tracked defendants (Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI) were screened; **none moved on this event, in either direction.**

The reasoning is the interesting part:
1. **A filed complaint is procedural, not a conduct finding.** Nothing has been decided; no defendant has responded on the merits.
2. **The conduct challenged is itself a safety commitment** — the trigger was Dario Amodei's 12 September essay proposing an industry-wide pacing agreement to slow capability gains for safety reasons, publicly echoed by the other labs' leaders the same day. The benchmark measures whether an institution recognizes, responds to, and reduces suffering. A claim that a safety pledge reduced subscriber value does not map onto any of the 40 behavioral anchors as a harm.
3. **Direction is genuinely ambiguous and forcing one would be indefensible.** Scoring it down would penalize a safety commitment. Scoring it up would credit a lab for someone else's lawsuit about it. The correct output is a recorded screening note with zero movement.

A countervailing positive item — Amodei's 12 September pacing essay and Anthropic's unilateral grant of permanent, employee-level access to third-party evaluators — is logged as a **positive watch item on S4 Structural Critique and A3 Blind Spot Mitigation**, not banked as score. It is a published commitment, not yet a verified track record, and even a full one-point S4 move would only shift the composite by roughly 0.6 points — far short of the filing line.

- Published scores do not appear to be systematically overstated for the Fortune 500 seed cohorts tested tonight — the three seed values exercised held within ±1.0 point of measured values. This is worth tracking over the next several cycles as more of the placeholder population gets individually tested, since it bears directly on how aggressively the coordinator should treat placeholder-cohort deltas going forward.
