## Impacts and Insights

- **Self-correction discipline is the load-bearing finding of the night, at a scale not previously seen.** 17 distinct corrections across 20 assessments — nearly one correction per entity — with the same failure class recurring in two different countries (Ukraine, India) on two different kinds of events (war, natural disaster). This is the pipeline doing exactly what it should, but the recurrence rate is now itself the story.
- **The Cerebras/Cognition finding demonstrates the anti-false-positive screen working as designed under real pressure.** Both entities show large deltas (-22.1, -10.0) that would ordinarily read as serious downgrades. The screen correctly identified both as measuring absence of assessment, not presence of misconduct, and routed both to calibration review instead of applying a conduct-framed score change.
- **GEO Group demonstrates careful scope discipline on a genuinely severe underlying pattern.** A record-setting national detention-death statistic was corroborated but NOT applied to GEO's own score, while a genuinely GEO-specific second death at the same facility WAS scored. This is the attribution discipline working in the harder direction — refusing to inflate a company-specific finding with system-wide statistics, even when the underlying severity is real.
- **Evidence gap pattern:** Pakistan and Tunisia were both held rather than filed, on a single-source gap and a double-count risk respectively — a reminder that a majority of tonight's "confirm" outcomes required active discipline to avoid over-counting, not passive agreement with the scanner's flags.
- **Out of scope for this cycle:** a separate Sahel-band calibration study (`research/SAHEL_BAND_CALIBRATION_2026-08-13.md`) completed 2026-08-13 found a large share of published country scores are uniform placeholder seeds, similar in kind to tonight's ai-labs finding.
