## Key Highlights

- **The benchmark caught its own scanner attaching a year-old death toll to a new strike.** The scan reported 32 people killed and almost 180 injured in the 31 July 2026 Kyiv strike, and called it the deadliest strike on the capital this year. Contemporaneous reporting gives one to three killed in Kyiv itself, and about eight to nine nationwide. The larger figures match the Kyiv strike of 31 July 2025 — a year earlier, almost to the day. The correction touched two scores (Ukraine, Kyiv) because both entities are reviewed on the same event; neither moved because of it, since neither controls what Russia does.
- **A second scanner claim in the same finding was also wrong, and independently corrected.** The scan asserted July 2026 was the worst month for Ukrainian civilian casualties since 2022. The UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine had already reported, two weeks earlier, that June 2026 held that record — 293 killed, 1,990 injured. Two factual errors in one flagged event, both caught before either reached a published score.
- **Neither Ukraine nor Kyiv lost points for being attacked.** Ukraine's State Emergency Service ran rescue operations; Kyiv opened metro stations as shelters and its mayor coordinated first responders. That is the entities' own conduct, and it is protective. Attribution — not sympathy for the target — is what kept both scores unchanged.
- **Uganda's proposal is a clean evidence-tier escalation, not a re-litigation.** The 26 July assessment already saw this pattern and declined to propose a change because the only source was tier-3 HRW reporting. The UN Human Rights Office's own statement of 30 July, at tier 5, is what moved this from a watch item to a formal −8.4 proposal. The published score has not changed; the proposal remains pending.
- **Two universities were cleared of the same Pentagon research-funding blacklist, one for the first time and one for the second.** Fudan University was already screened as a false positive on the Department of Defense's Section 1286 listing on 30 July. Shanghai Jiao Tong University received its first full assessment this cycle and was screened the same way: a foreign government's national-security funding decision describes that government's own risk posture, not the university's conduct toward the people it serves.

## Impacts and Insights

- **Self-correction is the load-bearing finding of the night.** Two separate, wrong casualty/severity claims about the same event (year-confused death toll; wrong "worst month" claim) were caught and corrected before either could inflate two published scores (Ukraine, Kyiv). This is the pipeline's fact-checking discipline working exactly as designed, on a high-salience geopolitical event where an inflated figure would have been highly visible if published.
- **Attribution discipline caught real work again tonight**, on three separate failure modes: a foreign government's funding-blacklist decision imported as if it were the listed university's own conduct (Shanghai Jiao Tong, Fudan), a detention-law renewal misdated to look like a response to a protest it preceded (India/Delhi), and a federally funded, locally operated facility misattributed to a state government (Louisiana).
- **Uganda demonstrates the evidence-tier escalation model cleanly.** A tier-3 HRW report alone did not clear the proposal bar on 26 July. The same underlying pattern, corroborated five days later by a tier-5 UN Human Rights Office finding describing a wider pattern, did. This is the intended behavior: standing watch items should convert to proposals when a higher-authority source corroborates and widens the record, not when the same evidence is simply re-read.
- **Evidence gap pattern:** four of fifteen assessments tonight (Saudi Arabia, Niger, Quezon City, JPMorgan Chase) were repeat flags on evidence already scored in the prior week to ten days. The scanner continues to resurface identical primary sources; the assessor's discipline against recounting is now doing routine, load-bearing work rather than catching rare edge cases.
