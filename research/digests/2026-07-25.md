## Impacts and Insights

- **Microsoft AI is the cleanest illustration yet of the sourcing bar functioning as a genuine constraint, not a formality.** The assessor did the full analytical work — calculated the lower composite, confirmed it crosses a band line, identified the exact subdimensions that moved — and then stopped short of applying it because the evidence, while real and negative, does not meet the bar the methodology sets for a band-crossing claim specifically. This is a materially different outcome from either "confirm" or "downgrade," and the digest and public briefing need to represent it as its own category: a finding held to a standard, not a finding suppressed or a finding asserted.
- **The scanner's date-contamination problem is now a two-night pattern with a consistent signature.** Both nights' errors involve a scan citing a real event, but attaching the wrong year or the wrong specific date to it, often because the underlying source (a Wikipedia year-in-review page, or in tonight's case a UN News article about a different date's attack) resembles the kind of source used for the correct claim. The assessor's fix — direct-fetch date verification against the primary source, not the scan's framing — is working. The risk is that this costs meaningful re-research time each night and could eventually slip through if assessor discipline lapses.
- **The attribution rule's six-entity application tonight is the widest simultaneous use of this rule seen in recent cycles.** It is not boilerplate — it correctly separated harm-done-to from harm-done-by across five very different entity types (a city, a country under missile attack, a country whose own strikes are scored, an occupied territory, and a state facing a non-state militia).
- **Evidence gaps:** Honduras's finding rests on a single tier-3 advocacy source with no Honduran government statement, court filing, or wire report located — the score move was deliberately kept small because of this. Islamabad's entire in-window record is empty; the confirmation rests entirely on standing structural context. Papua New Guinea's in-window evidence is a single line in a regional weekly roundup.
- **A recurring theme across the past several cycles (Guinea-Bissau, Becton Dickinson, Ecuador, the scanner corrections, and now Microsoft AI's evidence-standard hold) is that the pipeline's self-checking layers are doing substantial, real work night over night.** Microsoft AI in particular demonstrates that the sourcing bar is not just applied to confirm existing scores — it is capable of holding back a genuinely negative, real finding when the sourcing doesn't clear the bar, which is a stronger test of the methodology's discipline than a clean confirmation night.
