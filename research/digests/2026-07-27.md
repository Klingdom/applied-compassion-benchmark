This is the single most important finding of the night and is handled entirely as an index-maintenance referral, not a conduct upgrade.
- **Delhi's assessor-variance control held.** Delhi was anchored to its 2026-07-21 assessment (composite 31.9) and moved only on the five dimensions with direct in-window evidence, producing -4.7. An unanchored re-derivation from scratch would have produced roughly -6.5 — the 1.8-point gap is assessor-to-assessor variance, not conduct, and the anchored figure is what stands. This is the discipline that keeps the benchmark from drifting on re-measurement alone.
- **A fourth consecutive night of article-date-as-event-date errors.** UnitedHealth Group's 71% denial figure (HHS OIG, 8 June 2026) was nearly scored as July conduct; GEO Group's Cal/OSHA settlement (signed May 2026, reported 7 July) was dropped as pre-window; Atlanta's ~$1bn detention-warehouse story turned out to be Social Circle, Georgia in June 2026, a different municipality; Afghanistan's "80 injured / 100+ missing" figure could not be traced to any dated statement and was dropped in favor of the UN's verified 26 killed, nine injured. This class of error is now a recurring pattern across four straight nights (5 items on 07-24, 2 on 07-25, 6 on 07-26, 4 on 07-27) — the gate cannot catch it from a URL string alone, and it continues to be caught at the assessment stage by direct-source date verification.

- **Attribution rule did the most work of the night.** Eight entities — Afghanistan, Sudan, Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi, GEO Group, Detroit, Atlanta — were scored strictly on their own conduct, not on natural disasters, donor-state funding cuts, another city's policing, a lender's business decision, or federal enforcement policy.
