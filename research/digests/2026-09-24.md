## Impacts and Insights

- **Tonight is a case study in why "first measurement" and "decline" must be reported differently.** Three of four proposed changes here would read, to an uncritical eye, as three companies and cities getting worse. They did not get worse — the benchmark simply checked a number for the first time. Conflating the two would misrepresent both the entities and the benchmark's own history.
- **The Abu Dhabi finding is a genuine test of instrument symmetry.** A benchmark that only ever finds harm is not measuring anything; it is accusing. Abu Dhabi's court-ordered wage recovery, held to flag-for-review rather than fast-tracked to an upgrade because the sourcing is state-adjacent, shows the same evidentiary bar applied in both directions.
- **The Tronox/Freeport-McMoRan match is now a repeatable pattern, not a one-off.** Two independent Critical-band Fortune 500 assessments, five months apart, landed on the identical delta from the identical starting value. That is strong structural evidence the underlying placeholder data — not either company's conduct — is what needs fixing.
- **Evidence-tier discipline continues to hold the line on pending litigation.** Progressive's court verdict (tier 5, adjudicated) was treated differently, and correctly, from the pending-litigation items scored in recent cycles — the score moves on what a jury found, not on what a plaintiff alleged, and Progressive's own appeal keeps Reparative Action at the floor until the judgment is final.
