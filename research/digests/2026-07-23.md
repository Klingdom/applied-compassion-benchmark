- **Tonight illustrates the benchmark's methodology working as designed on a real-world coup.** Guinea-Bissau's military government's own actions — jailing an opposition leader, triggering suspension from three regional bodies — map directly onto Equity, Accountability, and Integrity dimension collapses. - **Screening rule 3 continues to do real work distinguishing victims from perpetrators within the same conflict cluster.** Ukraine and Palestine held steady despite worsening casualty figures because those casualties are inflicted by others; Russia's floor score, by contrast, was reconfirmed precisely because the escalation is its own conduct.
- **Evidence gap:** Guinea-Bissau's ACT, EMP, and SYS subdimensions were held at baseline for lack of specific new evidence — the coup's governance rupture is well documented, but service-delivery and humanitarian-response evidence under the transitional military government is thin. This is a real limitation on the confidence of the proposed 13.1, appropriately reflected in the proposal's "medium" confidence rating.
- **The recurring math-hygiene pattern (BD tonight; the composite_override drift candidates flagged for Mauritius, Vanuatu, Cummins, Houston; the Fortune-500 placeholder pattern flagged across 2026-07-20 through 07-22) suggests published-score data quality, not just conduct volatility, is an increasingly important axis of the nightly pipeline's work.** A dedicated registry-hygiene audit, rather than one-off per-entity fixes, looks increasingly justified.
