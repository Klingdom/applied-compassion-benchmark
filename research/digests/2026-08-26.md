## Significant Findings

### THE LEAD STORY: Peru's Triggering Event Is Retracted — A New Evidence-Lifecycle Failure Class

The scan flagged Peru on the reported killing of Ashéninka leader Américo Pascual Tumisha, sourced to Manila Times/AFP and Mongabay, both date-verified at scan time. **He is alive.** ORAU (Regional Organisation AIDESEP Ucayali), which issued the original alert, rectified it after a commission of the ACONADIYSH Frontier Indigenous Guard traveled by river to Yurúa and confirmed him safe with his family — he had been hunting. Five Peruvian outlets (La República, Inforegión, Caretas, El Comercio, Radio Nacional) confirmed the correction on 26 August 2026. The Rainforest Foundation Norway article that originally reported the killing now returns HTTP 404.

**This is classified as an evidence-lifecycle failure, not a date-verification failure.** Every existing check in the pipeline confirms a source is dated and real *at the moment it is read*. None re-checks whether it still stands. Both of the scanner's cited sources were correctly dated when the scan ran; the retraction happened afterward. The benchmark caught this because a human-verified correction reached it in time — not because any gate fired. In a faster or less fortunate cycle, this would have published a false death against a country's human-rights record.

The genuinely in-window Peru evidence points the opposite direction: on 24 August 2026 a Peruvian court sentenced four men to 15–35 years for the 2023 murder-for-hire of Indigenous defender Quinto Inuma Alvarado — "the first case in Peru involving the killing of an Indigenous defender to result in sentences this severe" (Legal Defense Institute, via Washington Times). Peru confirms at **36.2 (+0.3)** against a published 35.9. No proposal filed; movements offset almost exactly (Accountability/Empathy up on the sentencing and forest-custodian accreditation, Action down on documented protective failure toward Pascual Tumisha before the retraction).

**Recommendation for the pipeline:** add a retraction re-check on any score-moving single-incident report before a proposal is filed. This is the second time in three cycles a "the source was dated correctly and still wrong" problem has surfaced (cf. the 2026-08-19 queue-accounting correction) — worth tracking as a recurring class, not a one-off.

## Two Band Crossings Measured and Deliberately Not Filed

**Freetown (+19.3, Critical→Developing) and Abuja (+8.7, Critical→Developing)** — both upward, both screened out under the anti-false-positive rules, for different reasons:

- **Freetown:** the band-crossing evidence test **PASSES** (UNEP and World Bank/GFDRR at tier 4, plus IIED and two Sierra Leone news outlets). NOT FILED because screening check 3 fails — the positive evidence (Transform Freetown, the Rainy-Season Plan, #FreetownTheTreeTown) is **standing programme evidence, not new within-window conduct**, and absence of new harm is not positive evidence. The protest ban that originally triggered the scanner flag was issued by the Sierra Leone Police (a national force under the Public Order Act), not Freetown City Council — Lagos precedent applied, rejected as city conduct.
- **Abuja:** the band-crossing evidence test **FAILS** — every source located is Nigerian general press at tier 2; no tier-4+ source was obtained. Screening check 3 is marginal (the FCTA's demolition order and green-space ban are new within-window positive conduct, but demolition isn't unambiguously compassionate absent documented compensation/rehousing, which no source addresses).
- Both sit on **byte-identical placeholder dimension vectors** (2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 1.5, 1.5, 1.5, 2.0, 1.5 → 18.8), both `last_assessed: null`, both first-ever individual measurements. Referred upward as a coordinator-level placeholder-cohort calibration question, not filed as one-off upgrades.
- **Why the two are attributed differently:** Abuja has no elected mayor or council — the Federal Capital Territory Administration *is* the municipal government, so FCTA conduct (both the land-allocation decisions that worsened the flooding and the demolition/green-space response) is scored as city conduct. Freetown, unlike Abuja, has an elected city council distinct from the national government that issued the protest ban.

**Worth flagging to the founder:** the benchmark declined to publish a measured *improvement* it found, twice, in the same cycle, using the identical discipline that has held back low-confidence downgrades in prior cycles. That symmetry is itself a finding worth watching — it's evidence the anti-false-positive screen isn't one-directional.

## Impacts and Insights

- Tonight's most consequential finding isn't a score change at all — it's a limitation the benchmark found in its own verification process. Publishing the Peru retraction transparently, and using it to name a new failure class, is exactly the kind of self-correction that should be visible in the public record; a version of this belongs in the public briefing's framing, handled carefully to avoid implying the benchmark regularly gets facts wrong (it caught this one before publication).
- The Freetown/Abuja symmetry is a genuinely reassuring structural signal: a benchmark that only ever finds reasons to suppress bad news would not be credible; finding reasons to suppress *good* news under the identical rule is evidence the screen is honest rather than one-directional.
- GEO Group's coverage gap is a useful internal process finding: assessments of entities under active regulatory scrutiny should include a dedicated fresh search for the entity name plus regulator/subpoena/investigation terms, independent of what the scanner flagged, especially when the scanner's own dating is ambiguous (as it was here, with three merged events).
- The GM band crossing illustrates a recurring tension worth tracking across cycles: primary federal sources are increasingly returning HTTP 403 to automated fetches (this is now at least the second cycle this has affected a filing — cf. the 2026-08-16 Microsoft AI precedent cited in GM's own proposal notes). If this becomes systemic, it may be worth a standing policy on how much weight trade-press aggregation of a federal docket's contents can carry toward the evidence-tier bar.
