That is 0.7 raw points of movement across the whole profile. But Taipei's published dimension profile had exactly one dimension below 4.0 (the "weakness factor" in the composite formula), giving it the maximum 8.0-point integration premium. Moving three further dimensions below 4.0 collapses that premium to 2.0 — a mechanical 6.0-point loss that has nothing to do with the size of the underlying evidence. Roughly 6.0 of the 8.1-point composite fall is this formula effect; roughly 2.1 points reflect the evidence itself. The direction is clear and well-sourced; the magnitude needs human calibration before a band change is applied to the published index.

## Impacts and Insights

- Tonight is a genuine methodology first: Taipei is the first pending proposal held on **magnitude** grounds rather than **sourcing** grounds. This is a distinct failure mode from everything else in the queue and should be triaged separately — a coordinator reviewing the queue needs to know that four items (Baxter, Guinea-Bissau, Uganda, Microsoft AI) are waiting on evidence, while Taipei is waiting on a formula-calibration decision that no amount of additional sourcing will resolve.
- The Tier-1 coverage re-run is the clearest demonstration yet of why the coverage-accounting gate exists. The gap wasn't caught by noticing missing findings — it was caught by an honesty check on the scanner's own bookkeeping, and *that* check is what forced the re-run that then surfaced Mauritania and Monrovia. The lesson generalizes: process-integrity checks (does the log match the claim?) can catch failures that content-quality checks (is each finding well-sourced?) cannot.
- The attribution rule's load-bearing role continues to grow: 9 of 20 entities tonight required an explicit attribution decision (Mauritania, Guayaquil, Baku, Medan, Monrovia, Karachi-vs-Pakistan, Tegucigalpa, Ford Motor's no-injuries screen, Meta AI-vs-Meta Platforms scope separation). Getting this wrong in either direction would systematically miscalibrate scores across country/city pairs, parent/subsidiary pairs, and shared-triggering-event pairs.
- Austria's deliberate restraint and Taipei's magnitude flag are two sides of the same discipline: the benchmark is willing to under-report a finding (Austria) when sourcing is short of the bar, and willing to over-flag rather than under-flag a finding (Taipei) when sourcing is sufficient but the formula's behavior is uncertain. Both choices favor calibrated caution over either default.
