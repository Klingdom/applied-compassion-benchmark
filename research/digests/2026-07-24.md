### THE NIGHT'S MOST IMPORTANT FINDING IS NOT ABOUT ANY ENTITY

The scan systematically shifted **July 2025 events into July 2026**, affecting five of tonight's fifteen priority entities. All five were caught before they could change a score.

1. **Thailand and Cambodia** — the scanner reported a 24 July 2026 border war (38+ killed, ~300,000 displaced, 28 July Malaysia-mediated ceasefire), sourced to a single undated Wikipedia "2026 in Asia" page. Those are **July 2025** events. HRW World Report 2026 (Cambodia chapter) states verbatim: *"Fighting in July 2025 killed and injured civilians, including children, displaced around 300,000 people, and damaged religious and cultural sites."* A second round of fighting occurred in December 2025 (101+ killed). No primary source reports any Thailand–Cambodia armed clash between 2026-07-10 and 2026-07-24 — the scanner's own 28 July ceasefire date is also in the future relative to both the scan date and the run date, which should itself have been a red flag. Both countries were re-scored on genuine in-window evidence: Thailand on four UNHCR-recognised Chinese refugees held under pressure from Beijing (HRW, 13 Jul); Cambodia on a 2025-2026 civil-society crackdown and an anti-scam enforcement gap (fewer than 1 in 5 identified compounds investigated).
2. **Best Buy** — the 161-person Best Buy Health layoff notice that flagged the company was filed in California on 2025-07-11 (Healthcare Dive, verified by direct fetch).
3. **Delta Air Lines** — the $8.1 million DOJ False Claims Act settlement that flagged the company was announced in July 2025 (Aviation A2Z 16 Jul 2025, Claims Journal 22 Jul 2025).
4. **Ukraine and Russia** — the scanner's "1,674 casualties, record month" figure is the **July 2025** UN figure. The correct in-window figure (HRMMU, 14 Jul 2026) is June 2026 as the deadliest month since April 2022: 293 killed, 1,990 injured; H1 2026 at 1,396 killed / 7,978 injured, up 37% year-on-year.

**Common factor:** every contaminated item traced to a source whose URL carries no date — a Wikipedia year-in-review page, or an undated publisher slug. Recommend the scanner owner add date-verification gating specifically for undated-URL sources before entities reach the assessor. This is the third or fourth cycle in a row where a self-check inside the pipeline has caught and corrected a data-quality problem before it reached a published score (cf. 2026-07-23 Becton Dickinson math-hygiene finding) — the system working as designed, not a new failure mode.

## Impacts and Insights

- **Tonight is a strong illustration of layered self-checking working as designed.** The scanner made a real, non-trivial dating error (a phantom war, effectively) on two entities at once, plus three smaller instances elsewhere. The assessor caught all five independently, using primary-source date verification rather than trusting the scanner's framing. No entity's score moved because of contaminated evidence. This is the same pattern as the 2026-07-23 Becton Dickinson arithmetic catch — the pipeline is increasingly demonstrating that its internal review layers are not redundant paperwork, they are catching real errors before publication.
- **Ecuador demonstrates the value of an explicit, dated watch-item system.** The May baseline didn't just confirm a boundary case and move on — it named the specific evidence category that would resolve the boundary question, with a 60-day clock. That structure is what let tonight's reviewer immediately recognize the HRW report as the anticipated evidence rather than a fresh, unrelated finding requiring re-litigation of Ecuador's placement from scratch.
- **The attribution rule is carrying real analytical weight, not just a boilerplate disclaimer.** Four entities tonight (Ukraine, Iran, Russia, Kansas City) were confirmed specifically because the rule correctly separated "harm done to X" from "harm done by X," including a case where it worked in the entity's favor. This dimension of the methodology appears to be functioning exactly as intended across very different entity types (a country under attack, a country whose own attacks are scored, a city where federal and municipal conduct diverge).
- **Evidence gap:** Bangladesh's Action, Empathy, and Systemic Thinking subdimensions relied on structural context predating the specific 19 July HRW finding — the disappearance case is well-documented, but the replacement bill's exact operative text was not yet public at assessment time, which is a real limit on how far the watch flag's confidence should extend.
- **A recurring theme across the past several cycles (Guinea-Bissau, Becton Dickinson, now Ecuador and the five-item scanner correction) is that the pipeline is increasingly finding and correcting its own data-quality problems before they reach a published score, rather than generating fresh conduct-based findings at the same rate.** This is arguably evidence the methodology is maturing in the right direction, but it is worth tracking whether genuine new-evidence proposals (like Ecuador) are keeping pace with data-hygiene catches, or whether hygiene work is crowding out substantive review capacity.
