## Key Highlights

- **The Bangalore catch is the most important finding of the night, and it runs in the opposite direction of the original flag.** The scan proposed a crackdown story sourced to a 23 July 2026 Human Rights Watch statement said to document detentions in Bengaluru. The statement was fetched and read in full: it never mentions Bengaluru or Bangalore anywhere, and concerns New Delhi only. Had this been scored as flagged, the benchmark would have published a finding that was wrong in direction, not merely wrong in degree — the verified Bengaluru record is a peaceful, volunteer-supported, week-long-plus protest at Freedom Park with no documented dispersal. This is the sixth consecutive night of a date- or attribution-class scanner error caught before it reached a published score (following 07-24 through 07-28), and the most consequential of the run because the flagged direction of harm was inverted, not merely mistimed.
- **Milan's stale-year catch removed the entity's entire triggering claim.** The scan's headline claim — a "July 2026" Constitutional Court finding against migrant-detention practice — is judgment No. 96 of 3 July 2025, confirmed by direct fetch of the University of Padua Human Rights Centre page. With that dropped, Milan's entire in-window municipal-conduct record collapses to one tier-2 local item (the 23 July Gratosoglio public-housing clearance, conducted with the Comune's social services present), which is why confidence is set to low.
- **Carnegie Mellon is the only proposal tonight, and it is conduct-driven, not mechanical — the explicit opposite of Taipei's still-pending magnitude hold.** Both CMU's published and proposed dimension sets sit entirely below the 4.0 threshold that triggers the benchmark's weakness-factor/integration-premium formula, so that formula contributes zero to both readings. The full −5.5 is new conduct: expanded discipline, including a possible expulsion, against students for messages on the university's own designated free-expression structure. Confidence is capped at medium purely on source tier (student journalism plus interested advocacy groups), not on any formula ambiguity.
- **Arizona's confirmation is itself the finding.** The 17 July 2026 prison-healthcare receivership order that flagged Arizona tonight was already scored — it is the same order the 19 July 2026 assessment cites as the basis for the current published 36.2. Re-scoring it would have double-counted the same failure. Delta 0, correctly.
- **One lawsuit, three states, three separate findings — not three copies of one finding.** New Jersey, Minnesota and Colorado were all flagged on the same 26-state suit against FEMA/DHS. Coalition membership was treated as weak individual evidence and moved nothing for any of the three; each was then checked on its own conduct. Only Minnesota had a genuine distinct finding (a favorable detainer-law ruling after a 10-month defense). All three: delta 0.
- **Grid artifacts, not improvement.** Cairo and Bangalore's identical +0.3 deltas are the same 0.2-subdimension-grid rounding artifact declared on Guayaquil and Mauritania (07-28) — published values of 1.5/2.5/3.5 are unreachable on the grid, producing small positive drift with no underlying conduct change.

## Impacts and Insights

- **The Bangalore case is the single most important result of the night, independent of the fact that it produced zero score movement.** A benchmark's credibility rests as much on what it declines to publish as on what it publishes. Tonight's process caught a wrong-entity attribution before it became a scored downgrade, and the verified replacement evidence ran in the *opposite* direction from the original claim — the city was, on the record, accommodating protest, not suppressing it. This is the sixth consecutive night of this failure class, which is now a structural signal about the scanner's claim-generation stage rather than a series of unrelated one-off errors.
- **CMU and Taipei together make a clean methodology illustration.** Two pending proposals of similar magnitude (-5.5 and -8.1) are held for opposite reasons — one because the formula amplifies real-but-small movement (Taipei), one because the movement is entirely real but the sourcing tier is capped (CMU). Neither is a rejection; both are legitimate holds under different rules within the same queue, and distinguishing them clearly matters for how the queue is eventually resolved.
- **Two double-count safeguards fired in the same night (Arizona; the three-state coalition suit) without producing a single scored finding.** This is a real and reportable result: the discipline against re-scoring already-priced conduct and against multiplying one event across coalition members is functioning as designed, even though neither produced a headline number.
- **Evidence gaps are concentrated in two places tonight:** first-tier sourcing ceilings on politically contested campus conduct (CMU — student press plus interested advocacy groups, no independent government record), and thin single-source city records (Milan — one tier-2 local outlet is the entire in-window base).
