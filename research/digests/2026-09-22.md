## Significant Findings

### Amsterdam (global-cities) — 94.4 published → 72.0 proposed (−22.4), first-ever baseline, band crossing proposed (Exemplary → Established)

**The most interesting finding of the night, because it cuts in both directions at once.** The scanner flagged a 19 September 2026 joint letter from the mayors of Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Utrecht and The Hague asking the Dutch national government for roughly €100 million a year against a "dramatic" rise in homelessness. The assessor scored that letter **upward** — four mayors publicly naming a problem they cannot fix on their own is compassion functioning, not failing, and it raised Awareness and Systemic Thinking (A1 5/5, A4 4/5, S1 4/5, S3 4/5, S4 5/5). No part of the downgrade is charged to the mayoral letter.

The downgrade instead comes from evidence the scan never raised. The city's own statutory Ombudsman Metropool Amsterdam logged 2,942 complaints and signals in its 2025 annual report and found vulnerable residents "stuck in complicated rules, waiting times and slow procedures," mostly over care, debt, housing and social support — an independent verification of *inconsistency* that is incompatible with the published Exemplary band, which the methodology defines as practices "independently verified, consistent, and sustained." Separately, a €4.2 million welfare-fraud algorithm ("Smart Check"), built to be among the fairest in the world, was found by outside investigators to still discriminate against residents with migrant backgrounds and lower incomes, and the city council suspended it in May 2025 on its own initiative, with no court or regulator forcing the decision.

- **Confidence:** medium
- **Band change:** Yes — Exemplary → Established
- **Key evidence:**
  - Ombudsman Metropool Amsterdam, *Jaarverslag 2025* — 2,942 complaints and signals; vulnerable residents stuck in rules and waiting times (tier 5, government/statutory body)
  - MIT Technology Review (with Lighthouse Reports), 2025-06-11 — Smart Check discrimination finding and May 2025 suspension (tier 5)
  - Housing Rights Watch, 2025-06-03 — court ruling requiring the national minister to keep funding shelter for 28 people (tier 4)
  - NL Times, 2026-09-19 — the G4 mayors' joint letter: "confused, addicted, and visibly desperate" (tier 2)
- **Recommendation:** downgrade
- **Upward watch attached:** if the Dutch national government funds any part of the €100 million request, Action and Equity are re-tested and would move up.

### Columbia University (universities) — 44.5 published → 31.3 proposed (−13.2), first-ever baseline, band crossing proposed (Functional → Developing)

Columbia then paid a published $221 million federal resolution agreement (Treasury + EEOC), including a $21 million EEOC settlement — the largest public EEOC settlement in nearly 20 years, now in active payout — and accepted externally set discipline terms. A further $21m EEOC-adjacent StandWithUs settlement closed 2026-02-27. **The band would be unchanged even if both pending suits were dismissed** — the composite rests on the federal finding and the resolution agreement, not on the new allegations.

### Seoul (global-cities) — 60.9 published → 48.8 proposed (−12.1), first-ever baseline, band crossing proposed (Established → Functional)

Scored two-sided, as the finding requires. A Seoul Metropolitan Government division director used a contractor's corporate card 51 times over about a year to buy ₩21.03 million of personal goods — including a laptop for his child and hotel stays — after signing a ₩1.04 billion procurement contract with that same contractor. **The failure is Seoul's own**: its own personnel, its own procurement decision, its own internal control that missed it for a year. **The accountability is not Seoul's**: the detection, the criminal referral and the sanction recommendation all came from South Korea's national Board of Audit and Inspection, not from the city. Seoul is credited for being subject to and now on notice from that external audit (Awareness), but is not credited for a correction it has not yet been shown to have made itself (Accountability held down: AB1/AB2/AB4 all at 2/5).

Seoul's welfare machinery is real and keeps the score inside the Functional band rather than lower: ₩451.3 billion committed over five years against loneliness and lonely deaths, the country's first Director General of Care and Loneliness, and disability welfare spending raised to ₩1.7685 trillion in 2025 (+8.1% year over year).

- **Confidence:** medium

- **Key evidence:**

  - Board of Audit and Inspection of Korea — national constitutional audit body, confirming the detecting institution is national, not municipal (tier 5)
  - Seoul Metropolitan Government — ₩451.3bn "Seoul Without Loneliness" programme (tier 4)
  - Seoul Metropolitan Government — ₩1.7685tn 2025 disability welfare investment (tier 4)

## Impacts and Insights

- Tonight states the placeholder problem as precisely as it has been stated in this benchmark's history: three first-ever baselines (Amsterdam, Seoul, Columbia) each corrected a published score that had never been measured, and a fourth, larger finding (five Fortune 500 companies) shows the same pattern operating across two entire cohorts of 30 unrelated companies. In every case, the finding is about the benchmark's own unassessed seed data, not a story of declining institutional conduct.
- Amsterdam is the clearest demonstration this cycle of the methodology holding two-sided evidence honestly: the same flagging event (the G4 mayoral letter) was scored *upward*, while independent evidence the scanner never surfaced (the ombudsman report, the Smart Check finding) drove the downward move. Reporting both, rather than netting them silently, is the discipline this benchmark is built to apply.
- Attribution discipline is holding consistently across very different actors and stakes: El Salvador (a federal U.S. immigration decision), Georgia (a federal ICE raid at a private employer), and Anthropic (an individual employee's speech) all produced zero score movement against the entity that did not act.
- Evidence gaps remain honestly disclosed rather than papered over: Columbia's federal-agency sources returned HTTP 403 and are carried without quotes; Amsterdam's ombudsman report was read in secondary reporting rather than the primary Dutch-language document; SEI Investments' confidence is explicitly low because roughly 12 of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure rather than documented conduct.
