## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged South Sudan on the UN Humanitarian Coordinator's World Humanitarian Day statement of 19 August 2026, which reported that at least 36 humanitarian workers and contractors have been killed in South Sudan since the start of 2026 — already more than the 31 killed in all of 2025. South Sudan was last assessed on 2026-07-18 and sits at the absolute floor of the countries index.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**GATE WARNING 1 RESOLVED — the superlative claim is corroborated by dated primary sourcing.** The scan carried a record-setting claim ("already surpassing the 31 killed in all of 2025") that the gate flagged as resting partly on undated sourcing. This is the exact pattern behind the confirmed 2026-07-24 defect, so it was tested before use. The claim originates in a **statement by UN Humanitarian Coordinator for South Sudan Ramanathan Balakrishnan issued on World Humanitarian Day, 19 August 2026**, and is reproduced verbatim by Xinhua in a piece explicitly dated 2026-08-19: "At least 36 humanitarian workers and contractors have been killed in the line of duty in South Sudan since January, already surpassing the 31 deaths recorded throughout 2025." The Washington Post (URL path /2026/08/19/) and Sudans Post carry the same figures independently. **Three dated sources, one at tier 4 (UN mission).** The claim is cleared for use.

**ATTRIBUTION.** Xinhua's report of the statement does **not** attribute the killings to any named perpetrator; Balakrishnan calls on "the South Sudanese government and key parties to the peace accord" to fulfil their obligations. Much of the violence against aid workers in South Sudan is carried out by armed groups and opportunistic armed actors, not by the state directly. This assessment therefore scores **the state's protection duty and its response**, not the killings themselves as state acts. The state's own conduct that is scored is the failure to investigate, prosecute or guarantee access — which the UN Humanitarian Coordinator names directly.

**Date checks.** All three sources carry an explicit 2026 year in the URL path or byline. No year-confusion was found.

## Floor limitation

**South Sudan is at the arithmetic floor of the scale.** All eight dimensions are at 1.0 and the composite is exactly 0.0 of 100. A record-setting year for aid-worker deaths, with incidents running at more than double the 2025 rate, **cannot be expressed as a downward score movement**. This is recorded as a methodology finding.

It is worth stating plainly what this means for a reader: the benchmark's inability to move South Sudan's score is not a judgement that 2026 is no worse than 2025. The evidence says 2026 is materially worse. The scale simply stops at zero.
