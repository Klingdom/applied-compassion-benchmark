## The material-relevance test

**WHAT THE EVENT IS.** Algeria summoned Emirati ambassador Yousef Saif Khamis Subaa Al-Ali and gave him 48 hours to leave. Algeria then closed its airspace to Emirati-registered aircraft with effect from 11 September 2026, and had already begun procedures in February 2026 to terminate the 2013 bilateral air-services agreement. The Emirati foreign ministry replied within hours, expressing hope the decision "will be temporary." The underlying causes reported are alleged Emirati support for the banned separatist MAK movement, an expanding Emirati footprint in Libya, Mali and Niger, and Emirati backing of Morocco over Western Sahara.

**WHY IT DOES NOT REACH THE INSTRUMENT.** The Compassion Benchmark measures how an institution recognises, responds to and reduces suffering. All eight dimensions and all 40 subdimensions are about the entity's relationship to people in pain or need. A diplomatic rupture between two states is a foreign-policy act. Nothing in the located evidence identifies a person or population who suffers as a result:

- No source reports any Algerian or Emirati national stranded, expelled, denied consular assistance, or cut off from remittances.
- The airspace closure applies to Emirati-registered aircraft. No source reports cancelled routes affecting travellers, or a named group whose access to medical care, family or work was interrupted.
- No source reports an aid, humanitarian or health programme interrupted on either side.
- No source reports a detention, an injury or a death connected to the rupture.

Forcing this into a subdimension would require inventing a harmed population. That is the failure mode the screening rules exist to prevent, and the honest outcome is to say so.

**WHY NOT SCORE IT UPWARD EITHER.** Algeria's stated grievances include Emirati support for a separatist movement. A state defending its territorial integrity is not, on this evidence, an act of compassion toward any population. No upward reading is available.
