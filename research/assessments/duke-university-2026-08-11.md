## Key Findings

- The federal government says Duke Law broke the law on race in admissions — but no court has decided that. The Justice Department announced on 6 August 2026 that Duke University School of Law intentionally discriminated by race for three admissions cycles. It is seeking a voluntary settlement, not a court judgment, and Duke says only that it is reviewing the letter.
- The specific allegation is about who got in with the same grades. The DOJ found that "Black or Hispanic applicants were much more likely to be admitted than white or Asian applicants with the same academic credentials."
- Duke cut $299 million and its own auditors questioned how. Facing federal research funding cuts, Duke targeted a $350 million reduction by 2030, cut $299 million through buyouts and building closures, and laid off 45 employees. An audit then called those budget and employee cuts into question.
- New health-system staff now wait 15 years for the leave older colleagues reached in nine. Duke University Health System cut paid time off for employees hired after 1 January 2026, creating two tiers of staff doing the same work.
- This is Duke's first real assessment, and the published score largely held. The benchmark had never individually assessed Duke; its 50.8 was a bulk-import figure. Evidence-based scoring returns 47.5 — a difference of 3.3 points, too small to change the published score.
