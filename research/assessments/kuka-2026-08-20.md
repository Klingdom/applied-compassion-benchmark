## Baseline-establishment note

This is a **first-ever assessment**. KUKA AG does not appear in any published Compassion Benchmark index.

**Corporate status check (required before assessment).** KUKA AG is operating under that name, headquartered in Augsburg, Germany, with roughly 14,500 employees in more than 50 countries. It has been wholly owned by Midea Group of China since the 2021 squeeze-out completed the 2016 acquisition, and it continues to publish its own sustainability report ([KUKA Sustainability Report 2024](https://www.kuka.cn/-/media/kuka-corporate/documents/ir/reports-and-presentations/sustainability-reports/kuka_sustainability-report_2024.pdf?rev=-1); [KUKA and Midea press release](https://www.kuka.com/en-us/company/press/news/2021/11/press-release-kuka-and-midea)). No rename or insolvency event was found. A restructuring of the loss-making Systems division is under way and is discussed below.

**Evidence base and its limits.** KUKA's own reporting is self-published and does not count toward independent verification. What sets this record apart from its industrial peers is a body of **independent, third-party evidence about how the company handled a painful decision**: German trade press, regional press and IG Metall material documenting the negotiated restructuring at Augsburg. That evidence lifts Integrity and Awareness and is the reason KUKA is not scored identically to FANUC.

**Absence versus conduct.** Outside the restructuring, most of KUKA's low scores rest on absence of disclosure, not on evidence of harm. **Where that is the case, the row says so.**

**Disclosure density.** Of 40 subdimensions, **28 rest primarily on absence of disclosure** and 12 rest on positive located evidence.

## Key Findings

- **KUKA is the only industrial robot maker in this batch with independent evidence of how it treats its own people under pressure — and that evidence is largely favourable.** Facing years of losses in its Systems division, KUKA agreed with its works council and IG Metall that **every employee, including management and executives, would give up 10% of gross salary until the end of 2026** to protect jobs.
- **German co-determination does real work here.** The works council has statutory authority over restructuring and used it across weeks of negotiation. Signal Amplification scores 4 of 5 — the highest single subdimension score of the four industrial firms — because a low-power group's representatives demonstrably changed the outcome.
- **The good news is bounded. Between 215 and 560 jobs are still being cut** at Augsburg across 2025 and 2026, and IG Metall has publicly voiced concern for the site's future. The score reflects a painful decision handled comparatively well, not an absence of pain.
- **Outside the restructuring, KUKA discloses less than its peers.** Twenty-eight of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure. No lifecycle-support commitment, no upstream education programme and no human rights due diligence cycle were located — all of which competitors publish.
- **KUKA publishes a sustainability report despite being wholly owned by Midea and no longer listed.** That is voluntary and counts in its favour on Transparency, even though the report is self-published and not independently assured.

## Strongest Dimensions
- **Awareness (45.0)** — driven almost entirely by statutory works-council authority and its documented exercise.
- **Integrity (35.0)** — the highest Integrity score among the four industrial firms, earned on the shared salary sacrifice.

## Weakest Dimensions
- **Empathy and Equity, both at 25.0** — the absence floor. Nothing was located in either direction.
- **Action (30.0)** — no located lifecycle-support protocol, no outcome evaluation, no resource-gap analysis.

## Evidence Gaps
- No independently assured social reporting.
- No published outcome of the social plan for affected Augsburg employees.
- No lifecycle or long-run customer support commitment located.
- No analysis of employment effects of KUKA robots in customer plants.
