## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: Xcel Energy was flagged for a fire it has not been shown to have started.** The Aspen Acres lawsuit filed on 16 July 2026 names San Isabel Electric Association, a different company. The Colorado Division of Fire Prevention and Control says the cause is still under investigation. The claim was dropped and no score moved.
- **Why it matters: Xcel did pay for the fire it was accused of starting, without admitting it started it.** The company agreed in September 2025 to pay about $640 million to settle Marshall fire claims brought by roughly 4,000 plaintiffs, while admitting no fault. The Marshall fire killed two people and destroyed more than 1,000 homes and commercial buildings.
- **Why it matters: customers were not billed for the settlement.** Xcel said $350 million came from its remaining insurance coverage and none from its customers, which is unusual in utility wildfire settlements.
- **By January 2026 nearly all individual plaintiffs had signed.** Every insurance company that sued Xcel to recover money and agreed to settle has been paid out, though some plaintiffs may still proceed to trial.
- **Xcel has an approved wildfire mitigation plan with the Colorado Public Utilities Commission**, which is the main upstream, root-cause element in its profile.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Awareness (3.4), Action (3.4), Boundaries (3.4), Accountability (3.4), Systemic Thinking (3.4) and Integrity (3.4).** Xcel operates a regulator-approved wildfire mitigation plan, settled at scale ahead of a full liability determination, and shielded ratepayers from the cost.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (2.6).** Xcel publishes little on how outage burden, shutoff practice and disconnection fall across income and vulnerability groups, and the Marshall settlement was reached without an admission of fault, which limits what can be said about bias awareness and priority for vulnerable customers.

## Evidence Gaps

- The Colorado Division of Fire Prevention and Control has not published a cause determination for the Aspen Acres fire, so no in-window conduct finding is available for Xcel.
- Xcel does not publish disaggregated outage or disconnection data by income or medical vulnerability, capping EQ2 and EQ3 at 2.
- The Marshall settlement carries no admission of fault, so AB1 rests on the payment rather than on an acknowledgment.

## Claims Dropped on Verification

- Claim: "investigators identified an unmoored Xcel Energy power line spark as one of two ignition points" of the Aspen Acres fire. **Dropped — wrong entity.** The Colorado Sun (2026-07-16) and Colorado Public Radio (2026-07-16) both name San Isabel Electric Association as the defendant; the Colorado Sun mentions Xcel only in relation to the separate 2021 Marshall fire. The Colorado Division of Fire Prevention and Control states the cause remains under investigation.

## Anti-False-Positive Screening

Verification against two independently dated in-window sources establishes the defendant is San Isabel Electric Association and that the official cause remains undetermined. Under the rule that the entity which acted must be identified before a claim is scored, the claim was dropped and recorded. Directionality check: with the claim removed there is no in-window evidence, so no upgrade and no downgrade is proposed.
