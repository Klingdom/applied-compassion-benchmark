## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Meta Platforms on a settlement with a coalition of state attorneys general over the mental-health effects of Instagram and Facebook on children. Meta was last assessed on 2026-07-28.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Date: CONFIRMED.** The settlement was disclosed in a court filing and announced by state attorneys general on **26 August 2026**. The California Attorney General's press release carries that date, as do CNBC and NPR. The scanner's `evidence_date` of 2026-08-26 is correct. No year confusion.

**Amount: CORRECTED UPWARD, AND THE FIGURES DIFFER BY SOURCE.** The scanner recorded **$16.7 billion**. The California Attorney General's own release describes a **$17 billion** settlement over ten years. Other outlets report $16.68 billion and $18 billion. The most defensible statement is that the settlement is **between $16.7 billion and $17 billion over ten years**; this assessment uses the California Attorney General's $17 billion figure where a single number is needed and flags the spread. The spread does not affect any score.

**Coalition size: CORRECTED.** The scanner said "29 state attorneys general". The California Attorney General describes a bipartisan coalition of **51 attorneys general**; the underlying claims came from 29 states. Both figures are defensible for different things; the correction is recorded because the scanner's phrasing merged them.

**THIS IS A SETTLEMENT, NOT AN ADMISSION — SCORED ACCORDINGLY.** The California Attorney General's release describes a "proposed settlement" resolving allegations. It contains no admission of liability by Meta. Nothing in this assessment treats the settlement as a finding that Meta harmed children. What the settlement *does* evidence, and what is scored, is narrower and factual: (1) Meta accepted enforceable, specific product constraints it had not previously adopted; (2) Meta accepted **independent auditor oversight**; (3) Meta committed money earmarked for youth mental-health remediation. Those are corrective and reparative facts regardless of who was right about causation.

Both were previously scored at 1 because Meta had produced nothing but denial. That is no longer literally true, and holding the floor would misstate the record.

This is a **small upward movement inside the Critical band on an entity flagged for negative news**. That combination requires explanation, and it has one: the settlement is a legal defeat for Meta and simultaneously the first concrete corrective and reparative act on the record. The benchmark scores conduct, and a court-compelled correction is still a correction — scored at anchor 2, "under pressure, minimal", not higher. ## Key Findings

- **Meta agreed to pay about $17 billion over ten years to settle state claims that Instagram and Facebook harmed children.** The settlement was announced on 26 August 2026. Meta admitted no wrongdoing.
- **The settlement forces real product changes.** Users under 18 get a default 2-hour daily limit, a midnight-to-6am block, and no cosmetic-surgery filters. An independent auditor will check compliance.
- **Meta's score barely moves, and it moves up by 0.3 points.** That is because Meta was already at 7.8 out of 100 — third from bottom of 447 companies. Accepting a court-ordered fix is the first corrective act on its record, so Accountability rose from 1.0 to 1.4 out of 5.
- **A settlement is not an admission.** Nothing here treats the payment as proof Meta harmed children. What is scored is narrower: Meta accepted binding limits, outside auditing, and remediation money.
- **The staff-side record still points down.** Litigation alleging Meta used an AI system to pick workers with medical conditions for layoffs is live; a judge said it raises "serious questions going to the merits". That is an allegation, not a ruling.
