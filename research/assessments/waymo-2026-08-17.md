### Score Difference Analysis

**Awareness (+42.5 points scaled).** The published seed implies inconsistent, partly reactive detection. Waymo publishes crash counts broken down by city and by crash type across 220.6 million driverless miles through March 2026, and files every crash to the federal regulator. That is a formal pathway with regular review, which is anchor 4. It is not anchor 5, because no independent auditor checks it.

**Action (+37.5 points scaled).** The seed implies standards that exist on paper. Waymo's Efficacy score of 4 rests on a peer-reviewed crash-rate comparison published in Traffic Injury Prevention and an independent Swiss Re liability-claims analysis reporting roughly 90 percent fewer claims than advanced human-driven vehicles. Outcome data of that quality does not exist for any other lab in this cluster.

**Accountability (+20 points scaled).** Waymo publishes downloadable crash datasets and states the weaknesses of its own benchmark, including that no perfect human-versus-machine comparison exists and that many human crashes go unreported. Voluntarily naming the limits of your own favourable evidence is what anchor 4 asks for.

**Equity (+5 points scaled — barely moved, and it is the weakest dimension).** No autonomous vehicle company anywhere offers a fully driverless wheelchair-accessible ride. Waymo's wheelchair-accessible vans are driven by humans. Waymo backed a Washington DC robotaxi bill that never uses the word wheelchair, while a labour coalition opposed the same bill. The people with the greatest transport need are the ones the driverless service does not reach.

**Empathy (-2.5 points scaled — it fell).** In the KitKat case, Waymo said the cat darted under the car as it pulled away. Video obtained by Mission Local showed the cat under the vehicle for about 25 seconds before it drove off. That is a documented instance of the community account not being treated as primary evidence.

### Recommendation

**The published 35.9 is understated for Waymo and should be revised up to 42.5, moving the band from Developing to Functional.** The published value was a placeholder shared with three unrelated labs and carried no information about this company. The correction should be published with its qualification attached: Waymo reaches no dimension at 4.0, the open school-bus investigation is scored and holds the composite down, and Equity at 2.2 is a real finding about who the service does not serve.

## Key Findings

- **Waymo publishes more checkable outcome evidence about its own harms and benefits than any other lab in this study.** Through March 2026 it reports 220.6 million driverless miles, 94 percent fewer serious-injury crashes and 93 percent fewer pedestrian-injury crashes than human drivers in the same areas, with the underlying data downloadable as CSV files.
- **Independent verification exists in both directions, which is rare.** A peer-reviewed comparison appeared in Traffic Injury Prevention and reinsurer Swiss Re analysed Waymo's liability claims. Separately, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened Preliminary Evaluation PE25013 on 17 October 2025 into Waymo behaviour around stopped school buses.
- **The school-bus problem was found by others and recurred after the fix.** A Texas school district recorded 19 illegal passes of stopped school buses. Waymo recalled software for more than 3,000 vehicles with a 17 November 2025 remedy. A further violation was captured on 1 December 2025, two weeks later.
- **Waymo's weakest area is who it does not serve.** Equity scores 2.2 of 5. No company offers a driverless wheelchair-accessible ride, and Waymo supported a DC bill with no wheelchair requirement.
- **Waymo states the weaknesses of its own favourable data.** Its safety pages say plainly that no perfect comparison between human and machine driving exists today. That voluntary caveat is why Transparency scores 4.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Awareness (3.2) and Action (3.0)** are the strongest. Both rest on published, externally checkable outcome data rather than on stated commitments. Efficacy (AC3) and Root Cause Orientation (S1) both score 4, the only 4s in this study outside Waymo's Transparency.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (2.2)** is the weakest, driven by the absence of any driverless wheelchair-accessible service and by Waymo's support for legislation that omits an accessibility requirement. **Empathy (2.4)** and **Boundaries (2.4)** follow; both carry several absence-of-disclosure scores plus the KitKat validation failure.

## Evidence Gaps

No workforce, staff wellbeing or internal culture data was located, so Self-Sustainability and Internal Consistency both rest on absence. No disaggregated rider-outcome data by identity was located, so Non-Judgment and Bias Awareness rest on absence. The Traffic Injury Prevention article could not be retrieved directly (HTTP 403) and was sourced through its abstract and secondary coverage; its existence, title and journal are verified.

## Scoring Convention Applied

**Absence of disclosure scores 2, never 1.** The floor of 1 is reserved for positive documented evidence of a specific failure. This is the majority convention across the three most recent de-seeding studies and was applied identically to all seven labs in this study. **Absence of disclosure is not evidence of harm.** Where this company does not publish, the low anchor was scored and said so explicitly. A score of 4 requires published institutional data, a regulatory decision or third-party peer review; a score of 5 requires independent audit plus community testimony.
