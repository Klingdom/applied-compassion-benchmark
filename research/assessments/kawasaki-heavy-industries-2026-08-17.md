### Score Difference Analysis

This is the study's most important single result. Kawasaki Heavy Industries assesses at 34.4 against a published seed of 35.9 — a difference of 1.5 points, well inside the noise of any desk-based assessment, with no band change. The placeholder was approximately right. That happened because Kawasaki has both sides of a real evidence base. On the positive side it publishes whistleblower volumes, human rights due diligence conducted with an outside non-profit, worker interviews at overseas sites, supplier corrective action rates, and membership of an external remedy body. That lifts Awareness to 3.0 — the highest dimension score anywhere in this 14-entity study — and Accountability to 2.8. On the negative side, Japan's Ministry of Defense barred it from defence bidding from 26 December 2025 to 11 March 2026 for falsifying inspection data on 66 submarine engines over 33 years, and a separate slush-fund scheme ran for at least 40 years. Those hold Equity, Boundaries and Integrity at or near the baseline. The two sides very nearly cancel.

### Recommendation

The published score is approximately accurate and should be confirmed, not revised. The delta of -1.5 falls below the 5.0-point filing threshold and the band does not change, so **no change proposal is filed for this entity**. This is the only one of the 14 where that is true.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: this is the one company in the study whose placeholder score turned out to be right.** Kawasaki Heavy Industries assesses at 34.4 against a published 35.9 — a gap of 1.5 points, and no band change.
- **Kawasaki publishes numbers most companies hide.** In fiscal 2024 it received 92 whistleblower reports, found 15 compliance violations, and required corrective measures in 14 of them.
- **It told its own regulator about its fraud.** Kawasaki notified Japan's Ministry of Defense that it had falsified inspection data on 66 submarine engines supplied between 1988 and 2021. The Ministry halved the bidding ban to about two and a half months in recognition of that cooperation.
- **The misconduct was long-running and large.** Slush-fund purchasing for submarine crew ran for at least 40 years. Roughly ¥1.7 billion accumulated in six years to March 2024.
- **Kawasaki names its own customers as a human rights risk.** Its due diligence with Business for Social Responsibility lists "high-risk customers" among nine salient risks — unusual candour for a defence manufacturer.

## Strongest Dimensions

Awareness, at 3.0 of 5 — the highest dimension score in the whole 14-entity study — and Accountability at 2.8. Both rest on published, checkable institutional data rather than on statements of intent.

## Weakest Dimensions

Equity and Boundaries, both at 2.0. Nothing was located on how Kawasaki prioritises need, designs for access, or acknowledges historical harm, and no reparative action for the defence-contract misconduct was found.

## Scoring Conventions Applied

- **Absence of disclosure scores 2, never 1.** The floor of 1 is used only where positive documented evidence of a specific failure exists. This is the "2-convention", the more conservative of the two conventions live in this repository.
- **A score of 4 requires published institutional data, a regulatory decision or third-party peer review. A score of 5 requires independent audit plus community testimony.**
- 26 of 40 subdimensions sit at 2. Of those, approximately 20 rest on absence of disclosure rather than on located evidence of a practice.
