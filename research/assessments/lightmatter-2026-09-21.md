## Anti-false-positive screening (§3e-bis) — the proposal is WITHHELD

The delta is −6.2, which exceeds the 5-point magnitude trigger. **No proposal is filed, and the rule is stated in full.**

1. **Baseline provenance (check 1).** APPLIED_CHANGES contains no Lightmatter entry and no prior assessment exists. The published 37.5 is an original flat seed, never revised.
2. **No stale-baseline rationale used (check 2).** The rationale is disclosure absence, not the age of the score.
3. **Directionality (check 3).** Lightmatter was surfaced on **no evidence at all**, neither negative nor positive. A −6.2 downgrade with no documented conduct behind it has nothing to point at. The methodology's instruction to default to lower anchors on absence governs the *score*; it does not by itself justify *moving a published number*.
4. **Rationale consistency (check 4).** Nothing in the record conflicts.
5. **Calibration, and this is the decisive check (check 5).** The gap exists because a 363-person private company publishes no corporate responsibility data. That is true of a large share of the 50-entity ai-labs index. Moving Lightmatter alone on that basis, without applying the same lens to its 49 peers, would create exactly the peer-calibration inconsistency §3e-bis item 5 exists to prevent. **This is a cohort-wide calibration question about how the ai-labs index treats private companies with no disclosure obligation — not a Lightmatter finding.** It is recorded as `flag-for-review` and referred to coordinator/meta-level review.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Lightmatter's published score is not a measurement. All eight areas read exactly 2.5 out of 5, which is the fingerprint of a placeholder, and this is the first time anyone has researched the company.
- Why it matters: the score that came out, 31.3 out of 100, measures what Lightmatter publishes, not how it behaves. It is a private company of about 363 people with no responsibility report, no ESG disclosure and no published complaints process.
- Why it matters: that gap is not Lightmatter's alone, so it is referred rather than applied. Many of the 50 companies in the AI-labs index are private and publish nothing. Downgrading one of them tonight would rank companies by paperwork.
- Why it matters: the strongest thing Lightmatter does is upstream. Its photonic chips cut the energy AI computing needs at the source — a reported eight times the data throughput at one-fifth the power — which is why Systemic Thinking scores 2.8, its only score above the Developing band.
- Why it matters: employees describe a mission-driven team under strain. Reviews cite aggressive timelines and patchy coordination during fast growth, which is why Boundaries scores 2.0 out of 5.
