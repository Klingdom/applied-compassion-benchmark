## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Djibouti does one thing genuinely well and it is not political. UNHCR calls Djibouti a regional leader in refugee response and says the government has included refugees in national education, health care and employment. That is a real, externally recognised compassion practice and it lifts several scores.
- Why it matters: the same country jails its critics. Freedom House 2026 says opposition activity is severely constrained, journalists who criticise the president are regularly harassed or arrested, and human rights groups cannot register freely.
- Why it matters: hunger is now an emergency. A UN-backed monitor warned on 17 July 2026 that more than 256,000 people, about 25 per cent of Djibouti's population, face crisis or emergency hunger, including 70 per cent of camp residents.
- Why it matters: the rules were changed to keep one man in office. Parliament ratified a constitutional amendment on 2 November 2025 removing the age limit, and Ismail Omar Guelleh won a sixth term on 10 April 2026 against a single lesser-known challenger.
- Why it matters: the band changes but no proposal can be filed. Djibouti assesses at 17.5, which is inside the Critical band, but the 2.8-point gap from the published 20.3 falls below the 5.0-point threshold required to file a change proposal.

## Strongest Dimensions

Boundaries (2.0), Action (2.0) and Systemic Thinking (2.0). All three rest on the same fact: a long-standing open-door refugee policy with refugees folded into national systems, recognised by UNHCR and sustained through drought.

## Weakest Dimensions

Accountability (1.0, at the floor). No failure data is published, harm to detained journalists is unacknowledged, the constitution was amended rather than the course corrected, and no repair is offered. Further downward movement in Accountability is arithmetically unavailable.

## Evidence Gaps

- The Amnesty International Djibouti country page returned HTTP 504 and could not be read; Freedom House 2026 and UNHCR were used instead.
- No Djiboutian government budget contribution to refugee services could be separated from donor funding, so Action and Systemic Thinking are scored conservatively.
- Freedom House's published 2026 civil liberties figure appeared malformed in the retrieved summary and was not used numerically; only its narrative findings were scored.

## Attribution Discipline

Only the conduct of the Djibouti state is scored. Harm by non-state armed groups, by foreign states, and by natural disaster is excluded from the state's score. Where such harm occurred, what is measured is the state's own response to it. Every source date was checked against its event date, and publication dates were not treated as event dates.
