## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Algeria expelled thousands of people into the desert without checking who they were. Human Rights Watch says about 5,000 people were pushed to the Niger border between January and April, with at least five deaths, and Niger reported 16,000 more in April and May alone.
- Why it matters: the state punishes people for what they post. An Algiers court gave Mohamed Tadjadit five years in prison on 20 January 2025 for social media posts, and gave French journalist Christophe Gleizes seven years on 29 June 2025.
- Why it matters: Algeria has real services but no public record of failure. It provides free health care and education, yet publishes no outcome or failure data, and Human Rights Watch documented no prosecutions of perpetrators.
- Why it matters: the one visible act of mercy did not change the system. President Tebboune pardoned writer Boualem Sansal on 12 November 2025, but a pardon leaves the conviction standing and more than 200 activists and journalists remain jailed.
- Why it matters: the published score was never measured. Algeria's 20.3 is a placeholder shared byte-for-byte with 11 other countries; this assessment replaces it with evidence.

## Strongest Dimensions

Action (2.0). Algeria has genuine state capacity: free health care, free education and large universal subsidies funded from its own revenue rather than aid. The failure is in transparency and in who is excluded, not in whether services exist.

## Weakest Dimensions

Accountability (1.0, at the floor) and Integrity (1.2). All five accountability anchors are met at the lowest level at once: harm denied, practice continued after documentation, no data published, failures recurring, and no repair. Further downward movement in Accountability is arithmetically unavailable.

## Evidence Gaps

- Algeria's own social-protection outcome data (unemployment allowance coverage, health outcomes) could not be sourced from a current primary document, so Action and Equity are scored conservatively on the lower anchor.
- No Algerian government response to the Human Rights Watch findings was located, so Accountability is scored on absence of a response rather than a rejected one.
- Evidence availability is skewed toward rights reporting. Service-delivery evidence of comparable tier was not obtainable, which pushes Action and Boundaries down relative to a fully sourced picture.

## Attribution Discipline

Only the conduct of the Algeria state is scored. Harm by non-state armed groups, by foreign states, and by natural disaster is excluded from the state's score. Where such harm occurred, what is measured is the state's own response to it. Every source date was checked against its event date, and publication dates were not treated as event dates.
