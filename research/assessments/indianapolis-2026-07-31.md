### Score Difference Analysis

The -2.1 difference is partly a grid artifact — seven published dimensions sit at 2.5 — and partly real movement in Accountability (2.5 to 2.2) and Equity (2.0 to 1.8). The finding is carefully bounded. Dailen Brewer, 18, filed a federal complaint on 20 July 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Indiana against IMPD Sergeant Robyn Frazier and the City of Indianapolis, alleging false arrest, fabricated evidence, malicious prosecution, false imprisonment and defamation. Those allegations have not been decided by any court and are not scored as established misconduct. Three facts in the record are not in dispute, and those are what moved the score. First, Brewer was arrested on 12 April 2026 on a Level 1 felony attempted-murder charge and, unable to post bond, was held in the Marion County Adult Detention Center. Second, on 16 April 2026 a Marion County deputy prosecuting attorney dropped all criminal charges after reviewing the body camera footage. Third, as of 29 July 2026 IMPD had not corrected its public account of the incident. The third fact is an Accountability finding under the lowest anchor for harm acknowledgment, independent of how the lawsuit resolves. Against that sits a genuinely strong Action profile: an almost $28 million Office of Public Health and Safety budget for 2026, about $11 million for homelessness including the Housing Hub low-barrier shelter, $4.5 million for the Gun Violence Reduction Strategy, and a reported near 50% decline in murders since 2021.

### Recommendation

Confirm the published score of 35.9, band Developing. Retain a watch flag with two triggers: a ruling or settlement in Brewer v. Frazier and the City of Indianapolis, and any correction or non-correction of the public record by IMPD. A finding of liability would support a proposal on Accountability and Equity together; a correction of the public account would remove the Accountability finding entirely.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: a prosecutor dropped an attempted-murder charge after watching the officer's own body camera.** Dailen Brewer, then 18, was arrested on 12 April 2026 in Riverside Park in Indianapolis. On 16 April 2026 a Marion County deputy prosecuting attorney dropped all charges after reviewing the footage.
- **Why it matters: he could not buy his way out while it was sorted.** The charge was a Level 1 felony, so Brewer could not post bond and spent four days in the Marion County Adult Detention Center.
- **Why it matters: the public record still said he tried to kill a police officer.** As of 29 July 2026, more than three months after the charges were dropped, the Indianapolis Metropolitan Police Department had not corrected its public account of the incident. That, not the lawsuit, is what moved the Accountability score.
- **Why it matters: nothing has been decided in court.** Brewer filed a federal complaint on 20 July 2026 alleging false arrest, fabricated evidence, malicious prosecution, false imprisonment and defamation. No court has ruled. Those allegations were not scored as established misconduct.
- **Why it matters: Indianapolis spends seriously on the causes of harm.** The Office of Public Health and Safety has an almost $28 million budget for 2026, about $11 million goes to homelessness including the Housing Hub low-barrier shelter, $4.5 million to the Gun Violence Reduction Strategy, and the city reports a near 50% decline in murders since 2021.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Action (2.6) and Systemic Thinking (2.6).** Indianapolis funds an Office of Public Health and Safety designed explicitly to address the root causes of violence through social programmes, reports outcomes rather than activity counts, and sustained that investment despite losing about $10 million in forecast revenue to state legislation.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (1.8) and Accountability (2.2).** The city publishes no disaggregated policing outcome data by race, and the public account of an incident its own footage contradicted was still uncorrected more than three months later.

The movement rests only on the undisputed sequence: a Level 1 felony charge, four days in jail, all charges dropped by the prosecutor on 16 April 2026 after reviewing the department's own body camera footage, and no correction of the public account as of 29 July 2026. Directionality is consistent: negative evidence, negative movement, no upgrade.
