## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Future-date verification — the scanner's "September 1" warning is resolved.** 1 September 2026 is a **genuinely scheduled future date**: it is the start of Oracle's fiscal second quarter, and the reported plan is to reduce payroll before it. It is not a misdated past event. The **planned round has not been executed** and is scored as a plan, not as a completed action.

**Evidence correction — the June baseline overstated the scale.** The 2026-06-14 applied change cited "30,000 layoffs (~18% global workforce)". Oracle's own fiscal-2026 disclosure records a workforce decline of **21,000 people, a 13% decline**, to about 141,000. The correct figure is lower than the one used to set the current score. This is recorded because it cuts **against** deepening the score, not in favour of it.

**Mixed dated/undated sourcing.** The scanner listed two undated sources. Five independently dated reports of the same plan were located and are used here.

**Baseline provenance.** The published 14.7 was set on 2026-06-14 by a documented assessment of Oracle's own conduct — the fiscal-2026 reduction and the coercive severance terms attached to it. It is a recent, evidence-based baseline. No "stale baseline" reasoning is used.

**Attribution.** The layoffs, the severance terms and the capital allocation are all Oracle's own decisions.

### Analysis

The assessed composite is 15.0 against a published 14.7 — a movement of 0.3 points. The published score is confirmed.

The new evidence is a continuation of an already-scored pattern, and it points in two directions at once.

It deepens the pattern in one sense: Oracle is reported to be planning a further round of cuts, reaching double-digit percentages on some teams, timed to land before a quarter start. The financial logic is explicit and disclosed. Fiscal-2026 capital expenditure was $55.7 billion against $21.2 billion the year before, leaving Oracle $23.7 billion cash-flow negative. It raised $43 billion in debt and $5 billion in equity, and expects about $40 billion more. Payroll is the adjustment variable.

It weakens the case for going lower in another. Oracle's own filings put the fiscal-2026 reduction at 21,000 people, 13% of headcount — materially smaller than the 30,000 and ~18% figures used when the current score was set. Applying a further downgrade on a plan that has not been executed, when the executed figure is smaller than assumed, would compound an error rather than correct one.

The honest position is a confirmation with a dated re-test. Oracle sits at 14.7 in the Critical band with Empathy at 1.20 of 5, already close to the framework floor. What matters next is not the headcount number but the **terms**: the June assessment turned on coercive severance conditions, not on scale alone. Those terms for the September round are unknown.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Oracle is cutting people to pay for computers. Fiscal-2026 capital spending hit $55.7 billion, up from $21.2 billion, and the company is reported to be planning another round of job cuts before 1 September 2026.
- Oracle's own filings show its workforce shrank by 21,000 people, a 13% decline, in the year to 31 May 2026, to about 141,000, with $1.8 billion of restructuring charges and up to $2.1 billion anticipated.
- Why it matters for the score: the earlier figure was too high. The benchmark set Oracle's current score partly on a reported 30,000 cuts. The disclosed number is 21,000. That correction argues against cutting the score further, and it is recorded openly.
- The planned round has not happened. Managers have been asked for names; Oracle declined to comment. A plan is scored as a plan.
- The score is confirmed at 14.7 out of 100, in the Critical band. It will be re-tested after 1 September 2026, when the terms of any new round become known.

## Strongest Dimensions

Systemic Thinking (2.20 of 5). Oracle states the causal chain openly — AI adoption and capital expenditure driving headcount decisions — and discloses the financial detail. Naming the cause is not the same as addressing it, which is why the score is 2.20 rather than higher.

## Weakest Dimensions

Empathy (1.20 of 5) and Integrity (1.40 of 5). Affective resonance, perspective-taking, validation and cultural empathy all sit at the floor anchor of 1. Consistency under pressure is at 1: commitments to the workforce give way to capital commitments.

## Evidence Gaps

Oracle publishes financial and restructuring data but no workforce social-performance data: no severance terms, no redeployment outcomes, no grievance statistics. The terms of the planned September round are unknown and are the single most decision-relevant missing fact. Where disclosure is simply absent, the low anchor was applied and is stated as an absence of disclosure, not as evidence of harm.
