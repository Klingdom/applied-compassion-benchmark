## Key Findings

- **WHILL scores 26.2 of 100 because it publishes almost nothing about the disabled people who use its products.** Thirty-two of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure. **No adverse finding of any kind was located either.** WHILL is a private company of roughly 136 people with no legal duty to publish what this benchmark measures.
- **The strongest evidence about WHILL comes from the disability press, and it is mixed.** New Mobility, an established disability publication, headlined its review "Affordable Power and Portability for Some" and reports that **Medicare and Medicaid do not cover the Model C2**. A US$3,999 price is low for a power chair and still out of reach for anyone dependent on public coverage.
- **The airport fleets are a real access gain and a real consent problem at the same time.** At Heathrow, Anchorage, Miami, Los Angeles, Seattle and Detroit, a traveller can cross a terminal alone rather than wait for a human pusher. But the airport or ground handler buys the fleet. The person riding in the sensor-equipped vehicle did not choose it, and no consent or data-handling disclosure was located.
- **WHILL says the service frees airport staff for higher-touch support. No data supports that.** The claim appears in company announcements and nowhere else. Under this methodology an unevidenced benefit claim is scored as unevidenced.
- **Six of eight dimensions sit exactly at the absence floor of 25.0.** Only Equity and Boundaries rise above it, both on the strength of the airport service and the price point.
