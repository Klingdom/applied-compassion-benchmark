## Key findings

- Abridge has the strongest outcome evidence in this study, and it is real. Three peer-reviewed studies measure whether the product actually helps the clinicians it serves, including a Kaiser Permanente evaluation in NEJM AI covering 1,306 clinicians and more than 4 million patient encounters. That is the largest published independent validation of any ambient AI scribe.
- The company treats language as an equity problem, not a feature. Its speech recognition is validated for 28 languages, and it deployed at Cambridge Health Alliance, a public safety-net system where 43% of patients speak a language other than English at home, and at AltaMed, the largest Federally Qualified Health Center in the United States.
- Patient consent is the weak point, and it is documented rather than alleged. Abridge's terms make the clinician responsible for getting consent, and the American Bar Association reports that the consent script Abridge recommends does not say where the audio goes, how long it is kept, who else handles it, or whether it trains the next model.
- Three California patients sued Sutter Health and MemorialCare in federal court on 8 April 2026 over recordings made with the platform. Abridge is not a defendant and the claims are not decided. Nothing here is scored as proven harm.
- The score falls 24.0 points because the published 60.9 was never measured, not because Abridge was found to be doing something wrong. Most of the fall is missing disclosure on 25 of 40 subdimensions.
