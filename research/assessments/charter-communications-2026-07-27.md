## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Charter did not just lose a discrimination case, it was penalised for trying to make the complaint go away. A New York appeals court upheld on 23 July 2026 a finding that Charter Communications unlawfully retaliated against employee Jon E. Eisen after he filed an age-discrimination complaint.
- The Appellate Division, Fourth Department upheld the New York State Division of Human Rights determination, ordering $7,500 for mental anguish and a $50,000 civil fine and penalty payable to the New York State Comptroller, both with 9% interest from 13 June 2025.
- Charter sent Eisen three letters — in September 2019, June 2020 and March 2021 — pressuring him to withdraw his complaint, citing a binding arbitration agreement and threatening him with Charter’s legal costs and attorney fees.
- The court found that such threats could have dissuaded a reasonable person from pursuing the discrimination, and that substantial evidence supported the retaliation finding.
- The assessed composite of 24.4 is 2.2 points below the published 26.6. This is an adjudicated finding but of small financial magnitude against a single complainant, so it is a confirmation with a watch flag rather than a downgrade.
