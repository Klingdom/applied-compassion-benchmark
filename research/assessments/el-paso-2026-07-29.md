## Assessment Context

- **Attribution rule is decisive here.** Everything the scan flagged for El Paso is federal conduct: ICE's decision to extend the Camp East Montana operator's contract on a no-bid basis through 30 September 2027 (reported 27-28 July 2026), and the roughly fivefold year-over-year surge in ICE arrests with children separated from parents at street stops, homes, job sites and immigration check-ins (26 July 2026). Under the standing attribution rule, harm inflicted by another actor does not lower the subject entity's score. None of this is scored against the City of El Paso.
- **What the city itself did.** No in-window action by the City of El Paso was located. The city's most recent relevant own-conduct is pre-window: on 13-14 April 2026 the council unanimously approved revised ICE-coordination guidelines and directed staff to draft zoning revisions intended to deter construction of new detention facilities. Those guidelines are genuinely mixed — they also establish that officers must notify ICE when someone is found to be in the country illegally.
- **Consequence for scoring.** With no in-window municipal conduct and the federal conduct excluded by attribution, El Paso is confirmed at its published score. The -0.3 movement is a 0.2-grid artifact.

## Key Findings

- The attribution rule does almost all the work here. ICE extending the Camp East Montana operator's contract on a no-bid basis through 30 September 2027 at a potential added cost of $776 million is a federal decision, not a city one.
- The reported fivefold year-over-year surge in ICE arrests in the El Paso region, with children separated from parents at street stops, homes, job sites and immigration check-ins, is likewise federal conduct and is not scored against the city.
- No in-window conduct by the City of El Paso was found. The city's most recent own-conduct is the 13-14 April 2026 council decision on ICE coordination and detention-facility zoning.
- Those April guidelines are genuinely mixed: they restrict ICE operations in city-owned private areas and target future detention facilities through zoning, but they also require officers to notify ICE when someone is found to be in the country illegally.
- Composite moves -0.3 to 35.6, inside the 0.2-step scoring grid.
