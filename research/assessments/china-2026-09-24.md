## Why this entity was assessed

The State Council's Regulations on Exit-Entry Administration, Decree No. 841, took effect on 15 September 2026. Human Rights Watch reported on it on 16 September. China was last assessed on 2026-07-18 (confirmation, 19.5).

## Evidence-date, provenance, attribution and screening checks

**ATTRIBUTION: CLEAN.** A State Council decree is the act of the Chinese state. This is directly attributable conduct, not third-party conduct, not an allegation, and not a diplomatic position.

**EVIDENCE TIER: 3.** Human Rights Watch is a watchdog NGO. Only one source was located inside the window. The decree itself is a primary government instrument, but the analysis of its effect is HRW's. A single tier-3 source is enough to move two subdimensions and not enough to move a dimension vector materially.

**BASELINE PROVENANCE (read first).** The published 19.5 was **set on 2026-05-18 by a founder-approved band crossing** from 20.3 (Developing) to 19.5 (Critical), recorded in `research/APPLIED_CHANGES.md`. That change docked BND by a quarter step, from 2.0 to 1.75, on the EU's 20th sanctions package designating 16 Chinese entities for dual-use supply to Russia's military-industrial complex, and it formalised a new tier-3 BND sub-anchor for state facilitation of an ally's war conduct. The score was confirmed again on 2026-07-18. **This baseline is recent and was produced by China's own documented conduct. No stale-baseline argument is available or used here.**

**METHODOLOGY NOTE ON THE 1.75.** The published BND of 1.75 came from that quarter-step dock and cannot be reproduced from five integer subdimension anchors, which can only yield multiples of 0.2. Re-scoring BND from integers therefore produces a small mechanical shift independent of any evidence. This is disclosed so the -0.15 in BND is not read as a larger finding than it is.

**WHAT THE DECREE DOES.** HRW reports that the 19-article regulation authorises officials to "dissuade" citizens from travelling to "high-risk" countries and permits authorities to bar nationals from leaving for up to three years on vaguely defined security grounds. HRW states: "The Chinese government is tightening its rules to decide arbitrarily who and cannot leave China, violating the internationally protected right to leave one's country." HRW also reports that experts warn the restrictions "will further chill expression and academic freedom, increasing self-censorship."

**FLOOR LIMITATION.** Published 19.5 sits 19.5 points above zero, and Accountability and Integrity are already at the 1.0 floor, which is where an arbitrary, unappealable exit ban would otherwise land. The dimensions the decree bears on that are not already at the floor are Awareness and Boundaries. Those are the only two re-scored.

## Determination

**CONFIRM 19.5.** The measured 18.4 is 1.1 points below the published value. That is under the 5-point magnitude trigger, and the band does not change (Critical to Critical). Of the -1.1, roughly -0.4 composite points are the mechanical effect of re-deriving the quarter-step BND value from integer anchors. The evidence-driven component is the A5, B2 and B5 docks.

A single tier-3 source describing a decree that has been in force for nine days does not justify moving a founder-approved baseline. The decree's real effects — who is barred, on what grounds, with what recourse — are not yet documented. That is what the watch flag is for.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: China's score of 19.5 out of 100 holds, near the bottom of the index. A new border rule, Decree No. 841, took effect on 15 September 2026.
- Why it matters: officials can now stop a Chinese citizen leaving the country for up to three years. Human Rights Watch says the grounds are vaguely defined national security concerns.
- Why it matters: the rule also lets officials talk citizens out of travelling to countries the state labels high-risk. Human Rights Watch says this violates the protected right to leave your own country.
- Why it matters: the score barely moves because it is already very low. Accountability and Integrity are already at 1 out of 5, which is where an unappealable travel ban would otherwise push them.
- Why it matters: the rule passed with no published check on who it would harm. That is why Anticipatory Awareness drops to 1 out of 5.
