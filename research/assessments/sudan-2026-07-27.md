## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Sudan is not recovering from famine, it is sliding back toward it. The World Food Programme warned on 14 July 2026 that conflict, aid-funding cuts and shipping disruption threaten to reverse the gains made after famine took hold.
- WFP has cut the number of people it can assist from 5 million a year ago to about 3.5 million, and reduced rations in Tawila in Darfur, against a $646 million funding gap following cuts by the United States, European donors and Britain.
- Over 650,000 people have sought safety in Tawila, a once-small desert town now holding a population the size of Luxembourg, with widespread cholera and inadequate shelter.
- Almost 19.5 million people face crisis levels of hunger, and 825,000 children under five are expected to suffer severe acute malnutrition in 2026.
- Sudan sits at the published floor of 0 out of 100 — the lowest score on the countries index. A floor entity cannot move lower, so this is a confirmation at 0 with a sustained watch flag.
