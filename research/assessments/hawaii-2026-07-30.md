## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: Hawaii charged its own second-highest official.** A grand jury indicted Lieutenant Governor Sylvia Luke on 25 July 2026 on 12 counts including conspiracy to commit bribery, bribery and falsifying campaign committee reports. The charges were brought by the Hawaii Attorney General’s office after a months-long investigation.
- **Why it matters: an individual being charged is not the same as an institution failing.** The alleged scheme dates to 2022, when Luke chaired the state House Finance Committee. What the state did in this window was investigate and charge her. Governor Josh Green called on her to resign.
- **The scale of the case.** Four others were indicted, including a lobbyist, a former Public Utilities Commission chair, a state Department of Transportation airports administrator and a former state representative. The allegation involves $70,000 in campaign contributions tied to a COVID-19 testing contract.
- **Why it matters: this score was set eleven days ago and it holds.** Hawaii’s composite of 56.3 out of 100 was produced by the assessment of 19 July 2026, which corrected a broken bulk-import figure of 95.9. This assessment confirms it with a difference of exactly zero.
- **Hawaii’s deepest problem is unchanged.** About 870 people, close to 23% of everyone the state incarcerates, are held in a private prison in Eloy, Arizona, 2,600 miles away. A 2026 bill would require only a quarter of them returned by July 2029.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Accountability (3.8) and Systemic Thinking (3.8).** Hawaii commissioned and published an independent investigation of its own conduct in the Lahaina fire, produced 84 findings and 140 recommendations, and shared them with the national fire service. It also taxed its own dominant industry to pay for climate resilience and defended that tax successfully in federal court. The indictment of its own Lieutenant Governor by its own Attorney General is consistent with that pattern.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Boundaries (2.4)**, the only dimension in the Developing band. Hawaii struggles to tell people honestly what it can and cannot do, to decline with alternatives, and to sustain the workforce doing the helping. **Action (3.0) and Integrity (3.0)** follow: the state commits real money and then delivers slowly, and it holds nearly a quarter of its incarcerated population 2,600 miles away while framing its social policy in the language of ohana.
