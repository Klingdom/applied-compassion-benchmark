## Baseline-establishment note

This is a **first-ever assessment**. Intuition Robotics does not appear in any published Compassion Benchmark index. There is no prior score to compare against.

**Corporate status check (required before assessment).** Intuition Robotics is operating under that name. In March 2026 it announced ElliQ's availability as a Smart Care Device for eligible Washington State Medicaid recipients, and the New York State Office for the Aging published a project update on the ElliQ programme in February 2026 ([NYSOFA project update, February 2026](https://aging.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/02/nysofa-elliq-project-update-2026.pdf)). Total funding of about $83 million is reported as of July 2026. No acquisition, rename or insolvency event was found.

**Evidence base.** This entity carries an unusual evidence profile for a private company of its size: a **US state-government outcome evaluation** of a real deployment (tier 4), a **peer-reviewed journal article** that names limitations and unintended consequences (tier 4), a **ClinicalTrials.gov registration** (tier 5), and a **public-payer coverage decision** in Washington State (tier 4). That is the evidence class this benchmark is designed to reward, and it is why Intuition Robotics — a company with roughly $83 million raised — outscores a company with $10.1 billion of revenue in this batch.

**Disclosure density.** Of 40 subdimensions, **19 rest primarily on absence of disclosure** and 21 rest on positive located evidence.

## Key Findings

- **A US state government has published three years of outcome data on this company's product, and that single fact drives its score.** New York's Office for the Aging reports that 94% of ElliQ users feel less lonely and 97% feel better overall, across 834 enrolled older adults. Very few private robotics companies have anything comparable.
- **Evidence beats size in this benchmark, and this entity proves it.** Intuition Robotics has raised about $83 million. It scores 6.3 points higher than Intuitive Surgical, which earned $10.1 billion in 2025 — because a third party has examined what its product actually does to people and published the answer.
- **The company publishes its own limits.** Its peer-reviewed paper names who cannot use ElliQ — people without speech, hearing or hand movement — and says unintended consequences must be monitored. Voluntary, unflattering disclosure is what these anchors reward.
- **Consent is the open risk.** ElliQ's camera and microphone are always on in the homes of older adults, some of whom have cognitive impairment. A privacy policy exists and the design is opt-in, but no independent review of whether users genuinely understand what is collected was found.
- **Demand far exceeds supply, and nobody publishes what happens to the people turned away.** More than 3,500 older adults applied to the New York programme; 834 were enrolled. No alternative pathway for the other 2,600-plus was located.

## Strongest Dimensions
- **Action (60.0)** — the only dimension in this batch above 55, earned on a government-published multi-year outcome evaluation and sustained longitudinal engagement.
- **Awareness (55.0)** and **Equity (45.0)** — proactive detection is the product itself, and cost, digital-literacy and setup barriers are documented as removed through public-payer routes.

## Weakest Dimensions
- **Integrity (35.0)** — untested rather than failed. No leadership transition, no costly-commitment case, no internal culture evidence.
- **Empathy (40.0)** and **Accountability (40.0)** — strong affective evidence on one axis (users feel cared about) sits next to complete silence on non-judgment, cultural adaptation, complaint validation and repair.

## Evidence Gaps
- No outcome data disaggregated by race, income, language or disability.
- No published complaint or harm-reporting process for users or families.
- No employee-side evidence of any kind.
- No independent audit of consent comprehension in a population that may include cognitive impairment.
