## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the people counting Afghanistan's flood dead do not work for Afghanistan. The United Nations reported on 22 July 2026 that at least 26 people were killed, 23 of them in Nuristan province, after flash floods struck eastern Afghanistan on 20 and 21 July 2026.
- The floods destroyed at least 22 houses, severely damaged 45 more, and damaged four bridges, six road sections, irrigation systems and the water network — cutting safe drinking water for about 1,000 families around Parun.
- Response capacity came from outside the state: OCHA coordinated, UNICEF set up a field clinic in Parun, and mobile health teams and NGOs delivered care.
- Afghanistan sits at the published floor of 0 out of 100 — the lowest score on the index. The floor was set by the de facto authorities' own conduct, principally the formally recognised system of gender apartheid recorded in the 2026-05-29 applied change.
- The attribution rule applies to the flood itself: a natural disaster is not the state's conduct. What is scored is the absence of detection, warning, response and repair capacity. A floor entity cannot move lower, so the assessment confirms at 0.
