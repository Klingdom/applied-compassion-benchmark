## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: the thing Dhaka was flagged for did not happen in Dhaka.** The alleged enforced disappearance of Miraj Sheikh took place at Joymonir Ghol near the Sundarbans in Mongla, Bagerhat district — roughly 200 kilometres from Dhaka — and the force involved is the Bangladesh Coast Guard, a national body.
- **Why it matters: it is the first case of its kind in two years.** Human Rights Watch reported on 20 July 2026 that this appears to be the first known enforced disappearance case in Bangladesh since the July 2024 uprising.
- **Why it matters: the family was told two different things.** After Coast Guard officers reportedly detained Sheikh on the night of 10 April 2026 and took him away by speedboat, his family went to the Coast Guard office in Digraj and was told he was "on an operation". When they returned that afternoon, they were told he had never been there.
- **Why it matters: a court already ordered an investigation and it has not happened.** A Bangladeshi court ordered authorities to investigate on 12 July 2026. Human Rights Watch reported eight days later that they should comply with it.
- **Why it matters: the national government went the other way on prevention.** Human Rights Watch says the government should reverse its decision to scrap reforms aimed at preventing enforced disappearances. That reversal is national policy and it applies in Dhaka.
