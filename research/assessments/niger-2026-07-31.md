### Score Difference Analysis

The -3.7 difference reproduces the 2026-07-26 assessment exactly and has two parts. Part of it is a grid artifact: all eight published dimensions sit at 1.5, a value that five whole-number subdimension scores cannot produce, so the nearest reachable values are 1.4 or 1.6. The rest is a real finding, concentrated in Accountability and Integrity, both of which fall to the floor at 1.0. Human Rights Watch reported on 23 July 2026 that Niger's military junta has entrenched authoritarian rule and dismantled the institutions capable of holding it to account three years after the July 2023 coup. The specific findings that drive the floor are: at least 18 exiled opposition figures temporarily stripped of Nigerien citizenship since late 2024, most recently Mariama Djibrine in June 2026; the continued arbitrary detention of former President Bazoum and human rights defender Moussa Tiangari; and the fact that the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention issued opinions in February 2025 and May 2026 finding those detentions arbitrary and calling for release, which the junta ignored. Harm continuing after two formal UN findings is the definition of the lowest Accountability anchor.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: this is the same finding twice, not a new one.** The Human Rights Watch report dated 23 July 2026 was already assessed on 26 July 2026 and produced the identical result. Nothing new emerged between 26 and 31 July 2026, so this cycle confirms rather than adds.
- **Why it matters: Niger has been taking away people's nationality.** Since late 2024 the authorities have temporarily stripped at least 18 exiled opposition figures of their Nigerien citizenship, using a 2024 order that created a national database of people suspected of terrorism. The most recent was Mariama Djibrine in June 2026.
- **Why it matters: the UN said release them, twice, and nothing happened.** The UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention found in February 2025 and again in May 2026 that the detentions of former President Mohamed Bazoum and human rights defender Moussa Tiangari were arbitrary and called for their release. Both remain held.
- **Why it matters: criticism is now a prison offence.** The junta amended Niger's cybercrime law in 2024 to reintroduce prison sentences and fines for broadly defined public order offences.
- **Why it matters: three years in, the direction is still downward.** Human Rights Watch describes a junta that has consolidated sweeping and unchecked powers and systematically weakened the institutions capable of holding it to account, rather than stabilising.
