## The withholding decision, stated first

This assessment measures Tronox at 32.5, which is 13.7 points above the published 18.8 and crosses from Critical to Developing. Both filing triggers in Section 3f are met. **No proposal is filed anyway.**

The reason is Section 3e-bis rule 5 and a direct precedent. On 2026-07-21 the benchmark applied a **+13.7** upgrade to Freeport-McMoRan, from 18.8 to 32.5 — the same published value, the same measured value, the same delta as this assessment produces. The record in `research/APPLIED_CHANGES.md` states that the assessor routed that proposal as `flag-for-review`, that the founder applied it by explicit override, and that the assessor's calibration note said the correct remedy was "a band-wide review of never-assessed Critical-band Fortune 500 entities, not an isolated upgrade."

That an independent assessment of a different company in a different sector lands on exactly the same number from exactly the same starting point is not a coincidence. It is evidence that 18.8 is a **shared placeholder vector**, not a measurement of either company. Tronox's published dimensions (`1.5 / 2.0 / 2.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 / 1.5`) are **byte-identical to Penn Virginia and Rex Energy**, two rows this same cycle found to be defunct companies. A vector shared with dissolved entities cannot be a measurement of Tronox.

Filing an isolated upgrade here would relitigate that calibration one company at a time, which rule 5 forbids. The measured vector is recorded in full, with a subdimension sidecar, so that a band-wide review can use it. It is not proposed for application.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Tronox's published score of 18.8 out of 100 is not a measurement. It is the same eight numbers given to Penn Virginia and Rex Energy, two companies that no longer exist.
- Why it matters: measured against evidence, Tronox scores 32.5 out of 100 — 13.7 points higher, and into a different band. That is exactly the same jump the benchmark applied to Freeport-McMoRan in July 2026 from the same starting number.
- Why it matters: the benchmark is not filing that change. Fixing a shared placeholder one company at a time is how a ranking drifts, so this is referred for a review of the whole cluster.
- Why it matters: the positive evidence is Tronox's own report. It says it beat its 2025 targets, cutting emissions intensity 27% against a 25% goal. No outside body has checked that.
- Why it matters: the safety record is the firmest positive. Tronox reports record improvement in injury rates and six sites with zero injuries in 2025.
