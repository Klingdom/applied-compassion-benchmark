### Score Difference Analysis

The published vector `4 / 4 / 4 / 3.5 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4` is byte-identical across four robotics labs occupying consecutive alphabetical ranks. It is a never-assessed uniform seed. Apptronik has never been individually assessed against the 40 behavioural anchors before this report.

The published value asserts practice at the fourth anchor across all eight dimensions: published disaggregated data, formal pathways, documented reparative action, independent verification and community confirmation. Apptronik publishes none of those things. 38 of the 40 subdimensions here score at the low anchor of 2, which under this study's fixed rule means **no evidence was found in either direction** — not that harm was found.

### Recommendation

The published score is **overstated** and should be revised down to 26.2, band Developing. This is a seed correction, not a conduct downgrade.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Apptronik is valued at about $5 billion and publishes almost nothing a compassion benchmark can read. Its assessed score of 26.2 is the second lowest in the study, and it is a disclosure result, not a conduct result.
- Thirty-seven of 40 subdimensions score at the low anchor because no evidence was found either way. No adverse conduct of any kind was located — no lawsuits, no safety incidents, no labour findings, no regulatory action.
- The one third-party statement located is a supplier case study by Texas Instruments saying Apptronik "prioritizes safety and is helping to influence and maintain safety standards, specifically for humanoid robots". A supplier case study is not independent verification.
- Apollo runs at Mercedes-Benz sites in Berlin and Kecskemét, at GXO Logistics and at Jabil. No published outcome data, worker consultation record or union agreement was found for any deployment.
- The $935 million Series A is scored at exactly one subdimension, Resource Mobilization, and nowhere else. A funding round is not a compassion signal.
