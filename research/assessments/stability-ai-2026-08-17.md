### Score Difference Analysis

**PRE-ADJUDICATION CHECK PERFORMED, AND IT CHANGED THE FINDING.** The task brief warned against restating a filing as an outcome. The UK case is now an outcome. In Getty Images (US) Inc v Stability AI Limited [2025] EWHC 2863 (Ch), judgment of 4 November 2025, the court dismissed the secondary copyright and passing-off claims and found only a trademark infringement it described as historic and extremely limited. Getty abandoned its primary copyright and database-right claims before closing submissions, and was granted leave to appeal in December 2025. **Stability largely won, and that was scored.**

**Two US cases remain undecided** — Getty Images v. Stability AI, refiled in the Northern District of California on 14 August 2025, and Andersen v. Stability AI, third amended complaint February 2026. Neither was scored as proven harm.

**Awareness (-12.5 points scaled) and Empathy (-12.5 points scaled) fell on the training-data finding.** The Stanford Internet Observatory found 3,226 suspected child sexual abuse images in LAION-5B, assembled from the web with no consultation of child safety experts. Stability's public response emphasised that it trained on a filtered subset and did not release the version Stanford examined. That is distancing, which caps Validation and Historical Harm Acknowledgment at 2.

**Boundaries (-7.5 points scaled) held up best at 2.2**, because open-weight releases genuinely let others build without depending on Stability. That practice has since narrowed as the company moved to monetised, safety-filtered endpoints.

**Integrity (0 points scaled — the seed already had it at the low anchor, and it stays there).** Founder Emad Mostaque left in March 2024, Prem Akkaraju became chief executive in June 2024 alongside an $80 million recapitalisation, and the one practice with a clear compassion reading was narrowed afterwards.

**The published 32.8 is overstated and should be revised down to 26.2. The band does not change.** Thirty-one of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure, so the magnitude is an upper bound. **The Getty litigation should not be cited as evidence against Stability in any published material**: the UK case concluded largely in the company's favour and the two US cases are undecided.

## Key Findings

- **The Getty case has been decided and Stability largely won.** On 4 November 2025 the England and Wales High Court rejected the secondary copyright and passing-off claims and found only what it called a historic and extremely limited trademark infringement. Getty was granted leave to appeal in December 2025.
- **Two US copyright cases remain undecided** and neither was scored as proven harm: Getty Images v. Stability AI in the Northern District of California, refiled 14 August 2025, and Andersen v. Stability AI, third amended complaint February 2026.
- **The most serious located finding is the training data, not the copyright.** The Stanford Internet Observatory found 3,226 suspected child sexual abuse images in LAION-5B, the dataset behind Stable Diffusion, collected from the web without consulting child safety experts.
- **Stability's response distanced rather than acknowledged.** The company emphasised that it trained on a filtered subset and did not release the version Stanford examined. Historical Harm Acknowledgment therefore scores 2.
- **CORPORATE-STATUS ITEM: major restructuring, but the company operates.** Founder Emad Mostaque left in March 2024. Prem Akkaraju became chief executive in June 2024 with an $80 million recapitalisation, following losses above $30 million in one quarter on under $5 million of revenue.

## Scoring Convention Applied

**Absence of disclosure scores 2, never 1.** The floor of 1 is reserved for positive documented evidence of a specific failure. This is the majority convention across the three most recent de-seeding studies and was applied identically to all seven labs in this study. **Absence of disclosure is not evidence of harm.** Where this company does not publish, the low anchor was scored and said so explicitly. A score of 4 requires published institutional data, a regulatory decision or third-party peer review; a score of 5 requires independent audit plus community testimony.
