## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Lagos on the 2026-08-05 #TakeItBack anniversary protest, which security operatives dispersed with tear gas. Lagos has never been individually assessed (`last_assessed: null`) and its published composite of 20.3 sits 0.3 points above the Critical boundary, so the band label is unusually sensitive to any movement. This is a first full assessment.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Attribution correction — the scanner's flagged event is only partly the entity's conduct.** The 2026-08-05 protest was dispersed by "security operatives". Policing in Nigeria is a **federal** function: the Nigeria Police Force reports to the federal government, not to Lagos State. The tear-gassing of the Maryland gathering is therefore **not** scored as Lagos's own conduct. It is recorded as context.

**What IS Lagos's own conduct**, and what drives this assessment, is the waterfront demolition programme. The Lagos State Government cleared informal settlements including Makoko, displacing about 10,000 people and destroying more than 3,000 homes, schools and clinics. That is a state decision, executed by state agencies, and it is scored in full.

**Mixed dated/undated sourcing.** The scanner's two Lagos sources were both dated and both verified. Additional sources used here were checked for dates independently.

**Entity-boundary note.** The benchmark's "Lagos" record is a city entity. The governing authority whose conduct is measurable at city scale is the Lagos State Government. Federal action (policing, monetary policy, fuel-subsidy removal driving the cost-of-living grievances behind the protest) is excluded.

### Analysis

The published 20.3 sits 0.3 points inside the Developing band. On the evidence it belongs in Critical.

The decisive finding is the demolition programme. Between late December 2025 and February 2026 the Lagos State Government cleared waterfront settlements, destroying more than 3,000 homes, schools and clinics and displacing about 10,000 people. UN human-rights experts publicly condemned it. Residents report deaths during the clearances, including a newborn. Equity falls to 1.40 of 5 and Boundaries to 1.40 of 5, because people were removed from their homes with no individual assessment, no consent and no concrete alternative — the definition of the floor anchors for B4 and B5.

There is a genuine, documented counterweight, and it is scored. On 3 February 2026 the state government **suspended the demolitions** and agreed to engage UN agencies on assistance and durable solutions for those affected. That is a real course correction driven by harm evidence, and AB2 scores 3 of 5 as a result — the single highest Accountability subdimension.

The second counterweight is health. The Ilera Eko insurance scheme had enrolled 1,502,994 residents by May 2026, more than half of them women, and the 2026 budget of N4.237 trillion allocates N339 billion to health and N70 billion to social protection. That supports AC4 at 3 of 5. But the delivery method cuts the other way: Lagos has tied access to government services to insurance enrolment, which conditions public provision on compliance rather than need, and the state itself puts the health funding gap at N100 billion.

The assessed composite is 16.9. The fall is 3.4 points — below the magnitude trigger — but it crosses the band boundary from Developing into Critical, which is filable at any delta.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the government that is supposed to house people demolished their homes. The Lagos State Government cleared waterfront settlements including Makoko, destroying more than 3,000 homes, schools and clinics and displacing about 10,000 people between December 2025 and February 2026.
- United Nations human-rights experts publicly condemned the demolitions in February 2026. Residents reported deaths during the clearances, including a newborn.
- Why it matters: Lagos then stopped, and that counts. On 3 February 2026 the state suspended the demolitions and agreed to work with UN agencies on assistance and durable solutions. That is the strongest single piece of evidence in Lagos's favour.
- Health provision is real but conditional. The Ilera Eko scheme had 1,502,994 people enrolled by May 2026, over half of them women. Lagos also told residents there would be no government services without health insurance — access tied to compliance rather than need.
- The score falls 3.4 points, from 20.3 to 16.9, and crosses from the Developing band into the Critical band. Critical runs from 0 to 20; Lagos was published 0.3 points above that line.

## Strongest Dimensions

Action (2.20 of 5) and Systemic Thinking (2.00 of 5). A N4.237 trillion budget with N339 billion for health and N70 billion for social protection, plus a multi-year planning framework, put resource mobilisation and long-term planning above the rest of the profile.

## Weakest Dimensions

Equity (1.40 of 5), Boundaries (1.40 of 5) and Awareness (1.40 of 5). Under scarcity, resources flowed toward waterfront redevelopment and away from the poorest residents of those same waterfronts. No process exists for identifying who is being missed, and no harm assessment preceded the clearances.

## Evidence Gaps

Lagos State publishes budget documents and enrolment totals but no social-performance report, no disaggregated service-outcome data and no grievance statistics. Where disclosure is simply absent, the low anchor was applied and is stated as an absence of disclosure, not as evidence of harm. Whether the February suspension has held is not established in the public record and is the key open question.
