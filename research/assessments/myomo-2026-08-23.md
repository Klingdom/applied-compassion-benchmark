## Key Findings

- **Myomo's strongest compassion evidence comes from a regulator, not from Myomo.** The US Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services moved the MyoPro into the brace benefit category on 1 January 2024, after roughly five years of company argument. That single change replaced a 13-month rental with a lump-sum payment and opened the device to Medicare Part B patients. It is the only score of 4 in this assessment.
- **Being listed is why this company is better evidenced than its size suggests.** Myomo employs a few hundred people and earned $11.7 million in the second quarter of 2026. Because it trades on NYSE American, it must publish audited accounts, risk factors and — in November 2025 — a material weakness in its own internal controls. Private competitors of the same size publish none of that.
- **Clinical evidence exists but is thin, and Myomo says so.** Two studies are registered on ClinicalTrials.gov and a PLOS One case series reports functional gains. A systematic review of the device class notes small samples and short follow-up. The company publishes the evidence page rather than only the marketing claim.
- **Nothing was located about the people inside the company, or about who is missed outside it.** Empathy scores 25 of 100 — the absence floor. No staff data, no disaggregated patient data, no complaint-validation practice and no reparative action were found. **No adverse finding was located either.**
- **Insurance approval can take months, and Myomo publishes no response-time data.** A New York State external appeal record from 2022 shows a MyoPro denial reaching independent review. The statutory route exists; a company-side one was not found.
