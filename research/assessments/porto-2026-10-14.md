## Porto's first evidence-based score is 32.5, far below its unassessed 60.9. The evidence is thin, so the proposal goes to review rather than straight to a downgrade.

About 1,500 people marched in Porto on 10 October 2026 over housing costs (Euronews). Protesters then camped in Praça D. João I. On 12 October the city's municipal police, the national PSP police and the city company Porto Ambiente removed the tents. The council called the camp an 'illegal occupation and degradation of public space' (DNotícias/Lusa).

Porto gets credit for an annual homeless count (526 people in 2025, down from 730 in 2021), affordable-rent homes with subsidies, and network coordination. Local organisations say Housing First reaches very few people, emergency beds are occupied for years, and migrants are missing from the data.

Porto measures 32.5 (Developing). The published 60.9 has never been assessed. 13 of 40 measures are defaults because no evidence was found. The proposal is filed as flag-for-review.
