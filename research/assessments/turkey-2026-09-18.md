### Recommendation

## Key Findings

- Turkey's published score of 10.3 out of 100 holds. Between 12 and 14 September 2026, Turkish police raided seven LGBT rights groups and two HIV-support groups and arrested at least 112 people, 82 of whom a court ordered held.
- The raids are new state conduct of a kind the score had not yet captured: the target was civil-society organisations serving a marginalised health and identity population, not opposition politicians.
- The measured score fell 2.8 points, to 7.5 out of 100, which is below the 5-point filing threshold. The main reason it fell so little is the floor: 22 of Turkey's 40 subdimensions already sit at the lowest anchor, so there is almost no room left to register new harm.
- The evidence is from Human Rights Watch, a watchdog group (tier 3). No United Nations body or court has ruled on this operation yet, so confidence is medium.
- Turkey stays in the Critical band, where it has been since 23 May 2026.

## Strongest Dimensions

## Weakest Dimensions

## Evidence Gaps

- No tier 4 or tier 5 source (UN special procedure, European Court of Human Rights, treaty body) on the September 2026 operation was located. Human Rights Watch alone caps confidence at medium.
- The scan reports 112 arrested and 82 held; Human Rights Watch also gives 162 under investigation. Whether the 82 remain in detention as at 18 September 2026 was not established.
- No Turkish government statement responding to the Human Rights Watch report was located.
- The condition of the two HIV-support organisations and of their clients after the raids was not established. Interruption of HIV support would be an Action-dimension harm that is currently unmeasured.

## Scanner and source corrections

- The scan attributes its evidence to a global-cities regional sweep naming Ankara and cross-attributes it to the Turkey country row. That cross-attribution is sound: Human Rights Watch describes raids across five Turkish cities, which is national conduct, and Ankara is not the right unit.
- The scan's '(82 held under court order)' is confirmed verbatim by the source. The scan does not mention the 162 people under investigation or the operation name 'My Family Is Safe', both of which are in the source and are material.

## Watch flag

Downward: a United Nations special-procedure communication, a European Court of Human Rights interim measure, or evidence that the HIV-support organisations have stopped serving clients. Upward only on the release of those held and the withdrawal of the charges.
