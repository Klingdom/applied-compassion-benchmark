## Why this entity was assessed

UNHCR warned of rising displacement from Yemen, with nearly 3,000 people crossing the Bab el-Mandeb Strait into Djibouti in under a week in September 2026, and is planning for more than 10,000 arrivals.

## Evidence-date, provenance, attribution and screening checks

**MEASURE DJIBOUTI'S RECEPTION, NOT THE ARRIVAL.** The arrival of refugees is not conduct by Djibouti. It is the trigger. What this assessment measures is what the Djiboutian state did with 3,000 people who arrived in a week, and what its reception capacity is.

**BASELINE PROVENANCE (read first).** Published 17.5 was **set on 2026-08-20** by an applied band crossing from 20.3 (Developing) to 17.5 (Critical), based on the 2026-08-16 full 40-subdimension de-seeding assessment recorded in `research/APPLIED_CHANGES.md`. That assessment replaced a seed vector shared byte-identically across twelve countries. The baseline is five weeks old, evidence-based, and driven by Djibouti's own documented conduct. **No stale-baseline argument is available or used here.**

**ATTRIBUTION SPLIT — THIS IS THE CENTRAL FINDING.** Almost everything working in the reception is internationally funded and internationally run. Al Jazeera reports that people fleeing Houthi-government clashes "gather to receive supplies from the International Organisation for Migration at the Markazi refugee camp." Al Jazeera also reports that "The UNHCR's Djibouti operations are only 15 percent funded, with the agency appealing for $13.5m 'to ensure arrivals can access registration, reception, protection, and other essential services.'" That appeal is UNHCR's, not Djibouti's, and the funding gap is the donor community's failure, not Djibouti's conduct. Neither can be scored as Djiboutian resource mobilisation.

**WHAT IS PROPERLY ATTRIBUTABLE, AND IT IS POSITIVE.** Djibouti kept the border open and kept admitting people during the surge. Its 2017 Refugee Law and implementation decree grant refugees freedom of movement, access to employment, and inclusion in the national education and health systems — a framework UNHCR has publicly praised. Refugees at Markazi live in prefabricated housing with running water and electricity. Djibouti hosts roughly 33,360 refugees and asylum seekers, over 3% of its own population.

**THIS IS ALREADY SCORED.** The 2026-08-16 sidecar placed EQ1 Universality at 3/5 — the highest single subdimension in Djibouti's Equity dimension — precisely on that inclusion framework. The in-window evidence **confirms** that score under pressure rather than raising it. Anchor 4 for EQ1 requires coverage data disaggregated by population and a documented reduction in gaps. No such data was located, and a 15%-funded operation facing 10,000 more arrivals is not evidence of a narrowing gap.

## Determination

**CONFIRM 17.5.** Djibouti's refugee-inclusion framework is real, and it is the best thing in its profile. It is also already scored. The surge tested that framework and the framework held, with international money doing most of the work. Nothing in the window is Djiboutian conduct that clears an anchor threshold in either direction.

**Accountability remains at the 1.0 floor**, and nothing in this window touches it. Djibouti's domestic governance record — a presidency held since 1999, suppressed opposition, and no independent press — is what holds ACC, INT and EMP low, and refugee hospitality does not offset it.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Djibouti's score of 17.5 out of 100 holds. Nearly 3,000 people crossed from Yemen in under a week in September 2026, and Djibouti let them in.
- Why it matters: Djibouti does something most of its neighbours do not. Its 2017 refugee law lets refugees work, move freely and use national schools and clinics, and UNHCR has praised it.
- Why it matters: the reception is mostly paid for by other people. UNHCR's Djibouti operation is only 15% funded and is asking for $13.5 million.
- Why it matters: the country hosts about 33,360 refugees, more than 3% of its own population. UNHCR is now planning for 10,000 more arrivals.
- Why it matters: hospitality to refugees does not fix the rest. Djibouti's Accountability score stays at the lowest possible level, on a presidency held since 1999 and no independent press.
