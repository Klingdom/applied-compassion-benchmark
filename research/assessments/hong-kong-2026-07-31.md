### Score Difference Analysis

The -3.4 difference has a grid component — four published dimensions sit at 2.5 or 3.5 — and real movement in Boundaries (1.5 to 1.2), Accountability (1.5 to 1.4), Awareness (2.5 to 2.2), Equity (2.5 to 2.4) and Empathy (2.5 to 2.4). Awareness falls because independent bookshops, one of the few remaining channels through which low-power voices reach the public in Hong Kong, were themselves the target of the raids. Hong Kong Watch's briefing dated 30 July 2026 documents a third round of national security raids on independent bookstores in four months, with five people arrested and charged on suspicion of sedition and boxes of books seized. One independent bookshop announced its closure a day before police raided it. Hong Kong's top security official then told booksellers to ensure the titles they sell do not harm national security. The briefing also reports that jailed activist Joshua Wong is set to plead guilty in a second national security case that could keep him imprisoned well beyond his existing sentence. The findings that move Equity and Boundaries hardest are the ones about disability: a waiter with an intellectual disability was jailed for promoting a pro-Taiwan party, and two men with autism pleaded guilty to secession over membership of a banned independence group. Prosecuting people with intellectual disability and autism for political speech is the clearest possible failure of priority for the vulnerable and of meaningful consent to a legal boundary. Against that, Hong Kong's Action score of 3.4 is genuine and unchanged: the government's Typhoon Noul response on 26 July 2026 was coordinated, published and effective, with 18 injuries and 188 toppled trees reported openly.

A conviction extending his sentence, or a further escalation in four months, would support a proposal on Boundaries and Integrity together.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: a man with an intellectual disability went to prison for promoting a political party.** Hong Kong Watch's July 2026 briefing, dated 30 July, reports that a waiter with an intellectual disability was jailed for promoting a pro-Taiwan party, and that two men with autism pleaded guilty to secession over membership of a banned independence group.
- **Why it matters: this is the third round of bookshop raids in four months.** National security police raided independent bookstores again in July 2026, arrested five people on suspicion of sedition and removed boxes of books. One bookshop announced its closure a day before police arrived.
- **Why it matters: the government then told booksellers to police themselves.** After the raids, Hong Kong's top security official said booksellers should ensure the titles they sell do not harm national security — a standard the shops have to guess at.
- **Why it matters: Joshua Wong may not get out.** The briefing reports that the jailed activist is set to plead guilty in a second national security case that could keep him behind bars well beyond his existing sentence.
- **Why it matters: Hong Kong runs its city well, and that is in the score too.** Its Typhoon Noul response on 26 July 2026 was coordinated across departments and published openly, including 18 injuries and 188 toppled trees.
