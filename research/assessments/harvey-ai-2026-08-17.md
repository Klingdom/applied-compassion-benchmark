## Key findings

- Harvey AI does one thing that most labs in this study do not: it publishes its own error rate. Its BigLaw Bench post states that its Assistant model hallucinates about 1 claim in 500. Publishing that is not reputationally convenient.
- Independent reviewers push back on the framing. They note Harvey has not published independent hallucination benchmarks, and that on third-party comparisons it is 'consistently good, never great'.
- The access picture is thin. The Pro Bono Institute reports Harvey has a legal aid programme but that access is limited, and that free tools remain more accessible to legal aid organisations. Meanwhile roughly 70% of the AmLaw 10 uses Harvey.
- That gap is why Equity scores 2.0 out of 5 — the lowest dimension. A stated intention to support nonprofits does not yet match where the product actually goes.
- No source above tier 3 was obtained for Harvey. The score falls 19.6 points, and most of that is missing disclosure rather than located harm.

## Strongest dimensions

- **Awareness (AWR) at 2.4** and **Action (ACT) at 2.2**. These are the only dimensions where located positive evidence exceeds the absence baseline by a clear margin.

## Weakest dimensions

- **Systemic Thinking (SYS) at 2** and **Equity (EQU) at 2**. These rest almost entirely on absence of disclosure rather than on located failure.

## Evidence gaps

- 34 of 40 subdimensions are scored at 2 because no evidence was located in either direction. Under the fixed standard used across all 15 labs in this study, absence of disclosure scores 2, not 1.
- No independent audit and no beneficiary or community testimony of a working compassion practice was located. Nothing in this assessment scores 5 on any subdimension.
- This is a desk-based assessment. If Harvey AI operates grievance channels, disaggregated outcome review or reparative processes without publishing them, this assessment understates it.
