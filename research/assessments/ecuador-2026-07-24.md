### Recommendation

**DOWNGRADE**. The composite delta of -10.3 points exceeds the 5-point threshold. A change proposal has been filed at `research/change-proposals/ecuador.json` for human review. This assessor does not apply changes to published indexes.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: an independent investigation based on 62 interviews now documents torture, airstrikes on homes and drone attacks on fishing boats by Ecuadorian forces — and no prosecutions followed.
- Human Rights Watch published the findings on 21 July 2026. One detainee said: "They tortured me with electricity, with a taser. They poured water on me and then shocked me, and I passed out."
- Accountability failed at every stage. HRW found that "prosecutors did not file charges because soldiers failed to provide any evidence", and neither the Ecuadorian nor the US government answered its questions.
- The assessed score of 25.6 is 10.3 points below the published 35.9. Ecuador stays in the developing band, but drops from rank 84 toward the bottom of it.
- Ecuador is not scored at the level of a collapsed state. Competitive elections, an active Constitutional Court and a broadly free press still function, and the Court limited one emergency decree.

## Evidence Gaps

- The Ecuadorian government did not respond to HRW’s inquiries, so no official account of the San Martín operations exists.
- No independent audit of detention conditions or of the security-cooperation agreement with the United States has been published.
