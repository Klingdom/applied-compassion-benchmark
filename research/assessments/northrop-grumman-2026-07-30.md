## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: the plant is designed to emit one-tenth of a ton less than the amount that would trigger stricter federal rules.** Northrop Grumman’s proposed Waynesboro permit caps combined hazardous air pollutants at 24.9 tons a year. The federal major-source threshold is 25 tons a year.
- **Why it matters: hundreds of people turned out to say they do not believe the limit protects them.** More than 50 speakers addressed Virginia’s Department of Environmental Quality at Waynesboro High School on 23 July 2026. Many called for the permitting process to be halted.
- **What residents said they are worried about.** Long-term exposure to xylene, toluene and methyl isobutyl ketone, particularly for children, older adults and people with asthma or other respiratory conditions.
- **Why it matters: the decision is not Northrop Grumman’s to make.** The permit is granted or refused by the Virginia Department of Environmental Quality, which will hold another community forum before deciding. Only the company’s own conduct — the application design and how it engaged the community — is scored here.
- **Northrop Grumman sits at rank 257 of 448 in the Fortune 500 index** with a published composite of 35.9 out of 100, which is Developing. This assessment moves it -3.4 points, below the threshold for a change proposal.
