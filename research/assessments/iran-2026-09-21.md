# Compassion Benchmark Near-Floor Confirmation: Iran

**Entity type:** Country
**Assessment date:** 2026-09-21
**Outcome:** near-floor confirmation; no dimension moves; evidence tier behind the existing score upgraded
**Published:** 2.5/100 (Critical), countries rank 178 of 191

> This assessment is based on publicly available information and does not constitute a formal Compassion Benchmark Certified Assessment. For a comprehensive, assessor-led evaluation, visit compassionbenchmark.com/certified-assessments.

## Why this entity was assessed

The Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Iran reported to the UN Human Rights Council on Thursday 17 September 2026 that Iran's crackdown on the 2025–2026 protests amounted to crimes against humanity. Iran was last assessed on 2026-08-01. Iran's published composite of 2.5 sits 2.5 points above the absolute floor, so the expected outcome was corroboration rather than movement — and that is what this is.

## Source tier, stated plainly

**The Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Iran is a tier-4 source: an international-organisation mandate reporting to the UN Human Rights Council.** It is not a court and it is not a treaty body, so it is not tier 5. It sits above the watchdog-NGO tier that has carried most of Iran's evidence base to date.

## What it corroborates

Iran's published vector is already at or near the anchor floor on every dimension: EMP, EQU, ACC and INT sit at exactly 1.0, and AWR, ACT, BND and SYS at 1.2. The question is not whether the score should move but whether the evidence underneath it holds. The mission's findings answer that with four specific corroborations.

**1. The scale is nationwide, not localised.** The mission documented that "Iranian security forces carried out killings on a large scale in all 31 provinces", with the highest death tolls in Tehran, Karaj, Mashhad, Isfahan and Rasht. This corroborates the AWR and ACT floor. A state whose security forces kill across every one of its provinces is not failing to detect suffering; it is the source of it.

**2. Children were killed, including infants.** "At least 221 children were reportedly killed, some of them as young as two years old". This corroborates the EQU 1.0 floor directly. EQ1 Universality and EQ2 Priority for Vulnerable measure whether a state extends care to those with least power. A state that kills two-year-olds in the course of suppressing protest occupies the bottom anchor of both.

**3. Executions after expedited trials, with more queued.** "At least 29 men were executed between March and August 2026 after expedited trials" in connection with protests, and "more than 70 people, including five women and three boys, remained at imminent risk of execution." This corroborates the BND 1.2 and ACC 1.0 floors. An expedited trial ending in execution is the floor anchor for B5 Consent Orientation and for AB1 Harm Acknowledgment at once.

**4. The legal characterisation.** The mission found "Murder, imprisonment, torture and other inhumane acts committed as part of a widespread and systematic attack on civilians". This is the corroboration that matters most for the INT 1.0 floor: "widespread and systematic" means policy, not excess. It removes the reading in which these are individual abuses a state is failing to control.

## Why the score does not move

**Downward.** A move to the 0.0 harm-flag floor was considered and is not taken. The benchmark's own precedent governs: `research/APPLIED_CHANGES.md` records Iran held at 2.5 on 2026-05-26, 2026-05-28 and 2026-05-29 under an explicit SCALE-FLOOR RULING, with the floor methodology question recorded as pending rather than resolved by any single cycle. A floor designation also requires at least two distinct sources with at least one at tier 4 or above; tonight there is one tier-4 source. Neither condition is met.

**Upward.** Nothing in the record points up. There is no new positive conduct by the Iranian state in this window.

**So: no movement, and the value of tonight's work is the evidence, not the number.** The 2.5 was previously carried largely on watchdog-NGO tallies, principally Amnesty International's execution figures. It is now additionally supported by a UN Human Rights Council mandate's finding of crimes against humanity. The score is unchanged; the case for it is stronger.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: a United Nations mission has now called Iran's crackdown a crime against humanity. It reported to the UN Human Rights Council on 17 September 2026. It found murder, jailing and torture carried out as "a widespread and systematic attack on civilians".
- Why it matters: the killing was everywhere, not in a few places. Iranian security forces killed "on a large scale in all 31 provinces", the mission found. A government cannot call that a local failure of control.
- Why it matters: at least 221 children were killed, some as young as two years old. That is why Iran scores 1 out of 5 on Equity — the lowest score the benchmark gives — and the finding confirms it.
- Why it matters: Iran executed at least 29 men between March and August 2026 after rushed trials, and more than 70 people are still at imminent risk, including three boys.
- Why it matters: Iran's score does not change, and that is the point. It already sits at 2.5 out of 100, third-lowest of 191 countries. What changed is the strength of the proof: a UN mandate now says what human rights groups said before.
