## Birmingham's sixth redundancy round in 14 months fell entirely on minority-ethnic staff, and it will not show the equality assessment

The University of Birmingham's published 43.0 has never been assessed against evidence. This is its first baseline.

The in-window trigger is weak for Birmingham specifically. The Al Jazeera report of 24 September 2026 lists Birmingham among universities funded by BAE Systems; Birmingham said it takes police advice where necessary but does not take security direction from other external parties. That statement is not adverse evidence.

The stronger in-window evidence is about staff. On 11 September 2026 the Morning Star reported that every member of staff at risk in Birmingham's latest redundancy round is from a migrant or minority-ethnic background, that it is the sixth round in 14 months, and that the university has refused to release the equality impact assessment. The university says it ran a meaningful consultation. In 2024 Birmingham went to the High Court to remove a pro-Palestine encampment and won.

Birmingham is strong on access for students: a regulator-approved Access and Participation Plan for 2025-26 to 2028-29, Sanctuary Scholarships with full fee waivers for refugees, and targeted routes for care-experienced and Black students from the most deprived neighbourhoods.

Measured: 39.4 (Developing), down 3.6 from 43.0. The band-crossing trigger requires filing at any delta. The proposal is routed as flag-for-review: the crossing is 0.6 points below the line (less than one subdimension step), and the adverse evidence is tier-1/2 press with no court, government or international-body source.
