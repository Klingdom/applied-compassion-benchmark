## Key Findings

- **Intuitive Surgical is the most exposed robotics company on earth, and its score reflects the gap between that exposure and its compassion infrastructure.** Its systems were used in more than 3.2 million procedures in 2025. Its published accountability to the patients in those procedures is almost entirely what US law compels.
- **Regulation, not the company, supplies most of the good evidence.** The strongest scores — harm detection, pre-launch risk assessment, correction after the 2025 foot-pedal recall — all come from duties the FDA imposes. Under these anchors, compelled acknowledgment caps at 2. That is why Accountability sits at 40 of 100 despite an unusually rich public record.
- **The business model is built on dependency, and a federal court heard the details.** Instruments carry programmed usage limits and contracts restrict outside repair. Intuitive won the Surgical Instrument Service antitrust trial on how the market was defined, not by denying the restrictions. A separate hospital class action is still live.
- **331 people lost jobs in a year revenue grew 21%.** The cuts hit technicians and manufacturing staff at the Sunnyvale site, and a WARN Act investigation followed. Internal Consistency scores 2 of 5 as a result.
- **The Intuitive Foundation is the clearest compassion evidence found, and it sits outside the company.** A $45 million donation funds free surgical-training tools for low-resource countries. It lifts Systemic Thinking and Empathy — but it is a separate charity, not a change in how the operating business allocates resource.

## Strongest Dimensions
- **Awareness (55.0)** — the highest dimension. Post-market surveillance, MAUDE reporting and mandatory pre-market risk analysis give the company real detection machinery, even though the law requires it.
- **Action (50.0)** — large-scale outcome evidence, proportional field actions and a funded attempt to close the global surgical-training gap.

## Weakest Dimensions
- **Equity (30.0), Boundaries (30.0) and Integrity (30.0)** — access disparities are documented by outside researchers and unmeasured by the company; the instrument and repair restrictions are dependency-creating by design; and the layoffs, wage litigation and absence of any costly-commitment case leave Integrity near the absence baseline.

## Evidence Gaps
- No disaggregated data on which patients receive robotic surgery, by race, income or geography.
- No patient-facing complaint or advocacy channel; patients reach the company only through hospitals or litigation.
- ESG and human-rights content is self-published and not independently assured on social metrics; under this index's tightened test, self-published material does not count toward independent verification.
- Product-liability settlement terms are confidential, so reparative adequacy cannot be assessed.
