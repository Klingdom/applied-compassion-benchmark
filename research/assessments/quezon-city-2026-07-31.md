### Score Difference Analysis

The -0.9 difference is small and mostly a grid artifact: three published dimensions sit at 2.5 or 1.5, values that five whole-number subdimension scores cannot produce. The real movement is in Equity (1.5 to 1.4) and Accountability (1.5 to 1.4) and is driven by one dated event. On 27 July 2026, ahead of President Marcos Jr.'s State of the Nation Address, police arrested 51 people at a protest at Philcoa on Commonwealth Avenue in Quezon City. Thirteen were minors and three were paralegals. The stated legal basis was Quezon City ordinances on face covering, vandalism and ecological solid waste management. That basis matters for attribution: the arresting force is the Philippine National Police, a national agency, but the ordinances invoked are the city's own instruments, so the city is partly responsible for what its rules were used to do. Twelve minors were later released to their parents, nine people were charged, and thirty were freed pending investigation. Rights groups called the response "overkill" and, on 31 July 2026, condemned the subsequent interrogation of protesters. Against this sits a substantial positive baseline: a P43.3 billion 2026 city budget with more than P24 billion for social services, P6.1 billion for education, and the QCitizen ID widening access to city programmes. That baseline is why the movement is under one point rather than several.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: thirteen of the 51 people arrested were children.** Police arrested 51 people at a protest on Commonwealth Avenue in Quezon City on 27 July 2026, the day of President Marcos Jr.'s State of the Nation Address. Thirteen were minors and three were paralegals. Twelve minors were later released to their parents.
- **Why it matters: the legal basis was the city's own rulebook.** The stated grounds were Quezon City ordinances on face covering, vandalism and ecological solid waste management — not national security law. That is what makes this partly a city finding rather than purely a national police one.
- **Why it matters: the police called it a good day.** The Philippine National Police described the 27 July SONA as "peaceful, orderly" even after arresting 51 people. Rights groups called the response "overkill" and, on 31 July 2026, condemned the interrogation of those detained.
- **Why it matters: most of those arrested were not career activists.** They were members of Vandals United, Hiphop United Against Corruption, Skaters United and Defend the Hood — street and hip-hop artist collectives.
- **Why it matters: Quezon City spends real money on its residents.** Its 2026 budget is P43.3 billion, up 6.65% on 2025, with more than P24 billion earmarked for social services and P6.1 billion for education. That spending is why this assessment moves the score by less than one point.
