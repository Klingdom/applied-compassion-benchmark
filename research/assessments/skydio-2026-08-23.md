## Key Findings

- **Skydio scores 28.1 of 100. Its highest marks come from a decision that cost it money, not from its drone policies.** After Skydio sold drones to Taiwan's National Fire Agency, China sanctioned the company in October 2024 and cut off its battery supply. Skydio kept the commitment, told customers it would ration batteries to one per drone for months, and published an account of why. That earns three separate scores of 3 of 5.
- **The transparency tool is real. Whether it gets used is not Skydio's decision.** Skydio ships a Transparency Dashboard that can show residents the date, time, telemetry, reason and case number of every flight. **Agencies choose whether to switch it on.** The Chicago Reader found the Cook County Sheriff's Office flying Skydio drones with no agreement at all governing who owns or may use the data collected.
- **Civil-liberties findings in this file are about police departments, not about Skydio, and are scored that way.** The Electronic Frontier Foundation says agencies are breaking state drone-surveillance laws. The American Civil Liberties Union of Illinois calls the lack of safeguards "really, really problematic". A California court had to order Chula Vista police to release drone footage in 2025, after a four-year fight. **None of that is scored as Skydio conduct.** What is scored is that Skydio publishes no disaggregated data about where its fleets fly.
- **Nobody on the ground consents to any of this, and no consent architecture is possible in this model.** A police department buys the drone. The residents recorded from above do not. Consent Orientation scores 2 of 5 and would be hard to raise without a change to how these programmes are sold.
- **Empathy, Action, Equity and Accountability all sit at 25.0 — exactly the absence floor.** No complaint validation, no outcome data, no self-published failure report and no repair process were located.
