## Key Findings

- Why it matters: most of the deaths that flagged Dhaka did not happen in Dhaka. The flash floods and landslides that killed at least 44 people struck southeastern Bangladesh, several hundred kilometres from the capital. Under the attribution rule those deaths do not lower Dhaka's score.
- What does count is what the authorities did in Dhaka. Students blocked intersections at Science Lab and Uttara after the government refused repeated requests to defer Higher Secondary Certificate examinations during the floods, and police baton-charged protesters near parliament.
- The education minister rejected calls to cancel the examination but said students unable to sit because of flooding would be given another opportunity. That is correction under pressure, which is anchor-2 behaviour, not acknowledgment before investigation.
- Reported death tolls moved during the window, from 44 on 11 July to 51 in later national reporting. The lower, better-corroborated figure is used and the movement is recorded rather than resolved by inference.
- Dhaka assesses at exactly 20.0, which sits on the Critical and Developing boundary. Because the delta is below the 5-point threshold the published Developing band stands and no band change is recorded. Its highest dimension is Systemic Thinking at raw 2.4; its lowest are Boundaries, Accountability and Integrity, all at raw 1.4.
