### Analysis

The assessed composite is 32.5 against a published 35.9 — a fall of 3.4 points, below the filing trigger, with no band change.

Kenya's profile is genuinely split, and both halves are the state's own conduct.

On one side, protest policing keeps producing arrests and worse. Six activists were arrested on 17 August for demonstrating outside police headquarters. More than 350 people were arrested across Kenya on 25 June, at demonstrations marking the second anniversary of protests in which 60 people were killed by security forces. The Kenya Human Rights Commission says at least six activists were tortured by police after arrest in Nairobi.

On the other side, Kenya has an accountability institution that functions in public. The Independent Policing Oversight Authority is investigating the 25 June abductions and brutality allegations and says so openly. AB3 (Transparency) scores 3 of 5 on that basis, and AC4 scores 3 because IPOA is a resourced statutory body rather than a paper commitment.

The reason the score does not rise on that is what activists themselves are now saying: they are campaigning for IPOA to be given **arrest powers**, precisely because its findings do not convert into consequences. Investigation without enforcement is anchor 2 for correction willingness, not anchor 3.

**Cycle note.** Kenya has now carried a downward watch flag across two consecutive cycles. Neither gap reaches the filing trigger on its own. If a third protest-policing incident lands in the next window, the accumulated evidence should be reassessed as a package rather than incident by incident.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: in Kenya, protesting about police violence gets you arrested by the police. Six activists were detained on 17 August 2026 outside police headquarters in Nairobi while demanding the Inspector General's resignation. One had chained himself to the gates.
- More than 350 people were arrested across Kenya on 25 June 2026 at protests marking two years since security forces killed 60 people. The Kenya Human Rights Commission says at least six activists were tortured after arrest.
- Why it matters: Kenya does have a real watchdog, and it works in the open. The Independent Policing Oversight Authority is investigating the June abductions and brutality allegations and has said so publicly.
- The catch is enforcement. Activists are campaigning to give that watchdog the power to arrest, because its findings do not currently lead to prosecutions.
- The score is confirmed at 35.9. The assessed value of 32.5 is 3.4 points lower — the second consecutive cycle with a downward flag, which is recorded for escalation rather than acted on now.
