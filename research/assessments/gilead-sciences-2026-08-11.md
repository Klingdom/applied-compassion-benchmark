## Key Findings

- Gilead gave away its HIV prevention drug rights in 120 countries for free. It signed royalty-free licences with six generic manufacturers covering most of the world's HIV burden, and signed them before regulatory approval so supply could start the day it was allowed.
- It sells the drug at no profit until generics can meet demand. Partnerships with PEPFAR and the Global Fund grew from covering two million people in September 2025 to three million by April 2026.
- The same company paid $202 million to settle claims it bribed doctors. The Justice Department alleged Gilead used speaker programs to pay kickbacks to physicians to induce them to prescribe its drugs.
- And $246.8 million more to settle claims it kept generics off the market. The class action alleged Gilead agreed with Teva to delay generic versions of Atripla and Truvada, causing purchasers to overpay between February 2018 and September 2022.
- About 23,000 people are suing Gilead over kidney and bone injuries. Gilead discloses this itself in its SEC filings, which is genuinely unflattering disclosure. The pattern across all of it: Gilead acknowledges harm through court settlements rather than before them.

## Strongest Dimensions

Action (3.80/5). Royalty-free licensing to six generic manufacturers across 120 countries, signed ahead of regulatory approval and backed by at-no-profit supply, is the strongest documented compassion practice in tonight's cohort.

## Weakest Dimensions

Accountability (2.80/5). Two nine-figure settlements and roughly 23,000 product-liability plaintiffs describe a company that reaches repair through litigation rather than through acknowledgment.

## Evidence Gaps

- Gilead's access and pricing claims are self-published (tier 3) and lack an independent audit of who actually receives lenacapavir within covered countries.
- The Department of Justice and National Trial Lawyers URLs carry no machine-verifiable date and are used for claims only.
- No published workforce sustainability or internal culture data were located.
- No independent evaluation of the health outcomes of the PEPFAR and Global Fund lenacapavir programmes was available.
