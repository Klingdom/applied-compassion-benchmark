### Score Difference Analysis

The +0.3 difference is not a finding. It is worth being precise about why the settlement payouts do not raise the score. Money genuinely reached people who were harmed, which is the substance of reparative action, and that is why AB5 sits at 3 of 5 rather than lower. But the repair was compelled by an antitrust class action, was agreed in 2024 without an admission of wrongdoing, and is being distributed under court supervision. The methodology's higher anchors for reparative action require repair that is co-designed with the harmed parties and considered adequate by them, or a self-initiated systematic approach. A court-administered payout of roughly 2,000 dollars per average claimant does not reach those anchors. On the other side, the AAUP statement is external testimony bearing directly on internal consistency, which is already scored at 2 of 5, so it confirms rather than moves the existing finding. The two developments offset, and the composite holds.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: money reached people who were overcharged.** Northwestern students and alumni began receiving settlement payouts between 20 and 24 July 2026 from Northwestern’s 43.5 million dollar share of a combined 284 million dollar financial-aid antitrust settlement covering 10 institutions.
- **Why it matters: the repair was compelled, not offered.** The suit alleged that Northwestern and 16 other elite universities operated a price-fixing cartel that inflated the net cost of attendance for students on partial aid. Northwestern settled in 2024 without admitting wrongdoing.
- **Why it matters: the average payout is small.** If about half of the estimated 200,000 class members claim, the average claimant receives roughly 2,000 dollars. That is real, but it is not the kind of repair the methodology’s top anchors describe.
- **Why it matters: faculty representatives are alleging a governance problem.** The American Association of University Professors publicly backed a Medill professor on 22 July 2026 and alleged a "toxic corporate governance culture" at Northwestern.
- **Why it matters: the two cancel out.** One development is reparative, the other is critical of internal culture. The composite holds at 42.2, rank 72 of 100 universities, band Functional.
