## Key Findings

- Why it matters: in Kathmandu the people who went to check on flood victims were the ones who ended up in custody. Police detained three activists on 11 July 2026 after they visited the Kirtipur holding centre, where families displaced by forced evictions were sheltering from flooding.
- Majid Ansari was discharged from hospital after five days still in custody. He said he had received no arrest papers and that officers kept pressuring him to sign a document, which he refused.
- Ansari described being grabbed from behind and beaten, saying his vision became blurred and he could not see properly for some time. Police said he was not arrested, and that officers intervened when two sides clashed at the gate and he was injured in the process.
- Amnesty International Nepal called for his immediate and unconditional release, describing the detention as arbitrary.
- This is Kathmandu's first evidence-based baseline. The published 18.8 had no evidence behind any of its eight dimension values, so the change is mostly a change in what has been looked at, not a change in the city's behaviour.
