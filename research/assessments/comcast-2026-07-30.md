## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: the allegation is that humiliation was scheduled.** A former Comcast retail worker in Plainville, Connecticut says the store manager had the top salesperson tie the lowest-ranked salesperson to a chair each month and smash a cream pie in their face. The complaint says a posted chart named who had been and would be punished.
- **Why it matters: nothing here has been decided.** Comcast said it has “zero tolerance for harassment, humiliation, or any behavior that compromises a respectful and safe workplace” and that it disagrees with the claims. No court has ruled, so the assessment does not move any score on the allegation alone.
- **Why it matters: Comcast’s best programme has a rule that punishes the poorest customers.** Internet Essentials has served more than 10 million low-income households since 2011 at $14.95 a month. But customers cannot sign up if they owe any debt on any Comcast account — which excludes exactly the households most likely to have fallen behind.
- **The worker says he quit within a month.** He is seeking damages for constructive discharge and emotional distress after reporting the conduct.
- **Comcast sits near the bottom of the Fortune 500 index at rank 318 of 448.** The published composite of 26.6 out of 100 is Developing, meaning key structures exist but are inconsistent and reactive. This assessment finds no reason to move it.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Action (2.4).** Comcast operates at scale and has a functioning low-cost broadband programme with published pricing and a long history, which lifts Proportionality and Resource Mobilization above the rest of the profile.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Awareness, Empathy, Equity, Boundaries, Accountability, Systemic Thinking and Integrity, all at 2.0.** The profile is flat and low. The current complaint alleges that a documented, repeated practice ran at store level without detection, which is precisely the Awareness failure the 2.0 anchor describes.

## Evidence Gaps

- Comcast has not filed an answer to the complaint, so the allegations cannot be weighed against a company account beyond its short public statement.
- No public data on Comcast workplace complaint volumes, resolution rates or retail-channel turnover, so B1 and AB4 rest on the litigation record.
- Internet Essentials enrolment is reported as a cumulative total since 2011 rather than current active households, which limits confidence in EQ1 and EQ4.
