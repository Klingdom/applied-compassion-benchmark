# Screening Note: Rio de Janeiro

**Assessment date:** 2026-09-24
**Outcome:** screened, not assessed. No proposal on the Rio de Janeiro row.
**Published:** 21.9/100, Developing, rank #171 of 250 global cities. Canonical reconstruction returns 21.9 exactly — no math-hygiene issue.

## Attribution: this is federal conduct in a city, and that matters

On 18 September 2026 **President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva** launched a federal pilot programme against organised crime in Rio de Janeiro, expanding joint federal, state and municipal operations targeting cargo theft, weapons and drug trafficking, and organised-crime financial networks along the city's key corridors.

The launching institution is the **federal government of Brazil**. A president announcing a federal programme in a city is not the city acting. If this were scored on the Rio de Janeiro row, the benchmark would credit a municipal government for a decision taken in Brasília.

The programme's design makes the attribution problem explicit rather than incidental: it is a *joint* federal, state and municipal operation. Three levels of government share the conduct, and only one of them is the entity in front of the instrument.

## The deeper split: policing in Rio is a state function, not a municipal one

The Rio de Janeiro published score of 21.9, with Equity, Boundaries, Accountability and Integrity all at raw 1.5, plainly reflects the city's lethal-policing and favela-security record. But the **Polícia Militar do Estado do Rio de Janeiro** and the **Polícia Civil** both report to the *state* of Rio de Janeiro, not to the Prefeitura do Rio. The municipal government runs the Guarda Municipal, which has no investigative or armed-operations role.

So the conduct that most shapes this row's score is not the row's conduct, and the in-window item that might have moved it belongs to a third institution again.

## What would be assessable on this row, and was not found

The Prefeitura do Rio's own functions: municipal health and emergency care (SAMU and municipal hospitals), social assistance in favelas, housing and sanitation programmes, and the Guarda Municipal's conduct. **No in-window evidence about any of these was located.**

## Direction, if it were assessable

If the federal pilot were the city's conduct, it would point **upward** — a structured, resourced, multi-level intervention against the organised crime that drives the city's harm, at anchor 3 on S1 Root Cause Orientation and S5 Coalitional Compassion. A programme launch is not delivered help, so it would not go higher than that until outcomes exist.

But a positive-pointing item cannot be filed on an entity that did not perform it, any more than a negative one can. Screening rule 3 applies in both directions.

## What is flagged

This is the second city row in this cycle (with Kingston) and the third (with Kinshasa) where the in-window evidence is police or security conduct that the municipal entity does not control. That is a **structural feature of the global-cities index**, not a one-off.

Recorded as `flag-for-review` for coordinator-level guidance: how should the global-cities index treat police conduct in federated systems where policing is a state or national function? Options include an explicit attribution convention, a note on affected rows, or scoring the municipality only on municipal functions. This is a methodology question and is deliberately **not** encoded as a score change.

## Why no full assessment was run

The single in-window item is federal. Rio de Janeiro has never been individually assessed and deserves a first baseline, but it should be built on municipal evidence and should resolve the attribution convention first. `last_assessed` is not stamped.

## Triggers that would change this

- Conduct by the Prefeitura do Rio: a municipal health, housing, sanitation or Guarda Municipal decision.
- Outcomes from the federal pilot that name municipal delivery responsibility.
- A coordinator ruling on the federated-policing attribution convention.
