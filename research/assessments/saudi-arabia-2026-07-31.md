## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: five people were put to death after hearings that lasted minutes.** Human Rights Watch reported on 28 July 2026 that Saudi Arabia executed five Ethiopian migrants from Tigray on 27 July 2026 for nonlethal drug offences, and said authorities are "executing marginalized Ethiopian migrants after court hearings that sometimes barely last a few minutes without a lawyer or a translator."
- **Why it matters: this is a pattern with a running count.** At least 17 Ethiopian nationals have been executed on drug-related charges since the start of 2026, and roughly 79 more people are reported at imminent risk of execution on similar charges.
- **Why it matters: Saudi Arabia was told in advance and went ahead.** Human Rights Watch publicly called on 28 April 2026 for these executions to be halted. Three months later they were carried out. That sequence is why Accountability is scored at the floor.
- **Why it matters: the offence was not violent.** The charges were nonlethal drug offences — the men had accepted what they believed were legitimate job offers after fleeing Tigray and travelling through Yemen.
- **Why it matters: the total is external, not official.** The European Saudi Organization for Human Rights counted at least 116 executions in Saudi Arabia in 2026 as of 27 July. The state does not publish this data itself.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Awareness (1.6), Action (1.6) and Systemic Thinking (1.6).** These are relative, not absolute, strengths. The Saudi state is highly capable and knows precisely who it is detaining; the failure is not one of capacity or information but of how that capacity is used.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Accountability (1.0) and Integrity (1.0), both at the floor.** Documented harm continued after explicit external warning, no due-process data is published, and no repair of any kind is offered to the families of those executed.

## Evidence Gaps

- Saudi Arabia publishes no execution statistics, so the count of at least 116 executions in 2026 rests on external monitoring. This caps AB3 at the floor and is itself part of the finding.
- No independent access to the trials is possible, so the record of minutes-long hearings rests on HRW documentation rather than court transcripts.
- No evidence on the sustainability of Saudi Arabia's own frontline public services was found in-window, so B1 is scored conservatively at the low anchor rather than on evidence.
