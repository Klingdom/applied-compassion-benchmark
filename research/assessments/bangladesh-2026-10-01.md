## Bangladesh's new parliament undid the safeguards against enforced disappearance within months of taking power

On 6 and 10 September 2026 Bangladesh's parliament passed three laws: a National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) Act, an Enforced Disappearance Prevention and Redress Act, and a Special Response Battalion Act. Human Rights Watch reported on 22 September 2026 that together they reverse protections created by the 2024-2026 interim government. The NHRC loses its power to investigate enforced disappearances and to monitor detention places. The Rapid Action Battalion, a unit long accused of killings and disappearances, is renamed and keeps its personnel and powers with fewer safeguards.

The 2026-09-21 assessment did not cover these laws. It measured 35.0 on other grounds (death sentences in absentia; defendants denied their own lawyers) and did not file because the delta was -4.8.

This cycle re-scores three subdimensions. Blind Spot Mitigation (A3) falls from 3 to 2 because the body that looked for hidden detention abuse loses that power. Correction Willingness (AB2) falls from 2 to 1 because documented corrections were reversed. Consistency Under Pressure (I1) falls from 3 to 2 because the ruling party's reform commitments were dropped. Bangladesh measures 33.1 (Developing), 6.7 below the published 39.8. Filed on the magnitude trigger.
