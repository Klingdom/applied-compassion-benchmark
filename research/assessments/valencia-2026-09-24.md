# Screening Note: Valencia

**Assessment date:** 2026-09-24
**Outcome:** screened, not assessed. No proposal on the Valencia row.
**Published:** 60.9/100, Established, rank #39 of 250 global cities. Canonical reconstruction returns 60.9 exactly — no math-hygiene issue.

## The response was measured. It was competent, and it was not the city's.

The instruction was to measure the response to the wildfire. The response was located, assessed for quality, and then found to belong to a different institution.

**What the response was.** A wildfire broke out on 15 September 2026 at Tuéjar, in the Alto Turia Biosphere Reserve, and burned 690 hectares (later reported as stabilised at 835, with some reports of 880). The **Generalitat Valenciana** activated operational situation 2 under its forest-fire emergency plan because of potential threat to people and non-forest property, then scaled back to level 1 as the fire was contained. Around 300 personnel, 55 ground resources and 13 aircraft were deployed, reinforced by roughly 150 troops and 50 vehicles from Spain's **Unidad Militar de Emergencias**. A campsite and several farms were evacuated as a precaution. Two forest firefighters suffered minor injuries — one burned, one bruised when a fire engine overturned. No civilian casualties were reported.

On the benchmark's anchors that is a proportionate, pre-emptive response: escalation before harm, precautionary evacuation rather than reactive rescue, and de-escalation when the risk fell. It would score at anchor 3 to 4 on AC1 Responsiveness and AC2 Proportionality.

**Whose response it was.** Tuéjar is roughly 70 kilometres inland from the city of València, in the province but well outside the municipality. The responding institutions are the regional government (Generalitat Valenciana) and the Spanish state (UME). The **Ajuntament de València**, which is what the "Valencia" row in a global-cities index denotes, had no operational role.

Crediting a city row for a regional government's wildfire response would attribute conduct to an institution that did not perform it.

## The second in-window item has the same attribution problem, in the opposite direction

On **21 September 2026** the Spanish Congress held a fresh hearing on the October 2024 DANA floods, summoning former Generalitat president Carlos Mazón and former emergency deputy secretary Emilio Argüeso. Three associations representing victims and affected people demanded accountability from Mazón outside Congress that day. The floods killed 224 people (some counts 232), and Mazón resigned in 2025 after three demonstrations of roughly 100,000 people each.

This is a serious, live accountability failure, and it is in-window. It is also **regional**. The emergency-alert decision that the inquiry concerns was the Generalitat's, the resigned official was the regional president, and the worst-hit municipalities were Paiporta, Catarroja and others south of the city, not València itself.

So the window contains one item that would push a Valencia score up and one that would push it down, and **both belong to the Generalitat Valenciana, not to the city.** Netting them against each other on the city row would be doubly wrong.

## What this does flag

The published 60.9 rests on a vector of `3.5 / 3.5 / 3.5 / 3.0 / 3.5 / 3.5 / 4.0 / 3.0` — the 60.9 uniform-seed pattern, and `last_assessed: null`. The row has never been measured. Given that the region around it experienced Spain's deadliest flood in decades and an unresolved accountability process, an Established-band placeholder for this row is a live risk.

That is recorded as `flag-for-review` for a **first full baseline of the Valencia municipal row built on municipal evidence** — the Ajuntament's own flood and heat resilience planning, social services, housing, and its role in the 2024 recovery. It is explicitly **not** encoded as a score change here, per the rule that calibration concerns are not one-off proposals.

## Why no full assessment was run

Both in-window items attach to the regional government. A 40-subdimension municipal assessment assembled in this cycle would rest on out-of-window generic evidence while appearing to respond to the wildfire. `last_assessed` is not stamped.

## Triggers that would change this

- Conduct by the Ajuntament de València: an emergency plan activation, a heat or flood response, a housing or social-services decision.
- Any finding from the Congress inquiry that names municipal rather than regional responsibility.
- A fire or flood inside the municipal boundary.
