## Key Findings

- Why it matters: India’s government gave the students what they asked for, but only after the police had beaten them. Human Rights Watch reported on 23 July 2026 that on 20 July, security forces used tear gas and batons against a peaceful march on Parliament and shut down mobile internet in central Delhi.
- Delhi police forcibly removed activist Sonam Wangchuk from the Jantar Mantar protest site to hospital on 18 July 2026. He had been on hunger strike since 28 June 2026 and ended it, on day 26, on 23 July 2026.
- Amnesty International reported on 20 July 2026 that authorities suspended metro services and mobile internet, further restricting peaceful assembly. On 21 July the Supreme Court declined to hear allegations of abuse.
- Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on 25 July 2026. The government accepted all three protester demands: the resignation, compensation for families of students who died by suicide after exam retakes, and no punitive action against protesters.
- The net movement is +1.3 points, which is below the 5-point proposal threshold. It is driven by verified in-window government concessions, not by assessor variance, and no upgrade proposal was filed because the entity surfaced on negative evidence.
