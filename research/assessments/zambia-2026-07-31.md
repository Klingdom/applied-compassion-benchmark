### Score Difference Analysis

The -2.8 difference is partly a grid artifact — seven published dimensions sit at 2.5, unreachable from five whole-number subdimension scores — and partly real movement in Boundaries (2.5 to 2.2), Integrity (2.5 to 2.2), and Accountability and Empathy (each 2.5 to 2.4). The driver is dated and independently corroborated. Zambian police arrested Macpherson Mukuka, a journalist with the state broadcaster ZNBC, in Lusaka on 17 July 2026 and charged him under Section 10 of the Cyber Crimes Act No. 4 of 2025 with "prohibition of recording a private conversation without prior notice". The recording he published appeared to show public servants planning to work with polling stations to encourage voters to re-elect President Hakainde Hichilema on 13 August. He was held at Kabwata Police Station and remained in detention as of the 20 July 2026 report. He faces up to two years in prison, a fine of up to 200,000 penalty units — about $4,450 — or both. The Committee to Protect Journalists called on Zambia not to criminalise journalism carried out in the public interest, and the International Federation of Journalists issued a parallel statement. The reason the movement is limited to under three points is that Zambia retains a competitive electoral system, an active opposition, functioning courts through which the detention is being challenged, and open public debate in which rights groups and government are visibly clashing over the legislation. Those are real, and they are the difference between this and a country where the arrest would pass unremarked.

### Recommendation

Confirm the published score of 35.9, band Developing. Retain a watch flag with a specific trigger: the 13 August 2026 election. If further journalists are detained under the Cyber Crimes Act, or if Mukuka is convicted, the Integrity and Boundaries scores should be re-examined together and a proposal would then be supportable.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: Zambia jailed one of its own state broadcaster's journalists weeks before an election.** Police arrested Macpherson Mukuka of the Zambia National Broadcasting Corporation in Lusaka on 17 July 2026 and held him at Kabwata Police Station.
- **Why it matters: what he published was about the election itself.** The recording appeared to show public servants planning to work with polling stations to encourage voters to re-elect President Hakainde Hichilema on 13 August 2026.
- **Why it matters: the law used is barely a year old.** Mukuka was charged under Section 10 of the Cyber Crimes Act No. 4 of 2025 with recording a private conversation without prior notice. He faces up to two years in prison, a fine of up to 200,000 penalty units — about $4,450 — or both.
- **Why it matters: the watchdogs were specific.** The Committee to Protect Journalists called for his immediate release after four days in detention and said authorities should not criminalise journalism carried out in the public interest. The International Federation of Journalists issued a parallel statement.
- **Why it matters: this is one case, not a system collapse, and the score reflects that.** Zambia still has competitive elections, an active opposition, courts through which the detention is being challenged, and an open public argument between government and rights groups about the law. The score moves 2.8 points.
