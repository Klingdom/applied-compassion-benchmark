### Score Difference Analysis

The -2.2 difference has a grid component — five published dimensions sit at 2.5, unreachable from five whole-number subdimension scores — and a real component in Boundaries (2.0 to 1.8) and Integrity (2.0 to 1.8). On 22 July 2026 the Tbilisi City Court sentenced Vakho Sanaia, a journalist and host on the opposition-leaning TV Formula, to 14 days of administrative detention over Facebook posts mocking the Speaker of Parliament and an MP. He had already been fined GEL 6,000, about $2,280, on 10 July for the first post. Amnesty International covered the case in July 2026 under the headline "Georgia: Journalist jailed and comedian attacked for political satire", and Georgia's Media Advocacy Coalition stated that courts are being used to restrict freedom of speech. Set against that, Tbilisi City Hall runs a genuinely substantial municipal social programme: more than 100,000 surgeries and medical treatments financed, a 500-GEL medication voucher used by more than 70,000 socially vulnerable pensioners, home care co-financing for nearly 7,800 people, winter utility support for over 64,000 households, and 2,769 multiple sclerosis cases funded. Those keep Action at 2.6, the highest dimension in the profile.

### Recommendation

Confirm the published score of 32.8, band Developing. Retain a watch flag. The conversion trigger for a proposal is evidence that Tbilisi's municipal government — City Hall, not the court — has itself restricted civic space or withdrawn services from people on political grounds. Judicial conduct alone should not carry a municipal score far, and this assessment deliberately did not let it.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: a journalist went to jail for two weeks over a Facebook post.** The Tbilisi City Court sentenced Vakho Sanaia, a host on the opposition-leaning TV Formula, to 14 days of administrative detention on 22 July 2026 over posts mocking the Speaker of Parliament and an MP.
- **Why it matters: he had already been punished once for the same words.** Sanaia was fined GEL 6,000, about $2,280, on 10 July 2026 for the first post. He then posted again reproducing the words he had been fined for, and was jailed.
- **Why it matters: media lawyers say this is now the pattern.** Georgia's Media Advocacy Coalition said on 22 July 2026 that courts are being used to restrict freedom of speech. Amnesty International covered the case in July 2026 alongside an attack on a comedian.
- **Why it matters: the court is not the city government, and that limits how far this moves the score.** The Tbilisi City Court is part of Georgia's national judiciary. Tbilisi City Hall, which runs the city's services, is a separate institution. The score moved 2.2 points, not more, for that reason.
- **Why it matters: Tbilisi City Hall does deliver at scale.** It has financed more than 100,000 surgeries and medical treatments, gives a 500-GEL medication voucher used by over 70,000 socially vulnerable pensioners, co-finances home care for nearly 7,800 people and supports over 64,000 households with winter utility bills.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Action (2.6).** Tbilisi City Hall funds health, education and utility support at a scale that is unusual for a municipality of its size and publishes beneficiary counts for each programme.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Boundaries (1.8) and Integrity (1.8).** A journalist was punished twice for the same expression in the city's courts, and no municipal institution acknowledged or reviewed the pattern the Media Advocacy Coalition described.

- The division between Tbilisi City Hall and the Tbilisi City Court is not addressed by any source that treats the city as a single governance unit, so the attribution weighting here is an assessor judgment and is declared as such.
- No disaggregated data on who receives Tbilisi municipal services was located, capping EQ1, EQ3 and E3.
- No Tbilisi City Hall statement on the Sanaia case was found, so the municipality's own position is absent from the record.

## Anti-False-Positive Screening

Attribution examined and partially accepted, with the weighting declared. The Tbilisi City Court is an organ of Georgia's national judiciary, not of the Tbilisi municipal government, so a strict attribution screen would drop this flag entirely. It was not dropped, because the global-cities index scores a city as a governance environment for the people who live in it, and a court sitting in Tbilisi jailing a Tbilisi-based journalist changes the civic conditions of that city. The movement was therefore confined to Boundaries and Integrity — the dimensions that civic space touches — and was capped at 0.2 raw points each, rather than applied across the profile as it would be for a national-government entity. Directionality is consistent: negative evidence, negative movement, no upgrade. Baseline provenance: this entity has no prior assessment on record, so the published 32.8 carries no history that this finding could contradict.
