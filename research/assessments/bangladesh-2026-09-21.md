## Why this entity was assessed

On 15 September 2026 Bangladesh's International Crimes Tribunal sentenced seven former Awami League officials to death in absentia for crimes against humanity over the state's suppression of the 2024 student-led protests, in which at least 1,400 people died according to the United Nations. Bangladesh was last assessed on 2026-09-01.

## The instrument question: the ruling genuinely pulls both ways

The scanner flagged this as directionally ambiguous and declined to pre-judge it. That was correct, and the ambiguity is real rather than a way of avoiding a call. Both directions are set out here explicitly, with the dimensions each one moves.

### What points up

**A state is prosecuting its own predecessor for mass killing, and ordering money to the victims.** The tribunal found the defendants "responsible for conduct including the incitement to and ordering of killings during the crackdown" after an eight-month trial. It also "ordered the confiscation of half the assets belonging to the five men, saying that they will be distributed as compensation to the families of those who died or were injured."

That is not a gesture. It is a court-ordered transfer of resources to the families of the dead and injured, which is the behaviour AB5 Reparative Action and EQ5 Historical Harm Acknowledgment are written to detect. It moves:

- **AB1 Harm Acknowledgment 3/5** — the state has formally established, through its own judiciary, that state forces committed crimes against humanity. That is acknowledgment made before any external body compelled it.
- **AB5 Reparative Action 3/5** — asset confiscation earmarked for victims' families is a concrete reparative step in a specific case.
- **EQ5 Historical Harm Acknowledgment 3/5** and **EQ2 Priority for Vulnerable 3/5** — the beneficiaries named are bereaved and injured families.
- **E4 Validation 3/5** — the victims' losses are treated as the basis of the remedy.
- **ACC rises 2.2 → 2.4; EQU rises 2.4 → 2.6.**

### What points down

**The instrument is capital punishment imposed on people who were not in the room.** Sixty-four of the sixty-eight people convicted in these proceedings are not in custody. A state-appointed lawyer — not counsel of the defendants' choosing — argued they "were not directly involved in the killings; they have been convicted due to their fellow activists and supporters."

Human Rights Watch, at tier 3, documented the tribunal's procedural defects on 28 July 2026: prosecutors could seek arrest "without meeting any evidential threshold"; detainees held for months without written reasons and denied interlocutory appeal; trials able to begin three weeks after evidence disclosure; and "Trials in absentia are held without appropriate safeguards, including defendants' right to choose lawyers to represent them." HRW also found investigating officers' statements containing passages duplicated across multiple statements, casting doubt on their authenticity.

That moves:

- **B5 Consent Orientation 1/5** — a defendant denied counsel of choice in a capital case is the floor anchor for this subdimension.
- **E3 Non-Judgment 2/5**, **AB3 Transparency 2/5**, **AB4 Systemic Learning 2/5**, **I4 Values Alignment 2/5** — an interim government that came to power on the promise of ending Awami-era repression is using arrest powers with no evidential threshold and capital sentencing.
- **EMP falls 2.4 → 2.2; BND falls 2.6 → 2.4.**

### And the thing that decides how much weight the death sentences carry

**No one has been executed, and on the present record no one is positioned to be.** The sentences are in absentia against people outside the country. The harm from the capital element is prospective and, for now, largely symbolic; the reparative element is operative now. That is why the death sentences pull the score down without dominating it.

**The composite result is 35.0 — the same value measured on 2026-09-01.** That is not a silent netting to zero. Four dimensions moved: EQU +0.2 and ACC +0.2 up, EMP −0.2 and BND −0.2 down. They happen to cancel at the composite level. The per-dimension movements are the finding; the flat composite is an arithmetic consequence of it.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Bangladesh is prosecuting the people who ran the state when 1,400 protesters died. On 15 September 2026 its International Crimes Tribunal sentenced seven former Awami League officials to death. At least 1,400 people were killed in the 2024 crackdown, according to the United Nations.
- Why it matters: the court also ordered money to the victims. Half the assets of five of the convicted men are to be seized and paid to the families of those killed or injured. That is real repair, and it lifted Bangladesh's Equity and Accountability scores.
- Why it matters: the same ruling makes the process worse. All seven were tried without being present, and a state-appointed lawyer — not one they chose — spoke for them. Human Rights Watch found in July 2026 that the tribunal lets prosecutors arrest people without meeting any evidence test.
- Why it matters: that cost Bangladesh points on consent and empathy. Denying a person their own lawyer in a death-penalty case is the lowest score the benchmark can give for consent — 1 out of 5.
- Why it matters: the two halves cancel out to the same score, 35.0 out of 100, and that is worth saying plainly. Four of the eight areas moved. Two up, two down, by the same amount.
- Why it matters: the published score of 39.8 has now been measured at 35.0 three separate times. That is a 4.8-point gap, just under the 5-point line for filing a change. It is referred for review rather than pushed through.

## Watch Flag

Downward, retained and sharpened. Re-test AB5 and EQ2 on whether confiscated assets are in fact distributed — an unfulfilled compensation order would remove the only thing holding ACC and EQU up. Re-test AB2 on any procedural reform of the tribunal.
