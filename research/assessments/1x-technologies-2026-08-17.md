### Score Difference Analysis

The published vector `4 / 4 / 4 / 3.5 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4` is byte-identical across four robotics labs occupying consecutive alphabetical ranks. It is a never-assessed uniform seed. 1X Technologies has never been individually assessed against the 40 behavioural anchors before this report.

The published value asserts practice at the fourth anchor across all eight dimensions: published disaggregated data, formal pathways, documented reparative action, independent verification and community confirmation. 1X Technologies publishes none of those things. 37 of the 40 subdimensions here score at the low anchor of 2, which under this study's fixed rule means **no evidence was found in either direction** — not that harm was found.

### Recommendation

The published score is **overstated** and should be revised down to 26.9, band Developing. This is a seed correction, not a conduct downgrade.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: 1X Technologies is published as "Exemplary" in one index and "Functional" in another. The same company holds two Compassion Benchmark scores 31.4 points apart. Both are placeholders.
- NEO, the $20,000 home humanoid, relies on "Expert Mode": a 1X employee in the United States watches a customer’s home through the robot’s cameras in a virtual reality headset and drives the robot.
- 1X does build real consent machinery. Sessions happen only in windows the owner chooses, each needs explicit approval, and light rings signal when an operator is active. That is why Consent Orientation scores 3 rather than 2.
- The gap is bystanders. Independent legal analysis notes that children, family members and visitors in the home cannot meaningfully consent, and that 1X’s privacy policy has no section specific to robot camera feeds.
- Thirty-four of 40 subdimensions sit at the low anchor because no disclosure exists. 1X publishes no institutional data on harm, equity, staff wellbeing, repair or values governance.
