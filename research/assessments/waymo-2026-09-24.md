## Finding 1: the event is outside the window

The scan dates this item 2026-09-17, citing a Townhall aggregation published 18 September. The San Francisco Chronicle and CBS Bay Area reporting places the incident on **3 September 2026 at about 3:52 a.m.**, on the 800 block of 40th Avenue in the Richmond District.

This cycle's lookback window opens on **2026-09-10**. The event predates it by seven days. The scan's evidence_date is the aggregator's publication date, not the event date — the same pattern the scan's own alerts flag elsewhere in this cycle.

The event does post-date Waymo's last assessment (2026-08-17), so it is not already scored. It belongs to the 2026-09-03 to 2026-09-10 period, which prior cycles covered; it should be recorded as a carried item rather than treated as new.

## Finding 2: the factual record cannot support a score in either direction

The coordinator asked for this to be reasoned explicitly in both directions rather than netted to zero. Here are both readings, and then the reason neither can be scored.

**Reading A — harm prevention.** A loaded AR-style untraceable firearm was in a shared vehicle at 4 a.m. Waymo detected "a violation of our terms of service involving a firearm," stopped the vehicle, and alerted emergency services. Police recovered the weapon. Nobody was injured. On this reading the vehicle protected its occupants, the next rider, and anyone the weapon might have been used against. That would touch A1 Suffering Detection and A5 Anticipatory Awareness upward: a detection mechanism that acted before harm occurred.

**Reading B — surveillance by default.** A commercial robotaxi performed in-cabin detection of a passenger's possessions and initiated a police contact on its own account, with no request from law enforcement. The result was a high-risk vehicle stop and the arrest and booking of **two children** into a juvenile justice centre. On this reading Waymo exercised a police-referral function that no rider consented to and no public policy governs. That would touch B5 Consent Orientation and AB3 Transparency downward, and would raise an EQ3 Bias Awareness question about which riders such a mechanism is most likely to be used against.

**Why neither is scored.** The pivot between the two readings is *how the detection worked and what riders were told*, and that is precisely what is missing. The SF Chronicle reports that "authorities have not publicly detailed how Waymo's internal systems identified the firearm." No Waymo policy document describing autonomous, unsolicited police referral was located. No Waymo statement beyond the terms-of-service phrase was located. No court or juvenile-justice record is public, because the arrestees are minors.

Without knowing whether this was in-cabin audio, video, a rider report relayed to support staff, or a human operator's decision, the benchmark cannot tell a consent failure from a safety response. **Both readings are consistent with every fact on the record.** Scoring either would be inventing the missing fact.

## Triggers that would make this assessable

- **A Waymo disclosure of the mechanism.** If Waymo publishes how in-cabin detection works, what triggers a police contact, and what riders are told, that is a direct B5 and AB3 item and should be scored — in whichever direction the disclosure points.
- **A rider-facing policy.** A published policy on autonomous law-enforcement referral would move B5 and AB3 upward. Its continued absence, once asked for, would move them downward.
- **A pattern.** A second or third instance, especially with demographic pattern, would make EQ3 assessable.
- **A regulator or court statement** on whether a vehicle operator may initiate a police contact from in-cabin sensing would raise the evidence tier from 2 to 4 or 5.
