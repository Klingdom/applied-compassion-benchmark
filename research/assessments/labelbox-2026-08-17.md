## Key findings

- Labelbox scores lowest in this study, at 23.8, and it is the only one of the 15 where located adverse evidence, rather than missing disclosure, drives the result.
- The evidence concerns Alignerr, Labelbox's AI-training crowdwork platform. Four independent specialist sources describe the same mechanisms: unpaid evaluation phases, pay gated on a reviewer accepting the work, accounts deleted without warning, and no appeal path that workers have found to work.
- One review puts it concretely: if you spend five hours on tasks and the client closes the project before a reviewer reaches your queue, that work can go unpaid entirely. Workers report being owed $800 to $3,000 or more.
- Every one of those sources sits at tier 1 or tier 2 — gig-work review sites and worker forums. The finding is corroborated but not verified at tier 4, and should be treated as provisional. SOMO's tier-4 research documents the same pattern across the sector, but not at Labelbox specifically.
- Three subdimensions score the floor of 1: no appeal channel, a pay model that shifts risk onto the lowest-paid, and deactivation without explanation or alternative. Those are the only floor scores in this 600-subdimension study.
