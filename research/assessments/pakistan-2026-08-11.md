## Why this entity was assessed tonight

The scanner flagged Pakistan's new Foreign Media Facilitation Guidelines (2026), which require foreign correspondents and Pakistani staff of international outlets to obtain state accreditation and a no-objection certificate before reporting outside Islamabad, Karachi and Lahore, amid a crackdown on coverage of unrest in Pakistan-administered Kashmir.

## Key Findings

- Pakistan now requires state permission to report outside three cities. New Foreign Media Facilitation Guidelines require foreign correspondents and the Pakistani staff of international outlets to obtain a "no-objection certificate (NOC) from the information ministry for 'official assignments, especially for work outside Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi.'"
- Accreditation can be pulled for vaguely defined offences. The rules allow authorities to suspend accreditation for "acts against the ideology, sovereignty, security or public order of Pakistan."
- The timing points at Kashmir. The Committee to Protect Journalists reported on 6 August 2026 that the guidelines came days after the government criticised international coverage of Kashmir elections and protests, and restricted access to Al Jazeera's website.
- Pakistan's own human rights body called it a chilling message. The Human Rights Commission of Pakistan said the requirement sends a chilling message to critical reporting.
- One report is not enough to move a published score. The finding rests on a single watchdog source. It is recorded as a watch flag with a clear trigger: a second, higher-tier source would convert it into a formal proposal.
