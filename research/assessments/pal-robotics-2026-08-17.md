### Score Difference Analysis

The published vector `4 / 4 / 4 / 3.5 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4` is byte-identical across four robotics labs occupying consecutive alphabetical ranks. It is a never-assessed uniform seed. PAL Robotics has never been individually assessed against the 40 behavioural anchors before this report.

The published value asserts practice at the fourth anchor across all eight dimensions: published disaggregated data, formal pathways, documented reparative action, independent verification and community confirmation. PAL Robotics publishes none of those things. 15 of the 40 subdimensions here score at the low anchor of 2, which under this study's fixed rule means **no evidence was found in either direction** — not that harm was found.

### Recommendation

The published score is **overstated** and should be revised down to 43.1, band Functional. This is a seed correction, not a conduct downgrade.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: PAL Robotics scores 43.1, higher than three of the six labs published 1.6 points above it. The published ranking has the order wrong, not just the level.
- PAL is the only lab in the study that published an account of what went wrong in its own deployments. Its 2022 paper "Lessons Learnt from Deploying ARI in Residential Care" is voluntary, unflattering disclosure.
- Its ARI robot was tested with more than 100 elderly patients at Broca Hospital in Paris under the EU-funded SPRING project, coordinated by INRIA. The European Commission’s own Horizon Magazine reported the results.
- The hospital’s data protection rules forced the robot to stay in one room rather than roam. PAL worked inside that constraint rather than around it — evidence of consent and privacy discipline in a care setting.
- PAL gives away work competitors use. It builds on open ROS 2, mentors through Google Summer of Code and helps run ROSCon. Coalitional Compassion is its highest subdimension at 4 of 5.
