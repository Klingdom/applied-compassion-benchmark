## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Venezuela on the interim government's announcement of 14-15 August 2026 that 131 people considered political prisoners had been released under the Program for Peace and Democratic Coexistence. **This is the rare case where an entity surfaces on positive within-window evidence.** Venezuela was last assessed on 2026-07-18 and is published at 18.0 of 100.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**BASELINE PROVENANCE — checked before considering any upgrade (screen 3e-bis §1 and §2).** This is exactly the failure mode the screening rules exist to catch: a country undergoing a political transition, where it would be tempting to argue the published score "predates the transition". **That argument would be false and is rejected.** The record shows:

- Nicolás Maduro was removed on **3 January 2026**.
- Venezuela's published 18.0 was applied on **19 April 2026** — three and a half months *after* the transition, as an upgrade from 4.4 (see `research/APPLIED_CHANGES.md`).
- It was confirmed again on **2026-07-17** and **2026-07-18**, both times on the **current interim government's own conduct**.

**The published score is post-transition and recent. No "stale baseline" rationale is available or used.**

**DIRECTIONALITY (screen 3e-bis §3).** Venezuela surfaced on positive evidence, so an upgrade is not barred by the directionality screen. But the screen also requires that an upgrade rest on **specific new positive conduct by the entity itself** — which the prisoner releases are — and that it be weighed against countervailing within-window evidence, which it is below. The result is a net movement of +0.1 points. **That is a confirmation, not an upgrade.**

**ATTRIBUTION.** The releases were carried out by Venezuela's interim government under Acting President Delcy Rodríguez. That is the entity. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio's welcome of the releases is US conduct and is not credited to Venezuela. Foro Penal and Vente Venezuela are independent verifiers, not the entity.

**Verification discipline.** The government claimed 131 releases. Foro Penal independently verified "at least 40"; Vente Venezuela verified 43. **This assessment scores the independently verified floor, not the government's claim.** Foro Penal reported 391 political prisoners still detained as of 10 August 2026 (228 civilians, 163 military). Amnesty International's April 2026 update found hundreds still arbitrarily detained despite the February 2026 Amnesty Law.

**Date checks.** Al Jazeera URL path /2026/8/15/; CP24 /2026/08/15/. Congressional Research Service report dated 2026-08-13. All explicit 2026. No year-confusion found.

### Analysis

The assessed composite of 18.1 sits 0.1 points above the published 18.0, in the same Critical band. **This is a confirmation, and it is the correct outcome, not a null result.** The positive evidence is real: Accountability rises from 1.9 to 2.0 on a genuine, independently verified course correction. It is offset by Boundaries and Equity holding at 1.6 and by Action falling to 1.6, because 391 people remain detained, released detainees' legal status is unresolved, and the state's own claim exceeded independent verification by roughly three to one. A partial release that leaves three-quarters of the detained population inside is worth about a tenth of a point on a hundred-point scale. It also resolves the 0.40-point reconstruction residue carried in the digests since 2026-07-17: the assessed vector reconstructs cleanly to 18.1.

## Key Findings

- Venezuela holds at about 18 of 100. The score moves by one-tenth of a point — a confirmation, not an upgrade.
- Venezuela's interim government said it freed 131 political prisoners on 14-15 August 2026. Independent monitors verified far fewer: Foro Penal confirmed at least 40, and the opposition movement Vente Venezuela confirmed 43. The benchmark scores the verified number, not the claim.
- Foro Penal counted 391 people still detained as of 10 August 2026 — 228 civilians and 163 military. Roughly three in four of the detained population stayed inside.
- The releases are still real. They are the single clearest piece of positive conduct any entity in this cohort produced, and Accountability rises accordingly from 1.9 to 2.0.
- The benchmark specifically checked and rejected the argument that Venezuela's low score is stale because Maduro is gone. Maduro was removed on 3 January 2026. The 18.0 score was set on 19 April 2026 and confirmed twice in July — all after the transition, all on the current government's own conduct.

## Strongest Dimensions

Accountability (ACC, raw 2.00) is the strongest dimension and the only one to rise. It rests on a documented, independently verified course correction — the August release tranche, following 104 releases in January 2026.

## Weakest Dimensions

Boundaries (BND, raw 1.60), Equity (EQU, raw 1.60), Action (ACT, raw 1.60) and Systemic Thinking (SYS, raw 1.60) are jointly weakest. The binding constraint is the same in each: the machinery that produced mass political detention is untouched, so relief is discretionary rather than structural.

## Evidence Gaps

- The gap between the claimed 131 releases and the independently verified 40 to 43 could not be closed within the time budget; the assessment scores the verified floor.

- No post-release monitoring data on the legal status or safety of those freed was located.
