### Score Difference Analysis

The -0.3 difference is entirely a grid artifact from seven published dimensions sitting at 2.5, a value five whole-number subdimension scores cannot produce. The flagged event contributes nothing, because it is not Tacoma's conduct. On 30 July 2026 a three-judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit issued a published 2-1 decision holding that the federal government incorrectly interprets a 1996 statute on mandatory detention, with the result that noncriminal immigrants held at the GEO Group-run Northwest ICE Processing Center are entitled to bond hearings. The parties are the Northwest Immigrant Rights Project, which brought the class action, and the federal government. The Spokesman-Review's report of the same date states that hundreds of detainees across nine western states are affected. The City of Tacoma is not identified as a party to that litigation, and this was verified by fetching the article directly. The related dispute over paused Washington state health inspections at the same facility involves GEO Group, which refused entry, the State of Washington, which won the injunction, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement as the contracting agency, and a federal appellate panel that granted the stay. None of those actors is the City of Tacoma either. Tacoma's own conduct produced no new evidence inside this window.

Confirm the published score of 35.9, band Developing. No proposal. ## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: Tacoma has now been flagged twice for something it did not do.** The 30 July 2026 Ninth Circuit ruling concerns the federal immigration court and the GEO Group-run Northwest ICE Processing Center. The City of Tacoma is not a party to the case.
- **Why it matters: the ruling itself is good news for detainees, and it still is not Tacoma's doing.** The 2-1 published decision means noncriminal immigrants held at the facility are entitled to bond hearings, affecting hundreds of detainees across nine western states. The credit belongs to the Northwest Immigrant Rights Project and the court.
- **Why it matters: the inspection dispute is also someone else's.** Washington state health inspectors remain blocked from the facility amid more than 3,500 complaints of unsafe food, mould and inadequate medical care. GEO Group refused entry, the State of Washington sued, and a federal appeals panel paused the order.
- **Why it matters: Tacoma did act, earlier, and within its actual powers.** In May 2026 the council passed Ordinance No. 29105, barring civil immigration enforcement from city buildings, parks, parking lots and vehicles, and Resolution No. 41757 sent $20,000 to pay for detainee phone access and welcome-centre services.
- **Why it matters: Tacoma spends substantially on housing.** The 2025-2026 budget allocated $19.4 million to affordable housing, $11.3 million to emergency sheltering and $22.2 million to housing development and permitting.

The City of Tacoma is not a party to any of them, verified by fetching the Spokesman-Review article of 30 July 2026 directly.
