None of those actors is the City of Tacoma. Tacoma’s own conduct in the window produced no new evidence. ## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: the thing Tacoma was flagged for is not Tacoma’s doing.** The paused inspections concern the Northwest ICE Processing Center, which GEO Group owns and runs under a federal contract. The State of Washington sued, GEO Group refused entry, and a federal appeals court paused the order. The City of Tacoma is not a party to any of it.
- **Why it matters: Tacoma did act, but earlier and within its actual powers.** In May 2026 the council passed Ordinance No. 29105, which bans civil immigration enforcement from city buildings, parks, parking lots and vehicles. City leaders said plainly that they have limited authority over a privately owned federal contract facility.
- **Why it matters: the city put money where its jurisdiction reaches.** Resolution No. 41757 sent $20,000 of council contingency funds to Advocates for Immigrants in Detention Northwest to pay for phone access for people held in the detention center and for welcome-center services on release.
- **Tacoma spends substantially on housing and shelter.** The 2025-2026 budget allocated $19.4 million to affordable housing programmes, $11.3 million to emergency sheltering, $22.2 million to housing development and permitting, and $5.4 million to housing and homelessness services.
- **The encampment contract is designed around people’s belongings.** Tacoma raised its encampment mitigation contract from about $2.2 million to more than $4.6 million, and the work is specified to pick up clearly identified garbage while leaving tents, backpacks and personal items alone.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Action (2.6), Systemic Thinking (2.6) and Integrity (2.6).** Tacoma funds shelter and housing at meaningful scale for a city of its size, is expanding shelter beds and homeless villages in 2026, and used the authority it actually has — property control — rather than making claims it could not deliver on.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (2.0).** Tacoma publishes little disaggregated outcome data on who its housing, shelter and policing systems actually reach, and there is no independent audit of distributional outcomes. Historical harm acknowledgment is the thinnest subdimension in the profile.

## Evidence Gaps

- No published City of Tacoma outcome data disaggregated by race, disability or immigration status was found, which caps EQ3 and A1.
- The Office of Independent Investigations publishes case reports, but no aggregate annual accountability report with findings and corrective actions was located, capping AB3 at 3.
- Community testimony about whether the encampment mitigation practice is experienced as respectful is not systematically published, capping E1 and E4.

## Anti-False-Positive Screening

This is a **declared false positive**. The flagged event — the Ninth Circuit administrative stay of the Northwest ICE Processing Center inspection order, 21-24 July 2026 — is the conduct of GEO Group, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the State of Washington and a federal appellate panel. It is not the conduct of the City of Tacoma, which is not a party. Under the attribution rule it cannot move Tacoma’s score in either direction. Directionality check: no upgrade is proposed; Tacoma’s May 2026 ordinance is positive but falls outside the 16-30 July window and is recorded as standing context only. The -0.3 difference is declared a **grid artifact** from the unreachable published 2.5 values.
