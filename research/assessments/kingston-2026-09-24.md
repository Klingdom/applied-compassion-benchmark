# Screening Note: Kingston

## The double-count risk, addressed first

Jamaica was assessed on 2026-09-20 on this exact event. `research/assessments/jamaica-2026-09-20.md` measured Jamaica at 35.0 against a published 37.5, confirmed the score, and set a watch flag. It scored the killing of Amani Malcolm, 26, on North Street on the night of 18-19 September 2026, the protests that followed, and the wider policing record: INDECOM's report that police killed 215 people in under nine months, 67 people killed in 26 multiple-fatality incidents in 2026, and the continued absence of body-worn cameras during unmarked-vehicle operations.

**This note does not mirror that finding.** The question here is a different one: what did the *city* of Kingston do, or fail to do?

## Attribution: the conduct is not the city's

Policing in Kingston is carried out by the **Jamaica Constabulary Force**, a single national force under the national Ministry of National Security. The Kingston and St Andrew Municipal Corporation has no police powers, no authority over JCF operations, and no role in investigating police killings — that sits with INDECOM, also a national statutory body.

Every piece of in-window conduct the scanner surfaced is therefore national:

- The shooting itself, and the JCF's account of it (that Malcolm grabbed an officer's service firearm and then his M16 rifle) — JCF conduct.
- The heightened security presence in the Kingston Western Division and the protective measures for officers said to be under threat — JCF operational decisions, published on jcf.gov.jm.
- The oversight response — INDECOM, a national commission.

**No conduct by the Kingston and St Andrew Municipal Corporation was located in this window, in either direction.** No municipal statement, no community-safety intervention, and no service response to Denham Town or West Kingston residents was found.

## A non-state act that is not attributable to any scored institution

The scan reports that a man who refused to join the demonstration was assaulted and set on fire. That is conduct by private individuals during a riot. It is not conduct by the city, the police or the state, and the benchmark has no anchor that scores it against an institution. It is recorded and not scored.

## One genuinely separate item, referred rather than scored

Searching this window surfaced a **second** fatal police shooting and protest: residents mounted a fiery roadblock on Red Hills Road on 16 September 2026 over a different fatal police shooting (Jamaica Observer, 2026-09-16). The Jamaica 2026-09-20 assessment describes the 19 September protest and does not appear to cover this one.

This is JCF conduct and belongs on the **Jamaica** row, not the Kingston row. It is **referred to the next Jamaica cycle**, where it bears on whether INDECOM's group-killings trend is continuing. It is not scored here.

## Why no full assessment was run

A 40-subdimension assessment of the Kingston municipal row would have to be built almost entirely from out-of-window evidence about municipal services, because the in-window evidence attaches to a national force. That would produce a score shaped by an event while resting on none of it. The screening discipline applies: when the evidence attaches elsewhere, file nothing and record why. `last_assessed` is not stamped, because no scored assessment exists.

## Triggers that would change this

- Any conduct by the Kingston and St Andrew Municipal Corporation in response to the killings — a community-safety programme, a statement, a service intervention in Denham Town — would be city conduct and assessable.
- A structural finding about the city's own functions (waste collection, informal settlements, street lighting in West Kingston) would support a first full assessment of this never-assessed row.
