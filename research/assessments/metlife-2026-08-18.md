### Analysis

The assessed composite is 33.1 against a published 60.9 — a fall of 27.8 points and a crossing from Established into Developing. This is the largest movement in the cycle, and it carries the lowest confidence.

The driver is not adverse conduct. It is that MetLife discloses very little about the thing it actually does. Sixteen of forty subdimensions sit at the low anchor because no disclosure exists — a claims-paying insurer that publishes no disaggregated data on approval and denial outcomes, no claimant experience data, no grievance statistics and no independent verification of any of it. That is an **absence of disclosure**, scored at the low anchor and stated as such throughout. It is not a finding that MetLife mistreats claimants.

Two hard facts are scored. First, MetLife settled a class action brought by more than 6,000 retirees over how their pensions were calculated, for $23 million, **one day before trial**. The settlement permanently increases monthly benefits for married retirees who began receiving them from 2013 — which is substantive repair, not a bare cash payment, and AB5 scores 2 rather than 1 as a result. But it arrived at the last possible moment, so AB1 and AB2 stay at 2.

Second, MetLife's own annual report records that it received **no material adverse findings** from state insurance department examinations as of 31 December 2025, 2024 and 2023. That is a real, verifiable regulatory clean sheet and it supports AB3 at 3 of 5.

Where MetLife is strongest is internal. Its employee benefits and the global BeWell wellbeing initiative are detailed and market-tailored, giving B1 3 of 5 and I3 3 of 5. The MetLife Foundation has given more than $1 billion since 1976, supporting AC4 at 3 of 5. The pattern across the assessment is a company that documents how it treats its **employees** far better than how it treats its **customers**.

**Flag for review at 33.1 / Developing.** Delta -27.8 with a band crossing from Established to Developing. Both filing triggers are met and a change proposal is filed. Confidence is **low**. ## Key Findings

- Why it matters: MetLife's published score was never measured. Its eight dimension values are identical, digit for digit, to those of 21 other Fortune-500 companies.
- Why the score falls so far: MetLife publishes almost nothing about the thing it does. It pays insurance claims, and it releases no data on how many it approves, how many it denies, or how claimants experience the process.
- Sixteen of forty measures sit at the lowest anchor because no information exists — not because harm was found. That distinction is recorded throughout and matters for how this result should be read.
- MetLife settled a class action by more than 6,000 retirees over pension calculations for $23 million, one day before trial. The deal permanently raises monthly payments for married retirees who started receiving benefits from 2013.
- The score falls 27.8 points, from 60.9 to 33.1, and moves two bands. Confidence is low and the proposal is flagged for human review, not for application.

## Strongest Dimensions

Systemic Thinking (2.60 of 5). Long-horizon planning is inherent to insurance and is published, and MetLife participates in supervisory colleges chaired annually by its lead state regulator.

## Weakest Dimensions

Empathy (2.00 of 5). All five subdimensions sit at the low anchor because no independent evidence exists of how claimants experience MetLife. This is the single clearest example in the cycle of an absence of disclosure producing a low score.

## Evidence Gaps

The gaps are the finding. MetLife publishes no claims approval or denial rates, no claimant satisfaction data, no complaint volumes or outcomes, and no independent verification of its social programmes. Plaintiff-attorney marketing material asserting denial patterns was excluded as commercially motivated advocacy rather than evidence. A targeted evidence pass — state insurance department complaint indices, NAIC complaint ratios, and any independent claims audit — would materially change the confidence in this result and is recommended before any application.
