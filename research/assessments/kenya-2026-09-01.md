## Evidence-date, lifecycle and attribution checks

**THE ESCALATION CONDITION IS MET, AND IT WAS SET BY THE BENCHMARK'S OWN PRIOR ASSESSMENT.** Three protest-policing incidents are now on the record across three consecutive cycles, each documented separately:

1. **25 June 2026** — the Independent Policing Oversight Authority opened an investigation into abductions and police brutality at protests. Recorded in the 2026-08-18 assessment at tier 4.
2. **17 August 2026** — six activists were arrested outside police headquarters in Nairobi, including Julius Kamau, who had chained himself to the gates while demanding the Inspector General's resignation over police brutality. Recorded in the 2026-08-18 assessment at tier 2.
3. **28 August 2026** — police "fired tear gas at small-scale traders who are protesting against a hike in import duties" in central Nairobi. This cycle.

The 2026-08-18 assessment stated the escalation rule in advance, before this incident existed. Applying it now is following the benchmark's own standing instruction, not constructing a rationale after the fact.

**BASELINE PROVENANCE.** The published 35.9 was confirmed on 2026-07-08 with a downward watch flag, then confirmed again on 2026-08-18 at an assessed 32.5 with the watch flag escalated. It is a recent, evidence-based baseline that has been reviewed twice. **No stale-baseline reasoning is used or available.**

**DIRECTIONALITY.** Kenya surfaced on negative within-window evidence and this proposal is a downgrade. Direction matches evidence.

**ATTRIBUTION.** The tear gas was fired by Kenyan police. This is state conduct, directly and unambiguously. The traders' protest is not scored against Kenya; the policing of it is.

**COUNTERVAILING EVIDENCE — recorded, and it constrains the movement.** The Kenya Revenue Authority publicly defended and clarified the policy, stating the new benchmark was "designed to tackle the under-declaration and undervaluation of imported goods" and that the 3.2 million shilling figure was "a minimum reference point, not a fixed value for every container". A state agency explaining a contested policy to those affected is real engagement. Kenya also retains the Independent Policing Oversight Authority, a statutory body with a live caseload investigating police conduct — a genuinely functioning accountability institution that most entities in the Developing band do not have. Both are why Awareness, Boundaries and Systemic Thinking remain at 2.2 to 2.4 rather than falling further.

## Disclosure — what this score measures

This movement measures **documented conduct, not absence of disclosure**. Kenyan police fired tear gas at protesters; that is an act, recorded by an independent news organisation.

But the magnitude requires an honest caveat, and it is stated in the proposal file as well as here: **the -5.9 derives principally from a three-cycle pattern, not from the severity of the 28 August incident alone.** That incident is one tier-2 report with no reported injuries. What justifies the size of the move is that it is the third of its kind while the country's own police watchdog investigates the first. A reviewer who reads this as a 5.9-point penalty for one tear-gas dispersal will have misread it.

Countervailing evidence is credited explicitly and is the reason five of eight dimensions are held unchanged.
