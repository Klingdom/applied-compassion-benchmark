## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Zambia on Amnesty International's briefing of 2026-08-10, "Authoritarian practices eating away at human rights in Zambia", published three days before the 13 August 2026 general elections. Zambia was last assessed on 2026-07-31 at 33.1, which pre-dates the briefing and the election.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Dates verified.** The Amnesty briefing is dated 2026-08-10 and post-dates the 2026-07-31 assessment, so it is genuinely new evidence. The election was held 2026-08-13; Bloomberg dates the count suspension to 2026-08-14; Al Jazeera dates the observer reports to 2026-08-16 and the continuing count to 2026-08-17. All inside the window.

**Attribution — both directions.** The vague, hurriedly passed laws restricting assembly, association, expression and media freedom are acts of the Zambian state and are scored against it. The killing of a polling agent was carried out by persons unknown, not by the state; what is scored is the **state's response**, and that response was to arrest nine people. The Electoral Commission's decision to suspend counting to protect officials is likewise the state's own conduct and is scored as a protective act, not a harm.

**Scope note.** Only Awareness and Integrity were re-scored; the other six dimensions are carried forward unchanged. The sidecar contains only those two dimensions.

### Analysis

Two subdimensions move, both downward, and the composite falls 4.0 points — below the filing trigger.

A4 (Signal Amplification) falls from 2 to 1. Amnesty's briefing rests on May 2026 interviews with human rights defenders, journalists, student leaders and opposition politicians, and records journalists facing persistent harassment, intimidation, unlawful surveillance and criminal investigation for reporting on corruption and abuses. When the press is the channel and the press is under investigation, the channel is closed.

I4 (Values Alignment) falls from 2 to 1. Parliament passed the Public Gatherings Bill on 12 May 2026 to replace the 1955 Public Order Act, and an NGO Bill remains before it. Amnesty characterises these as broadly vague and hurriedly passed. Legislating against civic space while professing democratic renewal is a direct contradiction, and it has not been acknowledged.

Three things stop the score falling further, and all three are the state's own conduct. The Electoral Commission suspended counting on 14 August to protect officials from violence and resumed the same evening — a protective decision, disclosed with its reason. Police arrested nine people over the killing of a 57-year-old polling agent. And the election itself was genuinely contested, with the opposition able to run a parallel count and publicly dispute the result.

**Cycle note.** This is the second consecutive cycle in which Zambia assesses below its published 35.9 — 33.1 on 2026-07-31 and 31.9 now. Neither gap alone reaches the filing trigger, but the direction is consistent and is recorded as a watch flag.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Zambia tightened the law on protest and the press in the months before an election. Amnesty International published a briefing on 10 August 2026 documenting broadly vague, hurriedly passed laws restricting assembly, association, expression and media freedom.
- Journalists told Amnesty they face persistent harassment, intimidation, unlawful surveillance and criminal investigation — especially when reporting on corruption or human rights abuses.
- The 13 August 2026 election was violent in places. A 57-year-old polling agent was killed outside Lusaka. The Electoral Commission suspended counting nationwide on 14 August and resumed the same evening.
- Why it matters: the state also responded. Police arrested nine people over the killing, and the Electoral Commission said openly why it had stopped the count. Those are real accountability acts and they are scored.
- The score is confirmed at 35.9. The assessed value of 31.9 is 4.0 points lower — the second consecutive cycle below the published value, which is recorded as a watch flag rather than a change.
