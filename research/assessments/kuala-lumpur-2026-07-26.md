## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the demand that flagged Kuala Lumpur was aimed at the wrong capital. The scan recorded the Migrant Welfare Network as urging the Malaysian government. The primary sources show the letter was addressed to Bangladesh's head of government ahead of a state visit to Malaysia. The claim was corrected before scoring.
- The documented abuse is real and happens on the Malaysian side of the corridor. The network recorded allegations of excessive recruitment fees, debt bondage, unpaid wages, passport confiscation, inadequate housing, contract substitution and barriers to justice.
- The clearest number is the fee gap. Workers reportedly pay up to 600,000 taka to secure a job in Malaysia against an officially approved migration cost of about 78,990 taka — roughly seven and a half times the sanctioned amount.
- Malaysia halted labour recruitment from Bangladesh in June 2024 and it remains halted. That is a documented course correction and it holds Accountability above the placeholder value rather than below it.
- These are national labour-policy matters rather than Kuala Lumpur city-government conduct, which is why the finding informs the score only lightly and produces a confirmation.

## Strongest Dimensions

- Action (ACT) and Systemic Thinking (SYS), both at raw 2.8 — municipal delivery is functional and the recruitment suspension was a real correction.

## Weakest Dimensions

- Awareness (AWR) at raw 2.2. Abuse in the corridor was detected after the fact, not before.
