## Key Findings

- A UK tribunal let 880,000 businesses sue Google as one group. On 5 August 2026 the Competition Appeal Tribunal certified a GBP 5 billion opt-out class action alleging Google overcharged businesses for search ads. Businesses are included automatically unless they opt out.
- This is permission to sue, not a ruling that Google did anything wrong. The decision "does not amount to a finding that Google violated the law." Google says it will dispute the case vigorously. The benchmark scores it as rising legal exposure, not as proven harm.
- The chatbot suicide settlements happened in January, not August. Google and Character.AI agreed in principle on 7 January 2026 to settle five lawsuits from families whose teenagers died by suicide or self-harmed. The scanner carried this as an August item; it is seven months out of window and was corrected.
- Corporate diversity reporting collapsed across the Fortune 500 in 2026. Public disclosure of diversity work fell 65%, and participation in the Human Rights Campaign's Corporate Equality Index dropped from 377 companies to 131. Alphabet operates inside that shrinking disclosure environment.
- The published score held. Evidence-based scoring returns 38.7 against a published 40.0 — a 1.3-point difference, far below the 5 points needed to change anything.
