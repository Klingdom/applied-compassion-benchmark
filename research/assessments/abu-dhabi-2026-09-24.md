## Why this entity was assessed

On 21 September 2026 the Abu Dhabi Labour Court was reported to have recovered Dh7.8 million (about $2.1 million) in unpaid wages for 948 workers across three resolved collective disputes. Abu Dhabi has never been individually assessed (`last_assessed: null`).

## Evidence-date, provenance, attribution and screening checks

**DIRECTIONALITY (screening rule 3, applied in the positive direction).** Abu Dhabi surfaced on **positive** within-window evidence. Rule 3 requires that a proposal's direction match its evidence, and it cuts both ways: an entity surfaced on positive conduct evidence may move up. The positive conduct here is specific, in-window and attributable — a court delivering money to identified workers — and not merely the absence of new harm.

**ATTRIBUTION.** The Abu Dhabi Labour Court sits within the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department, an emirate-level institution, so the wage recovery is properly attributable to this row. The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MoHRE) and the Wage Protection System are **federal** UAE instruments. Their national figures are used as context for how the emirate's cases reach resolution, and are not scored as Abu Dhabi's own conduct.

**BASELINE PROVENANCE.** `research/APPLIED_CHANGES.md` has no Abu Dhabi entry and no proposal exists on disk. The published vector is differentiated rather than flat (`2.5 / 2.0 / 3.0 / 1.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.5 / 1.0`) but was never individually assessed. Canonical reconstruction of the published vector returns 21.9 exactly — **no math-hygiene issue.**

**SOURCE-INDEPENDENCE PROBLEM — THE REASON THIS IS FLAGGED, NOT RECOMMENDED.** Every positive figure in this assessment originates with the UAE government or an Emirati outlet: The National (UAE-owned), Arabian Business and Gulf News, all reporting MoHRE and court statements. The search returned no independent third-party audit of these figures. Under the evidence hierarchy, government-published institutional data is tier 4 only when it discloses failures; self-congratulatory enforcement statistics with no external verification sit closer to tier 2-3. That is why AC1 is held at 3/5 rather than 4/5, and why the recommendation is `flag-for-review` rather than `upgrade`.

**COUNTERVAILING EVIDENCE SOUGHT AND FOUND.** Human Rights Watch's World Report 2026 chapter on the UAE records that employers still hold disproportionate control under the kafala sponsorship system; that employers can file false "absconding" charges against workers who leave abusive jobs, exposing them to detention and deportation; that wage theft, illegal recruitment fees and passport confiscation continue in conditions that may amount to forced labour; that trade unions remain banned; and that the UAE has no non-discriminatory minimum wage. This holds EQU, BND and INT near the floor and is the reason the composite stays in the Developing band.

### Score Difference Analysis

Only three dimensions move materially, and they move for the same reason: the published vector placed Boundaries, Accountability and Integrity at or near the absolute floor, which asserts that nothing works at all. The in-window evidence shows that something does work. A court moved real money to 948 identified workers, and the state published the proportion of disputes it failed to resolve amicably. Those are anchor-3 behaviours, not anchor-1 behaviours.

Equally, nothing here disturbs the low end of the profile. The kafala system, the union ban and the absence of a non-discriminatory minimum wage keep EQU at 1.8, BND at 1.6 and INT at 1.6. The composite stays in Developing and moves 6.2 points within it.

**Why this is filed as flag-for-review rather than upgrade.** Every positive number comes from the UAE state or Emirati outlets reporting it. No independent verification exists. A reviewer may reasonably conclude that an emirate should not gain 6.2 points on its own press releases, and hold the score at 21.9 pending independent corroboration. That judgement belongs to a human, which is what `flag-for-review` is for.

### Recommendation

The published score is **understated on the evidence available**, but the evidence available is not independent. Propose 28.1 for review; hold at 21.9 if the reviewer requires independent verification of the wage-recovery and dispute-resolution figures. Confidence low: the delta is close to the 5-point threshold and the signals genuinely conflict.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: an Abu Dhabi court moved real money to real workers. It recovered Dh7.8 million, about $2.1 million, in unpaid wages for 948 workers across three cases, reported on 21 September 2026.
- Why it matters: it did not only pay the easy cases. 457 workers were paid in full and 491 got part of what they were owed, with the rest on scheduled instalments.
- Why it matters: none of this is independently checked. Every figure comes from the UAE government or Emirati news outlets, so the score moves less than the numbers alone would suggest.
- Why it matters: the reason the score stays low is structural, not incidental. Human Rights Watch reports that the sponsorship system still lets employers control workers, that unions are banned, and that there is no fair minimum wage.
- Why it matters: enforcement without voice has a ceiling. Workers can complain to the state, but they cannot organise, so the benchmark scores signal amplification at 2 out of 5.

## Strongest Dimensions

Action (raw 3.0), on a functioning dispute-resolution and wage-recovery machine.

## Weakest Dimensions

Boundaries and Integrity (both raw 1.6). Legal status still depends on an employer, consent is formal rather than real, and the sponsorship system is never named as the cause.

## Evidence Gaps

- No independent audit or ILO verification of the MoHRE or Abu Dhabi Labour Court figures was located. This is the single largest gap and the reason for the `flag-for-review` routing.
- No data on domestic workers, who are largely outside the labour-court route, was located.
- No Abu Dhabi-specific (as opposed to UAE-federal) human rights reporting dated inside the window was located.
