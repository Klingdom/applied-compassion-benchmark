## Entity identification

Moog Inc. is a New York Stock Exchange-listed company headquartered in East Aurora, New York, founded in 1951. It designs precision motion and fluid controls for military and commercial aircraft, satellites, launch vehicles, missiles, industrial machinery, marine and medical equipment, through three groups: Aircraft Controls, Space and Defense Controls, and Industrial Systems. Its inclusion in a robotics-labs index reflects its actuation and motion-control business rather than a robot product line.

### Score Difference Analysis

Moog is the second-largest disclosure surface in this study after Kawasaki Heavy Industries, and it still falls 20.9 points. The reason is the shape of what it publishes. Moog reports against three external frameworks and has board-level oversight of sustainability, which lifts Accountability, Systemic Thinking and Integrity slightly above the baseline. But the compassion framework asks about detecting distress, validating complaints, measuring whether help works, and repairing harm. On all of those Moog publishes nothing. Its human rights commitment is a policy statement dated August 2020 with no reported outcomes, no grievance statistics and no supply-chain audit results. Moog is also a defence contractor whose products control missiles and military aircraft; that is a compassion-relevant surface, and no evidence was located either way about how the company assesses the downstream effects of its defence sales.

### Recommendation

The published score is overstated and should be revised down to 27.5. This is a calibration correction, not a conduct finding: no regulatory action, safety finding or labour case against Moog was verified in the assessment window. Moog is a plausible candidate to score higher on a future cycle if its sustainability report is read in full.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: real ESG reporting is not the same as compassion infrastructure.** Moog Inc. reports annually against three external frameworks and still scores 27.5 of 100, because those frameworks measure climate and governance rather than how the company treats people in distress.
- **Moog's human rights commitment is clear but static.** It prohibits forced labour and respects union rights. The document is dated August 2020 and reports no outcomes, no grievance numbers and no supplier audit results.
- **Board oversight is real but thin.** Moog's board reviews sustainability twice a year through its Nominating and Governance Committee.
- **Moog is a defence contractor, which is a compassion-relevant surface.** Its products control military aircraft and missiles. No evidence was found either way about how Moog assesses the human effects of those sales.
- **Roughly 26 of the 40 scores rest on absence of disclosure — the second-lowest absence count in this study.** Moog publishes more than most of its peers here; it publishes little that this framework can score.

## Scoring Conventions Applied

- **Absence of disclosure scores 2, never 1.** The floor of 1 is used only where positive documented evidence of a specific failure exists. This is the "2-convention", the more conservative of the two conventions live in this repository.
- **A score of 4 requires published institutional data, a regulatory decision or third-party peer review. A score of 5 requires independent audit plus community testimony.**
- 36 of 40 subdimensions sit at 2. Of those, approximately 26 rest on absence of disclosure rather than on located evidence of a practice.
