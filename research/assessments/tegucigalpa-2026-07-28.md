## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Tegucigalpa is rationing water to about 1.6 million people and its own projection says supply runs to 22 August. Reservoirs Los Laureles and La Concepción sat at roughly 40 and 35 percent of capacity in late July 2026, and rationing intervals were extended to nine days.
- The drought itself is a natural hazard. Under the attribution rule, only the city's detection, response and structural preparation were scored.
- The municipality did respond: it published a distribution calendar, extended rationing as reserves fell, and published reservoir levels and projections. That keeps Awareness and Action at raw 2.0.
- Officials themselves name inadequate storage and deteriorated pipes as causes. That is municipal responsibility, and it is why Systemic Thinking falls to raw 1.8.
- Assessed composite 17.5 against a published 18.8. Delta -1.3, below the 5-point proposal threshold.

## Strongest Dimensions

- Awareness and Action (both raw 2.0). Reservoir levels, projections and a distribution calendar are published and updated.

## Weakest Dimensions

- Equity and Accountability (raw 1.4). Nothing shows households without tanks or the means to buy water are prioritised, and no systemic review follows earlier shortages.

## Evidence Gaps

- No independent audit of the rationing calendar's delivery in practice. No data on households entirely outside the piped network. Confidence is capped at medium.
