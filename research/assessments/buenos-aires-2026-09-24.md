# Screening Note: Buenos Aires

**Assessment date:** 2026-09-24
**Outcome:** screened, not assessed. No proposal on the Buenos Aires row.
**Published:** 46.9/100, Functional, rank #65 of 250 global cities. Canonical reconstruction returns 46.9 exactly — no math-hygiene issue.

## A protest is not a finding about the city

Thousands of people joined a "March of Anger" in Buenos Aires on 19 September 2026 against austerity measures and the socio-economic policies of President Javier Milei's government.

Three separate reasons prevent this from moving the Buenos Aires row.

**1. The grievance is national.** The austerity programme being protested is the federal government's. The Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires did not set it. A demonstration against national policy that happens to assemble in the national capital is not evidence about the capital's own conduct.

**2. The protest itself is not conduct by any institution.** The benchmark scores what institutions do. It scores a protest only through the institutional response — policing, medical provision, permit handling, or a decision changed as a result. **No such response was reported.** The single source describes the turnout and the grievance and nothing else.

**3. No policing evidence exists in either direction.** No tear gas, no detentions, no injuries and no use of Argentina's protocol on street blockages were reported at this march. That absence is recorded as an absence, and cannot be scored as either a harm or a restraint.

## Source quality is a further constraint

The scan's only source is **iz.ru**, a Russian outlet, reporting an Argentine domestic protest. No Argentine, regional or international outlet corroborating the 19 September march was located in this cycle's searching. A single low-tier, geographically distant source is not a basis for moving a published city score in either direction.

## A claim in the scan that is correctly excluded, and stays excluded

The scan itself notes that reports of mass detentions at Pinochet-anniversary commemorations on 12 September 2026 were "not independently sourced here and not relied upon for this evidence_date." That discipline is correct and is maintained here. Those commemorations are in Chile in any case, and have no bearing on the Buenos Aires row.

## What would be assessable, and was not found

Policing of the march by the Policía de la Ciudad, which *is* a city force in Buenos Aires — unlike Kingston, Bucharest, Kinshasa or Rio, where policing sits above the municipality. That makes Buenos Aires a row where protest policing genuinely would be city conduct. **The evidence simply is not there.** No account of the Policía de la Ciudad's handling of the march was located.

This is the cleanest available case of the distinction the benchmark needs: the attribution would have worked, and the evidence failed instead.

## Why no full assessment was run

There is no scorable in-window conduct. Buenos Aires has never been individually assessed and its published 46.9 is unverified, but a first baseline should be scheduled on its own evidence rather than assembled around an uncorroborated protest report. `last_assessed` is not stamped.

## Triggers that would change this

- Any reporting on how the Policía de la Ciudad handled the 19 September march, or a later one. That would be city conduct and directly assessable.
- A city-level service or social-assistance decision responding to the austerity programme's effects on Buenos Aires residents.
