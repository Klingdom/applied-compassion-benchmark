## Evidence-date, lifecycle and attribution checks

**LIFECYCLE RE-CHECK — PASSED, and this was a priority check.** The Kenscoff massacre is exactly the kind of severe, fast-moving claim that the 2026-08-26 Peru case showed can be retracted after publication. Both cited sources were re-fetched on the assessment date. **Both resolve and both still carry the same figures.** Al Jazeera (2026-08-26) states verbatim: "At least 47 people were killed, more than 50 others kidnapped, and another 22 people injured". NPR (2026-08-26) independently reports 47 dead and 50 kidnapped. The figures originate with the **United Nations Integrated Office in Haiti**, not with a single local alert, and are carried by two independent organisations. Neither source has been amended or retracted. The claim stands.

**DATE MISMATCH RESOLVED (gate warning 3, Haiti, two sources, 2 days).** The scanner recorded `evidence_date: 2026-08-24` while both URLs carry `/2026/8/26/`. There is no conflict. **The conduct date is the evening of 23 August into 24 August 2026** (the raid); **the publication date of both reports is 26 August 2026.** The scanner's evidence_date is the correct conduct date and the URL dates are the correct publication dates. Both are right. No year-confusion — both URLs carry explicit 2026 paths.

**ATTRIBUTION — the massacre is gang conduct and is NOT scored against the Haitian state.** Viv Ansanm is a non-state armed coalition, designated a foreign terrorist organisation by the United States government. The killings and kidnappings are its acts. Scoring them against Haiti would attribute a gang's crime to a state that failed to stop it, which is a different and lesser thing.

**What IS scored is Haiti's own conduct**, and there are findings on both sides of it:
- **Against:** residents of Kenscoff demanded the resignation of the local police chief, saying protection was not provided despite the community's proximity to the police station. That is a documented protective failure by the Haitian National Police.
- **For:** the government publicly called the attack a "heinous attack" and the prime minister's office stated "The government's authority will be restored, without weakness and without delay." A prompt public acknowledgment that names the harm is scored as validation, and it is why Empathy is not at a flat 1.0.

This is the same distinction applied on 2026-08-20, and it is the reason Haiti's Awareness, Empathy and Action sit at 1.2 rather than 1.0: the state's failure is one of capacity and provision, not an affirmative campaign of state violence.

## Key Findings

- At least 47 people were killed and more than 50 kidnapped in Kenscoff on 23-24 August 2026. The killers were the Viv Ansanm gang coalition, not the Haitian state, and the benchmark does not score a gang's crime against the state.
- What the benchmark does score is that the state did not protect them. Residents demanded their local police chief resign, saying protection was not provided even though the community sits near the police station.
- Haiti's government responded publicly within days, calling it a "heinous attack" and saying authority would be restored "without weakness and without delay." That prompt naming of the harm is why Haiti's Empathy score is not at the absolute minimum.
- Haiti scores 3.1 of 100. It scored 3.1 on 20 August too, on entirely different events. The same number twice from different evidence suggests this is a stable reading, not noise.
- Haiti has 4.7 points of downward room and the benchmark requires a 5.0-point move to file a change. Even total collapse across all 40 measures could not be filed. That is a flaw in the instrument, recorded here rather than papered over.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Systemic Thinking (1.4)**, marginally, and only because the Haitian state escalated the crisis to the UN Security Council, which convened an emergency meeting on 28 August 2026. That is recognition that the problem crosses systems and requires actors beyond the state. It is the highest thing found and it is still near the floor.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity, Boundaries, Accountability and Integrity, all at 1.0.** No evidence was located of the Haitian state distributing protection fairly, communicating honestly about what it can and cannot do, owning its failures, or maintaining commitments at cost. In a state that cannot protect a community adjacent to its own police station, these functions are not operating.

## Evidence Gaps

- No independent verification of the Haitian National Police's own account of the Kenscoff response was located. The protective-failure finding rests on residents' demands as reported by Al Jazeera.
- No in-window data on state services to the displaced was located. Action subdimensions rest on the absence of any evidenced response standard, and are scored 1 on the documented failure rather than 2 on absence, because the failure itself is documented.
- The outcome of the UN Security Council emergency meeting was not available at the assessment date.
- Haiti is near the floor, so most evidence gathered this cycle could not change the score in the downward direction even if it were more severe.

## Disclosure — what this score measures

This score rests on documented evidence of specific protective failure, not on absence of disclosure. **0 of 40 subdimensions are scored on absence.**

It must be stated plainly what this score is not: it is **not** a measure of the Haitian state having committed the Kenscoff killings. It did not. The killings were carried out by a non-state armed coalition. Haiti scores 3.1 because the state did not prevent, has not repaired, and cannot currently deliver the functions the benchmark measures — not because it is being held responsible for a gang's acts.

## Recommendation

**Confirm. No change proposal.** Delta -1.6, inside the threshold; no band crossing.

**Watch flag (downward):** escalate if Haitian state or state-aligned forces are directly implicated in civilian harm, or if the state obstructs the humanitarian or investigative response. Either would change the character of the finding from capacity failure to state conduct.

**Methodology finding recorded:** Haiti has 4.7 points of downward room against a 5.0-point trigger and is already in the lowest band. A real deterioration here is unfilable.
