## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: a case ending is not the same as a case being answered.** A federal court in Washington, D.C. dismissed a former Whole Foods overnight grocery worker’s discrimination claim on 24 July 2026 because he missed a filing deadline. The court did not decide whether what he described happened.
- **What he described.** The worker alleged that supervisors spoke Spanish in front of new hires who could not follow it, and that a supervisor knocked a box of merchandise toward him while mocking his appearance and intelligence.
- **Why it matters: Whole Foods has spent 18 months trying to undo a union vote its workers won.** Philadelphia workers voted 130 to 100 to join UFCW Local 1776 in January 2025. Whole Foods objected, lost before a regional director, appealed to the national board, and lost again in June 2026.
- **Why it matters: the union says the company punished the people who organised.** UFCW Local 1776 has filed several unfair labour practice charges alleging retaliation against union supporters, including reduced hours and terminations. These are charges, not findings.
- **Whole Foods remains in the upper half of the Fortune 500 index at rank 68 of 448.** This assessment moves it -3.4 points, which is below the threshold for a change proposal.
