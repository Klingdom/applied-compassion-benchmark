# Compassion Benchmark Attribution Screening Note: El Salvador

**Entity type:** Country  
**Assessment date:** 2026-09-22  
**Outcome:** confirmation; no dimension moves. The flagging event is a United States immigration decision and is not charged to El Salvador.  
**Published:** 15/100 (Critical), countries rank 146  
**Carried measurement:** 15/100 (Critical), measured in `research/assessments/el-salvador-2026-07-16.md`

## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged El Salvador because Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for roughly 200,000 Salvadoran holders in the United States expired on 9 September 2026, with no Federal Register notice published as of the scan and only a statement that an announcement would come "at the appropriate time." El Salvador was last assessed on 16 July 2026.

## The attribution question, which is the whole point of this screening

**TPS is a United States immigration designation. Its expiry is a decision of the United States government.** El Salvador is the entity under assessment in the countries index, and it did not make this decision, cannot make it, and has no legal power to reverse it. Charging a US policy choice to El Salvador's compassion score would be a category error of exactly the kind this benchmark corrected for Ukraine, Oakland and Los Angeles in recent cycles.

**So no dimension moves on the TPS expiry itself.**

## What *would* be scoreable, and what the evidence actually shows

Two things about El Salvador's own conduct are legitimately in scope:

1. **Whether El Salvador prepared for or responded to the prospect of large-scale return.** Nothing was located. A WOLA analysis of 2 September 2026 on the termination's impact contains no statement from the Salvadoran government about its position, its response, or any plan to receive returning citizens.
2. **Whether El Salvador advocated for its own nationals.** Reporting indicates Salvadoran officials have said little publicly beyond stating that the decision rests exclusively with the United States — described as a break with more than two decades in which governments across the political spectrum defended Salvadoran TPS holders. There is no indication of any agreement or joint effort to protect TPS beneficiaries, notwithstanding close relations between the two administrations.

**This is genuinely El Salvador's own conduct, and it is an absence of advocacy for roughly 200,000 of its own citizens.** It bears on A4 (Signal Amplification), AC4 (Resource Mobilization), S4 (Structural Critique) and I1 (Consistency Under Pressure).

## Why it does not move the score

Because it is already priced in, and the anchors are already at or near the floor.

The published 15.0 was set on 5 July 2026 as El Salvador's **first formal evidence-assessed baseline**, replacing an unassessed seed of 20.3, and it was driven entirely by the current government's own documented conduct: the constitutional amendment allowing indefinite presidential re-election, the May 2025 foreign-agents law, the arrest of anti-corruption chief Ruth López, Cristosal's forced exile after 25 years in the country, and a state of exception under which roughly 87,000 people have been detained without due process. A4 sits inside AWR at 1.6, S4 inside SYS at 1.8, I1 inside INT at 1.6, and AC4 inside the one dimension held deliberately high — ACT at 2.4, to credit the genuine reduction in homicide.

A government that forced its leading human rights organisation into exile is already scored as one that does not amplify the voices of people without power. Its failure to advocate for citizens abroad is the same behaviour, in a new setting. It is corroboration, not a discrete new finding, and the 2026-07-16 assessment already reached exactly this conclusion about July's evidence: same-conduct continuation, not a new event.

## Screening checks (§3e-bis)

1. **Baseline provenance — checked, and it matters here.** `research/APPLIED_CHANGES.md` records the 15.0 applied on 2026-07-05 as a band crossing from Developing to Critical, described as the "first formal evidence-assessed baseline," with the full evidential basis set out. `rotation-state.json` records `last_assessed: 2026-07-16`. The score is 79 days old and was set by El Salvador's own conduct.
2. **No "stale baseline" rationale is available or used.** Any argument that the 15.0 predates current conditions is false on the record.
3. **Directionality.** El Salvador surfaced on negative evidence. No upward movement is taken. No downward movement is taken either, because the measurable conduct is already at the anchors it would be moved to.
4. **Rationale-versus-history consistency.** This note is consistent with the 2026-07-05 apply and the 2026-07-16 confirmation.
5. **Not a calibration argument.** No claim is made about El Salvador's position relative to peers.
6. **Ledger discipline.** The scan deliberately avoided the ledgered "140 human rights defenders forced into exile" claim, which is a distinct and separately dated topic. Nothing in this note relies on it.
7. **Distinguishing absence of advocacy from absence of harm.** The finding here is a government's silence about its own citizens, which is conduct. It is not scored *upward* as an absence of harm, and it is not scored *downward* twice, because the same behavioural pattern already set the near-floor anchors in July.

## Determination

**CONFIRM 15.0 (Critical).** Delta 0.0, band unchanged, no filing trigger met. The downward-strong watch flag is retained and extended to cover any reception or reintegration commitment, or any Federal Register notice.
