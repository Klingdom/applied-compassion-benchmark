## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**PLACEHOLDER CORRECTION, NOT A DECLINE FINDING.** The published 60.9 was never an individual assessment. It is the known Fortune-500 seed value shared byte-for-byte with **twenty-two** companies, and this entity has never been assessed (`last_assessed: null`). Three cluster members were de-seeded on 2026-08-18 — HP Inc. to 42.5, Marriott International to 44.4, MetLife to 33.1 — all filed as `flag-for-review` rather than `downgrade`, for exactly this reason. This filing follows that precedent.

**ENTITY-RECORD OBSERVATION.** The published index name **"InterContinental Hotel"** does not match the registered entity, **InterContinental Hotels Group PLC** (trading as IHG Hotels & Resorts), and appears truncated. The slug `intercontinental-hotel` is retained for linkage consistency and the naming issue is **logged for coordinator review**, not silently corrected — the same handling given to the "Democratic Republic of C" truncation this cycle.

**UNLIKE THE OTHER FOUR ROTATION ENTITIES, REAL ADVERSE EVIDENCE EXISTS.** IHG carries a documented record of allegations it did not answer:
- The Business & Human Rights Resource Centre records IHG's **non-response** to allegations of migrant-worker exploitation at IHG-branded hotels in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Oman and the UAE — recruitment-fee charging, poor housing, withheld passports, low pay.
- BHRRC separately records IHG's **non-response** to allegations that Holiday Inn LAX, an IHG-branded property, was among Los Angeles County hotels that replaced striking workers with vulnerable migrants, some of them children, prompting a District Attorney investigation into wages, overtime and breaks.
- Unite the union reported IHG to the **UN Global Compact** over what it described as unethical anti-trade-union behaviour, citing nearly a decade of repeated backtracking on union access at London Holiday Inn and Crowne Plaza hotels.

**ATTRIBUTION AND THE FRANCHISE DEFENCE.** IHG operates a substantially franchised model and states it is not involved in hiring at franchised properties. That is a real structural fact and it is weighed. But it does not remove IHG's own conduct from scoring, for two reasons: IHG requires all IHG-branded hotels to adopt its Human Rights Policy or an equivalent, which is an assertion of standard-setting authority; and **the non-response to allegations is IHG's own act**, not the franchisee's. Franchisee employment decisions are attributed to franchisees; IHG's standard-setting, enforcement and response conduct is attributed to IHG.

**Date check.** The IHG Responsible Business Report covering calendar 2024 was published 2025-02-27. The BHRRC records and the LA County DA investigation are 2025-2026 items. The Unite complaint is 2019 and is used only as long-run pattern context, explicitly labelled. No item was treated as current that is not. No year-confusion found.

### Analysis

The assessed composite of 34.4 sits 26.5 points below the published 60.9 and crosses two bands, from Established into Developing. Unlike the other rotation entities in this batch, the movement is not driven only by placeholder correction and absence of disclosure: IHG carries a documented pattern of declining to answer serious labour allegations, which is itself company conduct and is scored in Empathy (Validation, 2/5), Accountability (Harm Acknowledgment, 2/5) and Integrity (Consistency Under Pressure, 2/5). IHG's genuine strength is legally-anchored disclosure — a Modern Slavery Statement under UK law that includes unflattering risk findings, scored at 3/5 for Transparency.

## Key Findings

- InterContinental Hotels has never been assessed by this benchmark. Its published 60.9 is the known 22-company Fortune 500 placeholder. This is the first real measurement, landing at 34.4.
- Unlike the other four rotation companies this cycle, IHG has real adverse evidence against it — and its distinguishing feature is that it did not answer.
- The Business & Human Rights Resource Centre records IHG declining to respond to allegations of migrant-worker exploitation at IHG-branded hotels across four Gulf states: recruitment-fee charging, poor housing, withheld passports.
- IHG also did not respond to allegations that a Holiday Inn LAX property was among Los Angeles hotels that replaced striking workers with vulnerable migrants, some of them children. The county District Attorney opened an investigation.
- IHG says it is not involved in hiring at franchised hotels. That is true and it is weighed. But IHG also requires every IHG-branded hotel to adopt its human rights policy — so it claims the authority to set the standard, and the choice not to answer is its own.
