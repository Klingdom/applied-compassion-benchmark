## Key Findings

- Cameroon's own women's ministry admitted its femicide figures are only "the tip of the iceberg." A government saying its own statistics understate a problem is unusual and the benchmark credits it.
- Human Rights Watch says the promises have not been kept. It documents inadequate shelters, too few staff for victim support, barriers to getting protection orders, and few arrests or prosecutions.
- HRW's closing line is the finding: "The government has made a lot of promises to women. It's time to keep them."
- Cameroon's published score of 11.3 was set on 20 August 2026, ten days before this evidence. It is a recent, evidence-based baseline, so no "stale score" argument was available and none was used.
- The score does not move. Cameroon's transparency improved by one step and its resource mobilisation fell by one step, and the two cancel exactly at 11.3 of 100.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Action (1.6)** and **Systemic Thinking (1.8)**. Cameroon has a national gender-based violence strategy, a national gender policy running to 2030, and a bill to create a standalone criminal offence. These are genuine upstream structures. They are scored for existing, not for working — HRW's finding is that implementation has not followed.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Integrity (1.0)**, at the floor and unchanged. The gap between an adopted national strategy, a national gender policy and a bill on the one hand, and inadequate shelters, insufficient staffing and limited prosecution on the other, is the definition of anchor 1 for values alignment: decisions that regularly contradict stated values without acknowledgment. HRW's own framing — promises made, promises unkept — is the finding.

## Evidence Gaps

- The specific femicide figures were not obtainable. The ministry describes "50 brutal murders in first four" months of an unspecified recent period, and states the count is incomplete. No reliable total exists.
- The companion UN Women Africa report referenced in the scan summary was not located and nothing is scored on it.
- No evidence was located on whether the standalone gender-based-violence offence bill has progressed, which would be the clearest test of whether the acknowledged surge produces action.
- HRW is a single tier-3 source for this cycle. No corroborating tier-4 source was located. This does not affect the outcome because no movement is proposed.

## Disclosure — what this score measures

This score measures the Cameroonian state's response to femicide, not the killings themselves. Cameroon is not scored for the acts of private individuals.

One movement in this assessment is **positive and is recorded as such**: the ministry's own admission that its data undercounts. Absence of disclosure is scored 2 throughout; the subdimensions scored 1 (A3, A4, A5, E1, E2, E3, AC5, EQ2, EQ3, EQ4, B2, B4, B5, AB1, AB4, AB5, S3, and all five Integrity subdimensions) rest on HRW's positive documentation of specific failures — inadequate shelters, insufficient staffing, barriers to protection orders, limited prosecution, chronic underinvestment.
