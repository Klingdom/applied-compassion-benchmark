## Attribution — resolved against the Lagos precedent

The 2026-08-18 cycle rejected a Lagos flag because tear-gassing was carried out by the **federal** Nigeria Police Force, not the city. **Abuja is different, and the difference is structural.** Abuja has no elected mayor and no city council. It is governed by the **Federal Capital Territory Administration**, headed by a federal minister, currently Nyesom Wike. The FCTA **is** the municipal government of Abuja; there is no separate city authority to distinguish it from. FCTA conduct is therefore scored as city conduct.

This cuts both ways and both are scored:

- **The FCTA is the authority that allocated the land.** Rights advocates attribute the severity of the flooding to "reckless land allocation and conversion of green space/drainage corridors and waterways for development" — decisions only the FCTA can make.
- **The FCTA is also the authority that acted.** Minister Wike visited flood-affected Maitama, ordered immediate demolition of all structures on waterways, said exceptions would not be made — "We will demolish all those houses found on the water channel. Some of them are senators. Some of them are big men." — and the FCTA banned conversion of designated green spaces, parks and gardens into commercial car-dealership lots.

**The rainfall is not scored against Abuja.** More than 90mm fell in under four hours. What is scored is what the FCTA did before and after.

## Key Findings

- **Abuja has never actually been measured before.** Its published score of 18.8 out of 100 uses the same eight numbers as Freetown's, a city 3,000 miles away. That is a placeholder, not a measurement.
- **Record flooding hit Abuja on 15 August 2026.** More than 90mm of rain fell in under four hours, submerging Maitama, Asokoro, Wuse and other districts.
- **The city government caused part of the problem and is now fixing part of it.** The Federal Capital Territory Administration allocated the land that blocked drainage, then ordered demolition of structures on waterways within four days.
- **The minister said he would not spare the powerful: "Some of them are senators. Some of them are big men."** That is a real political cost, and it is scored.
- **A first measurement puts Abuja at 27.5 out of 100, not 18.8 — but no score change is proposed.** The upgrade would rest entirely on Nigerian newspapers with no stronger source, and 32 of 40 sub-scores reflect the fact that Abuja publishes almost nothing, not anything it did.
