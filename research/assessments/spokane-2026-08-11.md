## Why this entity was assessed tonight

The scanner flagged NPR reporting of 3 August 2026 on a Spokane activist targeted at her home by federal agents after protesting ICE raids, as part of a pattern of conspiracy prosecutions against protesters. Spokane has never been individually assessed, so its published 35.9 is a bulk-import baseline.

## Scanner date / attribution corrections

- **ATTRIBUTION FALSE POSITIVE — the actor is the federal government, not the City of Spokane.** The conduct in the NPR story is a US Department of Justice prosecution and federal agents appearing at an activist's home. Neither is City of Spokane conduct. This matches the Louisiana screen of 2026-08-01 and the Tacoma screen of 2026-07-30: a federal enforcement decision describes the federal government, not the municipality where it occurs. The flag does not attach to Spokane.
- **One element IS city conduct, but it is out of window.** The article records that "local police used pepper balls to disperse the crowd and arrested about 30 people" at the underlying protest. That is Spokane Police Department conduct. It occurred in **summer 2025**, roughly a year before this cycle's window opened on 29 July 2026, and the May 2026 trial and August 2026 conviction are federal proceedings. The policing conduct is noted as background and is not scored as in-window evidence.
- **Countervailing city conduct located.** In February 2026 the Spokane City Council proposed immigration enforcement free zones covering homeless shelters, transit facilities and public parks, and banning federal agents from staging on city-owned property. This is protective municipal conduct in the opposite direction to the flag.

## Key Findings

- The activist was prosecuted by the federal government, not by Spokane. Jac Archer of Spokane Community Against Racism was convicted on federal conspiracy charges brought by the US Department of Justice. A federal prosecution describes the federal government's conduct, not the city's, so it does not count against Spokane's score.
- Spokane police did use force at the underlying protest — a year before this review window. Local officers used pepper balls and arrested about 30 people. That happened in summer 2025, roughly a year before this cycle's 29 July 2026 window opened, so it is recorded as background rather than scored.
- Spokane moved to keep federal immigration agents off city property. In February 2026, one day after the federal government moved to cut sanctuary-city funding, the City Council proposed immigration enforcement free zones covering shelters, transit and parks, backed by Mayor Lisa Brown.
- The city put money behind it. Spokane allocated $100,000 to support immigrants, and its 2025 homelessness strategy explicitly emphasises outreach over criminal enforcement.
- This is Spokane's first real assessment, and the published score held almost exactly. Evidence-based scoring returns 35.6 against a published 35.9 — a difference of 0.3 points.
