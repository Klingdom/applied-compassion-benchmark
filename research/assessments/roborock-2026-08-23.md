## Key Findings

- **Beijing Roborock Technology scores 26.3 of 100. It holds the only independent certification of consent and data handling located anywhere in this batch — and independent reporting has questioned the policy behind it.**
- **Roborock is certified against EN 303 645, Europe's consumer Internet-of-Things cybersecurity standard, and has appointed a Data Protection Officer under the General Data Protection Regulation.** No other company in this batch has an outside body checking how it treats data from inside people's homes. Consent Orientation scores 3 of 5.
- **A named news outlet reads the privacy policy less favourably.** KED Global reported on 3 July 2025 that the policy lets Roborock keep retaining data processed for purposes unrelated to the original collection, and share identification numbers, IP addresses and device details with the platform Tuya Smart. That is why the score is 3 and not 4.
- **Roborock tells buyers what its robots do not record, in plain words.** Its Trust Center states the vacuums do not record audio or video, that voice commands are processed on the device, and that video-call streams are encrypted for live viewing only and stored nowhere. Scope Clarity scores 3 of 5.
- **It publishes less about its own failures than its main competitor does.** Ecovacs publishes dated security advisories naming flaws in its products; no equivalent disclosure by Roborock was located. Transparency scores 2 of 5 — an absence finding, not a harm finding.
