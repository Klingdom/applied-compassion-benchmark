## Corrections to the scan and to prior cycles (append-only)

**CORRECTION TO THE 2026-09-14 SCAN (append-only; `research/scans/2026-09-14.json` and its digest are NOT edited).** The 2026-09-14 scan DROPPED a Lesotho AGOA candidate as 'both misdated and substantively wrong'. It reasoned that the reauthorisation was signed on 3 February 2026 and runs only to end-2026, and that 'the House-passed three-year extension to 2028 did not become law'. That drop was itself wrong. It conflated two separate US laws:

1. **February 2026 law.** The Sunday Times (4 Sept 2026) confirms: 'Trump signed legislation in February this year extending it to end-2026.'
2. **September 2026 law.** The same article reports that 'on Wednesday' the US President signed the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, which 'extends duty-free access to qualifying nations under the African Growth and Opportunity Act (Agoa) to 2028'. The Wednesday before 4 September 2026 is 2 September 2026. Lesotho Times (10 Sept 2026) independently reports that HR 6500 was signed into law on 2 September 2026, extending AGOA to 31 December 2028.

**Bill number.** The coordinator found that 'HR 6500' is not stated in the Sunday Times text, and this fetch confirms it. The only link is a hyperlink in that article to congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/6500. The Lesotho Times states 'HR 6500' in its text. The bill number is therefore SUPPORTED by one tier-2 text source plus a consistent hyperlink, but it was NOT confirmed against congress.gov directly. Treat it as reported, not verified.

**Other scan claims not verified.** '30,000-40,000 mostly-female garment workers' and 'buyers who cancelled 2025 orders have not yet returned' are not in the Sunday Times article, which never mentions Lesotho. The Lesotho Times supports workers on short time and exporter caution. The worker-count range is unverified here.
