### Score Difference Analysis

The published vector `4 / 4.5 / 4 / 3.5 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4` is byte-identical across six robotics labs occupying consecutive alphabetical ranks. It is a never-assessed uniform seed. Cyberdyne Inc. has never been individually assessed against the 40 behavioural anchors before this report.

The published value asserts practice at the fourth anchor across all eight dimensions: published disaggregated data, formal pathways, documented reparative action, independent verification and community confirmation. Cyberdyne Inc. publishes none of those things. 15 of the 40 subdimensions here score at the low anchor of 2, which under this study's fixed rule means **no evidence was found in either direction** — not that harm was found.

### Recommendation

The published score is **overstated** and should be revised down to 45, band Functional. This is a seed correction, not a conduct downgrade.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Cyberdyne holds the highest published claim the benchmark makes — "Exemplary" — on a placeholder it shares byte-for-byte with five other labs. Its real evidence supports "Functional", not "Exemplary".
- Cyberdyne won the world’s first public health insurance coverage for a robotic treatment, in Japan on 25 April 2016. Coverage was later extended to further rare diseases including HAM and hereditary spastic paraplegia. That is an unusually strong access result.
- The efficacy evidence is real and independent. A multicentre randomised controlled crossover trial (NCY-3001) across nine hospitals underpinned the insurance decision, and it is published in the peer-reviewed literature.
- Cyberdyne rents HAL rather than selling it, which lowers the cost barrier for patients — the opposite of the six-figure purchase model used by most exoskeleton makers.
- Nothing was found on empathy practice, bias monitoring, harm acknowledgment or repair. Fifteen of 40 subdimensions sit at the low anchor because no disclosure exists, not because harm was found.
