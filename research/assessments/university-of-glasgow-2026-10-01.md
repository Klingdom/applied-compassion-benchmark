## The top-ranked university leads the world on repairing its slavery past, and overrode its own community on arms

The University of Glasgow is ranked first of 100 universities at 62.5, a score that has never been assessed against evidence. This is its first baseline.

Glasgow's record on historical harm is the strongest this benchmark has seen in a university. It published a report on its own profits from slavery in September 2018, committed to a reparative justice programme, and runs a joint master's degree in reparatory justice with the University of the West Indies (third intake in 2025). Its Top-Up programme targets students from deprived areas, care-experienced students, refugees and young carers.

On current conduct the picture is weaker. In November 2024 the University Court voted not to divest from arms companies, after its own consultation found 81% of staff and 86% of students in favour. A student was banned from campus after a 2025 protest. Emails reported on 24 September 2026 show the university reassured BAE Systems, a funder, that it had told Police Scotland about a planned 2023 protest (Al Jazeera, 2026-09-24). In June 2026 it opened a voluntary severance scheme to cut staff over a forecast shortfall of about GBP 10 million.

Measured: 48.7 (Functional), down 13.8 from 62.5. Both the magnitude and band-crossing triggers are met. The proposal is routed as flag-for-review because the adverse evidence is tier-2 journalism and student press; no government, court or international-body source was located, which the band-crossing evidence rule requires.
