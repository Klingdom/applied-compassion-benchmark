## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Honduras is scored higher than its placeholder, not lower. It assesses at 26.9 against a published 20.3, a gain of 6.6 points, and stays in the Developing band. The upward result comes from measurable institutional machinery, not from good outcomes.
- Why it matters: the killing rate has almost halved. Honduras' homicide rate fell from 26.07 per 100,000 in 2024 to an estimated 15.30 in 2025, and the femicide rate fell from 7.73 to 4.75 per 100,000. The state publishes these numbers.
- Why it matters: powerful killers were convicted. Convictions in the murder of Indigenous leader Berta Caceres were upheld for eight people across 2024 and 2025, and three people were charged over the 2024 murder of environmental defender Juan Lopez.
- Why it matters: Honduras is paying a fortune to keep a promise. After repealing its special economic zones law in 2022, it faces an investor claim of up to $10.8 billion, roughly 60 per cent of its 2026 budget, and has not reversed the repeal.
- Why it matters: the harms are still severe. Sixty point one per cent of households live below the poverty line, one child in four under five is chronically malnourished, abortion is banned even when the mother's life is at risk, and the state's own human rights commissioner logged over 800 complaints against security forces.

## Strongest Dimensions

Integrity (2.4), Awareness (2.2), Action (2.2), Accountability (2.2) and Systemic Thinking (2.2). Honduras has a national human rights commissioner that publishes complaints against its own security forces, courts that convicted powerful killers, published outcome statistics that are improving, and a completed democratic handover of power.

## Weakest Dimensions

Empathy (1.6) and Boundaries (1.8). A total abortion ban including life-threatening pregnancies, no legal gender recognition for transgender people, and unimplemented Inter-American Court rulings on Garifuna land rights.

## Evidence Gaps

- The state of emergency's own operational data is not published, so its harms are scored from the human rights commissioner's complaint counts rather than from state disclosure.
- No independent evaluation of Honduran social programmes was located, so Efficacy rests on national crime and poverty statistics rather than on programme evaluation.
- Whether Inter-American Court reparations have reached Garifuna communities could not be established, so Reparative Action is capped at the partial anchor.

## Attribution Discipline

Only the conduct of the Honduras state is scored. Harm by non-state armed groups, by foreign states, and by natural disaster is excluded from the state's score. Where such harm occurred, what is measured is the state's own response to it. Every source date was checked against its event date, and publication dates were not treated as event dates.
