# Compassion Benchmark Assessment: Niger

**Entity type:** Country

> **Do not confuse Niger with Nigeria.** These are two different countries with separate records in this index. This assessment covers the Republic of the Niger, capital Niamey, population roughly 27 million.

## Why this assessment exists, and a traceability finding

Niger's published score of 12.5 out of 100 is a flat seed: all eight dimensions set to the identical value 1.5. That is the signature of a placeholder.

**Traceability finding.** This study was commissioned partly to check a suspicion that Niger had no assessment record at all. That suspicion is **wrong, and the real defect is different and worse.** Niger has five assessment files on disk — 12 June 2026, 13 June 2026, 26 July 2026, 31 July 2026 and 1 August 2026 — and three of them carry complete 40-subdimension sidecar files. The July and August runs each returned a researched composite of 8.8 out of 100.

None of them changed the published score. Each returned a difference of -3.7 points against the published 12.5, which is below the 5.0-point threshold required to file a change proposal. So each one confirmed the seed. The 1 August file states the position plainly: "This is the third flag on one report… No third downgrade is applied for the same report."

That is the structural defect this study exposes. **A flat seed set too high can never be corrected by routine cycles, because every honest correction is individually too small to file.** Niger was assessed three times, correctly, and the wrong number stayed published all three times. The present assessment is a full re-score against the Burkina Faso calibration anchor, which produces a difference large enough to file.

## Calibration anchor and attribution rule

**Anchor.** Burkina Faso's 2026-05-31 reweight (composite 6.3) is fixed and is not re-scored here. Its standard: a Tier-5 human-rights finding of state-perpetrated atrocity drives subdimensions to the 1 anchor; a 2 is reserved for residual functioning structure, usually externally funded, or for a formal position stated in words but disconnected from action.

**Attribution.** This is the decisive discipline for Niger, because most documented mass-casualty atrocity in Niger during the assessment window was committed by non-state armed groups, not the state. Islamic State Sahel killed "at least 46 worshippers, including 3 children" at Fambita mosque on 21 March and "over 70 worshippers, including 5 children" at Manda mosque on 21 June. **None of that is scored as Nigerien state conduct.**

Niger's own conduct, which is what is scored, comprises:

- A Nigerien military drone strike that "killed at least 17 civilians at a crowded market in January 2026."
- Detention of former President Mohamed Bazoum and his wife since July 2023 "with no access to family members or lawyers," ruled arbitrary by the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention.
- Dissolution of all political parties and suspension of dozens of civil society organisations.
- Retroactive amendment of detention law to extend maximum pretrial detention to four years after a detainee's lawyers applied for release.
- Withdrawal from ECOWAS and from the International Criminal Court.
- A failure to protect after warning: Human Rights Watch records that "the Nigerien army did not adequately respond to warnings of attacks." A failure to act on a warning is state conduct even though the attack was not.

### Score Difference Analysis

There are two separate differences here and they should not be confused.

**Difference one: seed versus assessment.** The published 12.5 is a placeholder with all eight dimensions at 1.5. It is not a finding. The gap of -8.8 points is the cost of never having assessed Niger's published record.

**Difference two: the prior 8.8 versus this 3.7.** Niger *was* assessed, three times, at 8.8. This assessment lowers that to 3.7, and the reason is calibration, not new evidence. The July and August runs scored Niger in isolation. This run scores Niger against the Burkina Faso anchor, which is stricter in five specific places: it does not award a 2 for anticipatory awareness where drone strikes killed civilians (A5); it treats a state that cannot or will not respond as a 1 on responsiveness and proportionality (AC1, AC2); it does not award a 2 on scope clarity where a junta's sovereignty claims overstate its protective capacity (B3); and it treats ICC withdrawal as active foreclosure of historical-harm acknowledgement rather than vague acknowledgement (EQ5). Applying the anchor consistently moves Niger from 8.8 to 3.7.

The prior 8.8 was not wrong on the evidence. It was uncalibrated against its Sahel peers, which is exactly the defect this study was commissioned to fix.

## Key Findings

- **Niger has been assessed three times and the published score never changed.** Runs on 26 July, 31 July and 1 August 2026 each returned 8.8 out of 100 against a published 12.5. Each difference of -3.7 points fell below the 5.0-point filing threshold, so no proposal was ever submitted. The placeholder survived three correct assessments.
- **The claim that Niger has no assessment record is false.** Five Niger assessment files exist, three with full 40-subdimension sidecars. The traceability defect is the opposite of the one suspected: the records exist and the published index ignores them.
- **A Nigerien military drone strike killed at least 17 civilians at a crowded market in January 2026.** Human Rights Watch found it violated the laws-of-war ban on indiscriminate attacks. This is state conduct and it drives the Awareness and Accountability scores.
- **The state changed the law rather than the conduct.** After lawyers for detained activist Moussa Tiangari applied for his release in June, the junta amended detention law to allow four years and applied it retroactively. That single act sets Correction Willingness to the lowest anchor.
- **The mosque massacres are not scored against Niger.** Islamic State Sahel killed at least 46 worshippers at Fambita in March and over 70 at Manda in June. Under the benchmark's attribution rule, armed-group killings are not the state's conduct. Niger is scored for failing to act on warnings it received, which is a different and smaller charge.

## Strongest Dimensions

- **Systemic Thinking (10.0/100, raw 1.4)** is the only dimension above the near-floor group, and only for stated structural critique. The junta holds a costly institutional position — exit from ECOWAS, withdrawal from the ICC — which the anchors credit at the 2 level even when it produces no protective action.
- **Boundaries (5.0/100, raw 1.2)** sits marginally above Mali's because Niger's state institutions still function in the capital. Mali's capital is under a fuel blockade. This is the single evidenced difference between the two countries' vectors.

## Weakest Dimensions

- **Equity, Accountability and Integrity all sit at raw 1.0**, the absolute floor, with all five subdimensions at the lowest anchor in each. **These cannot go lower.** Only a harm-flag designation of 0.0 would move them, and this assessment does not apply one: the dominant mass-casualty violence in Niger is non-state, and the state retains functioning institutions. That mirrors the Burkina Faso anchor's reasoning for stopping above the absolute floor.

## Evidence Gaps

- **No Niger-specific humanitarian needs figure was obtainable** in this window. The HRW World Report 2026 Niger chapter contains no displacement or needs numbers, and OCHA's Sahel products cover western Niger jointly with Burkina Faso and Mali. AC1 and AC4 therefore rest on conduct evidence rather than on coverage data.
- **Niger's own budget allocation is not published**, so proportionality (AC2) is inferred from the December general-mobilisation decree rather than from fiscal data.
- **Human Rights Watch World Report 2026 is an annual edition** covering calendar year 2025. Events are dated by day and month in the source; those dates are reproduced as published.

## Recommended Next Steps

- **Critical band:** Consider a [Certified Assessment](/certified-assessments). The nearest available movement off the floor would be a published investigation into the January 2026 market strike.
- **Pipeline fix, separate from Niger:** the 5.0-point filing threshold should not apply to entities whose published dimension vector is a flat seed. Three correct assessments were silently discarded by that rule.
