## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Thailand on a Human Rights Watch report of 2026-08-18 that Thai district and forestry officials and the military raided a Karen community in Phetchaburi province on 2026-08-10 and now threaten 26 villagers with deportation to Myanmar. Thailand was last assessed on 2026-07-24 at 31.3.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Dates verified.** The raid is dated 2026-08-10; the HRW report, JURIST and DVB all carry 2026-08-18. Both dates sit inside the window.

**Attribution — the check the cycle brief specifically called for.** The danger the villagers face in Myanmar is created by Myanmar, not Thailand, and Myanmar's conduct is **not** scored against Thailand. What is scored is Thailand's own decision chain: Thai officials and the Thai military conducted the raid, Thai authorities arrested 28 people including eight children and two pregnant women, Thai authorities transferred 26 to the Suan Phlu Immigration Detention Centre, and Thai authorities propose to deport them. Every link in that chain is Thailand's own conduct.

**Proportionality recorded.** Two of the 28 were released after officials verified their residence registration, which shows a functioning verification step exists. That is why EQ1 falls to 2 rather than 1.

**Scope note.** Only Empathy, Equity and Boundaries were re-scored; the other five dimensions are carried forward unchanged from 2026-07-24. The sidecar therefore contains only those three dimensions.

### Analysis

Three subdimensions move, all downward, all on the same finding.

E5 (Cultural Empathy) falls from 3 to 2. The Karen have lived in the Phetchaburi jungle for generations, long before Kaeng Krachan National Park existed, and hundreds of thousands of Karen in Thailand hold coloured identification cards as a recognised hilltribe group. Raiding that community for lack of citizenship documents is the opposite of extending empathy across cultural difference.

EQ1 (Universality) falls from 3 to 2. Twenty-eight people were arrested including eight children and two pregnant women. Two were released once their residence registration was verified — so a verification route exists — but 26 remain in detention facing prosecution and removal. That is an entire resident population effectively excluded from status.

B4 (Refusal Ethics) falls from 2 to 1. Thailand proposes to return people with no alternative offered, to a place Human Rights Watch says would put them "at risk of grave danger". HRW states this could violate Thailand's obligations under the UN Convention against Torture and the customary prohibition on refoulement.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Thailand is preparing to send 26 people into a war zone. On 10 August 2026 Thai officials and soldiers raided a Karen village in Kaeng Krachan National Park and arrested 28 residents, including eight children and two pregnant women.
- Human Rights Watch says deporting them "would put them at risk of grave danger" and could breach Thailand's obligations under the UN Convention against Torture and the ban on returning people to persecution.
- Why it matters: these are not new arrivals. Karen communities have lived in that jungle for generations, before Kaeng Krachan National Park existed, and hundreds of thousands of Karen in Thailand hold official hilltribe identification.
- Two of the 28 were released once officials checked their residence records. That shows a working verification route exists — and that it was not used before the raid.
- The score is confirmed at 32.8. The assessed value of 29.4 is 3.4 points lower, below the threshold for a change proposal. A downward watch flag is set: if the deportations proceed, this will be re-tested at once.
