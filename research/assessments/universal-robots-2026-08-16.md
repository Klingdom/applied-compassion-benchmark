### Score difference analysis

The published vector `AWR 3.5 · EMP 3.5 · ACT 3.5 · EQU 3 · BND 3.5 · ACC 3.5 · SYS 3.5 · INT 3.5` is byte-identical across ten robotics labs occupying consecutive ranks 19 to 28 in alphabetical order. It is a never-assessed uniform seed, not a measurement. Universal Robots (Teradyne) has never been individually assessed. This is its first full 40-subdimension review.

Universal Robots is the strongest performer of the ten, and it is the clearest case in the cluster where parent-level policy demonstrably governs the listed subsidiary: Teradyne’s group CSR programme states that it covers Universal Robots A/S and its global subsidiaries, and Universal Robots files its own statutory responsibility statement in Denmark. That parent coverage is credited. Teradyne’s wider conduct is not credited wholesale. The two 2025 layoff rounds are scored because they were executed by Teradyne Robotics on the Danish workforce that Universal Robots employs. Even with the strongest disclosure in the cluster, the assessed composite is 40.0, which is 20.9 points below the seed. The seed asked for a dimension mean of about 3.44, which on these anchors requires published disaggregated data, independent audit and community testimony across all eight dimensions. No industrial robotics vendor in this study has that.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Universal Robots scores highest of the ten de-seeded labs, and it still lands 20.9 points below its published number. The gap is disclosure, not misconduct.
- Universal Robots helped draft ISO/TS 15066:2016, the collaborative-robot safety specification that sets pain thresholds by body part. That is the single strongest piece of compassion evidence found anywhere in this cluster, and it benefits every competitor.
- Universal Robots A/S publishes an annual statutory corporate responsibility statement, and parent Teradyne publishes a group CSR report that explicitly covers Universal Robots A/S and its subsidiaries — rare tier-4 disclosure in this sector.
- Teradyne Robotics cut roughly a quarter of its division headcount across two rounds in 2025, about 140 people in January and a further 14% in November, including 6% in Denmark where Universal Robots is based.
- Nothing was found on empathy, equity or reparative action. Those four dimensions sit at the low anchor because no disclosure exists, which is an evidence finding rather than an adverse finding.

## Strongest Dimensions

Awareness (3.0) and Systemic Thinking (3.0). Both rest on the same fact: Universal Robots put its own staff on the ISO committee that wrote ISO/TS 15066 and then adopted a standard that limits the speed and force of its own products. Boundaries carries the single highest subdimension score in the assessment, B3 Scope Clarity at 4/5, because Universal Robots tells buyers in writing and before purchase that cell safety remains their responsibility.

## Weakest Dimensions

Empathy (2.4), Equity (2.4) and Boundaries (2.4). Universal Robots publishes no disaggregated outcome data, no bias investigation, no reparative-action record and no acknowledgment of automation’s displacement history. Integrity (2.4) is held down by the 2025 layoffs, which removed roughly a quarter of the division while the group’s stated commitments cover living wages and freedom of association.

## Evidence Gaps

The Universal Robots and Teradyne CSR PDFs could not be parsed directly; their content was obtained through search-engine extracts of those documents, so figures for health and safety, gender diversity and whistleblower use could not be verified line by line. No employee-review data, no independent audit and no community testimony were obtainable. Twenty-two of the forty subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure rather than on located conduct.
