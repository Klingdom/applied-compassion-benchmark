## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the headline that flagged Unitree carried the wrong date. The scan recorded the Pentagon flagging Unitree as Chinese military-linked technology on 20 July 2026. That was the article's publication date. The Department of Defense added Unitree to its Section 1260H list of Chinese Military Companies on 8 June 2026, which is outside the window.
- The designation is real and is corroborated by a law-firm client alert and by technology press, both dated June 2026. It is used here as labelled background, not as in-window evidence.
- A second claim was dropped entirely. The scan referred to research finding that Unitree's G1 humanoid continuously transmitted sensor data to Chinese servers. No dated source for that finding could be located inside or near the window, so it was not scored.
- What remains in-window is the commercial launch of the H1 Pro humanoid in Europe on 22 July 2026, reported as the first Chinese-made humanoid to enter Western commercial markets. A product launch is not a compassion harm and does not move the score.
- Unitree's standing weakness is unchanged: there is no published misuse policy, no reparative mechanism for people affected by deployed systems, and no public position that carries institutional risk.
