# Compassion Benchmark Attribution Screening: Los Angeles

**Entity type:** City

## Why this entity was assessed

FBI tactical units conducted coordinated arrests in South Los Angeles on 16 September 2026, charging figures connected to contracts administered by the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority with kickbacks, "ghost" programme participants and diversion of public homelessness funds.

## ATTRIBUTION: whose conduct is being measured?

**This is the central question and it must be settled before anything is scored.**

**What LAHSA is.** The Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority is a joint powers authority created by the City and County of Los Angeles. It is not a city department. The City of Los Angeles is one of two governing principals. This attribution rule was established for this entity on 2026-07-29 and applied again on 2026-09-14.

**Who was charged.** Michael Young, 46, founder of a Culver City-based nonprofit, is alleged to have "misappropriated more than $7.5 million in taxpayer funds" including through "programs administered by the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority". Lakiya Malone, 48, an employee of a nonprofit tied to LAHSA funds, is charged with taking "more than $180,000 in bribes and kickbacks" and with having "provided priority referrals of homeless housing participants, including 'ghost' participants who never lived at the sites". Donye Mitchell, 55, chief executive of a Los Angeles homelessness nonprofit, is charged over more than $1.2 million in grant money from a county-funded nonprofit. Alexander Soofer, former executive director of the nonprofit Abundant Blessings, has agreed to plead guilty.

**None of the charged individuals is a City of Los Angeles employee or official.** They are nonprofit contractors and their employees.

**Who is enforcing.** The FBI and the United States Attorney's Office. Federal enforcement conduct is federal conduct. It is not scored against the city, under the rule applied on 2026-07-29.

**Conclusion.** This is a federal criminal action against nonprofit contractors who received funds through a joint city-county authority. It is not a finding about the City of Los Angeles's own conduct. **No city dimension moves.**

## What it does do: it advances an existing watch

The 2026-09-14 assessment set a watch reading "a HUD Inspector General finding, or any court or audit finding, against LAHSA contracting". Criminal charges filed by a United States Attorney are a materially stronger evidentiary step than the congressional letter that prompted the 2026-09-14 assessment, and one defendant has agreed to plead guilty. The watch is **advanced and sharpened**, not satisfied: charges remain allegations until conviction or plea entry.

The city-relevant question this raises, which cannot yet be answered on evidence, is whether LAHSA's controls, which the city co-governs, failed to detect diversion running from 2018 to 2025. If a court, audit or inspector-general report establishes that, it would bear on AC3 Efficacy, AB4 Systemic Learning and EQ2 Priority for Vulnerable. No such finding exists.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: federal agents charged people with stealing money meant for homeless Angelenos. Arrests took place in South Los Angeles on 16 September 2026 over contracts administered by the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority.
- Why it matters: the people charged do not work for the city. They run or work for nonprofits that received the money. One is alleged to have taken more than $7.5 million and spent part of it on a nightclub and restaurant.
- Why it matters: LAHSA is not a city department. It is a joint authority run by both the City and the County of Los Angeles, so the city is one of two principals, not the sole owner.
- Why it matters: the city's score does not move, and that is deliberate. The benchmark measures the conduct of the entity it scores. Contractor crime and federal prosecution are other people's conduct.
- Why it matters: the open question is oversight. If an audit or court later finds that LAHSA's controls, which the city helps govern, failed to catch diversion running from 2018 to 2025, that would be a city finding. No such finding exists yet.
