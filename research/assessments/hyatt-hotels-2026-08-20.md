### Analysis

The assessed composite of 44.4 sits 33.5 points below the published 77.9 and crosses from Established into Functional. **The size of that gap is a measure of how far a placeholder can drift from evidence, not a measure of Hyatt's conduct.** No dimension is scored below 2.6, and Hyatt scores at anchor 3 on 25 of 40 subdimensions — a company with real programmes, real supplier screening and a genuinely resolved labour dispute, but with almost no independent verification of any of it. ## Key Findings

- Hyatt Hotels has never actually been assessed by this benchmark. Its published score of 77.9 is a placeholder shared letter-for-letter with ten other Fortune 500 companies. This is the first real measurement.
- The assessment lands at 44.4 of 100 — Functional rather than Established. That 33-point gap measures how far an unmeasured placeholder drifted from the evidence, not a decline in Hyatt's conduct.
- The scanner searched Hyatt individually this cycle and found no compassion-relevant news in either direction over 14 days. Confidence is low and this proposal should not be applied without a targeted evidence pass.
- Hyatt does have real programmes: an ESG committee covering forced labour and trafficking, supplier screening through EcoVadis, a published human rights statement and annual diversity reporting. Almost all of it is self-reported, with little independent verification.
- Hyatt's San Francisco workers ended a near three-month strike in December 2024 by ratifying a four-year contract unanimously. Striking is worker conduct; what the benchmark scores is Hyatt settling, and it is credited.
