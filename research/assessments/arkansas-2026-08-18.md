### Analysis

Two subdimensions move, both downward, and the composite falls 1.2 points — well below the filing trigger.

AB2 (Correction Willingness) falls from 3 to 2. Arkansas State Police confirmed that Trooper Arellano remains on active duty while the incident is investigated, and its spokesperson said only that "all uses of force are reviewed internally". Leaving an officer on the road after an internal report confirms he punched a stopped motorist is correction that is minimal and externally driven, not self-initiated.

EQ3 (Bias Awareness) falls from 3 to 2. The Arkansas Legislative Black Caucus and the NAACP Arkansas State Conference have publicly demanded answers. Arkansas has published no disaggregated use-of-force data in response and has announced no examination of racial disparity, so the state is at anchor 2 — some monitoring exists, disparities are not investigated.

What holds the score up is that a review process does exist and did produce a factual finding. A use-of-force report confirms what happened. AB3 (Transparency) therefore stays at 3. The complainant's counsel is now asking Arkansas State Police to publish the findings and to release prior use-of-force reports involving the same trooper; whether it does so is the decisive open question and is the basis for the watch flag.

Arkansas's underlying profile is unchanged: Systemic Thinking at 2.80 and Integrity at 2.60 remain its strongest dimensions, and Boundaries at 1.80 remains its weakest, driven partly by a documented strain in county jail health care for people in mental health crisis.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the trooper is still on the road. Arkansas State Police confirmed that Trooper Moisses Arellano remains on active duty while the agency investigates his punching of an 18-year-old woman during a traffic stop on 1 August 2026.
- A use-of-force report confirms the punch. The contested part is the legal characterisation, not the fact. Alisa Hackett filed a federal civil rights suit on 12 August 2026 alleging excessive force and retaliation for questioning the stop.
- The Arkansas Legislative Black Caucus and the NAACP Arkansas State Conference have publicly demanded answers. Arkansas has published no disaggregated use-of-force data in response.
- Why it matters: this is state conduct, not federal. The Arkansas State Police answers to Arkansas, so the incident counts against the state's score rather than being excluded on attribution grounds.
- The score is confirmed at 33.1. The assessed value of 31.9 is 1.2 points lower — well below the threshold for a change proposal.

## Weakest Dimensions
