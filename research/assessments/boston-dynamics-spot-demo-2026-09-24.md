# Screening Note: Boston Dynamics (SPOT demo) — re-screen

**Assessment date:** 2026-09-24
**Outcome:** screened, not assessed. No proposal on either Boston Dynamics row.
**Published:** 20.3/100, Developing, rank #90 of 92 robotics labs. Canonical reconstruction returns 20.3 exactly — no math-hygiene issue.

## This row is awaiting a founder decision, and nothing here pre-empts it

This row is covered by waiver `D23-boston-dynamics-spot-demo`, which describes it as a deployed configuration misfiled in an organisations index and holds its removal to the Deployed AI Audit product pending a founder decision that expires 2026-12-14. **No proposal is filed against a row awaiting a delisting decision.**

## This is the same flag, on the same evidence, seven days later

`research/assessments/boston-dynamics-spot-demo-2026-09-17.md` screened this row on 2026-09-17 and reached the same conclusion. That note established:

- ICE's plan to buy up to four Spot robots for up to $2 million is a **procurement forecast**, not a purchase. Boston.com (9 September) reported that "the government has not yet issued an RFP for this."
- The only in-window act by Boston Dynamics was restating standing policy: that "any attempted weaponization of Boston Dynamics' robots is strictly prohibited," and that law-enforcement customers must adopt a public use policy before deployment.
- The Electronic Frontier Foundation's warning that robots could make ICE raids more reckless is advocacy concern about possible future use.

## The protests are about a customer's intended use, not the company's conduct

The new element this cycle is the protests themselves: sustained weekly demonstrations outside ICE's Burlington, Massachusetts processing centre and a standout at the Museum of Science.

**Protests against a customer's intended use of a product are not conduct by the manufacturer.** The people protesting are objecting to what US Immigration and Customs Enforcement plans to do, and ICE is not an entity in this index. Screening rule 3 applies directly: a third party's procurement forecast, and third parties' objections to it, are not enacted conduct by Boston Dynamics.

Nor is public criticism of a company a finding against it. If it were, the benchmark would score reputation rather than behaviour.

## Which row any real evidence would attach to

The **company** row, "Boston Dynamics" (65.6), not this configuration row. The decision to sell, the terms of use, the weaponisation ban and the public-use-policy requirement are all company policy, and the company row already scores them (B4, S4, S5, I1 and I4, per the 2026-07-26 confirmation). An agency's forecast to buy a product is not conduct by a demonstration configuration.

No proposal is filed on either row, so the two rows cannot come into conflict.

## Why no full assessment was run

A 40-subdimension assessment of a row slated for possible removal, on evidence that attaches to a different row, would create a record the founder decision may void. `last_assessed` is not stamped, consistent with the 2026-09-17 note.

## Triggers that would change this

- **A contract award,** and any actual ICE use of Spot in an enforcement operation. Company conduct would then be assessable on the company row: whether Boston Dynamics enforces its terms, and whether ICE published the required use policy.
- **A founder decision on D23.** If the row moves to the Deployed AI Audit product, the deployment evidence belongs there.
