## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: nothing happened in this window, and that is the finding.** A dedicated search across lawsuits, labour, safety, regulatory and humanitarian signals found no compassion-relevant Singapore evidence between 16 and 30 July 2026. This assessment therefore confirms the existing score rather than moving it.
- **Why it matters: migrant workers are still outside the main labour law.** Singapore’s migrant workers remain excluded from the Employment Act, including its limits on daily working hours. Their visas are tied to their employers, who can cancel the contract and send them home.
- **Standing context (May 2026, outside the window):** 142 countries delivered recommendations to Singapore at its fourth Universal Periodic Review. They included abolishing the death penalty, repealing POFMA, extending anti-discrimination protection to LGBT+ people, strengthening migrant domestic workers’ rights and creating a national human rights institution.
- **Standing context (2025, outside the window):** Singapore carried out the highest number of executions for drug-related offences in more than two decades.
- **Singapore ranks 37th of 193 countries at 62.2 out of 100 — Established.** Its strength is delivery: Action and Systemic Thinking both score 4.0. Its weakness is Integrity at 2.8, driven by the gap between its stated commitment to fairness and the use of POFMA against civil society groups opposing the death penalty.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Action (4.0) and Systemic Thinking (4.0).** Singapore delivers public services with published performance data, plans on long horizons, and maps cross-system effects between housing, health, transport and employment more systematically than almost any comparable state.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Integrity (2.8).** POFMA has been used against the Transformative Justice Collective and a regional anti-death-penalty network, which is a direct gap between stated values and conduct under pressure. **Accountability (3.0) and Equity (3.0)** follow, the latter driven by the exclusion of migrant workers from core labour protections.

## Evidence Gaps

- No within-window evidence was found, so every subdimension rests on standing structural sources dated outside 16-30 July 2026.
- Singapore publishes no independent national human rights institution reporting, because none exists, which caps AB3 and A3.
- No disaggregated outcome data for migrant workers is published by the state, so EQ1 and EQ3 rest on Human Rights Watch and Amnesty reporting.
