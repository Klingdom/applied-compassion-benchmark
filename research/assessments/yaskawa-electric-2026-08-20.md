## Baseline-establishment note

This is a **first-ever assessment**. Yaskawa Electric Corporation does not appear in any published Compassion Benchmark index.

**Corporate status check (required before assessment).** Yaskawa Electric Corporation is operating under that name, headquartered in Kitakyushu, Japan, and listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange. It published the YASKAWA Report 2025 — its integrated annual report — in September 2025, and revised its group human rights policy in September 2025 ([YASKAWA Report 2025](https://www.yaskawa-global.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/YR2025E_A4.pdf); [Yaskawa human rights policy](https://www.yaskawa-global.com/company/csr/human-rights-policy/policy)). No acquisition, rename or insolvency event was found.

**Evidence base and its limits.** Yaskawa's evidence is predominantly **self-published**: an integrated report, an ESG data page, and a set of human rights policy and due diligence pages. Under this index's tightened test, self-published corporate material does not count toward independent verification. The one differentiator is that Yaskawa's disclosure describes a **named, cyclical human rights due diligence process aligned to the United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights**, including follow-up surveys, monitoring and a commitment to disclose. That is more specific than a values statement and is why Awareness scores above its industrial peers.

**Absence versus conduct.** No adverse conduct finding, safety scandal, labour ruling or regulatory action against Yaskawa was located. **Where a score below 3 rests on absence of disclosure, the row says so.**

**Disclosure density.** Of 40 subdimensions, **27 rest primarily on absence of disclosure** and 13 rest on positive located evidence.

## Key Findings

- **Yaskawa scores highest among the four big industrial robot makers on Awareness, and one mechanism explains it.** The company runs a human rights due diligence cycle aligned to the United Nations Guiding Principles — identify impacts, act, follow up, monitor, disclose — plus a **monthly** satisfaction survey of all non-consolidated employees. Most industrial peers describe channels; Yaskawa describes a cycle.
- **The cycle is described but its findings are not published.** Yaskawa commits to disclosing negative human rights impacts. No such disclosure was located. Until one appears, Transparency is capped at 3 of 5 and Accountability stays at 30 of 100.
- **Nothing adverse was found.** No safety scandal, labour ruling, regulatory action or litigation against Yaskawa was located. Twenty-seven of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure, which is a statement about what the company publishes, not about how it behaves.
- **Yaskawa does no upstream work the benchmark can see.** Two of its direct competitors run large school-certification programmes that transfer portable skills to workers. Nothing comparable was located here, so Root Cause Orientation scores 2 of 5.
- **Like every industrial robot maker in this batch, Yaskawa is publicly silent on what automation does to employment.** Interconnection Awareness and Structural Critique both score 2 of 5.

## Strongest Dimensions
- **Awareness (45.0)** — the highest of the four industrial firms, on the strength of a named, cyclical due diligence process and a monthly all-employee survey.

## Weakest Dimensions
- **Empathy and Equity, both at 25.0** — the absence floor. No complaint-validation practice, no coverage measurement, no disparity investigation and no cultural adaptation were located in either direction.

## Evidence Gaps
- No published output from the human rights due diligence cycle — the process is described, the findings are not.
- No independent assurance of social metrics.
- No third-party assessment of workplace conditions at Yaskawa or in its supply chain.
- No employment-effects analysis of any kind.
