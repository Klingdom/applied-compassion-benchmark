### Score Difference Analysis

The -0.3 difference is almost entirely a grid artifact: seven of Malta's eight published dimensions sit at 3.5, which five whole-number subdimension scores cannot produce. The only substantive movement is a small reduction in Accountability, and it is driven by one thing. On 30 July 2026 the three young men known as the El Hiblu 3 — who were minors at the time of the March 2019 incident and who face terrorism charges for refusing to be returned to Libya — appeared at a preliminary court hearing in Valletta. Their trial has still not begun. This is the seventh year of the case. UN human rights experts urged Malta in January 2026 to drop the charges, and Amnesty International titled its March 2026 statement "Seven years of injustice". The reason this does not move the score further is that it is not new: the prosecution existed when Malta was last assessed on 2026-05-05 and is already reflected in the published Equity score of 3.0, the lowest dimension in Malta's profile. The other item the scanner surfaced — a non-EU woman pregnant with her third child reportedly denied family reunification — traces to Malta Independent commentary dated 12 July 2026, five days before the window opens, and to a June 2026 case. It is recorded as standing context and was not scored as in-window evidence.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: seven years on, the trial has still not started.** Three young men known as the El Hiblu 3 appeared at a preliminary hearing in Valletta on 30 July 2026. They were minors when the events happened in March 2019. Their trial has not begun.
- **Why it matters: the charge is terrorism, and the act was refusing to be sent back to Libya.** The three were among more than 100 people rescued by the tanker El Hiblu 1 in March 2019. They protested a forced return to Libya. UN experts say they did not engage in violence or threats.
- **Why it matters: the UN has already told Malta to stop.** UN human rights experts urged Malta in January 2026 to end the prosecution. Amnesty International titled its March 2026 statement "Seven years of injustice". Malta has not dropped the charges.
- **Why it matters: Malta's family rules exclude the people who need them most.** ECRE documents that only recognised refugees may apply for family reunification in Malta, that subsidiary protection holders are excluded, and that migrant workers generally cannot meet the residence-prospects test. This is why Equity is Malta's lowest dimension at 3.0.
- **Why it matters: this is a confirmation, and the reason is timing, not indifference.** A preliminary hearing in a seven-year-old case is a continuation of a harm the published score already reflects, not a new one. The score moves by 0.3 points.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Action (3.6), Boundaries (3.6) and Systemic Thinking (3.6).** Malta delivers universal healthcare, education and social protection at high coverage for a state of its size, communicates its rules explicitly before people commit, and engages at EU level on the structural causes of Mediterranean migration.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (3.0).** Family reunification is closed to subsidiary protection holders and effectively closed to migrant workers, ECRE describes Malta's implementation of its own treaty obligations as minimalist, and no reparative action toward the El Hiblu 3 is evidenced.
