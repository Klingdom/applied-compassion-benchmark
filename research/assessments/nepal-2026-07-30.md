### Score Difference Analysis

**Attribution matters here and cuts both ways.** The nationwide shutdown of outpatient services from 27 to 29 July 2026 was called by the Nepal Medical Association, a professional body, not by the state. The denial of care to hundreds of thousands of patients over two days is therefore not directly Nepal’s institutional conduct. What is Nepal’s conduct is the condition that produced it: a repeated pattern of assaults on health workers with no effective protection, which the country’s own leading newspaper editorialised about on 29 July 2026. Awareness falls 0.2 raw on the failure to detect and act on that pattern before it escalated, Action falls 0.2, and Accountability and Systemic Thinking fall 0.2 each. Against that, the Health Ministry did intervene and the strike was withdrawn on the evening of 29 July, which holds Empathy, Boundaries and Integrity at 3.0. Equity falls 0.1 on the grid.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: a doctor was beaten and the country stopped treating people.** Dr Pramod Ram was assaulted at Narayani Hospital in Birgunj on 25 July 2026 by relatives of a 45-year-old patient who died in the emergency ward. The Nepal Medical Association responded by halting all non-emergency care nationwide.
- **Why it matters: patients paid for a failure that was not theirs.** Outpatient departments closed across government, NGO and private hospitals. The Kathmandu Post reported that patients who had queued since early morning were sent home untreated across two days.
- **Why it matters: the shutdown was the doctors’ decision, not the government’s.** Under this benchmark’s attribution rule, the Nepal Medical Association is a separate actor. What Nepal is scored on is failing to protect health workers from a recurring pattern of assault, which is what triggered the shutdown.
- **The government did act, late.** The strike was withdrawn on the evening of 29 July 2026 after the Health Ministry urged hospitals to keep services running.
- **Standing context (February 2026, outside the window):** Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International and the International Commission of Jurists jointly called on Nepal to publish its Commission of Inquiry reports into the violent crackdowns on the September 2025 protests. That remains outstanding and is background to the Accountability score.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Empathy (3.0), Boundaries (3.0) and Integrity (3.0).** The Health Ministry engaged directly with the medical association rather than confronting it, and the dispute ended in two days rather than escalating.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (2.4).** Access to care in Nepal is already unequal by geography and income, and a nationwide outpatient shutdown falls hardest on people who cannot pay for private or emergency alternatives. **Awareness, Action, Accountability and Systemic Thinking all sit at 2.8**, held down by the recurrence of attacks on health workers and by the unpublished inquiry reports from 2025.

## Evidence Gaps

- No official count of how many patients were denied care during the 27-29 July shutdown was published, so AC1 and EQ1 rest on newspaper estimates.
- No published data on the frequency of assaults on Nepali health workers was located, so A1 and AB4 rest on the editorial record.
- The Commission of Inquiry reports into the September 2025 crackdowns remain unpublished, which is itself the evidence for AB3.
