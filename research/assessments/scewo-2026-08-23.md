## Baseline-establishment note

This is a **first-ever assessment**. Scewo AG does not appear in any published Compassion Benchmark index, and no prior score, seed or placeholder existed for it. There is no published-comparison section because there is nothing to compare against. This report establishes the first number.

**Corporate status check — operating.** Scewo AG is a Swiss company based in Winterthur, founded in 2015 by Pascal Buholzer, Bernhard Winter and Thomas Gemperle out of an ETH Zurich and Zurich University of the Arts student project. It has raised about US$23.2 million, with a Series A closed on 27 December 2023 with RSBG participating, and it is recorded as a Venture Kick portfolio company. The strongest independent confirmation that the company is trading is a German public-payer record: the BRO carries medical-aid directory number 18.50.08.0001, which a manufacturer must hold and maintain to be reimbursable. No insolvency, acquisition or wind-down was located.

**Disclosure-capacity note.** Scewo AG is a small Swiss manufacturer. It has no obligation to publish an annual report, a bias audit, workforce data or outcome studies, and it publishes none. **A low score here measures those missing documents. It does not measure whether the company cares about wheelchair users — the BRO exists to let them climb stairs that the built environment refuses to remove.**

**⚠️ Absence of disclosure is not evidence of harm.** No lawsuit, safety recall, regulatory enforcement action or labour finding against Scewo was located. Where a score rests on nothing being found, the row says so.

**Disclosure density.** Of 40 subdimensions, **30 rest primarily on absence of disclosure** (75%) and 10 rest on positive located evidence.

## Key Findings

- **Scewo AG scores 26.9 of 100 because it publishes almost no outcome data — not because a stair-climbing wheelchair is uncompassionate.** Thirty-one of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure. **No adverse finding of any kind was located either.** Scewo is a small Swiss manufacturer with no legal duty to publish what this benchmark measures.
- **The strongest evidence in the file comes from a German public payer.** The BRO holds medical-aid directory number 18.50.08.0001, which a manufacturer must obtain and maintain for the device to be reimbursable. That listing is also the best independent confirmation that the company is trading.
- **Scewo publishes its price and its reimbursement odds, which most of this batch does not.** The BRO starts at 32,850 francs or euros excluding tax. The company sets out the route — home test drive, test report, prescription, then an in-home assessment by the Medizinischer Dienst — and states plainly that cover may be full, partial or refused. That honesty about what may go wrong earns the batch's joint-highest Boundaries score.
- **The BRO fixes the stairs for one person rather than fixing the stairs.** That is the design's strength and its systemic limit. No public position by Scewo on building-accessibility rules was located, and Systemic Thinking sits at the absence floor.
- **Nothing was located about the company's staff or about user data.** The chair carries sensors and balance telemetry; no consent, retention or withdrawal disclosure was found.

## Strongest Dimensions

- **Boundaries, 35.0 — the joint-highest Boundaries score in this batch of 15.** Open pricing, a published reimbursement route with its risks stated, and a device built to remove dependence on being lifted.
- **Equity, 30.0** — the German medical-aid directory listing and a structured pre-purchase test process.

## Weakest Dimensions

- **Awareness, Empathy, Action, Accountability, Systemic Thinking and Integrity — all 25.0, exactly at the absence floor.**

- No public position on the built-environment rules that create the need for the product.
