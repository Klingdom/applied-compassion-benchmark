## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Washington University in St. Louis on the US Equal Employment Opportunity Commission's lawsuit filed 2026-08-12, alleging the university retaliated against a Siteman Cancer Center programme manager who complained about race-based separation during a mandatory diversity training and then filed an EEOC charge. The university has never been individually assessed (`last_assessed: null`), so this is a first full assessment.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Dates verified.** The EEOC's own newsroom release and the Insurance Journal report both carry 2026-08-12. Higher Ed Dive and HR Dive published 2026-08-13. The underlying conduct is dated in the complaint: internal complaint January 2025, EEOC charge July 2025, position eliminated November 2025. No date conflict.

**Allegation status — recorded explicitly.** The EEOC complaint is an **agency-filed lawsuit, not an adjudicated finding**. A federal agency reached reasonable cause and chose to sue, which is stronger than a private allegation and weaker than a ruling. It is scored as a serious, credible, unresolved allegation. No subdimension is scored as though retaliation were proven.

**Attribution.** The layoffs, the merit-raise pause and the training design are the university's own decisions. The federal research funding reductions that the chancellor cites as a driver are federal action and are not scored against the university; only the university's *response* to them is scored.

### Analysis

The published 50.8 sits in the middle of the Functional band and, on this evidence, is too high. Three findings drive the fall.

First, Accountability (2.20 of 5). The EEOC alleges that an employee complained internally in January 2025, filed a charge in July 2025, had her duties transferred away, and had her position eliminated in November 2025. Whatever the merits, the university's public posture has been litigation rather than acknowledgment, and no corrective disclosure has been published.

Second, Boundaries (2.60 of 5). WashU cut 316 filled positions and 198 unfilled ones, saving about $52 million a year, paused construction and skipped merit raises for fiscal 2026. Local reporting records staff calling for greater transparency about how the cuts were decided.

Third, Integrity (2.40 of 5). The alleged conduct — a diversity training that separated staff by race, followed by retaliation against the person who objected — is a direct contradiction between stated values and institutional behaviour, and it is the university's own programme that is alleged to have caused it.

Against that: financial aid spending rose about $33 million since June 2022, the university is a QuestBridge partner, and the 2026-27 tuition rise was held to 4.5%, more modest than the prior year. Equity therefore holds at 3.00 rather than falling further.

The assessed composite is 41.9, an 8.9-point fall. It stays inside the Functional band, but only by 0.9 points.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the federal agency that enforces workplace discrimination law is now suing this university. On 12 August 2026 the EEOC sued Washington University in St. Louis, alleging it eliminated an employee's job after she complained about being separated from colleagues by race during a mandatory training.
- The complaint describes a nine-month sequence: internal complaint in January 2025, EEOC charge in July 2025, duties transferred away, position eliminated in November 2025. This is an allegation, not a court finding, and is scored as such.
- Why it matters: the cuts fell on staff, not on students. WashU eliminated 316 filled jobs and 198 vacant ones, saving about $52 million a year, and skipped merit raises for fiscal 2026. Local reporting records staff demanding to see how those decisions were made.
- Financial aid moved the other way. The university has spent about $33 million more on financial aid since June 2022 and held the 2026-27 tuition rise to 4.5%.
- The score falls 8.9 points, from 50.8 to 41.9. It stays in the Functional band by 0.9 points.
