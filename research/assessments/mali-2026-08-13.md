# Compassion Benchmark Assessment: Mali

**Entity type:** Country
**Sector/Domain:** Government (military junta under Gen. Assimi Goïta, since 2020/2021)
**Assessment date:** 2026-08-13
**Composite score:** 3.1/100
**Band:** Critical

## Why this assessment exists

Mali's published score of 12.5 out of 100 was never produced by an assessment. It is a flat seed: every one of the eight dimensions was set to 1.5, the same value, which is the signature of a placeholder rather than research. Four separate Mali cycles (24 June, 3 July, 4 July and 14 July 2026) all confirmed 12.5 and all refused to move it alone. They were right to refuse. The problem was never Mali's number in isolation. It was that Burkina Faso received a real 40-subdimension assessment in May 2026 and its three Sahel neighbours did not.

This assessment de-seeds Mali. It scores all 40 subdimensions against the same evidence standard used for Burkina Faso.

## Calibration anchor and attribution rule

**Anchor.** Burkina Faso's 2026-05-31 reweight (composite 6.3) is the fixed reference. It is not re-scored here. Its standard: a Tier-5 human-rights finding of state-perpetrated atrocity drives subdimensions to the 1 anchor. A score of 2 is reserved for a residual functioning structure — usually an externally funded one, such as UN humanitarian tracking — or for a formal position that exists in words but is disconnected from action.

**Attribution.** Harm by non-state armed groups is not scored as Malian state conduct. The JNIM fuel blockade of Bamako, running since September 2025, is JNIM's conduct, not Mali's. Prior Mali cycles held this line and this assessment holds it too. Two Mali-specific complications are handled explicitly:

- **Africa Corps is scored as state conduct.** The Russia-linked Wagner Group withdrew from Mali in June 2025 and was replaced by Africa Corps, a paramilitary force under direct Russian government control operating in Mali under contract with the Malian state. Force employed by the state is the state's conduct. Burkina Faso had no equivalent contracted foreign paramilitary in its 2026-05-31 evidence base.
- **Dozo militias are scored as state conduct** where they operate jointly with soldiers. Human Rights Watch places Dozo fighters and Malian soldiers at the same killings, opening fire together.

One score is affected by the Bamako siege without attributing it to Mali. B1 Self-Sustainability asks whether help comes from a stable foundation. Mali's capital is under a fuel blockade. That is a fact about the state's capacity, not a judgement about who caused it, and B1 measures capacity.

### Score Difference Analysis

The published 12.5 is not a research finding that this assessment disagrees with. It is a placeholder. All eight published dimensions carry the identical value 1.5, which no genuine 40-subdimension assessment would produce. The difference of -9.4 points measures the gap between a seed and an assessment, not a change in Mali's behaviour.

The largest movements are in EQU, BND, ACC and INT, which all fall to the 1.0 floor. Each is driven by a Tier-5 Human Rights Watch finding of state conduct, not by non-state harm. Fulani civilians including 23 children killed by soldiers and Dozo militia at Sarkala Werè on 14 May 2026. Twenty-two bodies in two mass graves. Twelve men disappeared from a cattle market. No government investigation. No reply to HRW's letter. Withdrawal from the ICC. These are the state's own acts and omissions.

SYS barely moves, from 1.5 to 1.4. The junta does hold and publicly defend a structural position, and the anchors give partial credit for structural critique that exists in communications even when it is disconnected from action.

### Recommendation

The published 12.5 is **overstated** and should be replaced with an evidence-based 3.1. A change proposal is filed at `research/change-proposals/mali.json` with `recommendation: flag-for-review` and `status: pending`. This is a de-seeding, not a downgrade for new misconduct.

## Key Findings

- **Mali's published score was never researched.** All eight dimensions sat at exactly 1.5, a flat placeholder. A full 40-subdimension assessment returns 3.1 out of 100. The 9.4-point drop measures a missing assessment, not new misconduct.
- **Malian soldiers and allied Dozo militia killed at least 31 Fulani civilians, including 23 children, at Sarkala Werè on 14 May 2026.** A survivor told Human Rights Watch: "They chased us like hunters pursuing guinea fowls." Four dimensions sit at the 1.0 floor because of state conduct like this.
- **No one has been held responsible.** Human Rights Watch found on 28 June 2026 that Mali's military authorities "failed to hold those responsible for grave violations accountable, fostering impunity and emboldening abusive commanders," and got no reply to its 11 June letter to the justice minister.
- **The JNIM siege of Bamako is not scored against Mali.** Armed-group harm is separated from state conduct throughout. The siege affects one score only, B1, which measures whether the state has a stable foundation from which to help — and Mali's capital does not.
- **Mali closed its accountability exits.** It left ECOWAS and announced withdrawal from the International Criminal Court, which had produced two convictions and reparation orders for Mali since 2013.

## Strongest Dimensions

- **Systemic Thinking (10.0/100, raw 1.4)** is the only dimension above the near-floor cluster, and only because the junta states a structural, anti-colonial diagnosis of the region's insecurity. The anchors give partial credit for structural critique voiced in communications. Nothing in Mali's resource allocation follows from it.

## Weakest Dimensions

- **Equity, Boundaries, Accountability and Integrity all sit at raw 1.0**, the absolute floor. All five subdimensions in each are at the lowest anchor. **Further downward movement is arithmetically unavailable** — 1.0 is the bottom of the scale. Only a harm-flag designation of 0.0 could move these lower, and this assessment does not apply one, because the conflict is genuinely multi-sided and residual UN-partnered humanitarian capacity persists. That is the same reasoning the Burkina Faso anchor used.

## Evidence Gaps

- **No documented Malian state follow-through programme was found**, which drove AC5 to 1. Burkina Faso scored 2 on the equivalent subdimension because a specific UN food-systems partnership was documented for it. If a comparable Malian programme exists and was simply not surfaced, AC5 would rise to 2 and the composite would rise by about 0.6 points.
- **Human Rights Watch World Report 2026 is an annual edition** covering calendar year 2025. Individual events inside it are dated by day and month but not always by year in the accessible text. Dates in this report are reproduced as published.
- **Mali's own budget allocation to civilian protection is not published**, so AC2 and AC4 rest on external documentation of where force was used rather than on fiscal data.

## Recommended Next Steps

- **Critical band:** Consider a [Certified Assessment](/certified-assessments) for a structured improvement roadmap. At this score profile the practical starting point is Accountability: a single investigation of a documented incident, published, would be the first movement available off the floor.
