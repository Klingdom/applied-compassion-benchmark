## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Date correction — the scanner's evidence_date was wrong.** The scanner recorded `evidence_date: 2026-08-06` against a Bloomberg URL dated 2026-08-11. The correct date is **2026-08-11**. Latvia's Ministry of Defence, Euronews and Bloomberg all date the launch of security operation "Vilkatis"/"Werewolf" to 11 August 2026. No 6 August event is corroborated by any source found. The scanner's "104 people stopped that day" figure also does not reconcile: the Latvian MoD and Euronews report "more than 100 attempts recorded on some days, including 16 August" and 9,521 prevented entries for the year, against the scanner's 9,310. The higher, later, officially sourced figures are used here.

**Attribution.** The border operation, the 2024 legal amendments permitting summary "prevention" of entry, and the January criminal proceedings against NGO workers are all acts of the Latvian state. The organised movement of people toward the border is attributed by Latvia to Belarus and Russia; that instrumentalisation is **not** scored against Latvia. What is scored is Latvia's own response to the people who arrive.

### Analysis

The published 62.5 is a seed value, not a measurement, and the evidence does not support it. The assessed composite is 48.8 — a fall of 13.7 points and a crossing from Established into Functional.

Three things pull the score down from the seed.

First, asylum access. Amnesty International documents that 2024 legal amendments gave border guards powers to "prevent" irregular entries, which in practice produce summary returns without individual assessment. The European Court of Human Rights began hearing landmark pushback cases against Poland, Latvia and Lithuania in February 2025; those cases remain undecided. B4 (Refusal Ethics) scores 2 of 5 as a direct result: people are turned away without an individual decision or an alternative.

Second, suppression of the people who witness it. In January the authorities opened criminal proceedings for smuggling against two members of the NGO "I Want to Help Refugees" for assisting migrants at the border. A4 (Signal Amplification) scores 2 of 5.

Third, minority treatment. Roughly 18,000 Russian citizens holding permanent residence had to pass a Latvian language test to renew their permits; 1,213 became liable for deportation. A parliament-backed proposal would restrict public media output to Latvian and languages of the "European cultural space". E3 and E5 score 2 of 5.

What holds the score up is real. Latvia is a functioning EU rule-of-law state with strong statistical capacity, an active ombudsman, published official data — the Ministry of Defence published the operation's design openly — and a substantial record of hosting Ukrainian refugees. Accountability's transparency subdimension scores 4 of 5 and Systemic Thinking reaches 3.40.

**Calibration check.** Poland, which faces the same Belarus border situation and has been individually assessed, sits at 42.2 Functional. Greece is 39.1, Croatia 40.6, Hungary 50.2. Latvia at 48.8 sits above Poland and below Hungary, which is where its stronger rule-of-law institutions and weaker minority-and-asylum record place it. The assessed value is consistent with the individually assessed European cohort; it is the seed value of 62.5 that is the outlier.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Latvia's published score was never measured. Its eight dimension values are identical, digit for digit, to those of the Czech Republic, Lithuania and Slovenia. Four neighbouring countries share one placeholder vector.
- Latvia launched a militarised border operation on 11 August 2026, named "Vilkatis" (Werewolf), adding drones, helicopters, aircraft and troops. Border guards have prevented 9,521 entries this year. Latvia says Belarus and Russia organise the movement, and that attribution is accepted here.
- Why it matters: the issue is not the border operation, it is what happens to the people at it. Amnesty International records that 2024 amendments let guards "prevent" entry in ways that produce summary returns. The European Court of Human Rights began hearing pushback cases against Latvia in February 2025 and has not ruled.
- Latvia opened criminal smuggling proceedings against two members of the NGO "I Want to Help Refugees" for helping migrants at the border. Roughly 18,000 Russian citizens had to pass a language test to keep permanent residence; 1,213 became liable for deportation.
- The score falls 13.7 points, from 62.5 to 48.8, and moves from the Established band to the Functional band. This is a correction of a placeholder, not a finding that Latvia has got worse.
