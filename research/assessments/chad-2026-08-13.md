# Compassion Benchmark Assessment: Chad

**Entity type:** Country
**Sector/Domain:** Government (dynastic authoritarian rule under President Mahamat Idriss Déby)
**Assessment date:** 2026-08-13
**Composite score:** 10.6/100
**Band:** Critical

## Why this assessment exists

Chad's published score of 10.9 out of 100 came from a near-flat seed: seven dimensions set to 1.5 and Equity set to 1.0. That is a placeholder shape, not a research finding. Chad's June 2026 file confirmed 10.9 without re-scoring the 40 subdimensions.

This assessment de-seeds Chad using the same evidence standard applied to Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger in this study. The headline result is unusual and worth stating up front: **the seeded number was close to right, but for none of the right reasons.** The composite moves only 0.3 points, from 10.9 to 10.6, while the internal shape changes substantially. Equity rises from 1.0 to 1.4. Accountability falls to the 1.0 floor. Action and Systemic Thinking rise to 1.8, the highest dimension scores anywhere in this four-country study.

## Calibration anchor and attribution rule

**Anchor.** Burkina Faso's 2026-05-31 reweight (composite 6.3) is fixed and not re-scored. Its standard: a Tier-5 human-rights finding of state-perpetrated harm drives subdimensions to the 1 anchor; a 2 is reserved for residual functioning structure or for a formal position stated in words but disconnected from action.

**Attribution.** Chad is a different case class from its three neighbours, and the attribution line matters differently. Boko Haram and its affiliates drive insecurity in the Lake Chad Basin and have displaced over 250,000 people. **None of that armed-group harm is scored as Chadian state conduct.** Nor is the Sudanese civil war, which produced the refugee inflow.

Chad's own conduct comprises political repression at home and refugee protection abroad, and both are scored:

- **Repression.** Opposition leader Succès Masra arrested 16 May 2025 and sentenced on 9 August 2025 to "20-years imprisonment and a fine of 1 billion CFA francs (US$1.8 million)." The main opposition coalition dissolved. Eight opposition leaders sentenced to eight years. Journalists charged with espionage. Presidential term limits eliminated in September 2025. An amnesty law shielding the perpetrators of the October 2022 protest killings.
- **Refugee protection.** Chad hosts over 1.5 million refugees and is described as "Africa's largest refugee host per capita." Since April 2023 it has admitted over 900,000 Sudanese refugees. It has an Asylum Law (December 2020) with an implementing decree (April 2023) and Global Refugee Forum commitments to integrate refugees into national systems. The government and UNHCR together relocated 67 per cent of those fleeing Sudan into settlements.

Both are the state's own conduct. Both are scored. That is why Chad's vector is the most internally uneven of the four countries.

### Score Difference Analysis

Chad is the instructive case in this study. **The seeded composite was almost exactly right and almost entirely wrong.** The number moves 0.3 points. The shape moves a great deal.

**Equity was the worst seed error.** The published index put Chad's Equity at 1.0, the absolute floor, apparently reasoning that a large refugee influx "strains coverage." Assessment finds the opposite. Chad has a 2020 Asylum Law with a 2023 implementing decree, has admitted over 900,000 Sudanese refugees since April 2023, is the world's fifth-largest refugee host per capita, and jointly relocated 67 per cent of Sudan arrivals into settlements. A state that extends legal protection at that scale is not an equity-floor state. Equity rises from 1.0 to 1.4. This is the only upward dimension movement anywhere in the four-country study.

**Accountability was the worst seed error in the other direction.** The published 1.5 implies partial accountability machinery. There is none. Chad passed an amnesty law that shields the perpetrators of the October 2022 protest killings — roughly 200 deaths — and HRW records it "continued to close all debate on justice three years on." No inquiry followed the 2024 election killings. Journalists reporting on security were charged with espionage. All five subdimensions are at the lowest anchor. Accountability falls to 1.0, the floor.

**Action and Systemic Thinking both rise to 1.8**, the highest dimension scores in this study, including higher than Burkina Faso, Mali or Niger on any dimension. This is a genuine finding, not generosity. Chad is the only one of the four countries operating a sustained, legally framed, multi-year protection programme for a population outside its own citizenry.

**Chad is a different case class from its three neighbours.** Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger are military juntas whose armed forces are documented killing their own rural civilians. Chad is a dynastic authoritarian state that jails its opposition and shelters other countries' refugees. The benchmark scores both patterns, and Chad's mixed profile is the result.

### Recommendation

The published composite 10.9 is **approximately accurate and should be confirmed at the composite level**, but the underlying dimension vector is wrong and should be replaced. The difference is -0.3 points, well below the 5.0-point threshold, so **no change proposal is filed.**

This creates the same structural problem documented for Niger in this study: a near-flat seed with two large, opposite, offsetting dimension errors passes the composite threshold test and is therefore never corrected. The recommended fix is a pipeline rule, not a Chad-specific one — a de-seeding assessment should be applicable on dimension-vector change even when the composite barely moves.

## Key Findings

- **Chad's published score was nearly right by accident.** The seeded 10.9 out of 100 sits 0.3 points from the assessed 10.6, but two large errors cancelled out: Equity was 10 points too low and Accountability was 12.5 points too high.
- **Chad hosts over 1.5 million refugees and is Africa's largest refugee host per capita.** It admitted over 900,000 Sudanese refugees after April 2023 under a 2020 Asylum Law, and with UNHCR relocated 67 per cent of arrivals into settlements. This is the single strongest documented compassion practice found anywhere in this four-country study.
- **Chad made accountability illegal.** An amnesty law shields the perpetrators of the October 2022 protest killings, in which roughly 200 people died, and Human Rights Watch reports it "continued to close all debate on justice three years on." No inquiry followed the 2024 election killings either. All five Accountability subdimensions score the lowest anchor.
- **Chad treats foreigners better than its own citizens.** Opposition leader Succès Masra was sentenced on 9 August 2025 to 20 years in prison and a fine of about US$1.8 million. Term limits were removed in September 2025. That gap between external generosity and internal repression is what holds Integrity at 1.2.
- **The refugee response is collapsing for lack of money, not lack of policy.** Available funding fell to roughly $4 per refugee per month in 2025, down from $11 in 2022, and more than 71,000 refugee families received no shelter assistance.

## Strongest Dimensions

- **Action (20.0/100, raw 1.8)** and **Systemic Thinking (20.0/100, raw 1.8)** are the highest scores in this study. Both rest on the refugee programme: a documented response pathway, sustained multi-year engagement since April 2023, a legal framework predating the emergency, and active membership of the seven-country Sudan Regional Refugee Response Plan.

## Weakest Dimensions

- **Accountability (0.0/100, raw 1.0)** is at the absolute floor with all five subdimensions at the lowest anchor. **Further downward movement is arithmetically unavailable.** The driver is legal rather than merely practical: an amnesty statute removes the possibility of acknowledgement, correction and repair for the state's largest documented killing of its own citizens.
- **Integrity (5.0/100, raw 1.2)** is close behind, because the one genuinely compassionate practice is also the one that attracts international praise, while unwatched domestic conduct runs the opposite way.

## Evidence Gaps

- **Chad's own fiscal contribution to the refugee response is not published.** AC4 and S1 rest on the joint UN–government appeal rather than on Chadian budget data. If Chad's domestic allocation were disclosed and material, AC4 could rise.
- **No refugee-community testimony was obtainable in this window.** Two relevant sources returned HTTP 403 and could not be read: the Refugees International brief on aid and accountability for Sudanese refugees in Chad, and the UNHCR press release on funding shortfalls. Per-refugee funding figures are therefore carried from secondary reporting. Without direct community testimony, no subdimension was scored above 2 anywhere in this assessment.
- **Reports of a January 2026 sexual-violence incident against Sudanese refugee girls in eastern Chad** surfaced in search results but could not be verified from a primary source in this window, so they were not scored. If verified, EQ4 and B4 would be reinforced at 1 rather than changed.
- **Human Rights Watch World Report 2026 is an annual edition** covering calendar year 2025. Dates are reproduced as published.

## Recommended Next Steps

- **Critical band:** Consider a [Certified Assessment](/certified-assessments). Chad has the most improvable profile of the four Sahel states in this study, because a functioning protection capability already exists and could be extended to Chadian citizens. Repealing or narrowing the October 2022 amnesty law is the single change that would move the most subdimensions.
