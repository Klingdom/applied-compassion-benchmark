## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: the UN now says out loud what watchdogs reported first.** On 30 July 2026 the UN Human Rights Office denounced Uganda’s crackdown on dissent, the erosion of the rule of law, increased military involvement in civilian institutions and shrinking civic space. UN High Commissioner Volker Türk said "Those who dare to speak out are silenced."
- **Why it matters: the harm has a count.** Since the 15 January 2026 elections, at least 50 opposition leaders and supporters, five human rights defenders and five journalists have been subjected to human rights violations, including enforced disappearance, torture and arbitrary detention.
- **Why it matters: civil society is being switched off, not just squeezed.** Ten leading civil society organisations have been suspended since January 2026, and the May 2026 Protection of Sovereignty Act carries prison sentences of up to 10 years for restricted foreign engagement.
- **Why it matters: soldiers are taking people the courts have already freed.** Human Rights Watch reported on 23 July 2026 that "It has been 13 days since armed soldiers seized Muwanga Kivumbi, a deputy president for the National Unity Platform, Uganda’s main opposition party." He was taken the day after a court released him on bail.
- **Why it matters: this is the second flag in six days.** Uganda was assessed on 26 July 2026 at 13.1 and flagged for review. The UN statement raises the evidence tier from watchdog reporting to a UN finding, which is what converts the flag into a proposal.
