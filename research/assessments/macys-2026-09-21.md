## Anti-false-positive screening (§3e-bis) — the band crossing is WITHHELD

The measured 37.5 is Developing; the published 41.3 is Functional. Under §3f that is a band crossing, which is normally filable at any delta. **It is not filed, and the rule is stated here in full.**

1. **Baseline provenance (check 1).** The published 41.3 was set by a documented assessment dated 2026-04-22. It is five months old and derived from Macy's own conduct.
2. **Rejected rationale (check 2).** The only rationale available for filing tonight would be "the published band is wrong." But the published value was produced by a prior researched baseline, and **no new within-window conduct evidence exists** — the scanner recorded `evidence_found: false`. A band flip driven purely by a second assessor re-reading the same public record 0.15 raw points differently is re-interpretation, not evidence.
3. **Directionality (check 3).** Macy's was surfaced on staleness with zero evidence, negative or positive. A downgrade needs documented conduct behind it, and there is none in this window.
4. **Rationale consistency (check 4).** Filing "the band is wrong" against a five-month-old researched baseline, on no new evidence, contradicts the documented history. That makes it a false positive.
5. **Calibration (check 5).** Whether a 0.15-raw-point disagreement should be allowed to flip a public band label at the 40/41 line is a **calibration question about band-boundary tolerance**, not a Macy's finding. It is referred, not encoded as a one-off.

**Disposition: confirm, with a band-proximity watch flag.** The published 41.3 sits 1.3 points above the Developing/Functional line (the canonical band function returns Developing at 40.0 and Functional at 40.1). One corroborated, in-window, tier-3-or-above negative finding would make this crossing filable on evidence rather than on re-reading.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Macy's was already assessed once, and the record nearly got missed. A full assessment dated 22 April 2026 sits on disk under a mangled filename, and it produced the 41.3 score published today. The tracking file wrongly says Macy's has never been assessed.
- Why it matters: Macy's cut about 1,050 jobs and is closing 14 stores in 2026. It filed the required 60-day layoff notices on 12 and 13 January 2026 for warehouse and fulfilment staff in Connecticut.
- Why it matters: its worker commitments are unusually concrete for retail. Macy's pays for a full education benefit for every colleague through Guild Education, about $35 million over four years, and set a $15 minimum hourly rate.
- Why it matters: union members get far better exits than the law requires. A California contract added $1.50 an hour, a bonus up to $1,200, and severance of up to 52 weeks. Only about 8% of Macy's employees are union-represented.
- Why it matters: the $5 billion social-purpose pledge ran out in 2025 and no replacement was found. Macy's reported nearly $3.2 billion directed by 2023. A commitment that expires during a cost-cutting year is why Integrity scores 2.4 out of 5.
- Why it matters: this assessment measured 37.5 out of 100, which would change Macy's public label from Functional to Developing — and it is deliberately not filed. The whole gap is 0.15 points of one assessor's reading, with no new evidence about Macy's conduct in the last two weeks.
