## Why this entity was assessed tonight

The scanner flagged severe flooding and landslides in Kerala from late July, intensifying 1-4 August 2026. India has been flagged repeatedly in recent cycles, and the last two assessments (2026-07-27 and 2026-08-01) both concerned the 20 July Delhi protest, so repeat-flag discipline applies.

## Scanner date / attribution corrections

- **Directionality note — this flag is a disaster, and the conduct in it is response.** A flood is not entity conduct. What is scorable is how the state responded. The record shows a functioning response: 27,048 people housed in 462 relief camps with a cap of 150 residents per camp, Rs 8 lakh to families of the dead, assistance for destroyed houses raised to Rs 12 lakh, and crop compensation increased 25 per cent. This is neutral-to-positive conduct, not the negative conduct the flag implies.
- **Year-confusion trap screened out.** Searches on Kerala flooding return large volumes of coverage from the **2018** Kerala floods (445 dead, quarter of a million in relief camps) and from 2019. Every figure used in this assessment carries a 2026 date: The National (2026-08-04) and The Watchers (2026-08-02). The 2018 figures are explicitly excluded. This is the same failure class caught three times on Kyiv casualty data.
- **Attribution note.** Flood relief in Kerala is led by the Government of Kerala, a state government, with national NDRF support. The country entity is credited only for the national contribution and the overall federal framework; most of the delivery is state-level.
- **No compounding of the Delhi protest finding.** The 20 July 2026 Sansad Chalo protest was assessed on 2026-07-27 and re-confirmed on 2026-08-01 at 16.9. It is reproduced here, not re-counted.

## Key Findings

- Kerala flooded, 26 people died, and the state got most people out. Near-cloudburst rainfall from 1 to 4 August 2026 killed at least 26 people. Kerala moved 27,048 people into 462 relief camps and capped each camp at 150 residents so conditions would stay liveable.
- The compensation was raised as the damage became clear. Families of the dead received Rs 8 lakh, assistance for completely destroyed houses was raised to Rs 12 lakh, and crop compensation rose 25 per cent for the 23,507 affected farmers.
- This flag points the wrong way, and the benchmark says so. A flood is not something a government does. What counts is the response, and Kerala's response was competent. India's score does not fall because it rained.
- A dangerous search trap was screened out. Most online coverage of "Kerala floods" describes the 2018 disaster, which killed 445 people. Every figure in this assessment carries a 2026 date. This is the same year-confusion failure caught three times on Ukraine casualty data.
- The score that produced India's 15.6 is about rights, not weather, and it has not changed. The Delhi protest findings from 27 July and 1 August 2026 are reproduced here, not counted again.
