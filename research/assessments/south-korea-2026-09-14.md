## Evidence-date, provenance, attribution and screening checks

**DATES CORRECTED.** The disability group ended its subway protests on 24 August 2026 (Korea Times), pre-window. The in-window event is the Seoul Metropolitan Council's passage of the two ordinances on 11 September (Kyunghyang). The agreement was with Democratic Party council members, not the national government or the mayor.

**STRIKE FIGURES CORRECTED.** The scan's '~100,000 workers' is union membership. The 4 September rally drew 30,000 per organisers and about 14,000 per police. The Seoul Economic Daily summary also references a 4 November date that could not be resolved (possibly a later planned full strike); not used.

**ATTRIBUTION.** The Seoul Metropolitan Council is a subnational public body of the Korean state. Its ordinances are credited modestly: one subdimension. The strike is lawful labour action and is not scored; the government's relocation policy is a regional-development dispute with no documented harm.

**STALE SOURCE REJECTED.** A search synthesis presented an E-9 migrant-worker job-mobility restriction as a September 2026 policy. On fetch the source is dated 5 July 2023. Excluded.

**BASELINE PROVENANCE.** The published 62.5 matches the 14-country placeholder, but South Korea was individually assessed on 2026-07-24 at 61.2. The placeholder happens to sit close to the measured value, and that sidecar is the starting point here.

## Key Findings

- Seoul turned years of disability protest into law. On 11 September 2026 its city council voted 69-37 to restore public jobs for people with severe disabilities and to require low-floor buses.
- A 4 September 2026 union rally against moving state banks out of Seoul was a policy fight, not a rights abuse. It went ahead freely.
- The scanner mixed up two dates. The disability protests ended on 24 August 2026; only the council vote falls inside this review period.
- A 2023 migrant-worker rule surfaced as if it were new. It was caught and excluded.
