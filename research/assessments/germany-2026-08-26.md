## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Germany on a charter deportation flight of Afghan nationals to Taliban-controlled Afghanistan. Germany's dimension vector was last set before June 2026; the 2026-06-04 change was a **math-hygiene reconciliation only** (stored composite corrected from 72.8 to 77.1 with every dimension unchanged), so no substantive re-score has occurred in this period.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**MIXED-SOURCING GATE RESOLVED.** The scan paired a dated Pajhwok URL with an undated Khaama URL. An undated source cannot corroborate. Two independently dated sources were obtained and fetched: Pajhwok (2026-08-25) and DAWN (2026-08-26). Gate cleared.

**NUMBER CORRECTED: 23, NOT "ROUGHLY 30".** The scan recorded "roughly 30 Afghan nationals", following Pajhwok's unattributed figure. DAWN, published 2026-08-26 and sourcing the German interior ministry, reports **23 Afghan men**. Infomigrants and Anadolu Agency agree on 23. The ministry figure is authoritative. **Corrected count: 23.**

**DATE CONFIRMED.** The flight left Leipzig/Halle Airport before dawn on **Tuesday 25 August 2026**. 25 August 2026 was a Tuesday. The scanner's `evidence_date` is correct; the DAWN URL dated 26 August is next-day coverage.

**ATTRIBUTION — this is German government conduct, and only that is scored.** The deportation was carried out by German federal police on a German government charter under an arrangement Germany brokered via Qatar. Nothing about conditions inside Afghanistan is scored against Germany; the Taliban's conduct is not Germany's conduct. What is scored is **Germany's own decision to remove people there, and how it did it.** Two facts sit on each side:

- **Adverse:** Pajhwok reports "the flight was the second instance of Germany carrying out a group deportation of Afghans, **including individuals without criminal records**". Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt has said returns will no longer be limited to criminals and threats. In July one deportee had no criminal conviction. 228 Afghan men have been returned in this legislative period.
- **Mitigating:** the interior ministry states all 23 had "come to the attention of the criminal justice system", and that they are "largely offenders convicted of serious crimes, including sexual offences and causing bodily injury". Removals follow individual legal process with judicial review. Public protest was visible and unimpeded — a banner reading "Stop Deportations — Refugees Welcome" was displayed inside the terminal.

**PROPORTIONALITY.** This is one flight of 23 people against a protection system that has admitted millions since 2015. The assessment moves one dimension down. It does not treat a single flight as a verdict on Germany's whole asylum record.

## Key Findings

- **Germany deported 23 Afghan men to Kabul on 25 August 2026.** The flight left Leipzig/Halle before dawn. The interior ministry says most had been convicted of serious crimes, including sexual offences.
- **The scanner's figure of "roughly 30" was wrong.** The German interior ministry's number is 23. That correction is recorded.
- **Germany is now deporting some people who have no criminal record.** Pajhwok reports this was the second group deportation to include such people, and one man removed in July had no conviction.
- **Germany's score falls 2.6 points, to 74.5 out of 100, and stays Established.** One area moves down: how Germany refuses protection. Two areas move slightly up.
- **A single judgement call would have doubled that drop.** Scoring one sub-question a point lower would push Germany's score to 71.9 and make it a filable change. That fragility is disclosed rather than hidden.
