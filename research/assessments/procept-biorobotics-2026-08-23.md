## Key Findings

- **PROCEPT BioRobotics scores 30.6 of 100 — second in this batch — because it does the one thing most robotics companies do not: it runs randomised controlled trials against an active comparator and publishes the numbers.** WATER III treated 186 men and reported ejaculatory dysfunction of 14.8% against 77.1% for laser enucleation, and stress incontinence of 0% against 9.1%.
- **Two independent public payers, not the company, produced its highest score.** The UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence gave Aquablation its strongest recommendation, putting the procedure on the National Health Service, and the US Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services granted both an inpatient New Technology Add-on Payment and an outpatient pass-through payment. Access Design scores 4 of 5 on that basis.
- **The company did not surface its own worst number.** Outside analysis of the FDA adverse-event database reported 16 patient haemorrhage cases across December 2024 and January 2025, an increase on prior months, and quoted a former sales employee saying urologists were not given the training the technique required. No company statement on that trend was located.
- **The patient does not choose the instrument, and the training gap is the practical version of that problem.** Consent Orientation scores 2 of 5. Where a novel mechanism can cause bleeding that a poorly trained operator will miss, informed use depends on the operator being trained — and the only located evidence on that is an allegation that they were not.
- **Thirty of 40 subdimensions still rest on absence.** No grievance channel, no staff wellbeing data, no disaggregated patient outcomes, no repair process, and no coalition contribution were located.

## Strongest Dimensions
