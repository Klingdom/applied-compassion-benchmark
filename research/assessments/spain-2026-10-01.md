## Spain passed two emergency housing laws six days after an 87-year-old was evicted. That is real action, and it does not change the score.

On 23 September 2026 an 87-year-old woman was evicted in Madrid. Protests followed in Madrid's Puerta del Sol and in 23 cities. On 29 September 2026 the Council of Ministers approved two Royal Decree-laws (emergency laws that take effect at once but need a parliamentary vote within 30 days). They extend the 'social shield' against evictions of vulnerable households to 31 December 2030, allow automatic renewal of tenancy agreements, and give tax relief to renters earning up to EUR 33,000 (La Moncloa, 2026-09-29).

This is credited where it belongs: in Equity (priority for vulnerable households) and Systemic Thinking (root causes). Both were already scored at the anchors this evidence supports (3 and 4). The eviction itself happened while the social shield was in force, which shows the gap the decree is meant to close. Responsiveness (AC1) stays at 2 because the Ceuta failure documented on 2026-09-14 has not been reversed, and the housing response came after mass protest.

Measured composite: 51.2 (Functional), unchanged from 2026-09-14. A proposal for 50.6 is already pending; this cycle corroborates it and does not file a duplicate.
