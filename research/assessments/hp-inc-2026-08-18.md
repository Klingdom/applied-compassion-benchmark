### Analysis

The assessed composite is 42.5 against a published 60.9 — a fall of 18.4 points and a crossing from Established into Functional. The published value was never a measurement, so this is a correction of a placeholder.

HP is genuinely strong in one specific place, and the assessment reflects that. Its supply-chain human-rights programme goes past audit into capability-building: weekly reporting of labour metrics, deep-dive assessments, a supplier Sustainability Scorecard, and in-depth coaching tailored to supplier-specific risks. Its annual Sustainable Impact Report discloses supplier non-conformances alongside achievements, which is why AB3 (Transparency) reaches 4 of 5 — the single highest subdimension in the assessment. Systemic Thinking reaches 3.20 on published multi-year targets with tracked progress.

Where HP is weak is close to home. Empathy scores 2.00 of 5 across all five subdimensions, because there is no independent testimony that HP's own employees or its supply-chain workers experience the programme as care rather than compliance. That is an **absence of disclosure**, scored at the low anchor and stated as such — it is not a finding that HP mistreats people.

The one hard conduct finding is the restructuring. HP announced in November 2025 that it would cut 4,000 to 6,000 employees through fiscal 2028 — up to 10% of its global workforce — at a cost of $650 million to save about $1 billion, citing rising component costs and an AI pivot. That drives B1 to 2 of 5 and I1 to 2 of 5.

The gap between the published 60.9 and the assessed 42.5 is almost entirely the gap between a supply-chain programme that is documented and an internal social record that is not.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: HP's published score was never measured. Its eight dimension values are identical, digit for digit, to those of 21 other Fortune-500 companies.
- HP does one thing unusually well. Its supplier programme goes beyond auditing into weekly labour-metric reporting, deep-dive assessments and tailored coaching, and it publishes supplier non-conformances alongside its achievements.
- Why it matters: HP discloses far more about its suppliers' workers than about its own. Empathy scores 2.00 of 5 across all five parts, because no independent evidence shows how HP's own employees experience the company. That is missing information, not proof of harm.
- HP announced in November 2025 that it would cut 4,000 to 6,000 jobs by fiscal 2028 — up to 10% of its global workforce — spending $650 million to save about $1 billion, citing component costs and an AI pivot.
- The score falls 18.4 points, from 60.9 to 42.5, and moves from the Established band to the Functional band. This corrects a placeholder. It does not mean HP has got worse.
