### Score Difference Analysis

The +1.0 difference is one of only two positive movements in this cycle and reflects a genuine directional point. Cargill was surfaced because of a lockout, but the events inside the 17-31 July 2026 window are both endings, not beginnings. On 29 July 2026 Cargill Meat Solutions and Teamsters Local 455 reached a tentative agreement covering more than 1,700 workers locked out of the Fort Morgan, Colorado beef plant since 20 May 2026 — roughly 70 days. The union said the deal "delivers needed improvements to wages and benefits". Ratification was still pending at the close of the window. Separately, on 23 July 2026 a federal judge granted preliminary approval to a settlement worth more than $4.9 million resolving claims that hourly production workers at three Pennsylvania Cargill plants were not paid overtime for preshift COVID-19 screenings and walking time across a seven-year period. That is court-supervised repair reaching a defined class, which together with the return to the bargaining table lifts Accountability from 2.0 to 2.4. Against it, Boundaries falls from 2.0 to 1.8 and Equity is expressed at 1.6: the lockout itself, which began outside the window, is the standing harm, and a 70-day income loss falls hardest on a low-wage workforce with the least reserve. The two effects largely offset, leaving a net movement of one point that is not close to the proposal threshold.

Confirm the published score of 23.4, band Developing. Retain a watch flag on two triggers — whether the Fort Morgan agreement is ratified, and whether the Pennsylvania settlement receives final approval with the class actually paid.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: more than 1,700 people were locked out of work for about 70 days, and it ended in the last three days of July.** Cargill Meat Solutions and Teamsters Local 455 reached a tentative agreement on 29 July 2026 covering workers locked out of the Fort Morgan, Colorado beef plant since 20 May 2026. The union said it "delivers needed improvements to wages and benefits".
- **Why it matters: nothing is final yet.** The Fort Morgan agreement still required a ratification vote, expected the following week. At the close of this evidence window on 31 July 2026, the workers had not yet voted.
- **Why it matters: Cargill will pay nearly $5 million for time workers already worked.** A federal judge granted preliminary approval on 23 July 2026 to a settlement worth more than $4.9 million for hourly production workers at three Pennsylvania Cargill plants, over unpaid overtime for preshift COVID-19 screenings and walking time across a seven-year period.
- **Why it matters: the score does not go up for ending a harm you caused.** Cargill was flagged for a lockout, and the only things that happened inside the window were the lockout ending and a wage claim settling. This assessment treats those as a return to baseline, which is why the score moves by 1.0 point and not more.
- **Why it matters: the gap is between what Cargill publishes and how it treats its own plant workers.** Cargill publishes an FY2025 human rights disclosure, supply chain grievance dashboards and a cocoa Child Labor Monitoring and Remediation System. None of that reporting covers the Fort Morgan lockout or the Pennsylvania wage claim.
