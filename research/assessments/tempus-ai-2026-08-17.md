## Key findings

- Tempus AI holds the strongest regulatory verification in this study. In May 2026 the United States Food and Drug Administration approved its tumor-only xT CDx, a 648-gene assay, making Tempus the first laboratory approved as a companion diagnostic for both tumor-only and tumor-normal profiling.
- It also runs the clearest access programme of the health labs: every United States patient can apply for financial assistance regardless of insurance status, and gets a decision and a maximum out-of-pocket figure at the time of applying.
- Against that sits the most serious consent question in the study. Class actions filed in Chicago federal court in April 2026 allege Tempus trained models on genetic data from Ambry Genetics, which it bought for $600 million in 2025, and sold data on hundreds of thousands of people to more than 70 clients in deals allegedly worth $1.1 billion, without written consent.
- The complaint calls Tempus's de-identification assurances 'contradictory and unconvincing'. None of this has been decided in court and none of it is scored as proven harm. What is scored is the absence of any located affirmative consent practice for secondary use.
- The score falls 25.3 points. Being a listed company earns Tempus genuine credit on transparency, at 4 of 5, that no private lab in this study can match.
