# Compassion Benchmark Confirmation Note: Thailand

**Entity type:** Country  
**Assessment date:** 2026-09-22  
**Outcome:** confirmation; no dimension moves. An active investigation is not yet a finding about conduct, and the state’s own response is two-sided.  
**Published:** 32.8/100 (Developing), countries rank 112  
**Carried measurement:** 29.4/100 (Developing), measured in `research/assessments/thailand-2026-08-18.md`

## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Thailand because its local-government recruitment examination scandal escalated on the scan date itself. Police were tracing roughly 2,000 suspected altered results and had seized 320.86 million baht in assets, with pressure mounting on Deputy Prime Minister and Interior Minister Anutin Charnvirakul over a trail said to reach his adviser, and opposition MP Rangsiman Rome publicly calling on the Prime Minister for answers. Thailand was last assessed on 18 August 2026 at a measured 29.4.

## Recency cuts both ways, as the cycle brief noted

**What are established facts:**

- Police have seized 320.86 million baht in assets and are tracing roughly 2,000 suspected altered examination results.
- An organised network collected bribes of between 350,000 and 800,000 baht per candidate depending on the position sought; earlier reporting put the potential total as high as 4.5 billion baht.
- The Interior Ministry's initial review of more than 15,000 successful candidates already appointed to local government positions found around 5,000 whose scanned answer-sheet scores did not match the published official results.
- The scandal has been extended to a check of all 480,000 examination papers.
- The National Anti-Corruption Commission has declared the matter a special case.
- Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul has said that where the Interior Ministry finds clear evidence of cheating it can immediately dismiss offenders and recover their salaries.

**What is not established:** who is culpable, whether the trail reaching a ministerial adviser leads anywhere, whether any appointment will actually be voided, and whether any salary will actually be recovered. Nothing has been adjudicated.

## Why no dimension moves

**The negative reading is real but already priced in.** That a network could sell roughly 5,000 local-government posts is evidence of deep corruption in Thai public appointment, and it directly implicates EQ1/EQ2 (who gets access to public office and therefore to public service delivery), AB4 (whether failures recur systemically) and I4 (whether decisions match stated values). But the 18 August 2026 assessment already measured **EQU at 1.8 raw — the lowest dimension in the vector — ACC at 2.0 and INT at 2.0.** A state whose equity score already sits at 1.8 and whose accountability and integrity scores already sit at 2.0 is already scored as one where public goods are distributed by something other than need and merit.

**The positive reading is also real, and it is why nothing moves down.** The detection worked. The Interior Ministry found the 5,000 mismatches through its own review, escalated to all 480,000 papers, the police traced and seized assets, the National Anti-Corruption Commission took the case as a special matter, an opposition MP publicly questioned the Prime Minister without apparent consequence, and the Prime Minister stated a sanction pathway. That is a functioning detection-and-referral chain, which is AB2/AB3 behaviour. Taken at face value it would argue for holding or slightly raising Accountability rather than lowering it.

**Taken together, the two readings offset and the net is zero.** Scoring this down would penalise Thailand for uncovering corruption. Scoring it up would credit an anti-corruption process that has not yet produced a single sanction. The defensible output is a confirmation with a two-sided watch flag naming exactly which future facts move the score in which direction.
