On Equity, a recorded 1,500% rise in hate speech against the North African community between 6 and 22 July 2026 drew no documented state counter-measure, and Amnesty International had to ask Spain to prevent discriminatory impacts "particularly on racialized people and children". Nothing in the window supports a move of 5 points or more.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: Europe's deadliest border day in years happened at a Spanish city, and Spain's national government declined to call it a national emergency.** Ceuta's regional president declared an "absolute humanitarian and social emergency" on 30 July 2026. Spain's Interior Ministry rejected a national emergency declaration, saying such measures do not cover migration crises.
- **Why it matters: the scale is hard to grasp.** Roughly 60,000 people crossed from Morocco into Ceuta over about 24-48 hours starting 30 July 2026. Ceuta has about 85,000 residents. At least 18 people were confirmed dead by 31 July, with wider in-window estimates in the 40s, from drowning and a crush at the Tarajal breakwater.
- **Why it matters: a court ruling changed the map, and the state did not get ahead of it.** Spain's Supreme Court ruled that people intercepted at sea cannot be summarily returned to Morocco. A Guardia Civil spokesperson said: "It has been a slow trickle since the Supreme Court's ruling, but today has been an explosion."
- **Why it matters: no one has yet found that Spain broke the law here.** Amnesty International's 31 July 2026 statement is a warning, not a finding. It reminds Spain that "the principle of non-refoulement is absolute and collective expulsions are prohibited in all cases." That is why this assessment confirms rather than downgrades.
- **Why it matters: racism rose before the crossings did.** A 1,500% increase in hate speech against the North African community was recorded between 6 and 22 July 2026 — before the surge — and Amnesty asked Spain to condemn it. No state counter-measure was documented inside the window.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Awareness (3.4), Boundaries (3.4), Systemic Thinking (3.4) and Integrity (3.4).** Spain kept its asylum system open under extreme pressure, complied with its own Supreme Court rather than legislating around it, was honest about the limits of the national emergency instrument, and named EU responsibility-sharing as the structural cause rather than treating Ceuta as a policing problem.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Action (3.2), Equity (3.2) and Accountability (3.2).** The operational response to a humanitarian event was troop reinforcement; the national emergency instrument the region asked for was declined; a documented surge in racist speech drew no state response; and no independent inquiry into the breakwater deaths was announced inside the window.

## Evidence Gaps

- Whether the returns of most of the roughly 60,000 people to Morocco were voluntary or coerced is not independently established in-window. This is the single largest gap and it caps B4, B5 and EQ2.
- No Spanish government after-action report, casualty investigation or reception-outcome data was published inside the window, capping AB3 and AC3.
- The number and treatment of unaccompanied children among the arrivals is not disaggregated in any in-window source, capping A2 and EQ2.
