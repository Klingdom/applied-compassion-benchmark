## Why this entity was assessed

Four paying subscribers filed a federal antitrust suit in the Northern District of California on 19 September 2026 against Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and SpaceXAI. Anthropic was last assessed 2026-09-15.

## The instrument question: does the benchmark say anything here?

**No, and this is the finding.**

The plaintiffs allege the companies "violated antitrust laws when they agreed to coordinate slowdown efforts" and that this coordination "would reduce the value consumers get for paid AI subscriptions". The trigger was Dario Amodei's essay of 12 September 2026 urging industry-wide cooperation on decelerating capability advances in favour of stronger safety measures, and same-day public agreement from the other labs' leaders.

Three reasons no dimension moves:

1. **A filed complaint is a procedural event, not a conduct finding.** Nothing has been decided. Representatives for all four companies did not respond to press requests for comment.
2. **The alleged conduct is a safety commitment.** The benchmark measures whether an institution recognises, responds to and reduces suffering. A claim that promising to slow down for safety cost subscribers product value does not map onto any of the 40 behavioural anchors as a harm.
3. **Direction is genuinely ambiguous and must not be forced.** Reading the suit as negative would penalise Anthropic for making a safety commitment. Reading it as positive would credit Anthropic on the strength of someone else's complaint about it. Neither is defensible. The correct output is a recorded screening note and no movement.

## The countervailing positive evidence, and why it also does not move the score

The Amodei essay of 12 September 2026, "We Must Pace the Frontier", is real in-window conduct evidence that the 2026-09-15 assessment did not capture. Reporting describes Anthropic unilaterally giving third-party evaluators permanent, employee-level access to its systems.

This is genuinely relevant to S4 Structural Critique (anchor 4: "Active advocacy documented, positions against short-term interest") and A3 Blind Spot Mitigation. It is not scored up tonight for two reasons. First, it is a published commitment and an announcement, not yet verified practice with a track record. Second, even taking S4 from 3 to 4 moves SYS by 0.2 raw, which moves the composite by roughly 0.6 points. That is nowhere near the filing threshold.

**This is recorded as a positive watch item, not banked as a score.**

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: four subscribers are suing four AI labs for promising to slow down. The suit was filed on 19 September 2026 in a San Francisco federal court. It says the promise cut the value of their paid subscriptions.
- Why it matters: the benchmark has nothing to say about it, and that is the right answer. A filed complaint is not a finding, and the behaviour complained of is a safety promise, not a harm.
- Why it matters: Anthropic's own September action is the more interesting fact. On 12 September 2026 Dario Amodei published an essay asking the industry to slow capability gains by one to two years, and Anthropic is giving outside evaluators permanent, employee-level access to its systems.
- Why it matters: that still does not move the score. It is a commitment, not yet a track record, and it is too small to reach the 5-point filing line.
