## Evidence-date, lifecycle and attribution checks

**NEVER-ASSESSED UNIFORM SEED — SHARED WITH THREE OTHER ENTITIES IN THIS SAME BACKFILL.** Regions' published composite is 60.9, from a vector of exactly 3.5 on seven dimensions and 3.0 on Equity, with `last_assessed: null`. **Nationwide (rank 44), Principal Financial (rank 46), Raymond James Financial (rank 47) and Regions Financial (rank 48) all carry that identical vector and identical composite**, at consecutive index ranks. This is the same defect class as Cerebras Systems (2026-08-16, identical 60.9 seed) and the 20.3 cluster (2026-08-20).

**WHAT DISTINGUISHES REGIONS FROM THE OTHER THREE: a federal consent order for a repeat violation.**

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau ordered Regions Bank to pay **$191 million** — $141 million in redress to consumers and a $50 million civil money penalty — for charging **"authorized-positive overdraft fees"**: overdraft fees on ATM withdrawals and debit card purchases **after telling customers they had sufficient funds**, in violation of the Consumer Financial Protection Act of 2010.

**It was not the first time.** The CFPB had already ordered Regions in 2015 to refund $49 million and pay a $7.5 million penalty for charging overdraft fees to customers who had not opted in and to customers who had been told they would not be charged. **The same category of conduct, twice, seven years apart.** That is the defining condition of anchor 1 for systemic learning: failures addressed individually, and the same failures recur.

**EVIDENCE CURRENCY — STATED PLAINLY, AND IT IS THE MAIN LIMITATION OF THIS FILE.** The consent order is dated **September 2022** and covers conduct from **August 2018 through July 2021**. The prior order is from **2015**. **None of this is current-window evidence and none of it is presented as such.** Presenting a 2022 order for 2018-2021 conduct as a current finding would be exactly the stale-statistic defect this cycle was warned about.

**The live element is recent and is what keeps the record open.** In **November 2025**, the Delaware Court of Chancery **declined to dismiss** a shareholder derivative lawsuit against Regions Bank board members over the $191 million consent order. That is within the currency rule, and it means the governance question arising from the consent order is still before a court.

**ATTRIBUTION.** The overdraft practices, the consent order, and the redress payments are all Regions' own conduct. The derivative suit is a third-party action **against** the company's directors and is treated as context establishing that the matter remains live — **it is an undecided allegation and is not scored as a finding.**

**DIRECTIONALITY.** Regions surfaced on rotation staleness, not on negative within-window evidence, and this assessment moves downward. There is no directionality conflict.

The canonical formula reproduces the published 60.9 exactly from the seed vector, so there is no math-hygiene issue.

## Key Findings

- Regions Bank charged customers overdraft fees after telling them they had enough money. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau ordered it to pay $191 million — $141 million back to customers and a $50 million penalty.
- It had already been ordered to do the same thing once before. In 2015 the CFPB made Regions refund $49 million and pay $7.5 million over overdraft charges. The same kind of failure happened twice, seven years apart.
- That evidence is old and this assessment says so. The order is from 2022 and covers 2018 to 2021 conduct. What is current is that in November 2025 a Delaware court refused to throw out a shareholder suit against Regions directors over it.
- Regions' published score of 60.9 was never based on research. It is a placeholder it shares with Nationwide, Principal Financial and Raymond James — four companies, one identical set of numbers, at consecutive ranks.
- Most of the 38-point drop is removing a placeholder, not punishing behaviour. Regions lands about 2 to 4 points below its three placeholder peers, and that smaller gap is the part the overdraft record actually explains.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Awareness (2.0)**, **Action (2.0)**, **Boundaries (2.0)** and **Systemic Thinking (2.0)** — all at the absence anchor, none distinguishable. No positive evidence was located on any of them. **Empathy (1.8)**, **Equity (1.8)**, **Accountability (1.8)** and **Integrity (1.8)**. All four fall on the same record. ## Evidence Gaps

- THE PRIMARY GAP IS CURRENCY. The consent order is from 2022 for 2018-2021 conduct; the prior order is from 2015. No 2026 conduct finding was located and none is asserted.
- No evidence was located on what Regions changed after the 2022 consent order. If a documented remediation programme exists, Accountability subdimensions AB2 and AB4 could move up materially.

## Disclosure — what this score measures

**Two disclosures, both required, and the first is the more important.**

**MOST OF THIS MOVEMENT MEASURES ABSENCE OF ASSESSMENT, NOT MISCONDUCT.** The published 60.9 was a never-assessed uniform seed. Replacing it with an evidence-based vector produces most of the -38.4 regardless of conduct. Reporting the whole figure as a decline in Regions' behaviour **would be factually wrong**. The three cluster peers with no adverse evidence at all land at 25.0 to 26.9; that band is what absence alone produces.

**A minority of the movement is conduct, and it is real.** Regions assesses roughly 2 to 4 points below its peers on the strength of a federal consent order for a repeat violation. That part is misconduct evidence in the ordinary sense.

**Disclosure density: 31 of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure and are scored 2, never 1.** The nine that do not — E4, EQ2, EQ5, AB1, AB2, AB4, AB5, I2, I4 — rest on the CFPB record and are dated 2015, 2022 and 2025 in their evidence rows.

## Recommendation

**FLAG FOR REVIEW — NOT A CLEAN DOWNGRADE, AND NOT A CLEAN DE-SEEDING EITHER.** This is a mixed case and it is routed to a human for that reason.

**Why it is filed when the other three cluster members were not.** Nationwide, Principal Financial and Raymond James have **no adverse evidence at all**; under anti-false-positive screening check 5 a pure calibration concern must be confirmed and routed for cohort review, not filed as a one-off baseline reset. Those three were handled that way. **Regions is different: it carries a $191 million federal consent order for a repeat violation.** Confirming an "Established" band label — which the methodology defines as practices that are "systematic, documented, and improving" — against that record would leave a published band label the evidence contradicts, which is the most visible error the benchmark can make.

**Recommended remedy, and it is the honest one:** handle Regions inside the four-entity cohort de-seeding study, not as an isolated overnight move. If the founder prefers to act now, the applied record must state that most of the -38.4 measures absence of assessment rather than misconduct, and that the conduct evidence is dated 2022 for 2018-2021 conduct with a live 2025 derivative proceeding.

**Confidence: medium.** High on the CFPB record, which is tier-5 primary. Low on its currency.
