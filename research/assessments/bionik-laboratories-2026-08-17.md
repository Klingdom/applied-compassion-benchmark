## Entity identification

Bionik Laboratories Corp. is a Delaware company headquartered at 80 Coolidge Hill Road, Watertown, Massachusetts. It sells the InMotion range of upper-limb rehabilitation robots, acquired when it bought Interactive Motion Technologies in 2016. The InMotion device is the commercial version of the MIT-Manus robot. The company traded over the counter as BNKL until it deregistered in June 2023.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: the benchmark labelled a company "Established" three years after it went dark.** Bionik Laboratories told the US Securities and Exchange Commission on 30 June 2023 that it was ending its duty to file public reports. It has filed nothing since.
- **The company's flagship product failed its largest independent test.** The RATULS trial, published in The Lancet in 2019, used the InMotion robot by name across four UK centres. Robot-assisted training did not improve upper limb function compared with usual care.
- **Bionik still markets a 15-20% improvement figure from its own whitepaper.** No evidence was found that the company reviewed or responded to the independent trial result.
- **The company was in visible financial distress before it went quiet.** In June 2023 it cut costs, lost four of its seven directors, and borrowed at 1% a month.
- **Roughly 32 of the 40 scores rest on the absence of any published evidence.** That is not a finding of harm. It is a finding that a private, deregistered company publishes almost nothing a compassion benchmark can read.

## Scoring Conventions Applied

- **Absence of disclosure scores 2, never 1.** The floor of 1 is used only where positive documented evidence of a specific failure exists. This is the "2-convention", the more conservative of the two conventions live in this repository.
- **A score of 4 requires published institutional data, a regulatory decision or third-party peer review. A score of 5 requires independent audit plus community testimony.**
- 37 of 40 subdimensions sit at 2. Of those, approximately 32 rest on absence of disclosure rather than on located evidence of a practice.
