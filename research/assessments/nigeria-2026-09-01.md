## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Nigeria on an SBM Intelligence report released in late August 2026 finding 1,411 kidnapping incidents and about 7,825 victims between July 2025 and June 2026, with Boko Haram collecting more than 90% of roughly N7.78 billion in ransom from just eight incidents. Nigeria was last assessed on 2026-07-17 (confirmation at 18.0, downward watch flag) and its published 18.0 was set on 2026-06-19 on FAO-WFP hunger-hotspot evidence.

## Evidence-date, lifecycle and attribution checks

**FIGURE CORRECTION — the scan's death toll is wrong.** The scan summary states "963 people killed". **Both primary-adjacent sources say 1,142.** Nairametrics (2026-08-27) reports "at least 1,142 people killed, up from 762 previously"; West African Pilot News (2026-08-29) independently reports "at least 1,142 people killed". The correct figure is **1,142**, a rise of roughly 50% year on year, and it is the figure used here. The scan's 963 does not appear in either cited source.

**REPORT-IDENTITY DISCREPANCY — recorded, not resolved.** The two secondary sources give the SBM Intelligence report **different titles and different release dates**. Nairametrics calls it "The Capture of Nigeria's Kidnap Economy" and publishes on 27 August 2026. West African Pilot News calls it "The Economics of Nigeria's Kidnap Industry, 2026" and says SBM released it "on Thursday (August 28, 2026)". The underlying statistics are identical across both. This is most likely one report described two ways, but **the benchmark did not verify which title and date are correct**, and no scoring rests on the title or on a one-day date difference.

**DATE MISMATCH RESOLVED (gate warning 3, Nigeria, 2 days).** `evidence_date: 2026-08-27` against URLs carrying 27 August (Nairametrics), 28 August (AllAfrica) and 29 August (West African Pilot News). These are a report release followed by wire pickups over the following two days. The evidence_date is the report date; the URL dates are publication dates. **Both are correct.** No year-confusion — all three carry explicit 2026.

**ATTRIBUTION — the kidnappings are non-state conduct and are NOT scored against Nigeria.** SBM Intelligence's report describes Boko Haram and armed bandit groups. These are non-state armed actors. Their abductions, killings and ransom-taking are their acts.

**What IS scored is the Nigerian state's protective and investigative conduct**, on both sides:
- **Against:** abductions rose 66% year on year (4,722 to 7,825) and deaths rose from 762 to 1,142 across the same period, with 85% of cases in the North. Boko Haram extracted roughly N7 billion — 90% of all ransom paid — from only eight incidents, including mass student abductions. Ransom on that scale, concentrated in so few events, indicates no state interdiction of the payment channel.
- **For:** Nigerian police rescued 75 people kidnapped from commercial buses in Kaduna State on 19 August 2026 — a documented, successful protective operation inside the window.

**Neither source says anything about the government's response.** Both were re-fetched and both were checked specifically for it. Nairametrics: "The article does not address government initiatives or security sector performance." West African Pilot News likewise. This is an evidence gap and is treated as one, not as a finding of failure.

## Key Findings

- Kidnappings in Nigeria rose 66% in a year. SBM Intelligence counted 7,825 people abducted between July 2025 and June 2026, up from 4,722. At least 1,142 were killed, up from 762.
- The scan reported 963 killed. Both source articles say 1,142. The benchmark uses 1,142 and records the correction.
- Boko Haram took about N7 billion — 90% of all ransom paid in Nigeria that year — from just eight incidents, including mass abductions of students. Money on that scale moving through so few events suggests nothing is stopping it.
- The kidnappers are not the Nigerian state, and the benchmark does not score their crimes against it. What it scores is that 85% of cases fall in the North and the gap is widening.
- Nigeria's police did rescue 75 people taken from buses in Kaduna State on 19 August 2026. That is credited. Nigeria confirms at 17.5 of 100, a 0.5-point move that is not filable.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Boundaries (2.0)**. Nigeria's federal structure and public communication about the security situation are comparatively frank, and the state does not systematically overstate what it can deliver. It is the only dimension at or above 2.0 and it is unchanged.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (1.4)** and **Systemic Thinking (1.4)**. Both fell this cycle and both fell on the same evidence: harm concentrated at 85% in one region, and a ransom economy dominated by a single armed group that the state is not evidenced as disrupting. Equity measures who gets protection; on this record, northern Nigerians do not.

## Evidence Gaps

- Neither source reports any Nigerian government or security-force response to the SBM findings. Both were checked specifically for it. This is recorded as an evidence gap, not scored as a failure.
- The SBM report is cited under two different titles and two different release dates by the two secondary sources. The discrepancy is recorded; no scoring depends on it.
- The SBM report itself was not obtained directly; scoring rests on two independent secondary accounts of it, which agree on every statistic used.
- The Kaduna rescue of 75 people is reported in the scan summary but the primary report of it was not independently re-fetched; it is credited conservatively as a single documented operation.

## Disclosure — what this score measures

This score measures the Nigerian state's **protective and investigative conduct**, not the conduct of the armed groups doing the kidnapping. Nigeria is not being scored for Boko Haram's acts.

Where evidence was simply absent — most importantly on the government's response to the SBM findings — subdimensions are scored at 2, the absence anchor, not 1. **Approximately 12 of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure rather than on documented failure.** The subdimensions scored 1 (EQ1, EQ2, EQ5, AB2, AB5, S1, S4, S5, E5, AC5, I5) rest on positive documented evidence: the regional concentration of harm and the uninterrupted ransom economy.

## Recommendation

**Confirm. No change proposal.** Delta -0.5, far inside the threshold; no band crossing.

**Watch flag (downward), now in its third cycle.** Nigeria has carried a downward flag since 2026-07-17. If a fourth consecutive cycle documents further mass-abduction escalation without any evidenced state interdiction, that pattern should be escalated to a proposal rather than confirmed again. Nigeria has 18.0 points of downward room, so unlike the near-floor entities in this cohort it can express a real movement.
