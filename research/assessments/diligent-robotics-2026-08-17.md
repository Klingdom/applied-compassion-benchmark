### Score Difference Analysis

The published vector `4 / 4.5 / 4 / 3.5 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4` is byte-identical across six robotics labs occupying consecutive alphabetical ranks. It is a never-assessed uniform seed. Diligent Robotics has never been individually assessed against the 40 behavioural anchors before this report.

The published value asserts practice at the fourth anchor across all eight dimensions: published disaggregated data, formal pathways, documented reparative action, independent verification and community confirmation. Diligent Robotics publishes none of those things. 29 of the 40 subdimensions here score at the low anchor of 2, which under this study's fixed rule means **no evidence was found in either direction** — not that harm was found.

### Recommendation

The published score is **overstated** and should be revised down to 31.9, band Developing. This is a seed correction, not a conduct downgrade.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Diligent Robotics is the weakest of the six labs published at 83.0, and it is also the one the index describes wrongly. It is now a Serve Robotics subsidiary, not a standalone company.
- Moxi was built to reduce nurse burnout, and that mission is genuine. But the only independent investigation of the programme found two hospitals removed the robot — Good Samaritan in July 2024 and Tacoma General in August 2025.
- Nurses told Proof News the robot needed constant escorting. One asked: "Why do we have the robot if we have a human with her all the time?" That is the opposite of the burnout relief the product promises.
- The company did correct course. Moxi 2.0, rolling out from 17 August 2026, processes its surroundings 10 to 15 times faster than the previous generation, addressing the navigation failures nurses described.
- All five Equity subdimensions score at the low anchor. No access programme, no disaggregated data and no pricing relief for safety-net hospitals was found. This is an absence of disclosure, not a finding of harm.
