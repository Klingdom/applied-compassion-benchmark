Jamaica was assessed on 2026-09-14, and this event post-dates that assessment, so it is genuinely new. What is scored is the statutory commission's published aggregate data, which is state-produced fact, not allegation.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Jamaica's police have killed 215 people in under nine months. The oversight commission INDECOM reported that figure on 14-15 September 2026, against 217 in the same period of 2025.
- Why it matters: group killings are rising even as the total holds steady. INDECOM counted 67 people killed in 26 multiple-fatality incidents in 2026, already more than the 62 killed in all of 2025.
- Why it matters: the cameras that would settle disputed accounts are still missing. INDECOM flagged the continued absence of body-worn cameras during unmarked-vehicle operations.
- Why it matters: the state does watch itself. INDECOM is an independent statutory commission that publishes numbers embarrassing to the government, which is why Accountability holds at 2.4 rather than falling.
- Why it matters: the 19 September protest shows the trust gap. Residents burned a vehicle and gathered at the hospital after police killed Amani Malcolm, 26. No police response to the community was recorded.
