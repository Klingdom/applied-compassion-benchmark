## ⚠️ THE SCANNER'S TRIGGERING EVENT DID NOT HAPPEN — RETRACTED AT SOURCE

**The scanner flagged Peru on the reported killing of Ashéninka leader Américo Pascual Tumisha. He is alive.**

The Regional Organisation AIDESEP Ucayali (ORAU), which issued the original alert, **rectified it**. A commission of the ACONADIYSH Frontier Indigenous Guard travelled by river to Yurúa and confirmed directly that Pascual Tumisha is safe and with his family. He had been hunting in the forest. The original report came from a community member's message to Pascual's son. ORAU has stated that the distance and limited connectivity in Onconashari prevented immediate verification.

This is corroborated by **five independent Peruvian outlets** on 26 August 2026: La República, Inforegión, Caretas, El Comercio and Radio Nacional. **Rainforest Foundation Norway's article reporting the killing now returns HTTP 404** — it has been taken down.

**Consequences for this assessment, stated plainly:**

1. **No killing is scored.** The scanner's `news_summary` — "was shot and killed while patrolling" — is factually wrong and must not propagate. This assessment does not treat it as evidence of anything.
2. **The scanner is not at fault for reporting it.** Both of its cited sources (Manila Times/AFP, Mongabay) carried the killing at the time of the scan, and both were date-verified. The correction emerged after publication. This is recorded as an **evidence-lifecycle** failure, not a date-verification failure — a category the current gate does not catch, because a source can be correctly dated and still be later retracted.
3. **What remains is genuinely evidential**, and it points in both directions.

## What actually happened in the window

**Adverse, and still valid:** AIDESEP reports that Pascual Tumisha "received neither a state response nor protective measures" despite having previously reported outsiders entering community territory. ORAU warns that intimidation is intensifying "due to the absence of the State". Mongabay's own framing stands: "Paper cannot stop a bullet; concrete action is needed on the ground to protect defenders." Onconashari residents have reported incursions tied to unauthorised copaiba-oil extraction since at least 2020.

**Favourable, dated, and significant:** On **Monday 24 August 2026** a Peruvian court sentenced four men for the 2023 murder-for-hire of Indigenous defender **Quinto Inuma Alvarado**: Genix Saboya Saboya and Limber Ríos Ruiz to 35 years each; Segundo Juan Villalobos Guevara, the timber operator accused of ordering the killing, to 28 years and 4 months; Jerly Saboya Saboya, an accomplice, to 15 years. Cristina Gavancho of the Legal Defense Institute calls it "The first case in Peru involving the killing of an Indigenous defender to result in sentences this severe." Tom Younger of Forest Peoples Programme says it shows impunity "is not inevitable." Inuma's son Kevin Arnol Inuma: "Before, there was no response from the state, so they were not afraid to kill. Now this sets a precedent."

**Also favourable:** the Ucayali regional government formally recognised Onconashari's Forest Surveillance and Control Committee in August 2025, accrediting members as forest custodians with authority to patrol their territory.

## Attribution

**Non-state actors, not the state.** Even in the original (now retracted) report, the alleged perpetrators were illegal copaiba-oil extractors, not state agents. Nothing in this cycle attributes violence to Peru. What is scored is the state's **protective failure** — no response or protective measures to a defender's reported threats — and the state's **response**, which includes a prompt prosecutorial investigation and, separately, a landmark sentencing.

## Key Findings

- **The event that put Peru on tonight's list did not happen.** Ashéninka leader Américo Pascual Tumisha is alive and with his family. The Indigenous federation that raised the alarm retracted it after a river commission checked in person.
- **Five Peruvian outlets confirmed the correction on 26 August 2026**, and one international NGO article reporting the killing has been taken offline.
- **A Peruvian court did something unprecedented on 24 August 2026.** It jailed four men for 15 to 35 years for murdering Indigenous defender Quinto Inuma Alvarado in 2023, including 28 years for the timber operator accused of ordering it.
- **His son said what changed: "Before, there was no response from the state, so they were not afraid to kill."** Lawyers call it the first Peruvian case over a defender's killing to bring sentences this heavy.
- **Peru's score barely moves — up 0.3 points to 36.2 out of 100.** Better accountability offsets a documented failure to protect: Pascual Tumisha had reported intruders and got no state response.

## Strongest Dimensions

Empathy (2.80) and Accountability (2.60). The Inuma verdict is a genuine, independently praised piece of state accountability, and the forest-custodian accreditation is real co-designed adaptation.

## Weakest Dimensions

Action and Equity, both at 2.20. A defender who reported threats received no protection, and the state is largely absent from the territory it has asked communities to patrol.
