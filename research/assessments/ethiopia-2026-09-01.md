## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Ethiopia on continued fighting in Tigray — the second sustained period of military confrontation between federal forces and the TPLF since the 2022 peace deal — documented in a security review dated 27 August 2026. Ethiopia was last assessed on 2026-08-11 (assessed 2.5) and sits near the floor at a published 4.7 of 100.

## Evidence-date, lifecycle and attribution checks

**SOURCING CORRECTION — the scan attributes a claim to Amnesty International that Amnesty does not make.** The scan summary reads: "amid drone strikes described by Amnesty International as renewing the risk of serious human rights violations." On direct re-fetch, **the Amnesty article contains no reference to drone strikes at all.** It describes "violent clashes in parts of Tigray...between Tigrayan forces and the Ethiopian National Defence Force (ENDF)", documents forced conscription and movement restrictions **by Tigrayan authorities**, and calls on the federal government to restore monitor and media access. The drone-strike evidence comes from Critical Threats (tier 2), not from Amnesty. **The attribution is corrected and the Amnesty source is used only for what it actually says.**

**EVIDENCE-DATE CORRECTION — the Amnesty source is 24 days older than the scan's evidence_date.** The scanner recorded `evidence_date: 2026-08-27`, which is correct for the Critical Threats security review. The Amnesty article carries `/2026/08/` in its URL and resolves to **3 August 2026** — outside the 2026-08-27 to 2026-09-01 scan window and nearly four weeks before the date the scan assigned to it. It is used here as in-period background (it falls inside the benchmark's 2-3 year currency rule) but **not** as within-window evidence. No year-confusion: the URL and content both carry 2026.

**ATTRIBUTION — separated in both directions, per the cycle instruction.**
- **Scored as Ethiopian state conduct:** the Ethiopian National Defence Force drone strikes. Critical Threats records these plainly — strikes across Tigray since mid-August, an out-of-session school in Merewa on 10 August, multiple locations in and around Mekele on 18 and 20 August, and eastern, northwestern and southern Tigray from 24 to 26 August. The ENDF is the state's own military.
- **NOT scored as Ethiopian state conduct:** the forced conscription campaigns and movement restrictions Amnesty documents. Amnesty attributes those to **Tigrayan authorities**, a regional non-federal actor. Attributing them to the federal government would be the mirror of the error the 2026-08-20 cycle corrected on Sudan.
- **Held open, not scored:** whether the strikes killed civilians. Critical Threats states that "Agence France-Presse and BBC citing conflicting reports as to whether civilians or TPLF fighters were harmed." **The civilian-casualty claim is not established and is not scored.** What is scored is the decision to strike a school building in a populated village, which is a failure of anticipatory awareness regardless of who was inside.

## Key Findings

- Ethiopia's own military bombed its own region. Critical Threats reports the Ethiopian National Defence Force "has conducted numerous drone strikes across the Tigray region since mid-August" — the first sustained military confrontation there since the 2022 peace deal.
- One strike on 10 August 2026 hit "an out-of-session school where TPLF fighters were sheltering" in Merewa. Whether civilians died is disputed; AFP and the BBC carry conflicting reports. The benchmark does not score the disputed casualties. It scores the decision to strike a school in a village.
- The scan credited Amnesty International with describing these drone strikes. Amnesty does not mention drone strikes at all. The attribution was corrected and the Amnesty article is used only for what it says.
- Amnesty's article is also dated 3 August 2026, not 27 August as the scan implied — 24 days older. It is used as background, not as within-window evidence.
- Ethiopia's assessed score falls 2.8 points, to 1.9 of 100. The benchmark needs a 5.0-point move to file a change and Ethiopia only has 4.7 points of room. A state bombing its own region cannot produce a filable score change.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Action, Equity, Accountability, Systemic Thinking and Integrity, all at 1.0.** No evidence was located of the federal government delivering, distributing, owning, addressing root causes of, or maintaining commitments about the harm in Tigray. Amnesty's central request — that the government restore access for independent monitors and media — is itself evidence that the state is preventing the outside verification these dimensions would require.

## Evidence Gaps

- Civilian casualties from the ENDF drone strikes are not independently established. AFP and the BBC carry conflicting accounts. The claim is not scored in either direction.
- Independent human rights monitors and media are excluded from conflict-affected Tigray, which Amnesty explicitly asks the government to remedy. This is the reason so many subdimensions cannot be scored above the floor: verification is unavailable because the state is withholding it.
- No within-window evidence was located on Ethiopia's national social protection architecture (for example the Productive Safety Net Programme), which could support higher Action and Equity scores if sourced. It could not be sourced in-window and was therefore not credited. This gap is stated rather than resolved by assumption.
- Ethiopia is near the floor, so the assessed -2.8 movement cannot be filed regardless of its merit.

## Disclosure — what this score measures

This score rests on documented evidence of specific failure, not on absence of disclosure. **0 of 40 subdimensions are scored on absence.**

Two things this score explicitly does **not** measure. It does not attribute the forced conscription and movement restrictions Amnesty documents to the federal government — Amnesty attributes those to Tigrayan authorities. And it does not treat the disputed civilian casualties from the drone strikes as established.

One thing it does not credit, and should be honest about: Ethiopia operates one of Africa's largest social protection systems, which would support higher Action and Equity scores. No within-window source for it was located, so it was not credited. That is an evidence gap running **in Ethiopia's favour**, and it is disclosed here rather than buried.
