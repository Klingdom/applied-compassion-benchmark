## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Hong Kong (priority 58, news score 35) on the 11 September 2026 sentencing of three leaders of the Hong Kong Alliance, the group that organised the city's Tiananmen vigil for three decades. They were sentenced on national-security charges of inciting subversion. Hong Kong was last assessed on 2026-07-31 (confirmation at 29.4, with a watch flag), and before that on 2026-07-26 and 2026-07-23 (both 32.5).

## Evidence-date, provenance, attribution and screening checks

**BASELINE PROVENANCE (read first).** The published 32.8 has no APPLIED_CHANGES entry and is the original baseline. Three assessments in July measured 32.5, 32.5 and 29.4, the last with a downward watch flag. No stale-baseline argument is used or needed.

**THE WATCH FLAG CONDITION.** The 2026-07-31 assessment said a further escalation within four months would support a proposal. Six weeks later, three peaceful vigil organisers received sentences of five to seven years, and the government issued a statement dismissing UN criticism as smears. This is a further escalation of the same pattern (bookstore raids, prosecutions of people with intellectual disability, now multi-year sentences for commemoration).

**DIRECTIONALITY.** Surfaced on negative in-window evidence; this is a downgrade. Direction matches evidence.

**ATTRIBUTION.** This entity is the Hong Kong SAR in the global-cities index. The National Security Law was imposed by Beijing, but the prosecution (Department of Justice), the arrests and characterisation (Police National Security Department), the sentencing (High Court) and the 12 September defence of the sentences (HKSAR Government) are all HKSAR institutions. They are scored against this entity.

**EVIDENCE TIERS.** Four independent sources. OHCHR (tier 4) and the HKSAR Government's own statement (tier 5, primary conduct) are the anchors. HKFP (tier 2) and HRW (tier 3) support. There is no band crossing, so the band test does not apply; it would pass regardless.

**DATES.** Conviction on 21 August 2026 (HRW; pre-window, context only). Sentencing on 11 September and the government statement on 12 September are both in window. The NPR and CNN copies cited by the scanner timed out; equivalent facts were verified via HKFP, OHCHR and HRW.

**DECOMPOSITION OF THE -5.9.** About -3.4 is the gap already measured on 2026-07-31 (bookstore raids and prosecutions). About -2.5 is the four in-window integer moves (E4, AB1, S4, I1). Neither part is a calibration adjustment: both rest on the HKSAR's own documented conduct across consecutive cycles.

### Recommendation

DOWNGRADE, filed: 32.8 to 26.9 (-5.9), staying in the Developing band. It rests on in-window conduct by HKSAR institutions: multi-year sentences for peaceful vigil organisers, and the government's categorical rejection of UN concern. That completes an escalation pattern flagged on 2026-07-31.

## Key Findings

- Hong Kong jailed the organisers of its Tiananmen vigil for up to seven years. On 11 September 2026 its High Court sentenced Chow Hang-tung, Lee Cheuk-yan and Albert Ho for inciting subversion.
- The judges said the case involved no violence, but still placed it in the 5-to-10-year sentencing band. The three had already spent five years in custody.
- The UN human rights office called the sentences regrettable and urged that they be overturned. Hong Kong's government dismissed foreign criticism as smears.
- This is the third step in a pattern the benchmark flagged in July, after bookstore raids and prosecutions of people with intellectual disabilities.
- Hong Kong's score is proposed to fall from 32.8 to 26.9. That is still in the Developing band, but close to mainland Chinese cities.
