## Scanner date / attribution corrections

- **DATE CORRECTION — the protest was 25 July 2026, not 1 August 2026, and it falls outside this window.** The scan states "Tens of thousands of Tunisians marked five years of President Kais Saied's rule with protests on August 1, 2026." The rally actually took place on **Saturday 25 July 2026** and was reported on **26 July 2026** by Al Jazeera (aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/26/...) and Africanews (africanews.com/2026/07/26/...). The Al Jazeera piece the scanner cited, dated 1 August 2026, is an **analysis article** about simmering public anger, not a report of an 1 August protest. The underlying event predates this cycle's 29 July window start by four days.
- **DOUBLE-COUNT SCREEN — this is the same event as the open Tunis proposal.** The open `tunis.json` first-baseline proposal, dated 2026-07-26, already scores this exact protest: "Thousands rallied on Habib Bourguiba Avenue in central Tunis on Saturday 25 July 2026 (reported 26 July) marking five years since President Kais Saied suspended parliament." Scoring it again against the country entity would double-count a single protest across two indexes.
- **Baseline provenance verified.** Tunisia's published 23.8 is not a stale bulk-import figure. It was set by a first individual assessment and **applied on 2026-07-14**, correcting an un-assessed placeholder of 34.4 downward on evidence of systematic repression, mass conspiracy trials and the sentencing of the former truth commission president to 25 years. Any "stale baseline" rationale for moving this score is invalid on the documented history.
