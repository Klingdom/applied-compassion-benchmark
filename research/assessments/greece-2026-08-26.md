## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Greece on a riot and mass escape at a migrant detention centre in the north of the country. Greece was last assessed on 2026-05-06, which produced a downgrade from 46.9 to 39.1 and a band crossing.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Date CONFIRMED.** The riot began shortly after 9pm on **Saturday 23 August 2026** at the detention centre in Sintiki, near Serres. 23 August 2026 was a Saturday. The scanner's `evidence_date` of 2026-08-23 is correct.

**THREE ACTORS MUST BE SEPARATED — as the cycle instruction requires.**

1. **Detainee conduct.** A group of detained migrants knocked down an internal fence, threw stones at police, damaged the building, common areas, security cameras, firefighting equipment and vehicles, and 35 escaped. **This is the detainees' own action and is not scored against Greece.** An entity is not marked down for being the target of a riot.
2. **Facility-operator and police conduct.** Police called reinforcements and used flash-bang grenades; reports of injuries range from three to five. Use of force in a detention setting is state conduct and is scored, but the record is thin: no source located alleges disproportionate force, and no injury is attributed to a specific police action.
3. **State responsibility for the conditions.** About 450 people whose asylum claims are being processed are held at this one centre. Greece's detention and asylum system is the state's own design and is scored. This is where the weight sits.

**MOST OF THE ADVERSE RECORD IS ALREADY PRICED — NOT RE-SCORED.** The 2026-05-06 assessment already priced 594-595 documented pushbacks affecting about 17,000 people, the February 2026 Coast Guard collision deaths, the February 2026 criminalisation of search-and-rescue work, and the Pylos indictment of 17 officers. The February 2026 migration law making NGO membership an aggravating factor is the same legislative package as the "Feb 5 2026 SAR criminalization" priced in May. It is carried here as standing context, not counted again.

**INTEGER-GRID DISCLOSURE — THIS EXPLAINS MOST OF THE -2.8.** Six of Greece's eight published dimensions sit at 2.5 or 3.0 half-steps. Five integer subdimension scores **cannot** average to 2.5 — the nearest achievable values are 2.4 and 2.6. This cycle requires on-grid integer anchors. Rounding the five 2.5s down to 2.4 alone accounts for **-0.5 raw points spread across five dimensions**, and AWR/SYS moving 3.0 to 2.8 accounts for most of the rest. **The delta is largely an artefact of the integer grid, not a finding of deteriorating Greek conduct.** It is recorded here so the next cycle does not read -2.8 as new harm.

**Confirm.** Delta -2.8, no band change, no change proposal filed. Five of the eight -0.1 movements are the integer-grid artefact described above, not findings. The only genuinely new evidence — the Sintiki riot — is predominantly detainee conduct, and no source alleges disproportionate force by Greek police. ## Key Findings

- **About 35 migrants escaped a Greek detention centre near Serres on 23 August 2026 after a riot.** Police used flash-bang grenades. Seventeen escapees were recaptured; reports say three to five people were hurt.
- **The riot was the detainees' own action, and Greece is not marked down for it.** What is scored is the system that held about 450 asylum applicants in one facility.
- **Greece's score does not change enough to file anything.** It moves from 39.1 to 36.3 out of 100, and stays in the Developing band.
- **Most of that 2.8-point drop is arithmetic, not news.** Greece's published scores use half-steps like 2.5, and five whole-number sub-scores cannot average to 2.5. The nearest legal value is 2.4.
- **The February 2026 law making NGO membership an aggravating factor still stands.** UN experts said in February that it criminalises the defence of human rights. That was already counted in May and is not counted again.
