## Key Findings

- Everything the benchmark could find about Gran Tierra Energy was written by Gran Tierra Energy. There is a human rights policy, a community engagement programme and sustainability reporting — all on the company's own website, none independently audited.
- The most concrete claim is 329 hours. Gran Tierra says its mobile "Te Escucha" offices logged 329 hours of direct engagement with remote communities near its Charapa and Chanangue operations in Ecuador.
- It also claims a perfect groundwater record. The company states that independent monitoring has confirmed it has fully safeguarded groundwater quality throughout its history. No linked audit supporting that claim was located.
- Self-reported evidence caps the score. Under the benchmark's evidence rules, stated commitments without verification cannot score above 2 out of 5. That is why the assessment lands at 23.1.
- The score would go up, and the benchmark is not moving it. A 4.3-point rise built entirely on a company's own marketing pages is not evidence of better conduct. It stays at the published 18.8.
