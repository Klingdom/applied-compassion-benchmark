## Key Findings

- **Locus Robotics scores 25.0 of 100 — the lowest possible score under this methodology when no adverse conduct is found. All eight dimensions sit exactly at the absence floor.** No lawsuit, safety incident, regulatory action or labour ruling was located. What was also not located is any published data about the human beings who work next to these robots.
- **Locus says its robots reduce warehouse injuries and publishes no injury data.** The claim appears in company statements. No study, no methodology and no customer injury rate were found. Under this methodology an unevidenced benefit claim is scored as unevidenced rather than credited.
- **The people Locus most affects are not its customers.** Warehouse associates wear Bluetooth tags for identification and have their individual units-per-hour recorded in the LocusHub dashboard. Their employer bought the system. No consent, retention or opt-out disclosure for that associate-level data was located.
- **Two Locus statements sit in tension and nothing reconciles them.** The company says associates set their own preferred pace. It also sells an optional gamification feature that runs productivity competitions and is described as adding 5 to 10 per cent to units per hour. Both cannot be fully true at once.
- **This score is not the same kind of 25.0 as a Chinese humanoid start-up's.** Twenty-nine of Locus's 40 subdimensions rest on absence; the other 11 rest on located evidence that simply does not clear a higher anchor. The disclosure-density figure exists to keep those two cases apart.
