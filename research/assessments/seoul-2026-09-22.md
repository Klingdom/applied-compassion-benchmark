## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Seoul on findings released 21 September 2026 by South Korea's Board of Audit and Inspection (BAI), which found that a Seoul city division director used a contractor's corporate credit card 51 times over roughly a year to buy personal items worth 21.03 million won, and filed a criminal complaint for bribery against the official and the contractor. Seoul had never been individually assessed (`last_assessed: null` in `research/rotation-state.json`), so this cycle produces the city's first formal evidence-based baseline rather than a targeted re-score.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Date verified.** The BAI released its report, titled "Public Sector Audit I on Entrenched Local Corruption," on 21 September 2026, inside the 2026-09-08 to 2026-09-22 window. The underlying conduct ran from May 2025.

**Attribution — and it cuts both ways, as the cycle brief required.** Two different actors must be separated.

*The failure is Seoul's.* The official was a Seoul Metropolitan Government (SMG) division director. He accepted a corporate card issued by a company run by an executive immediately before signing a 1.04 billion won goods procurement contract with that firm, and then spent on it for about a year — including a 3.78 million won laptop for his child and hotel stays of 880,000 won and 2.68 million won a night. That is Seoul's own personnel, Seoul's own procurement decision, and Seoul's own internal control that failed to notice for a year.

*The accountability is mostly not Seoul's.* The detection came from the BAI, South Korea's national constitutional audit body, not from SMG. The BAI, not SMG, filed the criminal complaint; the BAI, not SMG, recommended restricting the contractor's bidding eligibility and keeping the violation on the official's personnel record. SMG's role in the accountability chain so far is that it was *notified*. The official had already retired in June 2026, which limits the sanction available. So the benchmark credits an audit system that worked — but that system is the Republic of Korea's, and this index scores the city. Seoul earns the A3 credit for being subject to, and now on notice from, an external audit that found something significant; it does not earn AB1, AB2 or AB4 credit for a correction it has not yet been shown to have made.

**Not charged to Seoul.** National-level restrictions on migrant work visas and the structural flaws critics identify in Korea's state-sponsored labour migration programmes are central-government policy and are not scored against the city.

### Score Difference Analysis

The published 60.9 is an unassessed seed value, not a prior measurement: Seoul's dimension vector in `global-cities.json` is byte-identical to Toronto's and Vancouver's, which is the signature of a seeded cohort rather than independent scoring. Tonight is Seoul's first evidence-based baseline, so every difference below is a first measurement rather than a revision.

**ACC (published 3.5 raw / 62.5 scaled; research 2.2 raw / 30.0 scaled; -32.5).** This is the largest single gap and it is driven entirely by the within-window evidence. A published 3.5 implies a city that acknowledges harm before external establishment and reliably corrects course. What the BAI found on 21 September 2026 is the opposite pattern: roughly a year of supplier-funded personal spending by a division director, immediately after he signed contracts worth 1.28 billion won with that supplier, surfaced by a national audit body rather than by the city; a criminal complaint filed by the auditor, not the city; and a report framed around *entrenched* local corruption. Seoul's documented role so far is to have been notified.

**INT (published 3.0 / 50.0; research 2.6 / 40.0; -10.0).** The same facts depress I3 and I4. Seoul's external compassion programming is real and substantial; its internal integrity control on procurement was not.

**BND (published 3.0 / 50.0; research 2.4 / 35.0; -15.0).** Not driven by the audit. It reflects genuine absence of evidence: no published burnout or turnover data for frontline care staff (B1), no refusal protocol with tracked alternatives (B4), and no sourced consent architecture for the household risk-scoring that underpins early detection (B5). Scored at lower anchors with gaps recorded.

**ACT holds close (published 4.0 / 75.0; research 3.6 / 65.0).** Seoul's action machinery is its real strength and the published value is close to right: a five-year 451.3 billion won plan, risk-tiered social prescribing, published helpline volumes, and 1.7685 trillion won of disability welfare up 8.1% year on year.

**SYS falls only modestly (4.0 to 3.4)** and for a defensible reason: the root-cause and long-horizon work is strong, but structural critique and coalition contributions are documented at anchor 3, not 4.

No dimension moved up. That is not an artefact of a negative flag — Seoul's positive evidence was searched for and found in volume, and it is what holds AWR, ACT and SYS as high as they are.

### Recommendation

The published 60.9 (Established) is **overstated** on current evidence. The measured baseline is 48.8 (Functional), a delta of -12.1 with a band crossing from Established to Functional. A change proposal is filed on both the magnitude trigger and the band-crossing trigger.

Confidence is **medium** rather than high: the negative evidence is a Tier 4/5 national audit finding and is strong, but several Boundaries and Equity subdimensions rest on absence of published data rather than on adverse findings, and Seoul's Korean-language disclosure may contain material this English-language desk review did not reach. The proposal notes that the published value sits in a three-entity identical-vector cohort (Seoul, Toronto, Vancouver), which the coordinator may wish to review separately rather than entity by entity.

They are not credited to Seoul at AB1, AB2 or AB4, because the detecting and referring institution was the Republic of Korea's Board of Audit and Inspection, not the Seoul Metropolitan Government.

## Key Findings

- Seoul's welfare machinery is the real thing, and it is what keeps the score in the Functional band. The city committed 451.3 billion won (about USD 322 million) over five years to loneliness and lonely deaths, created the country's first Director General of Care and Loneliness, and raised disability welfare spending to 1.7685 trillion won in 2025, up 8.1% in a year.
- A national audit found Seoul’s own procurement controls failed for about a year. On 21 September 2026 the Board of Audit and Inspection reported that a Seoul division director used a contractor’s corporate card 51 times to buy 21.03 million won of personal goods, after signing contracts worth 1.28 billion won with that contractor.
- The accountability that worked was national, not municipal. The audit board detected the misuse, filed the criminal complaint and recommended sanctions; Seoul’s documented role so far is that it was notified, and the official had already retired in June 2026.
- Seoul targets help by measured need, which is unusual and earns credit. Residents screened as medium-to-high isolation risk receive a customised "Seoul Connection Prescription" rather than a standard service.
- The weakest area is Boundaries, and it is weak mainly for lack of disclosure: no published burnout or turnover data for frontline care staff, no refusal protocol with tracked alternatives, and no sourced consent route for the household risk-scoring that drives early detection.

## Strongest Dimensions

**ACT (3.6 raw)** and **AWR (3.4) / SYS (3.4)**. Seoul detects isolation through multiple formal channels, funds the response at a scale that is genuinely proportionate to the problem, and aims the money upstream at prevention rather than at symptom relief. The five-year plan is codified in a standing ordinance, so the horizon outlasts a budget cycle.

## Weakest Dimensions

**ACC (2.2 raw)** and **BND (2.4)**. Accountability is low because the only significant harm finding in the window was produced externally, referred externally and sanctioned externally, with no documented municipal correction yet. Boundaries is low largely because Seoul publishes little about staff sustainability, refusal practice or consent for the data-driven detection it relies on.

## Evidence Gaps

- No Seoul Metropolitan Government response to the 2026-09-21 BAI finding was published within the window. AB1, AB2 and AB4 are scored on what is documented, and all three should be re-tested as soon as a response appears.
- Korean-language SMG disclosure was not searched. Material on staff turnover, refusal protocols and consent may exist there and would raise B1, B4 and B5.
- No independent evaluation of the loneliness programme’s outcomes has been published yet, which caps AC3 at 3 and S2 at 4.
- No formal acknowledgment of a specific historical harm by SMG was located, so EQ5 is scored at the lower anchor with the gap recorded rather than treated as denial.
