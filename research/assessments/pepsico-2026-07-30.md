## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: a worker says she told PepsiCo about anti-gay harassment for two years and it kept happening.** A former employee sued on 24 July 2026 in federal court in Pennsylvania. The complaint says coworkers refused to share a restroom with her, used a homophobic slur, and told her they could not stand gay people. PepsiCo has not filed a response and no court has ruled.
- **Why it matters: this is an allegation, not a finding.** The assessment treats it as an unproven claim. It moves the Empathy score by 0.1 raw points, not more.
- **Why it matters: PepsiCo pushed its own climate deadline back by ten years.** The company extended its net-zero target from 2040 to 2050 during the 2025 reporting cycle. That is a long-horizon promise weakened when it became inconvenient, and it lowers the Systemic Thinking score.
- **PepsiCo still publishes detailed ESG data.** From 2026 the company uses its ESG Topics A–Z resource as the primary source of progress reporting, and reports regenerative agriculture practices across 3.5 million acres in 2024 against a 10 million acre goal for 2030.
- **Standing context (2024, outside the window):** the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission sued PepsiCo over the failure to accommodate and the firing of a blind call-centre employee, a case the company later settled. It is background, not a within-window finding.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Awareness (4.0) and Action (4.0).** PepsiCo maintains multiple formal reporting channels, publishes disaggregated ESG data across agriculture, water, packaging and climate, and operates structured programmes with measurable targets and published progress.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (3.0).** PepsiCo publishes goals but little evidence that resources demonstrably flow to the highest-need populations in its supply chain, and no independent audit of distributional outcomes. **Empathy (3.4), Accountability (3.4) and Integrity (3.4)** follow, weighed down by the pattern of employment claims and by the net-zero deferral.

## Evidence Gaps

- PepsiCo’s answer to the 24 July 2026 complaint is not yet on the docket, so the allegations cannot be weighed against a company account.
- No independent audit of PepsiCo’s internal harassment-handling process is public, so E4 Validation and AB1 Harm Acknowledgment rest on litigation records and company policy statements.
- Supply-chain worker outcome data is reported at programme level rather than disaggregated by population, capping EQ3 at 3.
