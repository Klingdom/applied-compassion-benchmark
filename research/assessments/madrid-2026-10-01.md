## Madrid's city hall helped get an evicted 87-year-old home within a week. Its first real score is still well below the placeholder.

Madrid's housing protests in late September 2026 were aimed mainly at the national and regional governments. This assessment scores only the Ayuntamiento de Madrid (city council). National decrees and regional conduct are excluded, as in the Valencia assessment of the same date.

The city's in-window conduct was good. On 23 September 2026 Mayor José Luis Martínez-Almeida offered the evicted woman, Maricarmen, "un recurso habitacional de manera inmediata" (an immediate housing place) and listed the social services already supporting her: telecare, 90 hours of home help and SAMUR Social (Madrid Actual, 2026-09-23). The municipal housing company EMVS then mediated an agreement that let her return home (El Correo Gallego, 2026-09-29). These are credited.

The city also measures homelessness every year. Its 2025 count found 1,015 people sleeping rough, down 9% from 1,116 in 2024, and it is expanding shelter places from 1,210 to 1,428 in 2026 under the Dignitas strategy (2022-2027).

Against that: about 4,500 people, at least 1,800 of them children, in sectors 5 and 6 of the Cañada Real settlement have lived without electricity since October 2020. The European Committee of Social Rights found in 2025 that Spain violated the European Social Charter. The decision is against Spain; responsibility for Cañada Real is shared between the state, the Comunidad de Madrid and the city under a 2017 regional pact, so it is scored against the city with low confidence and only in Equity and Accountability.

The published 65.6 was never assessed. Measured: 48.1 (Functional). Filed on both triggers, confidence low.
