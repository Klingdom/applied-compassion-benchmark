## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the scanner reported no evidence for Citigroup, and there was some. On 24 July 2026 a New York federal judge ruled that two former Citibank employees must take their proposed class and collective claims to arbitration, on allegations that the bank underpaid workers by requiring inaccurate time reporting.
- The ruling does not decide whether the underpayment happened. It decides where the question will be answered, and arbitration means the answer will not be public.
- That matters for two subdimensions. B5 consent orientation, because an arbitration clause limits the forum in which consent can be contested; and AB5 reparative action, because a private forum produces no published remedy.
- The standing context is a multi-year workforce reduction. Public records show five WARN notices across two states in 2026 affecting 384 workers, following disclosed cuts of over 300 New York roles in May 2026 and 265 at the Tribeca headquarters in March 2026.
- Citigroup confirms at an assessed 60.0 against a published 62.5, a sub-threshold difference of -2.5.
