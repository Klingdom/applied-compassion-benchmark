## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Pakistan on a Committee to Protect Journalists report that journalists Razi Tahir and Muhammad Saif were seized from their Islamabad office on 1 August 2026 and taken to an undisclosed location, amid a crackdown on reporting about Pakistan-administered Kashmir. Pakistan was last assessed on 2026-08-11 and is published at 17.2 of 100.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Date correction.** The scanner dated the CPJ report loosely to "August 2026". The precise dates matter: the two journalists were **seized on 1 August 2026** by men in police uniform; **CPJ issued its call on 5 August 2026**. The conduct date is 1 August 2026 and the reporting date is 5 August 2026. Both are inside the 14-day window.

**ATTRIBUTION.** The seizure was carried out by men in police uniform in Islamabad, and Islamabad police have not acknowledged the detention. This is Pakistani state conduct, or conduct by persons the state has failed to identify or disavow, and is scored against Pakistan. The Kashmir protests themselves are civilian conduct and are not scored. Harm caused in Indian-administered Kashmir is not attributed to Pakistan.

**Baseline provenance.** Pakistan's published 17.2 was applied on **2026-05-14** following a band crossing from Developing to Critical, on Pakistan's own documented conduct (HRCP 2025 documentation, 531,700 Afghan refugees expelled, religious-minority killings). It is a measured, entity-specific baseline, not a placeholder. A separate 2026-05-21 proposal that referenced a pre-May-12 baseline of 22.7 was correctly held and never applied.

**Year check.** CPJ URL path carries /2026/08/. IANS carries a 20260804 timestamp. Explicit 2026. No year-confusion found.

## Key Findings

- Pakistan holds at about 17 of 100. The assessment lands 0.9 points below the published score, well within the confirmation range.
- Journalists Razi Tahir and Muhammad Saif were taken from their Islamabad office by men in police uniform on 1 August 2026 and moved to an undisclosed location. Islamabad police have not acknowledged the detention and the two men remain unaccounted for.
- Both had been reporting on unrest in Pakistan-administered Kashmir, where authorities suspended internet services and imposed severe press restrictions.
- Pakistan's score is not a placeholder. It was set on 14 May 2026 when the country crossed from Developing to Critical on its own documented conduct, including the expulsion of 531,700 Afghan refugees.
- Pakistan still operates real social-protection machinery and a functioning if pressured court system — Tahir was granted bail in July after an earlier detention. That is why the score sits above the floor rather than at it.
