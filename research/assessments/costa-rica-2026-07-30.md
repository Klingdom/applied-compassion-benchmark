## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: nothing has been passed yet.** Costa Rica’s president delivered six security bills on 27 July 2026, but the headline measure — a constitutional amendment on extraditing Costa Rican citizens — was not filed and is due on 3 August 2026. This assessment does not score bills that do not exist yet.
- **Why it matters: one of the six bills would remove sentence benefits.** The package includes a proposal that people classified as dangerous criminals serve their full prison sentences without benefits. That is the element most likely to matter for this benchmark if it becomes law.
- **Costa Rica has allowed extradition of its own nationals only since 2025**, and only for international drug trafficking and terrorism. The proposal would widen that list to five further offences.
- **Standing context (2025-2026, outside the window): the migrant record is Costa Rica’s weakest area.** Human Rights Watch called on Costa Rica in March 2025 to allow migrants deported from the United States to seek asylum. The country’s National Torture Prevention Mechanism found migrants at the CATEM facility were effectively held against their will and unable to leave.
- **Costa Rica ranks 11th of 193 countries at 81.4 out of 100 — Exemplary.** The score is driven by consistently strong performance across seven of eight dimensions, with Equity the single exception.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Awareness, Empathy, Action, Boundaries, Accountability, Systemic Thinking and Integrity, all at 4.0.** Costa Rica maintains formal detection and response pathways, publishes performance data including failures, and sustains long-horizon commitments — most famously the abolition of its army and the redirection of that spending to health and education.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (3.4).** The gap is concentrated on migrants and deportees. Detention conditions at CATEM have been criticised by the country’s own torture prevention mechanism and by the Jesuit Migrant Service, humanitarian permits carry no work rights, and prison overcrowding contributes to health problems and inadequate access to health services.

## Evidence Gaps

- The text of the six security bills delivered on 27 July 2026 is not yet public in full, so their compassion-relevant provisions cannot be scored in detail.
- No 2026 published data on prison population against capacity was located, so EQ2 rests on older State Department reporting.
- No independent audit of CATEM conditions after the April 2026 grant of temporary humanitarian status was found, capping EQ4 and B4.
