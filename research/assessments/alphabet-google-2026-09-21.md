## Why this entity was assessed

Alphabet/Google, through Google DeepMind, is a named defendant in the federal antitrust suit filed in the Northern District of California on 19 September 2026 alongside Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceXAI. Alphabet was not part of the 2026-09-20 screening that produced zero movement for the other three. It is screened here for consistency. Alphabet was last assessed on 2026-08-11.

## The precedent, read first

The 2026-09-20 Anthropic screening note was read before anything was scored. It produced zero movement on three grounds: a filed complaint is a procedural event, not a conduct finding; the alleged conduct is a safety commitment, which does not map onto any of the 40 behavioural anchors as a harm; and the direction is genuinely ambiguous, so forcing it either way is indefensible.

**Alphabet's position on this event does not differ materially, and so the same outcome follows.** The reasoning is mirrored, not re-derived.

## Applying the same screen to Alphabet

**Same complaint, same four defendants, same procedural status.** The suit was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California on Friday, 19 September 2026 by four named individuals who pay for ChatGPT, Claude, Grok or Gemini subscriptions, on behalf of a proposed nationwide class. It claims the companies "violated antitrust laws when they agreed to coordinate slowdown efforts". Nothing has been decided. "Representatives for Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and SpaceXAI did not immediately respond to a request for comment Saturday."

**Same challenged conduct: a safety commitment.** The trigger was Dario Amodei's 12 September 2026 essay "We Must Pace the Frontier". Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis called the proposal "the right path forward", according to the complaint. A claim that promising to slow capability gains for safety reasons reduced the value of a paid subscription does not describe a failure to recognise, respond to or reduce suffering. It does not reach any of the 40 anchors as a harm.

**Three differences from Alphabet's position were checked, and none changes the outcome:**

1. **Hassabis's endorsement was softer than the other labs' commitments.** Reporting describes his support for pacing as related and softer rather than an explicit commitment. If anything that weakens the complaint against Alphabet. It does not create a harm.
2. **Alphabet has separate in-window conduct: the DeepMind Institute launch on 17 September 2026.** Google and Google DeepMind researchers launched it "to advance the conversation around artificial general intelligence", aiming "to surface differing views between Google, Google DeepMind, and the broader global research community around AGI", with Shane Legg, James Manyika and Demis Hassabis as directors. This is genuinely relevant to S4 Structural Critique and A3 Blind Spot Mitigation. **It is not scored.** It is a launch announcement with no track record, no disclosed funding and no disclosed independence arrangement — exactly the reason Anthropic's pacing essay was recorded as a watch item rather than banked on 2026-09-20. Treating a same-week institute launch as evidence of practice would be a reputational-announcement credit, which I2 Non-Performance exists to resist.
3. **Alphabet has a live, unrelated AI-safety-pledge dispute — and it is out of window.** A cross-party group of 60 UK parliamentarians accused Google DeepMind of breaking the Frontier AI Safety Commitments over a delayed Gemini 2.5 Pro safety report. That open letter was released on 29 August **2025**, about a model released in March 2025. It is a year outside this window and is not new evidence. It is noted here only so that a later cycle does not mistake it for a 2026 event.

## Why nothing moves

1. **Procedural event.** The 19 September filing scores nothing.
2. **Directionality.** No downward move on an undecided complaint; no upward move on the absence of harm, and none on an institute launched four days before this assessment.
3. **No double-count.** The 2026-08-11 vector is carried, not re-credited.
4. **Consistency.** Moving Alphabet on this event when Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI were held on the same event on 2026-09-20 would make the benchmark's treatment of a single lawsuit depend on which night the entity happened to be flagged.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: four subscribers are suing Google, Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceXAI for promising to slow AI down. The case was filed on 19 September 2026 in a San Francisco federal court. It says the promise cut the value of their paid subscriptions.
- Why it matters: the benchmark has nothing to say about it, and that is the right answer. Nothing has been decided in court, and the behaviour complained of is a safety promise, not a harm to anyone.
- Why it matters: the same lawsuit moved nothing for Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI five days ago. Moving Google alone would mean the benchmark's verdict depends on which night a company gets flagged. So Google's score stays at 40.0 out of 100.
- Why it matters: Google DeepMind did do something new this week. On 17 September 2026 it launched the DeepMind Institute to air disagreements about artificial general intelligence. It is four days old, with no published funding or independence terms, so it earns a watch flag and no points.
- Why it matters: Google sits exactly on a band line. At 40.0 out of 100 it is labelled Developing; at 40.1 it would be labelled Functional. A one-tenth-of-a-point move changes the public label, so this boundary stays under watch.
