## Cornell answered a gang-rape lawsuit with privacy rules and a task force, while the state reopened the case

A former student sued Cornell University on 14 September 2026. She alleges seven students sexually assaulted her at the Chi Phi fraternity house in 2024 and that Cornell failed to protect her and mishandled discipline. The Tompkins County District Attorney reopened the criminal investigation, and the New York Attorney General announced a review of Cornell's initial response (Boston Globe, 2026-09-30).

Cornell's 21 September statement says the fraternity chapter is barred, points to a March 2026 task force on campus sexual assault, and says it will respond "through the legal process". It does not acknowledge any failure.

This is Cornell's first evidence-based score. The published 54.7 was never assessed (rotation-state last_assessed: null). Cornell is strong on need-based aid: families earning up to $75,000 pay nothing toward the full cost of attendance (Cornell Chronicle, 2026-03-23). It is weaker on treatment of protest and staff. Cornell's own Faculty Senate said in November 2025 that temporary suspensions were used to "deter, retaliate, or compel", and about 85 percent of suspended students were protesters. Cornell also paid $60 million to settle a federal funding freeze in November 2025 without admitting wrongdoing.

Measured: 41.9 (Functional), down 12.8 from 54.7. Filed on the magnitude trigger.
