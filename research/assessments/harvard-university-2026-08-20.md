## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**ATTRIBUTION — neither flagged item is Harvard conduct.**

1. **The court dismissal is a federal court act, not a Harvard act.** The 2026-08-18 assessment already made this correction and scored the ruling only for what it evidences about Harvard's own choice to litigate rather than settle. **No new credit is taken here.** Judge Richard G. Stearns found the alleged discrimination "too isolated and episodic" to support a Title VI violation. That is a judicial finding about the government's case, not a compassion finding about Harvard.
2. **The DOJ whistleblower disclosure is a Justice Department matter, not a Harvard act.** Haley Van Erem, a longtime lawyer in the DOJ Civil Rights Division, alleges that "the outcomes of the investigations were predetermined, without regard to the evidence." That is an allegation about federal conduct. It is scored here **only** for the single Harvard-relevant fact it contains: that a DOJ attorney warned internally that the proposed settlement demands on Harvard "were unrelated to remedying antisemitism and likely violated the First Amendment" — which bears on whether Harvard's refusal to settle was reasonable. It marginally corroborates the Integrity finding the 08-18 assessment already made.

The **whistleblower disclosure is dated 2026-08-17** — the House Judiciary Committee letter transmitting it carries "August 17, 2026" in its filename and text. **MIXED DATED/UNDATED SOURCING RESOLVED (gate warning 2).** The one undated scanner source (Higher Ed Dive) is excluded from load-bearing use. Corroboration comes from the primary congressional disclosure document plus two dated outlets.

**NEW HARVARD OWN-CONDUCT EVIDENCE THIS CYCLE.** The one genuinely new own-conduct item is the completion of the Faculty of Arts and Sciences restructuring: **165 staff positions eliminated** across the division against a **$365 million budget shortfall**, with layoffs beginning in late July 2026 and the reallocation of responsibilities due to complete by mid-October 2026. ## Key Findings

- Harvard holds at 53.8 of 100 against a published 52.3 — a confirmation, unchanged from the assessment two days earlier.
- Neither headline event this cycle is something Harvard did. A federal judge dismissed the government's lawsuit, and a Justice Department lawyer blew the whistle on her own agency. The benchmark scores institutions on their own conduct.
- The whistleblower disclosure does add one Harvard-relevant fact: a DOJ attorney warned internally that the settlement terms pressed on Harvard were unrelated to fixing antisemitism and probably unconstitutional. That supports Harvard's decision to fight rather than settle, where Brown and Columbia settled.
- The real Harvard conduct this cycle is internal. The Faculty of Arts and Sciences eliminated 165 staff positions against a $365 million shortfall, with the restructuring due to finish by mid-October 2026. Harvard's endowment is about $57 billion.
- Harvard's weakest area remains internal consistency: strong external commitments alongside job cuts and graduate workers on about $26,000 a year whose pay the university treats as a financial-aid question it will not bargain over.
