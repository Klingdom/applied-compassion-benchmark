# Screening Note: Kinshasa

**Assessment date:** 2026-09-24
**Outcome:** screened, not assessed. No proposal on the Kinshasa row.
**Published:** 7.8/100, Critical, rank #232 of 250 global cities. Canonical reconstruction returns 7.8 exactly — no math-hygiene issue.

## The double-count risk, addressed first, and what differs

DR Congo was assessed on 2026-09-17 on this exact crackdown. `research/assessments/democratic-republic-of-c-2026-09-17.md` measured DR Congo at 3.1 against a published 2.3 and confirmed the score. It established that the protests occurred on **Tuesday 15 September 2026** (the EWN URL date of 16 September is the AFP wire's publication date, not the event date), scored the tear gas, warning shots and arrests, recorded that the police commander denied arresting peaceful protesters while opposition leaders called the arrests arbitrary, and found the score floor-limited: the crackdown attaches to subdimensions already at 1 (E4, B5, AB1, I4), so nothing could move down.

**What differs between the two rows: nothing.** This is the central point of this note.

The force that fired tear gas is the **Police Nationale Congolaise**, a single national force. The arrests were made "nationwide", not by a city authority. The constitutional-revision proposal that triggered the protests is President Félix Tshisekedi's, and the opposition C64 coalition called a national day of action, of which Kinshasa was one venue among several cities.

The Ville-Province de Kinshasa has a governor and a provincial assembly, but no command over the national police and no role in the constitutional question. **No conduct by the Kinshasa city-province authority was located in this window, in either direction.**

## Why scoring this on both rows would be a double count

Scoring the same tear gas, the same arrests and the same constitutional proposal against both the DR Congo row and the Kinshasa row would record one government's conduct twice in the index, and would move a city score on facts that contain no city decision. The benchmark would then report two independent findings where there is one event.

The honest statement is the one this note makes: **the crackdown is already scored, once, on the national row, where the conduct belongs.**

## Floor limitation on this row as well

Kinshasa is published at 7.8, which is 7.8 points above zero, with Boundaries, Accountability and Integrity already at 1.0. A protest crackdown attaches to exactly those dimensions. Even if attribution were clean, there is almost nowhere for the score to go.

## Why no full assessment was run

Kinshasa has never been individually assessed. A first full assessment would be valuable — the city's own functions (water, sanitation, flood response, waste, street children) are a genuine and separate subject, and its 7.8 is among the lowest in the global-cities index. That assessment should be built on municipal evidence, not on a national crackdown. `last_assessed` is not stamped.

## Trigger that would change this

Conduct by the Kinshasa provincial government — a municipal police action, a curfew, a service withdrawal, or a response to injured or detained residents — would be city conduct and assessable on this row.
