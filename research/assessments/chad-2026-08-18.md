## Why this entity was assessed

Chad was assessed on 2026-08-13 in the Sahel calibration study at 10.6 (delta -0.3 against the published 10.9; no proposal filed). The scanner surfaced a genuinely new event that post-dates that assessment: on 2026-08-16 President Mahamat Idriss Déby pardoned opposition leader and former prime minister Succès Masra, who was serving a 20-year sentence after a trial Human Rights Watch called politically motivated. Per the cycle brief, this is a **targeted re-score of the Accountability dimension only**, not a re-derivation of the 08-13 assessment.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Dates verified.** The pardon decree was published Friday 14 August 2026 and Masra was released late that evening; TimesLIVE and Sowetan carry 2026-08-15, Africanews carries the reconciliation speech on 2026-08-16. All are inside the 2026-08-04 to 2026-08-18 window and all post-date the 2026-08-13 assessment. The scanner's `evidence_date: 2026-08-16` is the speech date; the **conduct date is 2026-08-14** (decree) with release the same evening.

**Directionality.** A pardon and release is potentially **positive** conduct. It was assessed on its merits, not assumed to be a downgrade.

**Mixed dated/undated sourcing.** The scanner listed one undated source (bssnews.net). It was disregarded. Two independently dated sources (Africanews, TimesLIVE) carry the finding.

**Scope note.** Only Accountability was re-scored. The other 35 subdimensions are carried forward unchanged from `research/assessments/chad-2026-08-13.md` and are reproduced here for completeness. The subdimension sidecar therefore contains **only the five Accountability subdimensions**, per the partial-assessment rule.

### Analysis

One subdimension moves. AB2 (Correction Willingness) rises from 1 to 2.

The pardon is real and it is the government's own act. It freed nine jailed opposition figures, not one, and it came four days after the president publicly promised releases to foster reconciliation. Under the AB2 anchors that is "correction eventually, under pressure, minimal" — anchor 2 — because it followed sustained international criticism, including Human Rights Watch's finding that the trial was politically motivated.

It does not reach anchor 3 ("at least one significant course correction based on harm evidence") for a specific reason. The decree **cancels the sentences but does not quash the convictions**. Masra and the others keep criminal records and remain barred from standing for office. The state has not accepted that the prosecutions were wrong; it has exercised mercy while preserving the legal instrument that removed its rivals from electoral competition.

AB1 (Harm Acknowledgment) therefore stays at 1: no harm has been acknowledged. AB3, AB4 and AB5 stay at 1: no transparency, systemic learning or reparative action follows the pardon.

Accountability moves from 1.00 to 1.20. The composite moves from 10.6 to 11.2.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Chad's government released its jailed main rival, and that is a genuine step — but it stopped halfway. On 14 August 2026 President Mahamat Idriss Déby pardoned Succès Masra and eight other jailed opposition figures.
- The pardon cancels the prison sentences but leaves the convictions standing. Masra keeps a criminal record and cannot run for office. Mercy was granted; the finding of guilt was not withdrawn.
- Masra's response was conciliatory. He told supporters: "We need forgiveness, unity and peace."
- Chad hosts about 2.28 million forcibly displaced people, including more than 1.33 million Sudanese refugees — an enormous humanitarian load carried mostly on donor funding.
- The score moves 0.3 points, from 10.9 to 11.2. Chad remains in the Critical band, which starts at 0 and ends at 20.

## Strongest Dimensions

Action (1.80 of 5) and Systemic Thinking (1.80 of 5), carried forward from the 2026-08-13 assessment. Chad's willingness to keep its border open to Sudanese refugees at very large scale is the single clearest positive in its record.

## Weakest Dimensions

Accountability (1.20 of 5), still the lowest dimension even after the pardon. Four of its five subdimensions remain at the floor anchor of 1: no harm acknowledgment, no transparency, no systemic learning, no reparative action.

## Evidence Gaps

Chad publishes almost no institutional performance data, so 35 of 40 subdimensions rest on external human-rights reporting rather than disclosure. Where disclosure is simply absent, the low anchor was applied; this is an absence of disclosure and is not treated as evidence of harm. Whether the pardon is followed by any restoration of political rights is unknown and is the decisive open question.
