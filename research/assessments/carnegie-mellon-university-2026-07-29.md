## Assessment Context

- **In-window event.** Between 13 and 29 July 2026 Carnegie Mellon expanded Title VI/IEX investigations of students who painted pro-Palestinian messages on the Fence — the university's own century-old designated free-expression structure — from 8 individuals to 10, and increased interim partial campus-access restrictions from 7 to 9. One student faces possible expulsion.
- **The university's own framing.** CMU states the messages constituted 'discriminatory harassment on the basis of race, national origin, and/or religion' and that they 'crossed the bounds of our community norms'. Some of the messages at issue were painted by Jewish students criticising Zionism.
- **Second in-window item.** On 28 July 2026 CMU revised its discrimination policy and code of business ethics following a 'C' rating from an external advocacy organisation, and characterised a prohibition on anti-Israel conduct that appeared in the revised text as a typographical error.
- **Response.** Nine advocacy organisations including Jewish Voice for Peace and Palestine Legal issued a joint condemnation on 28 July 2026, and an Academic Workers United petition had passed 400 signatures by 29 July 2026.
- **Sourcing limitation, stated plainly.** All sourcing is tier 2 to 3: independent student journalism quoting the university directly, and advocacy organisations that are parties to the dispute. There is no tier 4 or 5 source. No band crossing results from this assessment, so the ≥tier-4 requirement for band crossings is not engaged, but confidence is capped at medium for this reason.

## Key Findings

- Carnegie Mellon expanded Title VI investigations of Fence painters from 8 to 10 people and interim campus-access restrictions from 7 to 9 between 13 and 29 July 2026, with one student facing possible expulsion.
- The Fence is CMU's own designated free-expression structure. The university's stated basis for sanction is that painting on it constituted 'discriminatory harassment on the basis of race, national origin, and/or religion'.
- Some of the sanctioned messages were painted by Jewish students criticising Zionism, which is why the Empathy non-judgment subdimension scores 1/5.
- On 28 July 2026 CMU revised its discrimination policy and code of business ethics after a 'C' rating from an external advocacy organisation, and described a prohibition on anti-Israel conduct in the revised text as a typographical error.
- Nine advocacy organisations condemned the actions on 28 July 2026 and an Academic Workers United petition had passed 400 signatures by 29 July 2026 — the internal contest is documented, which is why Integrity internal consistency stays at 3 rather than falling further.

## Strongest Dimensions

## Weakest Dimensions

- Empathy (2.4), Accountability (2.4) and Integrity (2.4) — viewpoint-differentiated treatment on a designated free-expression space, expansion rather than correction after external condemnation, and no acknowledgment or repair toward the restricted students.
