## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Guinea abolished most of its opposition in one night. On 7 March 2026 the government dissolved 40 political parties, including the three main opposition groups, by late-night decree.
- Why it matters: two men have been missing for two years and no one has answered for it. Amnesty International reported in July 2026 that abductions and disappearances continue with impunity, two years after activists Oumar Sylla and Mamadou Billo Bah vanished.
- Why it matters: Guinea did hold a landmark trial, then undid much of it. A court convicted Moussa Dadis Camara of crimes against humanity for the 2009 stadium massacre on 31 July 2024 and ordered reparations. He was pardoned on 28 March 2025, which FIDH called an affront to the victims.
- Why it matters: there is real money now and a real plan for it. Guinea raised its 2026 budget 18.3 per cent and announced a roughly US$1 billion sovereign wealth fund to channel mining revenue into education and agriculture. That lifts Systemic Thinking well above the rest of Guinea's profile.
- Why it matters: the election did not settle the transition. Mamady Doumbouya won 86.7 per cent in December 2025 after running in violation of the transition charter he was meant to uphold, and the UN said the campaign was marred by intimidation.

## Strongest Dimensions

Systemic Thinking (2.6). Guinea has a funded, published, fifteen-year plan to convert mining revenue into education, agriculture and industry, and it carried a crimes-against-humanity trial of a former head of state through to conviction.

## Weakest Dimensions

Boundaries (1.2) and Empathy (1.2). The state dissolved 40 political parties, broke its own transition charter, and disappeared two activists who remain missing after two years.

## Evidence Gaps

- A reported suspension of three opposition parties dated 23 August was checked and excluded. It matches the confirmed Human Rights Watch World Report stale-year pattern, where a month reference in a report published in 2026 refers to calendar year 2025.
- No outcome data on the additional health and education allocations was located, so Action is scored on the allocation itself rather than on effect.
- Whether the court-ordered reparations from the 2009 massacre trial have actually been paid could not be established, so Reparative Action is capped at the gesture anchor.

## Attribution Discipline

Only the conduct of the Guinea state is scored. Harm by non-state armed groups, by foreign states, and by natural disaster is excluded from the state's score. Where such harm occurred, what is measured is the state's own response to it. Every source date was checked against its event date, and publication dates were not treated as event dates.
