## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Uzbekistan's placeholder was too low. It assesses at 27.5 against a published 20.3, a gain of 7.2 points, and stays in the Developing band. The gain comes from measurable social protection machinery and two real corrections.
- Why it matters: aid now reaches four times as many families as it did in 2017. The National Agency for Social Protection and Inson service centres were delivering to roughly 2.3 million needy families by 2023, administered through a Single Registry built with the World Bank and UNICEF.
- Why it matters: the state dismantled its own forced labour system. Human Rights Watch confirms the government has ended state-imposed forced labour of cotton pickers, though risks remain for cotton and wheat farmers.
- Why it matters: police who tortured people went to prison. A Tashkent court convicted three officers in May 2025 and sentenced one to 10 years, which is unusually severe for the region.
- Why it matters: the worst harms remain unexamined. There has never been an independent investigation into the 2005 Andijan massacre, accountability for the July 2022 deaths in Karakalpakstan is still denied, and a blogger remains in psychiatric detention the UN has ruled arbitrary.

## Strongest Dimensions

Systemic Thinking (2.8) and Action (2.6). Uzbekistan has a national target to halve poverty, a digital registry that removes access barriers, a four-fold expansion in families reached since 2017, and it ended its own state-imposed forced labour system.

## Weakest Dimensions

Empathy (1.4) and Boundaries (1.6). Same-sex conduct is criminal with at least 16 prosecutions in 2025, domestic violence cases are still pushed into reconciliation, shelters and legal aid are restricted, and a blogger remains in arbitrary psychiatric detention.

## Evidence Gaps

- Poverty coverage figures date to 2023 and 2024. Whether the 2026 halving target was met could not be established, so Efficacy rests on programme modification rather than on the target outcome.
- No independent evaluation of Inson service centre outcomes was located.
- Uzbek government statements on Karakalpakstan were not located, so Accountability is scored on the documented denial reported by Human Rights Watch.

## Attribution Discipline

Only the conduct of the Uzbekistan state is scored. Harm by non-state armed groups, by foreign states, and by natural disaster is excluded from the state's score. Where such harm occurred, what is measured is the state's own response to it. Every source date was checked against its event date, and publication dates were not treated as event dates.
