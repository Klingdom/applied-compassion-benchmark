## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Austria removed more people than it accepted, and the government called that a success. Austria carried out more than 7,000 deportations and removals in the first half of 2026 while new asylum claims fell 44 percent to about 4,900, reported on 28 July 2026 as the first such reversal in more than 20 years.
- The family-reunification suspension is the main driver. Arrivals through that route fell from an estimated 6,000 in the first half of 2024 to about 50 in the first half of 2026.
- That is family separation as policy, and it falls on recognised refugees, mostly from Syria and Afghanistan. It is scored on Equity alone, which is the dimension the evidence speaks to.
- A claim about Austria's first deportation of a Syrian man and a United Nations inquiry into his disappearance was DROPPED. Verification places that deportation on 3 July 2025 and the UN letter on 6 August 2025, not 2026. It is a stale-year trap.
- Assessed composite 81.8 against a published 83.0. Delta -1.2, below the 5-point proposal threshold. Austria stays in the Exemplary band.
