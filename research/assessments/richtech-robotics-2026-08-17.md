### Score Difference Analysis

Richtech deploys robots into senior living homes — a setting where the people served are among the most vulnerable in this entire study — and publishes nothing at all about their experience. No resident consultation, no complaint pathway, no outcome measurement and no accessibility work was located. What Richtech does publish, it publishes because the Securities and Exchange Commission and Nasdaq compel it, and in 2026 that disclosure failed: two fiscal years of audited accounts cannot be relied upon, a material weakness the company said it had fixed had not been fixed, and Nasdaq found the company out of compliance with its filing rule. The published Accountability score of 2.5 is not supportable against that record. The fall of 11.5 points is modest because the seed was already low.

### Recommendation

The published score is overstated and should be revised down to 24.4. The band does not change, but the composite delta of -11.5 clears the filing threshold. The Accountability finding here is a genuine conduct finding, not an absence-of-disclosure correction.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: Richtech Robotics puts robots in senior living homes and publishes nothing about how residents experience them.** No resident consultation, complaint pathway or outcome measurement was located.
- **Two fiscal years of Richtech's audited accounts cannot be relied upon.** Its Audit Committee reached that conclusion on 9 June 2026 and the company is restating fiscal 2024 and 2025.
- **A control failure the company said it had fixed had not been fixed.** The material weakness identified as at 30 September 2025 was not remediated as previously indicated, and a further weakness is expected.
- **Nasdaq found Richtech out of compliance on 22 May 2026** for failing to file its quarterly report on time. The company submitted a compliance plan on 20 July 2026.
- **Richtech wrote off $9.8 million of software in one quarter** while reporting about $3.9 million of revenue for the nine months to 30 June 2026.

## Scoring Conventions Applied

- **Absence of disclosure scores 2, never 1.** The floor of 1 is used only where positive documented evidence of a specific failure exists. This is the "2-convention", the more conservative of the two conventions live in this repository.
- **A score of 4 requires published institutional data, a regulatory decision or third-party peer review. A score of 5 requires independent audit plus community testimony.**
