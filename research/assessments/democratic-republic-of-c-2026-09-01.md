## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged the Democratic Republic of Congo on the 21 August 2026 agreement between the government and the M23-led alliance on a roadmap for peace talks, alongside continued fighting in South Kivu — a mixed picture the scanner itself described as "government-backed diplomatic progress alongside unresolved active conflict." The DRC was last assessed on 2026-08-20 (assessed 2.5) and sits near the floor at a published 2.3 of 100.

## Evidence-date, lifecycle and attribution checks

**DATE MISMATCH RESOLVED (gate warning 3, DRC, 2 days).** The scanner recorded `evidence_date: 2026-08-21` while the Al Jazeera URL carries `/2026/8/23/`. No conflict. **The talks ran 17-21 August 2026 in Switzerland and the roadmap was announced on 22 August; Al Jazeera published on 23 August.** The scanner's evidence_date is the negotiation-conclusion date; the URL date is the publication date. Both are correct. No year-confusion.

**ATTRIBUTION — worked in both directions, which is the whole point on this entity.**

- **A negative allegation against the state was found and is NOT scored.** Al Jazeera reports that "An M23 spokesman alleged DRC forces conducted drone strikes on August 22 in Minembwe, claiming civilian casualties and significant damage to civilian infrastructure." **Al Jazeera states it could not verify this claim.** It comes from a party to the conflict, about its adversary, and is uncorroborated. **It does not score.** Allowing an armed group's unverified allegation to move a state's score would be the mirror image of the error corrected on Sudan.
- **Positive state conduct was found and IS scored.** The DRC government sustained five days of negotiations and agreed "a roadmap setting out sequenced steps and timelines for negotiations", a standardised method for reporting ceasefire violations, and a mechanism to address implementation problems. That is the state's own conduct and it is credited under Integrity and Systemic Thinking.
- **The Ebola epidemic is not state conduct.** What is scored is the state's detection and response. Both are visible: the government declared the outbreak on 15 May 2026 and publishes case and death figures, including unflattering ones — which is credited. The response's failure is also documented: the World Health Organization warned the outbreak is "outpacing the response".

**MATERIAL EVIDENCE THE SCAN DID NOT SURFACE.** The scanner's DRC summary is entirely about M23 peace talks. It does not mention that during the same window, **the DRC's Ebola outbreak became the deadliest in the country's history.** UN News (17 August 2026) reports 2,325 deaths against 4,945 confirmed cases and a 46 percent fatality rate, "surpassing the 2,299 deaths recorded in the DRC's 2018-2020 outbreak". This is a tier-4 source and it is the most consequential DRC evidence in the period. It is incorporated here.

## Key Findings

- The DRC's Ebola outbreak became the deadliest in the country's history during the assessed window. UN News reported on 17 August 2026 that it had killed 2,325 people, passing the 2,299 deaths of the 2018-2020 outbreak. The scan did not surface this at all; it was found in assessment.
- The World Health Organization said the outbreak is "outpacing the response." With 4,945 confirmed cases and a 46 percent fatality rate, this is the clearest single measure of state capacity in the period.
- The DRC government did something genuinely constructive: it sustained five days of talks and agreed a roadmap with the M23 alliance, including a standard way of reporting ceasefire violations. That is credited.
- An M23 spokesman accused DRC forces of drone strikes on Minembwe on 22 August, claiming civilian casualties. Al Jazeera could not verify it. The benchmark does not score an armed group's unverified accusation against its adversary, and it did not here.
- The DRC scores 3.1 of 100 and has 2.3 points of downward room against a 5.0-point filing trigger. Less than half the movement needed to file a change is arithmetically available.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Systemic Thinking (1.2)** and **Integrity (1.2)**, both marginally above the floor and both for the same reason: the government stayed at the negotiating table for five days and came away with a sequenced roadmap and a ceasefire-violation reporting standard, while fighting continued on the ground. ## Weakest Dimensions

**Action, Empathy, Equity and Boundaries, all at 1.0.** The WHO's finding that the epidemic response is being outpaced is the decisive evidence. A 46 percent fatality rate across 4,945 cases is not a system with response standards, proportionality or follow-through; it is a system being overwhelmed.

## Evidence Gaps

- The M23 allegation of DRC drone strikes on Minembwe could not be verified by Al Jazeera and is not scored. If it were independently established it would materially change the Integrity and Accountability findings.
- Death and case figures differ between sources and dates (2,184 per WHO at 12 August; 2,325 per UN News at 17 August; approximately 2,911 per government figures reported by Reuters at end-August). The UN News figure is used because it is tier 4 and explicitly dated.
- No evidence was located on the DRC government's own account of its epidemic response, so Accountability rests on the fact of publication rather than on its content.
- The DRC is near the floor, so no evidence gathered could produce a filable downward movement.

## Disclosure — what this score measures

This score rests on documented evidence of specific failure, not on absence of disclosure. **0 of 40 subdimensions are scored on absence.**

Two explicit exclusions. The epidemic itself is **not** scored as state misconduct — a filovirus outbreak is not something a government does. Only detection and response are scored. And the M23 allegation of state drone strikes on civilians is **not** scored, because the reporting organisation could not verify it.

The government's publication of unflattering epidemic figures is credited **upward**, not treated as an admission of fault. Transparency about a failing response is better than silence about one.

## Recommendation
