Shanghai Jiao Tong University had already confirmed its investigation by 2026-07-27 (Hong Kong Free Press). The finding is still assessable because it has never been scored, but the scanner's characterisation of the disclosure date is wrong and is corrected here.
- **Sourcing condition: CLEARED.** The scan gate required a second dated source because one cited URL (scmp.com) was undated. Four independently dated sources corroborate the substance: Retraction Watch 2026-07-23 and 2026-07-30, Hong Kong Free Press 2026-07-27, and CNN 2026-07-30. Nature's editor's note is a tier-5 primary institutional document. The undated Science and SCMP URLs are used for claims only, never for quotes.
- **Section 1286 framing NOT re-imported.** The 2026-08-01 cycle declared SJTU's appearance on the US Department of Defense Section 1286 list an attribution false positive: a foreign government's funding-risk decision describes that government, not SJTU's conduct. That framing plays no part in tonight's scores. Tonight's evidence is SJTU's own conduct toward a patient and a scientific journal.

## Key Findings

- A university concealed a child's death in one of its own experiments for about 17 months. A six-year-old girl died in March 2025, seven days after a Shanghai Jiao Tong University team infused an experimental gene-editing therapy into her spinal fluid. The death only became public on 23 July 2026, through an outside investigation by Science and Retraction Watch.
- The scientific paper left her out. Retraction Watch reports the team's Nature paper "did not disclose the girl's death or the family's financial contribution." Nature has since attached a note saying "concerns have been raised regarding this article, including with the data presented."
- The family paid about $860,000 toward the treatment they hoped would save their daughter. That payment funded preclinical work, and reporting describes informal payments to members of the research team. When a family funds the study its child joins, informed consent is compromised at the root.
- The trial was approved before the animal safety results were final. All four monkeys given the therapy in the preclinical study developed moderate to severe liver damage.
- The university acted only after it was caught. Shanghai Jiao Tong University set up a special task force for a comprehensive investigation — but did so within days of the external exposure, not within 17 months of the death.
