## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Niger answered a request for release by changing the law. Human Rights Watch reported on 23 July 2026 that after lawyers for human rights defender Moussa Tiangari applied for his release on 19 June, the authorities amended the law a week later to extend maximum pretrial detention to four years, renewable once.
- Three years after the July 2023 coup, the junta still holds deposed President Mohamed Bazoum, his wife, former interior minister Hama Amadou Souley and Tiangari.
- The United Nations Working Group on Arbitrary Detention found the detentions of both Bazoum and Tiangari unlawful in opinions issued in February 2025 and again in May 2026, and called for their release. Neither has been released.
- Jihadist violence in Niger is inflicted by non-state armed groups. Under the attribution rule that harm does not lower Niger's score; the detentions and the legislative change are the state's own conduct and do.
- Niger remains in the Critical band. The score is confirmed rather than lowered because most of what the July report documents is standing conduct already carried in the published value.
