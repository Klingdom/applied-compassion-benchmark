## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Sri Lanka on escalating prison unrest inside the window: a riot at Mahara Prison on 1 August, riots leaving three dead and 23 injured reported on 7 August, and troop deployment on 7 August after a rooftop protest at the Pallansena open-air facility. Sri Lanka has never been individually assessed (`last_assessed: null`), so this is a first full assessment.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Dates verified.** Al Jazeera and Euronews both carry 2026-08-07 for the fatal riots and the troop deployment. The larger July event at Negombo Prison, which claimed 27 to 28 lives, is dated 2026-07-08 to 2026-07-10 and sits outside the window but inside the two-to-three-year evidence horizon; it is used as context and as the trigger for the government response that is scored.

**Mixed dated/undated sourcing.** The scanner listed one undated source (srilankabrief.org). It was disregarded. Two independently dated sources carry the in-window finding.

**Attribution.** Prisons are operated by the Sri Lankan state and the overcrowding is a direct product of the state's own Ratama Ekata anti-drug crackdown, which raised the prison population from about 28,000 in late 2024 to about 41,000 in 2026. This is the entity's own conduct throughout. The government's response is likewise its own conduct and is scored as a genuine positive.

### Analysis

The assessed composite is 30.0 against a published 34.4 — a fall of 4.4 points, below the filing trigger, with no band change. The published score is confirmed.

The harm is severe and self-inflicted. Sri Lanka's prisons hold roughly 41,000 people in a system designed for about 10,000. That is not an accident of demand: the government's own anti-drug crackdown, combined with bail-tightening laws, drove the population up by roughly 13,000 in under two years. Twenty-seven to twenty-eight people died at Negombo in July; three more died in August. Euronews records troops deployed to three prisons in a day.

The response is unusually direct, and it is scored. Justice Minister Harshana Nanayakkara accepted responsibility before Parliament, visited the prison, and announced legislative changes to facilitate bail and to introduce house arrest with electronic monitoring as an alternative to remand. He also announced a three-member investigation including a retired Supreme Court judge. That combination clears anchor 4 for AB1 (Harm Acknowledgment) and anchor 3 for AB2 and AB3. Accountability at 2.80 of 5 is Sri Lanka's **strongest** dimension — an unusual profile, and the reason the score does not fall further.

What holds it down is that the correction has not yet worked. Three more prisoners died in August, weeks after the July response, in the same conditions. AB4 (Systemic Learning) scores 2 of 5 for exactly that reason, and rights groups have gone to the US and Europe to press the government over continuing prisoner deaths and abuse.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Sri Lanka's prisons hold four times the people they were built for, and the government's own policy put them there. Roughly 41,000 inmates are in a system designed for about 10,000, up from about 28,000 in late 2024 after an anti-drug crackdown and tighter bail rules.
- People are dying. Twenty-seven to twenty-eight died at Negombo Prison in July 2026. Three more died and 23 were injured in riots reported on 7 August 2026. Troops were deployed to three prisons in a single day.
- Why it matters: the government owned it, which is rare. Justice Minister Harshana Nanayakkara accepted responsibility before Parliament, visited the prison and announced bail reform and house arrest with electronic monitoring instead of remand.
- Accountability is Sri Lanka's best dimension at 2.80 of 5 — higher than its Awareness, Action or Equity. That is an unusual shape for a country in the Developing band, and it is the main reason the score holds.
- The score is confirmed at 34.4. The assessed value of 30.0 is 4.4 points lower, inside the tolerance for a desk assessment, so no change is proposed.

## Strongest Dimensions

Accountability (2.80 of 5). A minister accepting responsibility in Parliament before any legal obligation, a visit to the site, a named independent investigation panel and a concrete legislative remedy together clear anchors that most Developing-band states do not reach.

## Weakest Dimensions

Equity (2.00 of 5), Boundaries (2.00 of 5) and Integrity (2.00 of 5). Custodial staff and prisoners alike operate at four times design capacity, families and lawyers had access restricted, and the crackdown that caused the crisis has not been reversed.

## Evidence Gaps

Sri Lanka publishes prison population totals but no custodial death statistics, no grievance data and no disaggregated outcome reporting. The three-member investigation had not reported at the time of assessment. Where disclosure is simply absent, the low anchor was applied and is stated as an absence of disclosure, not as evidence of harm.
