## Evidence-date, lifecycle and attribution checks

**DATE MISMATCH RESOLVED (gate warning 3, Sudan, 3 days).** The scanner recorded `evidence_date: 2026-08-20` while listing a second Al Jazeera URL carrying `/2026/8/23/`. Both are correct and describe different things. The 20 August item is the IOM displacement report and is the conduct/report date. The 23 August item is a follow-up newsfeed piece on the same displacement into el-Obeid, published three days later. **The 20 August date is used.** Both URLs carry an explicit 2026 path. No year-confusion.

**LIFECYCLE RE-CHECK — one scanner claim NOT confirmed and therefore NOT scored.** The scan summary states "on 20 August 2026 RSF attacks on villages in North Kordofan killed six civilians". On direct re-fetch, the cited Al Jazeera article **does not carry that dated attribution or that figure**. It reports ongoing RSF attacks on villages near el-Obeid without attaching them to 20 August or to a six-civilian toll. **The six-civilian figure is dropped and does not appear anywhere in this assessment.** The displacement figure (over 200,000, attributed to IOM) and the cumulative war toll (at least 59,000) are confirmed verbatim and are used. Both source URLs resolve; neither has been amended or retracted.

**ATTRIBUTION — separated in both directions, per the cycle instruction.** The Rapid Support Forces are a non-state armed group. Their attacks on villages are **not** scored as Sudanese state conduct. The Sudanese Armed Forces are the state's own military and their conduct **is** scored as state conduct — as it was in the 2026-08-20 cycle, which corrected the record *against* the state when the North Darfur strike on a traditional court proved to be an SAF strike rather than an RSF one.

**One attribution deliberately left open.** The source reports "intense fighting and drone strikes" disabling el-Obeid's main power transformer, and separately reports RSF drones targeting road traffic. It **does not establish who struck the transformer**. That strike is therefore **not** scored as state conduct. What is scored is the state's failure to restore electricity needed to run hospitals and pump water — a failure of provision, which is attributable regardless of who fired.
