## Assessment Context

- **In-window event.** LAHSA released the 2026 Greater Los Angeles Homeless Count on 24 July 2026. Homelessness in the City of Los Angeles rose 3.4% to an estimated 45,194 people, unsheltered homelessness in the city rose about 7.9%, and the county total rose 1.2% to roughly 73,040. This reverses two consecutive years of decline.
- **Attribution is mixed and handled explicitly.** Reductions in California's Homeless Housing, Assistance and Prevention grant programme and federal safety-net cuts are external actions and are not scored against the city. What is scored against the city is its own strategy's efficacy and the fact that a pause on new enrolments and permanent placements in the Time Limited Subsidies programme — an operational decision inside the local system — is the mechanism LAHSA itself identifies for the increase.
- **Countervailing positive.** The city and LAHSA published the deterioration promptly and in detail. Publishing an unflattering primary outcome measure on schedule is genuine transparency and holds Accountability up.

## Key Findings

- Homelessness in the City of Los Angeles rose 3.4% to an estimated 45,194 people, with unsheltered homelessness up about 7.9%, erasing two consecutive years of decline (LAHSA count released 24 July 2026).
- LAHSA attributes the increase to a pause on new enrolments and permanent placements in the Time Limited Subsidies programme — an operational decision inside the local system, which is why it is scored against the city.
- The mayor attributed the reversal to cuts to California's HHAP grant programme and to federal safety-net reductions. Those are external actions and are not scored against the city.
- The city published the deterioration in full and on schedule, which is genuine transparency and is the reason Accountability transparency stays at anchor 4.
- Composite moves -1.5 to 34.4; band unchanged at Developing.
