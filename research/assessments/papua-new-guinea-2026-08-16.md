## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Papua New Guinea's placeholder was far too low, and that is this study's largest single correction. It assesses at 33.1 against a published 20.3, a gain of 12.8 points. The band stays Developing but the rank moves substantially.
- Why it matters: the country now has a ten-year national plan against violence toward women. Prime Minister James Marape released the National Strategy to Prevent and Respond to Gender-Based Violence 2026-2035, and UNFPA called it strong national leadership.
- Why it matters: the plan reaches into hospitals and police stations. The National Department of Health launched clinical guidelines for family support centres, and the Prime Minister proposed dedicated reporting systems at every police station and an end to misuse of compensation payments in domestic violence cases.
- Why it matters: the harm remains enormous. Sixty four per cent of women in Papua New Guinea report experiencing gender-based violence, over 80 inter-ethnic killings were reported in Enga province in the year, and there is about one police officer for every 1,845 people.
- Why it matters: tribal violence is not scored as state conduct. Inter-ethnic killings and the Enga landslide are attributed to non-state actors and natural disaster. What is scored is the government's response to them.

## Strongest Dimensions

Systemic Thinking (2.6) and Awareness (2.6). Papua New Guinea has a published ten-year prevention strategy, national clinical guidelines, family support centres in hospitals, and a parliamentary committee that reports publicly on gender.

## Weakest Dimensions

Boundaries (2.0). Frontline capacity is chronically thin at about one police officer per 1,845 people against a UN-recommended one per 450, which limits what any policy can deliver.

## Evidence Gaps

- The strategy launched in 2026, so no outcome data exists yet. Efficacy is scored on design, not on results.
- No Human Rights Watch World Report 2026 chapter for Papua New Guinea was located; Amnesty International and UN and government sources were used instead.
- The policing ratio comes from a tier-3 aggregator rather than a primary national statistic, so Responsiveness and Self-Sustainability carry that limitation.

## Attribution Discipline

Only the conduct of the Papua New Guinea state is scored. Harm by non-state armed groups, by foreign states, and by natural disaster is excluded from the state's score. Where such harm occurred, what is measured is the state's own response to it. Every source date was checked against its event date, and publication dates were not treated as event dates.
