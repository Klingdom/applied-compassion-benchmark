## Baseline-establishment note

This is a **first-ever assessment**. Symbotic Inc. does not appear in any published Compassion Benchmark index.

**Corporate status check (required before assessment).** Symbotic Inc. is operating under that name, headquartered in Wilmington, Massachusetts, and listed on Nasdaq as SYM. It files annual and quarterly reports with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, including a Form 10-Q/A filed 4 December 2024 ([Symbotic Form 10-Q/A](https://ir.symbotic.com/static-files/79bc3d82-403e-47d6-b739-c7d9a052e27c)). No acquisition, rename or insolvency event was found.

**Evidence base.** Symbotic is a US-listed company, so financial and governance disclosure exists at tier 4–5. What is almost entirely absent is disclosure about **people** — the warehouse workers who work alongside its systems. The one substantial accountability record found concerns investors, not workers: a self-identified accounting restatement in November 2024.

**Absence versus conduct.** No safety finding, labour ruling or regulatory action about worker treatment was located. **Where a score rests on absence of disclosure, the row says so.**

**Disclosure density.** Of 40 subdimensions, **32 rest primarily on absence of disclosure** and 8 rest on positive located evidence.

## Key Findings

- **Symbotic's only substantial accountability record is about money, not people.** In November 2024 the company found revenue-recognition errors itself, disclosed them, restated fiscal 2024 results and named a $30 million to $40 million hit. That self-disclosure is why Accountability scores 40 of 100 while five other dimensions sit at the floor.
- **Warehouse automation is the largest single site of robot-driven change to human work, and Symbotic publishes nothing about it.** No worker channel, no reskilling programme, no employment-effects analysis was located. Systemic Thinking scores 25 of 100.
- **Thirty-two of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure.** No safety finding, labour ruling or regulatory action about worker treatment was located either.
- **Being publicly listed helped less than expected.** SEC filings gave good evidence about internal financial controls and nothing about the people the systems work beside.
- **A securities class action over the same accounting conduct is pending.** Defending litigation is not repair, so Reparative Action stays at 2 of 5.
