## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the state shot at people who disputed an election result. Human Rights Watch reported on 12 November 2025 that police and gendarmes fired live ammunition at protesters; UN sources estimate at least 48 people were killed and opposition groups say 55.
- Why it matters: up to 2,000 people were detained, including children. A pool of 149 volunteer lawyers counted them. At least six are held at the State Defense Secretariat, where Human Rights Watch has documented routine torture.
- Why it matters: one court did push back. On 19 March 2026 Cameroon's Supreme Court quashed the life sentences of the ten Anglophone leaders known as the Nera 10 and ordered a fresh hearing. That is a real correction and it lifts the Accountability score above the floor.
- Why it matters: 2.9 million people need aid and almost no one is watching. Cameroon topped the Norwegian Refugee Council's list of the world's most neglected displacement crises in 2025, with 844,953 people internally displaced.
- Why it matters: Cameroon also shelters others. It hosts 460,000 refugees from the Central African Republic and Nigeria, which is genuine burden-sharing and is scored as such.

## Strongest Dimensions

Action (1.8) and Systemic Thinking (1.8). Cameroon maintains national systems and hosts 460,000 refugees, and it participates in regional security coalitions. These are relative strengths inside a very low range, not good performance.

## Weakest Dimensions

Integrity (1.0, at the floor). Paul Biya has ruled since 1982, term limits were removed in 2008, and no leadership transition has ever tested whether any compassionate practice would survive one. Further downward movement in Integrity is arithmetically unavailable.
