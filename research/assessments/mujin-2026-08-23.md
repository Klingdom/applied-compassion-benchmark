## Key Findings

- **Mujin, Inc. scores 25.6 of 100. Its one above-floor score exists because somebody other than Mujin examined the question its product raises.**
- **Dated independent reporting in Fortune and TechCrunch in April 2026 documents that Japanese warehouse automation is filling roles employers cannot fill, not roles people want.** Japan's population fell for a fourteenth straight year in 2024 and its working-age population is projected to shrink by nearly 15 million over two decades. Interconnection Awareness scores 3 of 5 on that corroboration.
- **The corroboration is about Japan, not about Mujin's sites.** Mujin publishes no workforce study, no worker-outcome data and no transition provision. That is why the score caps at 3 rather than 4.
- **The company's own human claim is unevidenced.** Mujin states that robots improve productivity and safety, and reports throughput comparable to three to four workers per station across more than 100 sites. No injury, strain or workload data was located behind either statement.
- **Thirty-seven of 40 subdimensions rest on absence, and this is silence by choice.** Mujin has raised about US$411 million, is valued above US$1 billion and is preparing for a listing. It can afford to publish what its systems do to people.
