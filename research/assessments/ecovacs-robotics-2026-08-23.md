## Key Findings

- **Ecovacs Robotics scores 26.3 of 100. It is the only company in this batch where documented harm and documented repair both appear in the record, and the two roughly cancel.**
- **A researcher told Ecovacs in December 2023 that its home robots could be remotely taken over. The company did not reply.** The flaw became public at the DEF CON 32 conference in August 2024 and was still unpatched in October 2024. Suffering Detection and Validation both score 1 of 5 — the only scores of 1 given to a consumer company in this batch, and both rest on that documented sequence, not on absence.
- **Real people were watched and harassed in their own homes.** A lawyer in Minnesota reported his Deebot X2 broadcasting racial slurs while the attacker viewed the live camera feed; similar incidents were reported in Los Angeles and El Paso. Researchers showed an attacker could connect over Bluetooth from around 130 metres and **switch off the camera warning sound** so nobody in the house would know.
- **Then Ecovacs fixed it, and kept fixing it in public.** It shipped patches, opened a vulnerability-reporting channel, and now publishes dated security advisories naming the researchers who find its flaws. The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency has published an advisory on the DEEBOT vacuum and base station. Correction Willingness and Transparency both score 3 of 5.
- **Nobody appears to have gone back to the people who were harmed.** No repair, compensation or outreach to the named victims was located. Patches protect the next household, not the last one.
