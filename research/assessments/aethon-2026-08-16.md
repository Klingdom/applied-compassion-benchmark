## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the index calls this company "Aethon (Teradyne)". Aethon has been owned by ST Engineering since August 2017 and has never been a Teradyne company. That is an entity-record error, separate from the score.
- In April 2022 researchers disclosed five zero-day flaws in Aethon TUG hospital robots, one rated 9.8 out of 10. They allowed remote takeover of robot fleets, access to real-time camera feeds of patients and staff, and disruption of medication delivery, elevators and doors.
- Aethon did not find those flaws. A cybersecurity firm hired by one of its hospital customers found them during an audit, and they reached the company through a federal disclosure process.
- The response was competent and fast. ST Engineering was told on 6 January 2022, and a patch was tested and confirmed effective on 4 April 2022 and rolled out to all fleets, with firewall help for hospitals. That drives the Action score of 3.0, the highest of Aethon’s eight dimensions.
- No repair was made to the patients or staff whose privacy was exposed, and no post-incident architecture review was published.

## Strongest Dimensions

Action (3.0), where all five subdimensions score 3. The 2022 incident response was dated, fleet-wide, proportional to the exposure rather than to the complaint, and followed through past the patch to firewall assistance. Accountability (2.6) follows, on a full and specific public acknowledgment and a documented course correction.

## Weakest Dimensions

Awareness (2.2) and Boundaries (2.0). Aethon did not detect its own most serious harm exposure; a customer’s auditor did. The architecture rested on absolute trust between server and robots, that limitation was not communicated to hospitals in advance, and patients whose camera feeds could be captured had no consent framework. Systemic Thinking (2.2) is low because the product is logistics symptom-relief rather than root-cause work.

## Evidence Gaps

The dominant conduct evidence is four years old. The JekyllBot:5 disclosure of April 2022 falls outside the preferred two-to-three-year window, and no more recent Aethon-specific conduct evidence — positive or negative — was located at all. That staleness is why confidence is recorded as low. ST Engineering’s Sustainability Report 2025 is tier-4 group-level disclosure, but no passage was located confirming that it reports on Aethon specifically, so it is credited only as parent-level governance context. Twenty-three of the forty subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure.
