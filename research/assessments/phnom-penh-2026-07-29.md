## Assessment Context

- **Attribution rule applied.** The forced criminality, confinement and violence inside the scam compounds are inflicted by transnational criminal networks, not by the city or the Cambodian state. That harm does not lower Phnom Penh's score. What is scored is the authorities' own conduct toward the people released from those compounds — how survivors are classified, sheltered, fined and detained once they are in Phnom Penh's streets and immigration facilities.
- **Window discipline.** The Amnesty International findings (8 June 2026) and the NPR reporting (21 June 2026) sit before the 2026-07-15 window start and are used as labelled standing structural context. The in-window items are the AP/UN trafficking-surge report of 28 July 2026 and the 29 July 2026 meeting between the head of Cambodia's Committee for Combatting Technology-Based Fraud and the UN Special Rapporteur.

### Recommendation

The published score of 18.8 (Critical) is confirmed. ## Key Findings

- The city's own conduct, not the criminal networks', is what is scored. Survivors released by the state's own crackdown are being classified as immigration violators and charged overstay fines of roughly US$10 per day.
- Amnesty International found that none of 73 survivors it interviewed had been recognised as a trafficking victim, despite all of them meeting the international definition (8 June 2026, pre-window structural context).
- The in-window government position, given to the UN Special Rapporteur on 29 July 2026, is that enforcement must be balanced against humanitarian considerations — a statement of intent with no structural mechanism attached.
- There is one trafficking shelter with a waitlist in the hundreds against thousands of released workers.
- Composite is unchanged at 18.8; the entity is already in the Critical band and the in-window evidence is continuation, not escalation.

## Strongest Dimensions

- Awareness (2.0) and Systemic Thinking (2.0) — the state does detect and count the released population, and it engages with international mechanisms, including hosting the UN Special Rapporteur's visit.

## Weakest Dimensions

- Boundaries (1.4) and Accountability (1.4) — the fine regime immobilises survivors, the single shelter turns people away without alternatives, and documented police extortion of trafficking victims has drawn no acknowledged correction.
