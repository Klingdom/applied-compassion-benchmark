## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: the people of Tripoli had to shut down their own city to be heard about the electricity.** Protests spread across Tajoura, Souq al-Jumaa, Al-Sarraj, Janzour and other districts from 26 to 28 July 2026 over daily blackouts. Al Jazeera reported the outages ran for hours each day. The complaint list also included water cuts, a failing health system and rising prices.
- **Why it matters: the protest itself is the detection system.** There is no evidence of a working municipal channel through which residents could raise service failures before the streets filled. Problems reached decision-makers only through road blockades and the closure of public buildings.
- **Why it matters: the dispute is about who controls the utility, not how to fix it.** Al Jazeera reported on 29 July 2026 that the electricity crisis sits inside a conflict between Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Dbeibah and the chairman of the General Electricity Company of Libya that has paralysed the sector’s management.
- **Why it matters: the escalation now threatens everyone, including the protesters.** Protesters stormed the Mellitah oil and gas complex about 90 kilometres west of Tripoli and closed gas lines feeding power plants. The electricity company warned this could cause a complete blackout across multiple areas.
- **Standing context (February 2026, outside the evidence window):** a joint UNSMIL and OHCHR report found systematic violations against migrants, asylum seekers and refugees in Libya, including in detention, committed with impunity. This is background to Tripoli’s floor score, not a new within-window finding.
