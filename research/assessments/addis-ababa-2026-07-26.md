## Key Findings

- Why it matters: the harm protested in Addis Ababa was not done by Addis Ababa. Thousands gathered at Meskel Square on 18 July 2026 under the banner "Stop the Abductions; Let Peace Prevail," but the alleged abductions and forced recruitment are attributed to authorities in the Tigray region, hundreds of kilometres north.
- Under the attribution rule, harm inflicted by another actor does not lower this entity's score. The Tigray allegations are scored against neither Addis Ababa nor its city administration.
- The city did allow a large demonstration to proceed at its central square. That is a mild positive for civic space, but the rally aligned with the federal government's position and was publicly rejected by the party it targeted as serving that agenda, so it does not demonstrate general tolerance for dissent.
- The city's own standing weakness is displacement from large-scale redevelopment, which holds Equity at raw 1.4 and Accountability at raw 1.2.
- Addis Ababa remains in the Critical band at an assessed 15.6 against a published 18.8, a sub-threshold gap that does not change the band.
