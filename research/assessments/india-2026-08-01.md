### Score Difference Analysis

The +1.3 difference is not a finding. It reflects the fact that the published 15.6 places India in the bottom forty of 193 countries, and a detailed re-score of 40 subdimensions on the current evidence lands marginally above that floor rather than below it. One element of the scan summary does not survive checking and is corrected here. The scan states that the protest was followed by "a subsequent invocation of extraordinary detention powers." The renewal of National Security Act detention powers for the Delhi Police Commissioner was issued on 7 July 2026 and took effect from 19 July 2026 through 18 October 2026 — before the protest — as a routine periodic renewal, and Delhi Police publicly stated that claims linking it to the protests were misleading. The protesters' own lawyers subsequently confirmed that all detainees had been released. The excessive-force finding stands on the HRW and Amnesty documentation; the detention-powers framing does not, and it is not scored.

### Recommendation

Confirm the published score of 15.6, band Critical, with a watch flag. No proposal. The conversion trigger would be an independent inquiry finding on the 20 July 2026 policing, or prosecution of protest organisers under national security law rather than under ordinary public-order provisions.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: the underlying finding is real and already scored.** Human Rights Watch reported on 23 July 2026 that Indian authorities used tear gas and batons against largely peaceful student and youth protesters in New Delhi on 20 July 2026, with "one young woman protester in critical condition."
- **Why it matters: one part of the scan summary does not hold up.** The National Security Act detention powers cited in the scan were renewed on 7 July 2026, effective 19 July, before the protest. Delhi Police said linking them to the protests was misleading, and lawyers for the protest movement confirmed detainees had been released.
- **Why it matters: internet was cut in the capital.** Telecommunication providers reportedly received orders to shut down mobile internet services in parts of central Delhi on 20 July 2026.
- **Why it matters: this is the second look at the same evidence.** India was assessed on 27 July 2026 on this material and confirmed. Scoring it again would compound one event into two downgrades. It is not done here.
- **Why it matters: surveillance was part of the response.** HRW records that protesters were subjected to surveillance using facial recognition systems.
