## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Zimbabwe changed its constitution to delay the next election by two years. The government gazetted Constitutional Amendment No 3 on 16 February 2026, extending presidential and parliamentary terms from five to seven years and pushing the 2028 vote to 2030.
- Why it matters: students who objected were taken off campus. Human Rights Watch reported that Marlvin Madanda, 23, and Lindon Zanga, 21, were abducted while campaigning at Chinhoyi University of Technology in November 2025 and found the next day, allegedly assaulted and tortured.
- Why it matters: the law now lets the state shut down charities. The Private Voluntary Organizations Amendment Act, signed on 11 April 2025, allows authorities to deregister nongovernmental groups and seize their assets with little to no judicial recourse.
- Why it matters: Zimbabwe also did something genuinely humane. President Mnangagwa signed the Death Penalty Abolition Act on 31 December 2024, ending capital punishment for all crimes and requiring dozens of people under sentence of death to be resentenced.
- Why it matters: renewed advocacy about 2018 is not new evidence. Human Rights Watch's August 2026 piece on the 2018 post-election abuses concerns eight-year-old events. It is scored here only as evidence that accountability is still absent today, not as a fresh incident.

## Strongest Dimensions

Systemic Thinking (1.8), Action (1.6) and Equity (1.6). Zimbabwe abolished the death penalty, has a 2026-2030 national development strategy with health as a core pillar, and has a health insurance bill before Parliament. These are thin positives in a very low range.

## Weakest Dimensions

Empathy (1.2), Boundaries (1.2), Accountability (1.2) and Integrity (1.2). Students were abducted and tortured, charities can be dissolved by administrative act, health workers are unpaid and unequipped, and the constitution was rewritten to postpone an election.

## Evidence Gaps

- Human Rights Watch's 3 August 2026 piece on the 2018 abuses was checked. It is renewed advocacy about a 2018 event, not a new incident, and is used only for the ongoing accountability failure.
- The National Health Insurance bill and NDS2 rest on tier-3 sources; whether the bill launched in June 2026 could not be confirmed.
- No Zimbabwean government response to the abduction reports was located, so Accountability is scored on the absence of one.

## Attribution Discipline

Only the conduct of the Zimbabwe state is scored. Harm by non-state armed groups, by foreign states, and by natural disaster is excluded from the state's score. Where such harm occurred, what is measured is the state's own response to it. Every source date was checked against its event date, and publication dates were not treated as event dates.
