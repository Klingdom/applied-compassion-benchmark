## Entity identification

Omron Robotics and Safety Technologies, Inc. is the robotics arm of OMRON Corporation, a company listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange. The subsidiary was formed from OMRON's 2015 acquisition of Adept Technology. **Attribution ruling:** OMRON Corporation's group instruments — the Modern Slavery Act Statement, the supplier audit programme, the whistleblowing system and the occupational safety data — are group-level and cover subsidiaries, so they are credited and named. The group's restructuring, which cut jobs in the same business, is charged in the same way. It would be indefensible to credit the subsidiary for the parent's audits while excusing it from the parent's job cuts.

### Score Difference Analysis

Omron Robotics is the second-strongest entity in this study and the strongest in Cluster B, and the reason is specific: OMRON Corporation lets an outside body inspect its factories and then publishes what the outside body found, including the bad parts. The Responsible Business Alliance audit of its Vietnam site in November 2025 identified overtime miscalculation and excessive working hours among a contractor's security staff, and OMRON published it. That single practice lifts Awareness to 2.6 and Accountability to 2.6 — the highest of any Cluster B lab. It is the same variable that separated the strongest labs in the concurrent Exemplary-cluster study: whether the company submits its own effects to outside examination and publishes the result. What OMRON does not have is anything on the compassion framework's human-facing anchors. Nothing was located on complaint validation, cultural adaptation, access design, consent or repair. And the NEXT 2025 programme, which removed about 2,000 jobs while the company was profitable, holds Boundaries and Integrity at the baseline.

### Recommendation

The published score is overstated and should be revised down to 31.3, which is still the second-highest assessed value in this 14-entity study. The record name should be checked: the index says "Omron Robotics" but the operating company is Omron Robotics and Safety Technologies, Inc., and all the scoreable disclosure belongs to the parent, OMRON Corporation.

## Key Findings

- **Why it matters: publishing your own bad audit result is worth more than any policy statement.** An outside auditor found overtime miscalculation and excessive working hours at OMRON's Vietnam site in November 2025. OMRON published the finding.
- **The people the audit protected do not work for OMRON.** The excessive hours were worked by employees of a contracted security company on OMRON's site — the least powerful group there.
- **OMRON re-audits the same sites.** Its Dalian factory in China was audited again in October 2025 and kept PLATINUM recognition, "as was in the previous audit".
- **OMRON cut about 2,000 jobs while profitable.** The NEXT 2025 programme, announced 26 February 2024, completed 1,206 retirements in Japan on 20 July 2024 and 1,055 overseas. That holds its Boundaries and Integrity scores at the baseline.
- **Attribution matters here.** All the scoreable disclosure belongs to the parent, OMRON Corporation, not to the robotics subsidiary. It is credited because it is group-level, and the parent's job cuts are charged for the same reason.

## Scoring Conventions Applied

- **Absence of disclosure scores 2, never 1.** The floor of 1 is used only where positive documented evidence of a specific failure exists. This is the "2-convention", the more conservative of the two conventions live in this repository.
- **A score of 4 requires published institutional data, a regulatory decision or third-party peer review. A score of 5 requires independent audit plus community testimony.**
- 30 of 40 subdimensions sit at 2. Of those, approximately 24 rest on absence of disclosure rather than on located evidence of a practice.
