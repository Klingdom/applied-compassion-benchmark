## Baseline-establishment note

This is a **first-ever assessment**. Pudu Robotics does not appear in any published Compassion Benchmark index.

**Corporate status check (required before assessment).** Shenzhen Pudu Technology Co., Ltd. is operating under the name Pudu Robotics, founded in 2016 by Zhang Tao. Cumulative shipments have passed 130,000 units across more than 85 countries and regions. The company raised $150 million in April 2026 at a $1.5 billion valuation and is preparing a Hong Kong listing ([Caproasia, Pudu IPO plans](https://www.caproasia.com/2026/06/04/china-commercial-service-robotics-company-pudu-robotics-plans-china-or-hong-kong-ipo-raised-150-million-at-1-5-billion-valuation-in-2026-april-founded-in-2016-by-felix-zhang-tao-investors-include/); [Nikkei Asia, Pudu eyes Hong Kong listing](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/pudu-robotics-eyes-hong-kong-listing-amid-geopolitical-risks)). No acquisition, rename or insolvency event was found.

**⚠️ Most low scores rest on absence of disclosure, not on evidence of harm.** No lawsuit, safety incident, labour ruling or regulatory action against Pudu was located. **Where a score rests on absence, the row says so.**

**What differentiates Pudu from its closest peer.** Pudu holds **third-party certifications that touch directly on compassion-relevant risk**: CE-MD and CE-RED conformity for the PUDU T300 with compliance to EN ISO 3691-4 and EN 1175, and ISO/IEC certifications for information security and privacy. It also co-published, with Deloitte, what is described as the first environmental, social and governance practice guide for the service robot industry. Those are the three subdimensions where Pudu scores above the floor.

**Disclosure-regime caveat.** Pudu is headquartered in a jurisdiction that does not require the social reporting this benchmark reads. Part of the drag on transparency-dependent subdimensions is structural rather than behavioural. A Hong Kong listing would change that.

**Disclosure density.** Of 40 subdimensions, **37 rest primarily on absence of disclosure** and 3 rest on positive located evidence.

## Key Findings

- **Pudu Robotics has shipped more than 130,000 robots into 85-plus countries and publishes almost nothing about their effect on people.** Thirty-seven of 40 subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure. **That is a finding about disclosure, not about conduct** — no lawsuit, safety incident, labour ruling or regulatory action was located.
- **Pudu is the only company in this batch with third-party certification covering data privacy.** Its ISO/IEC information security and privacy certifications, obtained in May 2024, give outside assurance over data collected by robots operating around people. Consent Orientation scores 3 of 5 because of it.
- **Its safety evidence is real and externally assessed.** The PUDU T300 holds CE-MD and CE-RED certification with documented compliance to EN ISO 3691-4 and EN 1175 — a notified-body assessment, not a company claim.
- **Pudu wrote an environmental, social and governance guide for its whole industry with Deloitte — and does not appear to publish one about itself.** No ESG report on Pudu's own performance was located. That gap is the single most striking thing in the record.
- **A Hong Kong listing would force disclosure.** Pudu raised $150 million in April 2026 at a $1.5 billion valuation and is preparing to list. Listing brings mandatory environmental, social and governance reporting, which would materially change what this benchmark can read.

## Strongest Dimensions
- **Boundaries (30.0), Systemic Thinking (30.0) and Awareness (30.0)** — privacy certification, an industry-level ESG guide co-authored with Deloitte, and notified-body product safety certification.

## Weakest Dimensions
- **Five dimensions tied at 25.0** — Empathy, Action, Equity, Accountability and Integrity, all at the absence floor.

## Evidence Gaps
- No ESG or sustainability report about Pudu's own performance.
- No outcome data on effects on customers, workers or care recipients.
- No accessibility disclosure for people using wheelchairs, walkers or guide dogs in spaces these robots share.
- No employment-effects analysis for the service workers whose tasks the robots absorb.
