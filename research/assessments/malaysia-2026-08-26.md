## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Malaysia on a Human Resources Ministry arrangement restricting foreign-worker recruitment from Nepal to 25 registered agencies. Malaysia was last touched on 2026-06-03 as a rotation confirmation that held the baseline and recorded no dimension-level evidence.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**DATE RESOLVED IN FAVOUR OF THE SCANNER.** The scan recorded `evidence_date` 2026-08-22 against a URL dated 2026-08-24. The **arrangement took effect on 22 August 2026** — Online Khabar states Malaysia's Human Resources Ministry introduced it on that date. The 24 and 25 August URLs are subsequent coverage of an effective date. **The scanner's `evidence_date` is correct.** All URLs explicit 2026; no year confusion.

**ONE SOURCE IS POST-WINDOW AND IS DISCLOSED.** The Free Malaysia Today report carrying the four-country recruitment associations' joint statement is dated **27 August 2026**, one day after the scan window closed. It is used as **corroboration of an in-window policy**, not as an independent trigger, and it moves no score that is not already supported by in-window sourcing.

**ATTRIBUTION — this is Malaysian government conduct.** The Foreign Worker Centralised Management System is operated by Malaysia's own Human Resources Ministry, and the decision to admit only 25 Nepali agencies is Malaysia's own. Nepal's objection and the withdrawal of the listed agencies are **other parties' responses** and are not scored as Malaysian conduct; they are used as evidence that the arrangement is contested.

**THE HARM IS PROSPECTIVE AND CONTESTED, WHICH LIMITS THE MOVEMENT.** No worker has been documented as harmed. What is documented is a structural objection: four national recruitment associations state that limiting access "raises serious concerns about fair competition, transparency, equal opportunities and the rights and interests of migrant workers", and Nepali associations argue the arrangement inflates recruitment costs borne by workers. That is a credible, specific mechanism of harm to a population with a long-documented vulnerability, and it moves Equity, Boundaries and Integrity. It does not support a larger movement.

## Scoring convention

**Absence of disclosure scores 2, never 1.** No subdimension here scores 1. The five subdimensions at 2 rest on the documented policy and the documented objections to it, not on missing disclosure — except EQ3 and EQ5, where Malaysia publishes no disaggregated migrant-worker outcome data and no historical-harm acknowledgment, and absence is scored at 2 and is not evidence of harm.

### Score difference analysis

**Boundaries falls 0.4**, the largest movement, on two subdimensions: the arrangement narrowed suddenly with no published selection criteria (B3), and excluded licensed agencies and their workers were offered no alternative pathway (B4).

**Equity falls 0.3** because the cost of a narrowed channel falls on migrant workers paying recruitment fees — a population with a long-documented vulnerability in this corridor.

**Integrity rises 0.1** on balance: the published 2.5 already priced expression restrictions, and Malaysia's international positions and durable statutory protections offset the values-alignment problem this policy creates.

**Three dimensions do not move at all.** Awareness, Action and Systemic Thinking are unaffected: Malaysia responded quickly, the centralised system is itself an anti-exploitation intervention, and cross-border effects surfaced and are being engaged.

### Recommendation

**Confirm.** Delta -3.1, no band change, no change proposal. This is a real but bounded finding about one administrative arrangement, already being unwound in practice, with no worker documented as harmed. A **downward watch flag** is set for the three conditions under which it would become score-moving.

## Key Findings

- **From 22 August 2026 Malaysia allowed only 25 Nepali agencies to send workers to the country.** Malaysian employers had previously been able to use any agency licensed by Nepal.
- **Nepal's government objected, and so did recruitment associations from four countries.** They said limiting access "raises serious concerns about fair competition, transparency, equal opportunities and the rights and interests of migrant workers."
- **Malaysia has not said how it picked the 25.** A Malaysian commentary site headlined it as "the 25-agency question Malaysia won't answer".
- **The arrangement is already unravelling.** The 25 listed agencies are withdrawing their names after talks with Nepal's labour ministry.
- **Malaysia's score falls 3.1 points to 43.8 out of 100 and stays Functional.** No worker has been documented as harmed, so the movement is limited to how the change was made and who pays for it.

## Strongest Dimensions

Awareness, Action and Systemic Thinking, all 3.00. Malaysia's centralised recruitment platform is itself an intervention against exploitative brokerage, and it engaged source-country objections within days.

## Weakest Dimensions

Equity (2.20) and Boundaries (2.60), both Developing. A channel was narrowed with no published criteria and no alternative for those excluded.

## Evidence Gaps

- **No Malaysian government explanation of the selection criteria was located** — the central unknown.
- No data located on actual recruitment costs before and after the change, so the alleged cost inflation is a mechanism, not a measurement.
- The Free Malaysia Today source is dated 27 August 2026, one day after the scan window closed; disclosed above and used only as corroboration.
- The 2026-06-03 rotation confirmation recorded no dimension-level evidence, so this assessment is effectively the first with a documented basis for most subdimensions.
