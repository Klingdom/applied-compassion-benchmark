## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Zambia on the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights' statement of 19 August 2026 expressing concern over arrests and detentions of opposition figures before and after the 13 August general election. Zambia was assessed two days ago, on 2026-08-18, at 31.9 with an explicit **downward watch flag** — "second consecutive cycle below the published value; escalate if the count dispute produces further violence". **The OHCHR statement post-dates that assessment and is genuinely new evidence. This is the escalation the watch flag anticipated.**

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**BASELINE PROVENANCE — checked before proposing (screen 3e-bis §1).** Zambia's published 35.9 is not recent. The 2026-08-18 assessment scored 31.9 (delta -4.0, confirm, downward watch) and the 2026-07-31 assessment scored 33.1. **This is the third consecutive cycle below the published value, each on the entity's own conduct.** The trajectory is documented, not assumed. No "stale baseline" rationale is used here; the rationale is new within-window state conduct.

**DIRECTIONALITY (screen 3e-bis §3).** Zambia surfaced on **negative** within-window evidence (a UN human-rights adverse statement) and this proposal is a **downgrade**. Directionality matches.

**ATTRIBUTION — both directions, as in the 08-18 assessment.** The election-night raid, the arrests of opposition officials, and the refusal to disclose their whereabouts or charges are **acts of the Zambian state** and are scored against it. The Human Rights Commission being denied access to a detainee is state conduct. Conversely, the opposition's own allegations (that officials were unarmed and shot at, that a former minister was shot and is missing) are contested claims by an interested party and are **not** treated as established; they are recorded as allegations. The re-election of President Hakainde Hichilema with about 60 percent of the vote is an electoral outcome, not conduct, and is not scored.

### Analysis

The assessed composite of 28.7 sits 7.2 points below the published 35.9 and remains in the Developing band. The falls are concentrated in Accountability (2.5 to 2.0), Awareness (2.5 to 2.0), Integrity (2.5 to 1.8) and Equity (2.0 to 1.8). Empathy, Action, Boundaries and Systemic Thinking hold up comparatively well, because Zambia's civil administration, social-protection system and international engagement remain genuinely functional. The decline is specific: it is a rights-and-accountability failure in the post-electoral security domain, not a general institutional collapse.

## Key Findings

- Zambia falls to 28.7 of 100 from a published 35.9, staying in the Developing band. This is the third assessment in a row to land below the published score, each on Zambia's own conduct.
- The UN's top human rights official, Volker Türk, publicly urged Zambia on 19 August 2026 to stop arbitrary arrests and to tell detainees why they were arrested. He warned Zambia's democratic credentials are at risk of erosion.
- Police raided a property on election night where the main opposition leader was present. Eleven opposition officials were arrested. Days later the government still had not said where they were held or what they were charged with.
- Zambian police denied the country's own Human Rights Commission access to one of the detainees. The oversight body was blocked in exactly the case it exists for.
- What is not scored against Zambia: the opposition's claim that a former minister was shot and is missing. That is a contested allegation by an interested party, recorded but not treated as established.

## Strongest Dimensions

Action (ACT, raw 2.60) and Empathy (EMP, raw 2.40) are the strongest. Zambia's routine public administration, health and social-protection delivery continue to function, and the state engaged with UN human-rights mechanisms rather than rejecting the criticism outright.

## Weakest Dimensions

Integrity (INT, raw 1.80) and Equity (EQU, raw 1.80) are the weakest. Zambia campaigned on democratic restoration and then detained opposition officials without disclosing charges — a direct contradiction of stated values with no acknowledgment. Equity falls because political affiliation now conditions liberty and because the designated access route for detainees was blocked.

## Evidence Gaps

- The government has disclosed neither the charges nor the location of the detainees, so the assessment cannot evaluate whether the detentions have any lawful basis. This is scored at the low anchor as absence of disclosure, not as proof of unlawfulness.
- The opposition's allegations of shooting and disappearance are uncorroborated by any independent source located and are not load-bearing in scoring.
- No Zambian government written response to the OHCHR statement was located within the time budget.
