Assessed 11.9 against published 13.1 — delta -1.2, no band change. Confirmation.

Two subdimensions moved, both down, both tied to the specific content of the constitutional changes rather than to the fact of the vote.

**S4 (structural critique) 2 to 1.** The new constitution lets the president appoint and dismiss the prime minister and cabinet, create or abolish ministries, and dissolve parliament in a grave political crisis. A government rewriting the constitution to concentrate power in itself is the opposite of questioning the structures that sustain the problem. Anchor 1.

**B5 (consent orientation) 2 to 1.** The main opposition party, PAIGC, boycotted, and civil society groups argued the vote threatens the return to democracy. A consent process whose principal opposing party is absent from the ballot is a formality in the relevant sense. Anchor 1.

Everything else is held at the 2026-07-23 values. The referendum is a continuation of the coup arc already scored on 2026-08-16, not a new category of conduct, and re-scoring it broadly would double-count.

## Key Findings

- Guinea-Bissau's military junta rewrote the constitution and won. Voters approved the new text on 30 August 2026 by 70% to 30%, with turnout above 55% — about 544,060 people.
- The new constitution lets the president appoint and dismiss the prime minister and cabinet, create or abolish ministries, and dissolve parliament. The junta took power in a coup in November 2025.
- The main opposition party boycotted the vote. A referendum without the principal opposition on the ballot is not a full test of consent, and the benchmark scores it that way.
- The vote itself was run without violence. The electoral commission said it took place "without any incidents that would call into question the credibility of the vote." That is credited, and it is why the score falls only 1.2 points.
- Guinea-Bissau's 13.1 was set on 16 August 2026 on the junta's own conduct. It is recent and evidence-based, so no "outdated score" argument was available and none was used.

## Strongest Dimensions

**Empathy (2.0)**, **Action (2.0)** — held, not raised. The state continues to run an electoral apparatus that reached over half the registered electorate without recorded incident. That is functioning administrative capacity and the benchmark does not pretend otherwise.

## Weakest Dimensions

**Equity (1.0)**, **Accountability (1.0)** and **Integrity (1.0)**, all at the floor and all unchanged. A government installed by coup, suspended by ECOWAS, the African Union and the CPLP, holding an opposition leader in jail, and now expanding its own constitutional powers, offers nothing at any of these anchors above the minimum.

## Evidence Gaps

- Final certified results were not available at the assessment date; the 70/30 split and the 544,060 turnout figure are provisional results announced by the National Electoral Commission on 1 September 2026.
- No independent international observation mission report was located, so the electoral commission's own assessment of credibility is uncorroborated.
- No evidence was located on the status of the jailed opposition leader cited in the 2026-08-16 applied record.
- The December 2026 elections the junta has scheduled had not occurred. They are the decisive test and are outside this window.

## Disclosure — what this score measures

This score measures the junta's own conduct: calling the referendum, the content of the constitutional changes it proposed, and the conduct of the vote.

It explicitly credits countervailing evidence. A high-turnout, incident-free vote is real and is scored — it is the reason the movement is -1.2 rather than larger. Absence of disclosure is scored 2 throughout; the floor scores rest on documented conduct (coup, regional suspensions, opposition detention, constitutional power concentration), not on silence.

## Recommendation

**Confirm. No change proposal.** Delta -1.2; no band crossing.

**Watch:** the junta-scheduled December 2026 elections are the decisive event. If they are held and power transfers, that is positive conduct capable of supporting a real upward movement. If they slip or are conducted under the boycott conditions of this referendum, Guinea-Bissau has 13.1 points of downward range and a filable movement is arithmetically available — unlike five other entities in this cohort.
