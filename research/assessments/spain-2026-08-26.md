## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Spain on a Human Rights Watch call for Spain and Ceuta authorities to shelter thousands of migrants and asylum seekers left in the street after a mass crossing from Morocco. Spain was last assessed on 2026-07-31, which produced a confirmation at 58.1 against a published 60.0.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**MIXED-SOURCING GATE RESOLVED.** The Human Rights Watch statement is dated **21 August 2026** in its own URL and was fetched directly; Arab News coverage carries the same date. Two dated sources, one a named international rights organisation. Gate cleared.

**SCANNER FIGURES CORRECTED — THE SCALE IS MUCH LARGER THAN RECORDED.** The scan described "about 1,500 arrivals July 20-29". Human Rights Watch documents something else entirely: **tens of thousands swam from Morocco to Ceuta on 30-31 July 2026**. The Spanish Interior Minister says **5,000 remain**; local organisations estimate **up to 10,000**. Authorities reported **between 1,385 and 2,168 unaccompanied children**. The scanner both understated the number and mis-dated the crossing by roughly ten days. Corrected: mass crossing **30-31 July 2026**; population remaining 5,000-10,000; unaccompanied children 1,385-2,168.

**ATTRIBUTION — the migration event is not scored; the state's response is.** Nothing about the crossing itself is scored against Spain. Human Rights Watch is explicit that **both Spanish central authorities and the Ceuta local government share responsibility** for the response. Spain sits in the countries index, so national-government conduct is what is scored here:

- **Adverse:** people were left "in a legal vacuum" for almost three weeks; over 1,000 men and boys in makeshift huts on Trampolín beach; hundreds sleeping on cardboard in warehouse districts; 700-750 women in open-air sports facilities; sanitation so inadequate that people used parks, vacant lots and the sea.
- **Mitigating and scored:** the Spanish Interior and Inclusion ministries **did announce tent camps on 17 August 2026**. That is a real state response, within about two and a half weeks. Human Rights Watch's finding is that it fell short of need, not that nothing was done.

### Score difference analysis

**Action falls 0.8 raw points, the largest single movement.** AC1 Responsiveness drops from 4 to 2 because the gap between a mass arrival on 30-31 July and a ministerial shelter decision on 17 August is eighteen days, during which Human Rights Watch documented people without adequate shelter, food or sanitation. That is the definition of standards existing but not being met.

**Equity falls 0.5**, driven by EQ2. Between 1,385 and 2,168 unaccompanied children were among the unsheltered, and 700-750 women were in open-air facilities exposed to sexual violence. Priority for the vulnerable is stated Spanish policy; the allocation did not follow it.

**Awareness and Empathy each fall 0.5** because the scale and duration of the need were established by an outside organisation rather than by the state's own detection, and because the people affected did not experience the individual treatment Spain's system is designed to provide.

**Boundaries, Accountability, Systemic Thinking and Integrity move only 0.1-0.2** because Spain did act, did publish its own figures, and does maintain genuine upstream and EU-level engagement. This is not an assessment of a state that did nothing.

**The movement is fully linear.** All eight dimensions were already below 4.0 at the published values, so the integration premium was already zero and no formula threshold effect is involved. The 9.4-point fall is the raw evidence, not an artefact.

## Key Findings

- **Thousands of people slept rough in Ceuta for almost three weeks after crossing from Morocco.** Human Rights Watch reported on 21 August 2026 that they were "in a legal vacuum, exposed to sexual violence and without adequate shelter, food, or sanitation".
- **Between 1,385 and 2,168 of them were children travelling alone.** Around 700 to 750 women were housed in open-air sports facilities.
- **The scanner's numbers were far too small.** It recorded about 1,500 arrivals on 20-29 July. The crossing was on 30-31 July and Spain's own Interior Minister says 5,000 people remain; local groups say up to 10,000.
- **Spain did respond, eighteen days later.** Two ministries announced tent camps on 17 August 2026. Human Rights Watch says they fell short of what was needed. That response is why four of eight areas barely move.
- **Spain's score falls 9.4 points to 50.6 out of 100 and stays in the Functional band.** The drop is all evidence — no scoring-formula quirk is involved.
