## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Haiti on a Haitian Times report of 2026-08-12 that gangs have escalated attacks since the start of August, including a Kokorat San Ras attack on Gros-Morne over the weekend of 7-8 August 2026 that reportedly killed at least a dozen residents. Haiti was last assessed on 2026-07-23 at 5.0 against a published 4.7.

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Dates verified.** The Haitian Times report is dated 2026-08-12 and the underlying attack 2026-08-07 to 2026-08-08, both inside the window. The UN figure of 613 killed and more than 18,000 displaced covers 6 March to 31 July 2026.

**Attribution — decisive here.** The killings at Gros-Morne were carried out by the Kokorat San Ras gang, a **non-state armed group**. Gang violence is not the Haitian state's conduct and is not scored against it. What is scored is the state's **response**, and that response is where Haiti's floor scores come from: there is no functioning protective capacity, and the anti-gang effort has been substantially outsourced to the private military contractor Vectus Global.

**Floor discipline.** Haiti already sits at the floor anchor of 1 on 25 of 40 subdimensions. New reports of the same pattern of harm cannot move a score that is already at the floor, and inventing movement to reflect news volume would be a false positive. This is recorded as a confirmation.

**Scope note.** No subdimension was re-scored. All values are carried forward from 2026-07-23. **The subdimension sidecar is omitted**, per the partial-assessment rule.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Haiti's score cannot fall further on this evidence, because it is already at the bottom. Five of eight dimensions sit at 1.00 of 5, the lowest value the framework allows.
- Gangs attacked Gros-Morne in the Artibonite region over the weekend of 7-8 August 2026, reportedly killing at least a dozen residents and displacing others.
- The United Nations recorded 613 people killed and more than 18,000 displaced in Port-au-Prince alone between 6 March and 31 July 2026. About 1.4 million Haitians are displaced in total.
- Why it matters for the score: gangs are not the government. The benchmark scores each entity's own conduct, so gang killings do not lower Haiti's score. What is scored is the state's ability to protect people, and that is what sits at the floor.
- The score is confirmed at 4.7 out of 100. The assessed value of 5.0 is 0.3 points higher — the same result as the 23 July 2026 assessment.

## Strongest Dimensions

Awareness (1.60 of 5) and Action (1.60 of 5). Haiti retains a minimal detection and response capability, largely through international monitoring rather than its own institutions.

## Weakest Dimensions

Equity, Boundaries, Accountability, Systemic Thinking and Integrity all sit at 1.00 of 5 — the framework floor. There is no functioning mechanism for prioritising the most vulnerable, for consented or dignified refusal, for acknowledging harm, for addressing root causes, or for maintaining commitments under pressure.

## Evidence Gaps

Haiti publishes essentially no institutional performance data. The entire assessment rests on UN monitoring and journalism. The accountability arrangements governing the Vectus Global contract are not public, which is a material gap given that entity's role in the use of lethal force.
