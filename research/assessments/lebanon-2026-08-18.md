## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged Lebanon on a UN News report of 2026-08-18 stating that although roughly 860,000 conflict-displaced people have returned home, the humanitarian and displacement crisis remains "far from over", with nearly one in four people facing crisis or emergency food insecurity. Lebanon was last assessed on 2026-07-18 at 17.2 (confirmation, attribution-hold maintained).

## Evidence-date and attribution checks

**Date verified.** The UN News item is dated 2026-08-18, the final day of the scan window. The underlying IPC analysis covers April to August 2026 and was published 2026-04-29.

**Attribution — the decisive check, and the reason this is a confirmation.** The displacement and food-insecurity figures are **conflict-caused**. They are the consequence of armed conflict in southern Lebanon, not of decisions taken by the Lebanese state. Scoring them against Lebanon would attribute a third party's harm to the entity. Consistent with the attribution-hold recorded on 2026-07-18, only the Lebanese state's **own response** is scored.

**What the returns evidence shows.** The return of about 860,000 people is a genuine improvement in the humanitarian picture. It follows the cessation of hostilities rather than any Lebanese state programme, and the state's role in facilitating it is real but modest. It is credited at AC5 and no higher.

**Scope note.** No subdimension was re-scored on new institutional-conduct evidence. All 40 values are carried forward from 2026-07-18. **The subdimension sidecar is therefore omitted**, per the partial-assessment rule.

### Analysis

Nothing in the window changes Lebanon's institutional position. The assessed composite is 18.1 against a published 17.2 — a movement of 0.9 points, which is noise.

The evidence in the window is humanitarian-status evidence, not conduct evidence. Acute food insecurity rose from about 874,000 people in November 2025 to March 2026, to about 1.24 million between April and August 2026. In southern Lebanon and Nabatieh more than 80% of markets stopped functioning, and up to 78% of farmers reported being unable to reach their land. All of that traces to conflict.

Where the Lebanese state is measurable, it remains where it was: a fiscally hollowed administration whose humanitarian response is delivered largely by UN agencies. The World Food Programme reached more than 700,000 people; the state did not. That is why Action holds at 2.00 and Accountability at 1.40 rather than moving in either direction.

The one genuine positive — roughly 860,000 people going home — is scored, but it follows the end of hostilities rather than a Lebanese programme.

### Recommendation

**Confirm the published 17.2 / Critical.** Assessed 18.1, delta +0.9, no band change. The attribution-hold recorded on 2026-07-18 is maintained: conflict-caused harm is not scored against the Lebanese state. No change proposal is filed and no sidecar is written.

## Key Findings

- Why it matters: Lebanon's crisis is getting worse even as people go home. About 860,000 displaced people have returned to southern Lebanon, but the United Nations says the crisis is "far from over".
- Acute food insecurity rose from about 874,000 people in late 2025 to about 1.24 million between April and August 2026 — nearly one in four people in the country.
- Why it matters for the score: this harm was caused by armed conflict, not by the Lebanese government. The benchmark scores each entity's own conduct, so these figures do not lower Lebanon's score. What is scored is how the state responds.
- The response is largely not the state's. The World Food Programme reached more than 700,000 conflict-affected people with food and cash. Lebanon's own administration has neither the money nor the machinery to match that.
- The score is confirmed at 17.2 out of 100, in the Critical band. The assessed value of 18.1 is 0.9 points higher — well inside normal noise.

## Strongest Dimensions

Awareness (2.00 of 5), Empathy (2.00 of 5) and Action (2.00 of 5). Lebanon still has functioning ministries and a real, if overwhelmed, public administration — enough to sit above the floor anchors that Accountability sits on.

## Weakest Dimensions

Accountability (1.40 of 5). Correction willingness, systemic learning and reparative action all sit at the floor anchor of 1. The state has neither acknowledged nor repaired the governance failures that preceded the conflict, including the 2020 Beirut port explosion.

## Evidence Gaps

Lebanon publishes almost no institutional performance data, so the assessment relies on UN and IPC humanitarian reporting. That evidence measures conditions rather than conduct, which limits how much can be said about the state's own decisions. Where disclosure is absent, the low anchor was applied; this is an absence of disclosure, not evidence of harm.
