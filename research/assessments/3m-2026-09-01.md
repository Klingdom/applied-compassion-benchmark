## Why this entity was assessed

The scanner flagged 3M on a lawsuit by the City of Hastings, Minnesota over decades of PFOA contamination of the city's public drinking-water supply. 3M was last assessed on 2026-06-05 and is published at 20.3.

## Evidence-date, lifecycle and attribution checks

**DATE CORRECTION.** The scan states the lawsuit is "dated 30 August 2026". **The filing date is 26 August 2026**, in Dakota County District Court. 30 August is the Fox 9 publication date. Both are inside the window; the record is corrected.

**PRE-ADJUDICATION STAYS PRE-ADJUDICATION — per the cycle instruction, and it is decisive here.** The City of Hastings **alleges** that 3M's Cottage Grove facility contaminated five municipal drinking-water wells through PFOA air emissions. **No court has decided this.** The allegation is not scored as an established fact and does not move any subdimension. What is scored is 3M's **conduct in response**, which is documented and is company conduct.

Separately, the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency filed its own suit in May 2026 alleging ongoing PFAS releases connected to the same Cottage Grove facility. That is a state regulator rather than a city, which is a higher-quality accuser — but it too is **an allegation, not a finding**, and it is likewise not scored as established.

**COUNTERVAILING EVIDENCE — verified independently, and it is substantial.** 3M announced in December 2022 that it would exit PFAS manufacturing by the end of 2025, and **it completed that exit on schedule.** This is confirmed by the company's annual report and by Star Tribune reporting. Voluntarily ending manufacture of a profitable product line, on a self-imposed three-year deadline, is a documented major course correction and is scored as one.

**The exit's limits are also recorded, because they matter.** 3M exited manufacture of PFAS *chemicals*. It continues to make thousands of products that *contain* PFAS, and expects to keep using third-party PFAS-containing parts where regulatory or industry standards require them. The correction is real and it is narrower than the headline. Both facts are scored.

**3M's response to Hastings is company conduct and is scored.** 3M said: "The City's decision to pursue litigation is unfortunate." It also asserted that "trace levels" found are "safe based on decades of research", and cited $850 million provided to mitigate PFAS in Minnesota in 2018 and $10.3 billion committed nationally in 2024. Characterising a city's decision to sue over its own drinking water as "unfortunate", and asserting safety while a state regulator alleges ongoing releases, is scored down under Validation and Harm Acknowledgment. The settlement payments are scored up under Reparative Action.

Assessed 25.6 against published 20.3 — **delta +5.3**. Both values are in the Developing band; no band crossing.

**A +5.3 delta meets the magnitude filing trigger. This assessment deliberately does NOT file an upgrade proposal, and the reason is the anti-false-positive screening.**

Screening check 3 states that an entity surfaced on **negative** within-window evidence must not yield a **positive** proposal, and that an upgrade requires specific **new within-window** evidence of positive conduct by the entity itself. 3M fails that test on both counts:

- It was surfaced on a contamination lawsuit — negative evidence.
- The positive conduct driving the assessed increase, the completed PFAS manufacturing exit, was **completed at the end of 2025**, roughly eight months before this window opened. It is legitimate current evidence under the two-to-three-year currency rule, but it is not new within-window positive conduct.

Filing an upgrade on 3M in the same cycle it was surfaced for allegedly contaminating a city's drinking water would be precisely the false positive the screening exists to prevent.

**There is also a calibration question, and it is routed rather than acted on.** 3M's published composite is **20.3** — the same value that the 2026-08-20 de-seeding study identified as an unassessed seed across a cluster of entities including Cameroon, Guinea-Bissau, Djibouti, Mauritania and Republic of Congo. Screening check 5 is explicit: calibration concerns must be recorded as `flag-for-review` for coordinator-level review, never encoded as a one-off proposal. That is what is done.

## Key Findings

- The City of Hastings, Minnesota sued 3M on 26 August 2026, alleging its Cottage Grove plant contaminated five city drinking-water wells with PFOA. No court has ruled. The benchmark does not score an allegation as fact.
- The scan dated the lawsuit 30 August. It was filed 26 August; 30 August is the news report's date. Corrected.
- 3M did what it said it would do on PFAS manufacturing. It promised in December 2022 to stop making these chemicals by the end of 2025 and it finished on schedule. That is a real, costly, voluntary change and it is credited.
- The promise was narrower than it sounds. 3M stopped making PFAS chemicals but still makes thousands of products that contain them. Both facts are scored.
- 3M's assessed score of 25.6 is 5.3 points above its published 20.3 — enough to file a change. The benchmark deliberately does not file it. 3M was flagged for an alleged contamination, and you do not upgrade a company in the cycle it was flagged, on conduct that finished eight months earlier.

**First: this assessment declines to file a change it is arithmetically entitled to file.** The assessed composite exceeds the published by 5.3 points. It is not filed, because 3M was surfaced on negative evidence and the positive conduct is out-of-window. This is recorded so the gap is visible rather than hidden.

**Second: part of that gap is calibration, not conduct.** 3M's published 20.3 is the same value the 2026-08-20 study identified as an unassessed seed across a cluster of unrelated entities. Whether 3M's 20.3 is a real assessment or a seed is a calibration question for the coordinator, routed as `flag-for-review`. It must not be resolved by a one-off upgrade on 3M.

Absence of disclosure is scored 2, never 1. **Roughly 14 of 40 subdimensions rest on absence rather than on documented conduct**, mostly across Awareness, Boundaries and Systemic Thinking, where 3M publishes little an assessor can verify.
