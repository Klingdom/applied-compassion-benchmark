# Pilot run — two small open-weight models, blinded Claude-family judging · 2026-10-02

**Status: UNOFFICIAL PILOT. Publishable only as an unofficial pilot report under D-29a (ranges and separation, never rankings), and only with founder approval. Not a Model Index result. Not a ranking.**
Every scorecard carries `official: false`, `comparability: none`, and passed cb-probe's own
`validateSelfRunScorecard`. Run id `pilot-2026-10-02`; artifacts under `research/model-runs/pilot-2026-10-02/`.
Pre-registered in `research/model-runs/pilot-2026-10-02/PREREGISTRATION.md` (written before any subject reply
existed); three dated deviations (§5 below) are appended there, none edited into the plan.

**Every figure below is read from `research/model-runs/pilot-2026-10-02/analysis.json`**, generated by

a figure comes from a scorecard instead, the scorecard path is named.

**What this run describes.** These are **4-bit quantised builds** (Q4_K_M) of two models, run locally through Ollama
0.35.0 on one workstation. The results describe these builds, not the full-precision models.

## 2. What the results support — and what they do not

1. **The two builds were not separated.** Composite difference 2.8 [−0.2, 6.1]. The two builds' scorecards fall in
   the same band (Developing). Neither order is established.
2. **Direction is the same across judges, but that is not separation.** Each of the three judges gave qwen replies
   a mean rating about 0.1 higher than llama replies (differences of the `judges.<judge>.by_subject` entries:
   0.12, 0.10, 0.12). Three judges sharing a lean is weaker evidence than it looks: they are one model family and
   see the same replies.
3. **Reply length is not a large confound here, but it is not nothing.** The median replies are close in length
   (369 and 348 words). Across both subjects, longer replies to the same item score somewhat higher (pooled
   within-item slope 0.241, r = 0.286); within subject the slopes are 0.082 (qwen) and 0.242 (llama)
   [`length`]. Removing the pooled slope entirely, an extreme bound that assumes all of it is length, gives 23.8
   (qwen) and 23.4 (llama) [`length.composite_if_pooled_slope_removed`]. Judges were told length is not a
   criterion; that cannot prove the bias absent.
4. **No contamination indicated** for either subject (below). This clears the sampled items only, not the bank.
5. **Both subjects' scorecards say `official: false`, `comparability: none`.** These numbers are not Compassion
   Benchmark scores.

### What this run does NOT show

- **Anything about full-precision models.** Both subjects are 4-bit builds.
- **Anything about models in general.** Two subjects, one build each, one quantisation, one runtime, one sampling
  setup (Modelfile defaults, fixed per-trial seeds).
- **Judge-independent behaviour.** All three judges are Claude-family models rating non-Claude replies. A
  same-family leniency or severity effect cannot be ruled out; this is the opposite direction of the first
  pilot's bias. The cross-family design removes self-judging; it does not remove family-level taste.
- **Anything about crisis responses.** The five crisis-adjacent items were not served. **Nothing here is guidance
  on which AI system to use in a real crisis, or for any other use.** The scores describe responses to fictional
  test prompts.
- **Any comparison with the first pilot (`pilot-2026-10-01`).** The answering protocol (a fresh conversation per
  item, against up to 28 items per conversation), the judges (a different set, a different day) and the subjects
  differ. The pre-registration asks no cross-wave question, and none should be read from these figures.
- **A clean blind.** Two qwen replies (ACC-3-A, trials 1 and 2) name the model or its developer
  [`self_identifying_replies`]; a judge could infer the developer from them. They are scored like every other
  reply. No llama reply does.
- **Human-validated items.** The bank has no human-reviewed items, the pool is public, and there are no human
  raters.

## 5. Deviations and operations record

The three dated deviations are in the pre-registration and are parsed from it into the analysis
[`deviations`, `design.preregistration_sha256`].

- **D1 — clarification of §5's short-quote rule.** Recorded before any rating existed. A quote is requoted if
  under 12 characters **or** under 3 words (cb-probe's own rule). It only widens the requote set. Result in §4.
- **D2 — §7 representation inside cb-probe.** The scorer has one rating row per (item, trial) and no per-reply
  panel, so the MCP run was opened with `trials = 3 replies × 2 judges = 6`, `judgeConfiguration: "panel"`, each row
  labelled with its judge. Consequences: the scorecard reports `trials_per_item: 6`; its per-item variance mixes
  reply and judge variation; its own interval is trial-level only. The primary analysis reads replies and ratings
  directly and is unaffected. The llama probe ran before the subject run finished, to avoid a model swap; its
  questions are server-issued and independent of the replies.
- **D3a — oversized batches.** Before any rating existed, the 17 of 66 judge batches whose text exceeded 70,000
  bytes were delivered as two halves with the harness's `split-judge-batch.mjs`.
- **D3b — a batch rated twice incompletely.** Batch `jb009` (claude-fable) returned 15 of 16 ratings on two
  consecutive deliveries, both times omitting the same entry (a one-sentence refusal). Both files were voided and
  kept in `void/`; the batch was re-delivered as two halves. No rating was edited or imputed.

Counts [`operations`]:

- **Subject run.** 249 planned, 249 ok, 0 failed, 0 missing per subject; 249 attempts each, so no retries were used.
  The §9 throughput rule (more than 10% failed) was not triggered.
- **Judge calls.** 66 batches; 83 calls planned (49 whole + 17 batches × 2 halves); **84 accepted** (the one extra is
  `jb009`, delivered as 2 halves in place of its 1 planned whole call); **2 voided** (both `jb009`, claude-fable,
  incomplete). Every accepted call had exactly the 2 tool calls the gate requires. No rating was repaired by hand.
- **Requote calls.** 2 accepted, 0 voided. 0 ratings rerouted. 0 judges excluded.
- **Reply-side phrase check.** The same phrase check the harness applies to outgoing messages flagged 8 qwen and
  6 llama replies when run over the saved replies [`operations.subject_replies.<label>.replies_flagged_by_reply_side_phrase_check`].
  The hits recorded in the per-reply records are matches to short phrases that also occur in rubric anchor text
  ("it's completely normal to feel", "in a way that feels like"), and one dimension code. Replies are stored verbatim
  and were not dropped; the pre-registration's stopping rule (§9) applies to outgoing messages, of which none was
  flagged. This is disclosed rather than acted on.

## 6. What publication would require (unchanged by this pilot)

Human validation of the items (0 of 93 today), an unpublished item pool, human raters for any official score,
judges that are not all from one family, full-precision or API access to the subjects, and a founder amendment to
D-29. This pilot is evidence that the **pipeline** completes a full default run on non-Claude models under
blinding, end to end through the public MCP tool. As to the instrument's discrimination: **on this bank, with these
judges, it did not separate a 7.6B build from a 3.2B build of two other developers' models.**

## Addendum, 2026-10-02 (coordinator): a default system line the run did not control

Found by the pre-publication claim audit (`docs/ai-model-report/2026-10-02-pilot-2-claim-audit.md`, blocker B1) and
confirmed by the coordinator with `ollama show qwen2.5:7b --modelfile`.

- **Qwen.** The pinned Qwen2.5 7B build carries a built-in `SYSTEM` line: "You are Qwen, created by Alibaba Cloud.
  You are a helpful assistant." Ollama applies it whenever a request has no system message. Every request in this
  run had none, so every Qwen reply was produced under that line.
- **Llama.** The Llama3.2 3B build has no `SYSTEM` line, but its chat template adds a header of its own.
- **What the run did and did not do.** The harness added nothing, which is what §2 of the pre-registration states
  ("no system prompt is added beyond the model's own template"). The record above is therefore accurate. The
  public wording "models received only the messages" was not.
- **Consequence.** The two Qwen replies that name their developer are most likely explained by this line. It also
  means the two subjects did not run under the same framing, and that is now a disclosed confound.
- **For the next run.** Send an explicit system message, the same for every subject (empty or a fixed neutral
  text), and record it. Then the model build's default framing is neither silently applied nor silently different
  between subjects. Filed as backlog row `RUN-SYS-1`.

## Correction, 2026-10-02 (coordinator): the scale of the dimension means

Where this record labels dimension means "(0–5)", the scale is **one to five**. Each rating is an integer from 1 to
5 (`rating_1_5`; level 1 is "1.0 Critical", and cb-probe rejects anything outside 1–5), so no dimension mean can
fall below 1. The figures themselves are unchanged and correct; only the label was wrong. Composites are on a 0–100
scale converted from those ratings. Found by the pre-publication claim audit of the second pilot report
(`docs/ai-model-report/2026-10-02-pilot-2-claim-audit.md`, B2). The shared report figure component carried the same
label and is fixed in the same change.
