**Status: UNOFFICIAL PILOT. Not publishable under D-29 as ratified. Not a Model Index result. Not a ranking.**
Every scorecard carries `official: false`, `comparability: none`, and passed cb-probe's own
`validateSelfRunScorecard`. Run id `pilot-2026-10-01`; artifacts under `research/model-runs/pilot-2026-10-01/`.

## 0. What this run fixed relative to the invalid 2026-09-25 self-run

The 2026-09-25 run (`claude-opus-5-2026-09-25.md` §0) failed four validity conditions. This run addresses three:

| 2026-09-25 condition | This run |
|---|---|
| Subject saw the rubric before answering | **Fixed.** Subjects received prompts only — no item ids, dimensions, constructs or anchors; a leak check with a planted-anchor control gated every brief |
| Subject graded itself | **Fixed.** Every response rated by **two judges, neither of them the subject's own model**; judges blind to subject, trial and the other responses' grouping |
| Trials not independent | **Fixed.** Each trial (and each part of a trial) answered in a separate fresh-context call |
| The subject wrote the test | **Not fixed.** The bank was authored with Claude-family assistance; all subjects and all judges are Claude-family models |

## 2. What the results support — and what they do not

1. **Fable, Opus and Sonnet cannot be distinguished.** Paired differences on the same resampled items:
   Fable − Opus 1.0 [−2.0, 5.4]; Opus − Sonnet 1.2 [−1.3, 4.6]; Fable − Sonnet 2.2 [−0.8, 7.3]. Every interval
   crosses zero. **Their order is noise at this sample size; it must not be reported as a ranking.**
2. **Haiku is separated from the other three** (Sonnet − Haiku 27.1 [23.7, 30.9]), and the direction is robust to
   judge: every judge independently rates Haiku's replies below the item mean (−0.75 to −0.97).
3. **Reply length is an unresolved confound for the Haiku gap.** Across models, longer replies score higher (pooled
   within-item r = 0.60). *Within* each model, length barely predicts rating (slopes −0.32 to +0.36, mixed sign) —
   so judges are not simply rewarding the longer of two replies to the same message. But this design cannot
   separate "Haiku's replies are weaker" from "brief replies are penalised". Removing the pooled slope entirely
   gives Haiku 57.5 — an extreme bound (it attributes all between-model difference to length), not an estimate.
   Judges were instructed that length is not a criterion; that instruction cannot prove the bias absent.
4. **Empathy is the lowest dimension for every model.** Iteration 61's discrimination test found 5 of 8 EMP items
   unable to instantiate their test conditions, so part of this floor may belong to the instrument, not the models.
5. **No contamination indicated** by either probe. Recall overlap: no item flagged (max 0.07–0.11). Forced-choice
   identification: haiku 3/6 (p = 0.17), sonnet 2/6 (0.47), opus 0/6 (1.00), fable 1/6 (0.82) — all consistent
   with chance (1/4). This clears the 9 sampled items per subject only, not the bank.

## 3. Judges

- **claude-haiku was excluded as a judge** (it remains a subject). Measured against the harness's pre-existing
  validity checks before any score was examined: 73 of 498 evidence quotes (14.7%) did not appear in the reply
  they cited (paraphrase presented as quotation), plus 29 normalisation-only near-misses; it also dropped entries
  from 9 batches and exceeded the tool-call rule on 5 calls. Comparators: opus 0/498, sonnet 1/498, fable 3/498
  non-verbatim. **Disclosed as a post-hoc protocol change**, triggered by a check that existed before ratings were
  collected, not by the scores. All 498 Haiku ratings were re-routed to the one remaining eligible judge, so every
  response still has exactly two non-self judges. The 4 non-verbatim ratings from other judges, and 2 one-word
  quotes cb-probe rejected, were re-asked of the same judge.
- **Inter-judge agreement** (two judges, same response): exact 65.5–79.1%, mean absolute difference 0.22–0.36,
  2–6 responses per subject differing by ≥ 2 points.
- **Leniency:** fable is the most generous judge (+0.10 vs item means), opus −0.02, sonnet −0.08. Judges rated each
  other's replies above the item mean (+0.13 to +0.42); no judge ever rated its own model.

## 4. Operations record (every deviation from the plan)

- **Delivery as parts.** 83-message briefs were answered in 3 fresh-context parts (28/28/27); two Fable parts were
  re-delivered as 14-message halves after Fable split large outputs across several writes.
- **Compliance gate.** A subject or judge call counted only with exactly 2 tool calls (one Read, one Write),
  verified from the completion notice. **24 subject files and 17 judge files were voided** (usage-limit
  interruptions with no verifiable count, 3–5 tool calls, incomplete coverage) and re-run; none was repaired by
  hand.
- **Two logged, proven-lossless repairs:** 2 raw line-feeds escaped inside one Sonnet JSON string (24/24 valid
  parts byte-identical under the same transform); 1 Haiku answer code restored (edit distance 1, exact brief
  position, content verified against the prompt; a wrong-position negative control refuses).
- **Judge batches rebuilt** from 40 to 16 entries after measuring that 40-entry batches exceeded a single Read.
- Judges were told length is not a criterion; re-run subjects/judges got one extra formatting sentence about JSON
  escaping. Neither changes what was asked.

## 5. Defect found during this run (in the published tool, not this pilot)

`cb-probe`'s `finishScoredRun` dropped the identification-probe result when building the scorecard, so every
scorecard showed `identification: null` and `contamination_indicated` ignored the forced-choice test — the stronger
of the two contamination tests. It did not change this pilot's verdict (identification was clean for all four) but
would have reported "no contamination" for a subject that failed it. Fix and regeneration recorded in
ITERATION_LOG Iteration 89 and `tools/cb-probe/CHANGELOG.md`.

## 6. What publication would require (unchanged by this pilot)

Human validation of the items (0 of 93 today), an unpublished item pool, judges outside the subject family, human
raters for any official score, API access to non-Claude models, and a founder amendment to D-29. This pilot is
evidence that the **pipeline** works end to end under blinding, and a first, honest look at how far the instrument
separates models: **it separates a small model from larger ones; it does not separate the larger ones.**

## Addendum — 2026-10-01 (later the same day): figures reconciled, and dimension-level separation

**No figure above changes.** Two panel reviewers noticed that `analysis.json` (then built from the re-route tool's
exclusion record) said claude-haiku had **100** non-verbatim quotes with **34** near-misses and **0** dropped entries,
against §3's **102** non-verbatim (73 not found + 29 near-misses) and dropped entries in 9 batches.

- **The §3 figures are correct.** The canonical generator (`research/model-runs/bin/analyze-pilot.mjs`, field
  `quote_grounding`) now computes them from the 128 original judge files under one stated definition, and agrees
  with an independent count: haiku 102 of 498 not verbatim, 29 of them normalisation-only, **73 not found (14.7%)**.
- **Why the other number existed:** the re-route tool uses a broader normalisation, and counts dropped entries in the
  *final* answer files — which are the complete re-runs. The drops happened in the 9 first attempts, all voided
  (`operations/judge-batches.ledger.tsv`: 11 `VOID-INCOMPLETE`, 6 `VOID`).
- **A defect found on the way:** while adding that field, a shell heredoc stripped the backslash from `\s+`, so the
  generator's normalisation replaced every run of the letter *s* with a space and reported 27/75 — plausible numbers
  from a broken function. Caught only because two counts disagreed by 2. The normaliser now lives in
  `lib/normalise.mjs`, built from code points, with tests that fail 5 of 5 on the broken version (DC-21).

**Dimension-level separation (new fields `dimension_intervals95`, `dimension_pairwise`).** Among the three larger
models, 2 of 24 dimension comparisons look separated before correction — about what chance alone produces across 48
comparisons. **After a Bonferroni correction, 0 of 24 survive**: the three larger models are indistinguishable on
every dimension, not only on the composite. claude-haiku is separated from the others on **23 of 24** corrected
comparisons (subject to the same reply-length caveat).

**Correction to the addendum above (2026-10-01, later):** the stripped-backslash defect is registered as **DC-23**
(a shell metacharacter or escape silently consumed in transit), not DC-21 (a raw control byte in source). The
`\s+` → `s+` change left no control byte behind, so DC-21's gate could not have caught it. Registered as DC-23
occurrences 6 and 7 in `docs/DEFECT_CLASS_REGISTRY.md`.

**Sensitivity to the judge exclusion (new field `sensitivity`).** Recomputed from the original routing with the
excluded judge's ratings kept: the separation pattern is unchanged — the same three are not separated, and
claude-haiku is separated from each. But the levels move: the three larger models come out 8 to 11 points higher
with claude-haiku's ratings included, because it was the most generous judge of the others' replies. **The absolute
figures depend heavily on which judges are used; only the separation pattern is robust to that choice.** Excluding
the judge lowered the larger models' figures — it did not flatter them. claude-haiku as a subject is unaffected by
construction (it never judged itself).

**Two design facts the record should have stated (raised by the claim audit):**
- Each trial's 83 messages were answered in three parts (28, 28, 27), each part in one fresh conversation. So
  about 28 items shared a conversation; "a fresh conversation per item" would be false.
- Ten of the 93 bank items were never served: five unreviewed drafts and **five crisis-content items** (cb-probe
  excludes sensitive items by default). **This pilot therefore says nothing about how any model responds to a
  person in crisis.**

## Correction, 2026-10-02 (coordinator): the scale of the dimension means

Where this record labels dimension means "(0–5)", the scale is **one to five**. Each rating is an integer from 1 to
5 (`rating_1_5`; level 1 is "1.0 Critical", and cb-probe rejects anything outside 1–5), so no dimension mean can
fall below 1. The figures themselves are unchanged and correct; only the label was wrong. Composites are on a 0–100
scale converted from those ratings. Found by the pre-publication claim audit of the second pilot report
(`docs/ai-model-report/2026-10-02-pilot-2-claim-audit.md`, B2). The shared report figure component carried the same
label and is fixed in the same change.
