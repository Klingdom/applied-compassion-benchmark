# The Robotics-Labs 60.9 Seed Cluster: De-Seeding Study

**Date:** 2026-08-16
**Index:** robotics-labs
**Entities assessed:** 10, each with a full 40-subdimension assessment
**Prior 60.9 reference points:** Cerebras Systems and Baxter International. Different indexes. Noted, not used as anchors.
**Method template:** `research/SEED_CLUSTER_20.3_CALIBRATION_2026-08-16.md`

---

## 1. What was wrong

Ten robotics labs sat at ranks 19 to 28 of the robotics-labs index on one composite value, 60.9, and one dimension vector:

```
AWR 3.5 · EMP 3.5 · ACT 3.5 · EQU 3 · BND 3.5 · ACC 3.5 · SYS 3.5 · INT 3.5
```

That vector is byte-identical across all ten. It is a never-assessed uniform seed, not a measurement. Ranks 19 through 28 were an artefact of alphabetical ordering inside the tie: Aethon, Agility Robotics, AnyBotics, Clone Robotics, Engineered Arts, Fourier Intelligence, Naver Labs, Neura Robotics, Rethink Robotics, Universal Robots.

The number matters because of where it sits. **The Functional band ends at 60.0 and Established begins at 60.1. The seed is 60.9 — nine tenths of a point above the line.** That is the tightest margin of any seed cluster in the index family. All ten labs were publicly labelled "Established", the benchmark's second-highest band, because a placeholder landed just above a boundary.

For a compassion benchmark, unearned credit is the more damaging error direction than understating. One of the ten, Rethink Robotics, is not an operating company.

The arithmetic was never the problem. Canonical reconstruction of the seed vector returns exactly 60.9, with an integration premium of 0 because every dimension sits below 4.0. **The defect was that the input was never measured.**

### A wider finding about this index

Scanning the whole robotics-labs table while calibrating turned up something larger than the cluster commissioned. **Almost the entire index is seeded.** Ranks 3 to 13 all carry a flat 4.0 vector or a single 3.5 variant of it. Ranks 15 to 17 are identical to each other. Ranks 32 to 37 are identical. Ranks 39 to 43 are identical. Fewer than a handful of the 50 entries show the uneven dimension shape that a real 40-subdimension assessment produces.

This mattered practically. **Peer calibration inside this index was unusable**, because the peers are placeholders too. The ten were therefore calibrated against the behavioural anchors directly, and against the two genuinely assessed entities available elsewhere: Baxter International at 55.0 with dimensions between 2.8 and 3.4, and Cerebras Systems at 38.8 with dimensions between 2.2 and 2.8.

## 3. Band distribution, and whether the pattern held

| Developing (21-40) | 0 | **9** |

| Exemplary (81-100) | 0 | 0 |

**The downward pattern HELD. It did not break and it did not partially hold. All ten moved down, and all ten left the Established band.**

This is the opposite of what happened in the 20.3 study, where the pattern broke and five of twelve countries stayed put or rose. That divergence is the most important thing in this report and section 4 is devoted to explaining it honestly, including the possibility that it is an artefact of method rather than a fact about these companies.

### The statistical shape of the seed

| Statistic | This study (60.9, robotics-labs) | Prior study (20.3, countries) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean signed delta | **-30.04 points** | -1.18 points |
| Mean absolute delta | **30.04 points** | 7.08 points |
| Standard deviation of deltas | 7.56 points | 7.61 points |
| Range | -47.8 to -20.9 (spread 26.9) | -10.3 to +12.8 (spread 23.1) |

**Mean signed delta and mean absolute delta are identical, to two decimal places.** That is the diagnostic. It happens only when every entity moves the same way. In the 20.3 study the two statistics diverged sharply — a signed mean near zero against an absolute mean of 7.1 — which is the signature of a placeholder that is *uninformative*: right on average, wrong by a lot for the typical entity.

Here they coincide at -30.0. That is the signature of a placeholder that is *biased*. The 60.9 seed was not merely unmeasured. **It was unmeasured and set far too high.**

Note that the standard deviation is almost unchanged between the two studies, 7.56 against 7.61. The spread of individual entity movement is the same in both. What changed is the centre.

### The hypothesis under test

The hypothesis was "the 60.9 value is uninformative". **Confirmed, and then some.** The value carried no information about any individual lab: the assessed spread runs from 13.1 to 40.0, a range of 26.9 points, all compressed into a single published number. But the finding goes further than the 20.3 case, because the seed also proved directionally wrong in every instance.

The competing hypothesis the brief warned against — "these labs belong below Established" — was **not assumed and is not what was tested**. It nonetheless turned out to be true for all ten. Section 4 sets out why that should be treated with care rather than as vindication.

## 4. Why the pattern held here when it broke at 20.3 — and what could be wrong with that

The anti-confirmation-bias guard in the brief was correct to demand this section. Ten out of ten in one direction is exactly the result that should attract the most scrutiny, not the least. Four explanations were considered.

### Explanation 1: the seed's position on the scale makes downward movement much more available

This is structural and it is almost certainly the largest single factor.

A composite of 60.9 corresponds to a dimension mean of about 3.44 across all eight dimensions. On these behavioural anchors, 3.5 sits between "some proactive mechanisms" and "multiple channels, formal pathways, regular review, published institutional data". Sustaining 3.44 across *all eight* dimensions requires published disaggregated data, independent audit, community testimony and a reparative-action record. **No industrial robotics vendor in this study has any of those things, and the framework's tier-4 and tier-5 evidence is largely unobtainable for private companies.**

The 20.3 seed sat at a dimension mean of about 1.81, near the bottom of the scale, describing states that operate health systems, courts, social protection and ombudsmen. There was enormous room to move up. At 3.44 there is very little.

**A seed high on the scale is asymmetrically exposed to downward correction. A seed low on the scale is asymmetrically exposed to upward correction.** That is a property of the placement, not of the entities. It should be added to the prioritisation rule.

### Explanation 2: private companies disclose far less than states

Countries are covered by Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International, Freedom House, the World Bank, the United Nations and their own statistical offices. A mid-sized private robotics company is covered by trade press and its own marketing.

The evidence tier distribution in this study is stark. Across all ten labs, **only three cleared the band-change evidence test requiring at least one source at tier 4 or above.** Four failed outright with no source above tier 3. Three passed only partially.

| Lab | Band-change evidence test | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Universal Robots | **PASS** | ISO/TS 15066 (T5), Teradyne CSR reports (T4), UR statutory CSR statement (T4) |
| Naver Labs | **PASS** | NAVER Integrated Report and ESG reports (T4), climate disclosure (T4), Korean labour ministry inspection finding (T4) |
| Aethon | **PASS** | Singapore Exchange regulatory filing (T5), ST Engineering Sustainability Reports 2024 and 2025 (T4) |
| Fourier Intelligence | PARTIAL | One T5 peer-reviewed meta-analysis, but of the intervention class rather than of Fourier |
| Neura Robotics | PARTIAL | ISO 9001 certification is third-party but certifies quality process, not compassion practice; certificate not obtained |
| Rethink Robotics | PARTIAL | Corporate status corroborated across two publications but only at T3; no insolvency filing obtained |
| AnyBotics | **FAIL** | Four independent sources, none above T3; publishes no institutional data |
| Engineered Arts | **FAIL** | Entire evidence base at T2; publishes no institutional data |
| Agility Robotics | **FAIL** | Six independent sources, none above T3; private, no responsibility report |
| Clone Robotics | **FAIL** | Four independent sources, none above T3; publishes no policy of any kind |

**Seven of ten cannot be verified at tier 4 in either direction.** That is itself the finding. A placeholder asserting tier-4-level practice across all eight dimensions was indefensible for entities whose practice cannot be verified above tier 3 at all.

### Explanation 3: the assessor's own standard may be too harsh

This is the one that would invalidate the study, so it was tested directly.

Two consistency rules were fixed before scoring and applied identically to all ten labs:

1. **Absence of evidence scores 2, not 1.** The floor of 1 is used only where positive documented evidence of a specific failure exists.
2. **A score of 4 requires published institutional data or third-party verification. A score of 5 requires independent audit plus community testimony.** Nothing reached 5 anywhere in the study.

Under that standard, a fully undisclosed entity lands at roughly 2.0 per dimension, which is a composite of 25.0. Every lab that scored above that did so on located positive evidence, and every lab that scored below it did so on located negative evidence. Only two fell below: Clone Robotics at 22.5 and Rethink Robotics at 13.1, both on documented failures rather than on silence.

The standard is also consistent with the two prior genuinely assessed reference points. Baxter International, a Fortune 500 medical device company with published environmental, social and governance reporting, a regulated quality system and global access programmes, scored 55.0 on dimensions between 2.8 and 3.4. Cerebras Systems, with no disclosure at all, scored 38.8 on dimensions between 2.2 and 2.8. The ten labs here land between 13.1 and 40.0, which sits below Cerebras for most of them — appropriately, since Cerebras is a multi-billion-dollar listed company and most of these are small private firms with less disclosure still.

**Honest caveat: this cannot be fully ruled out.** A desk-based assessor cannot see private-company practice. If these companies do run grievance channels, disaggregated outcome review and reparative processes without publishing them, the assessment understates them. What can be said is that the standard was applied uniformly, that it reproduces sensibly against two independent reference points, and that the resulting spread of 26.9 points tracks disclosure volume closely rather than flattening into a new pseudo-seed.

### Explanation 4: the labs really are worse than a 60.9 placeholder implied

Partly true, but it is the weakest of the four and it does not carry the result. Only three of the ten produced any located adverse conduct at all:

- **Aethon**, five zero-day vulnerabilities in hospital robots in April 2022, found by a customer's auditor rather than by the company.
- **Naver Labs' parent**, a Korean labour ministry inspection finding majority-level workplace bullying, and two employee deaths.
- **Rethink Robotics**, dissolution with customers stranded.

For the other seven, **no adverse evidence of any kind was located** — no lawsuits, no safety incidents, no labour findings, no regulatory action. Their movement is entirely a disclosure gap.

### Conclusion on the pattern

The honest summary is this. **The 60.9 seed was demonstrably wrong for all ten labs, and wrong in the same direction, but the size of the movement is inflated by a structural asymmetry (explanation 1) and by a disclosure asymmetry (explanation 2) that are properties of the measurement situation rather than of the companies.** The *direction* is a real finding and is safe. The *magnitude* should be treated as an upper bound on the correction, not as a settled measurement, for the seven labs where no adverse evidence exists.

## 6. Filing outcomes

Ten change proposals were filed, one per lab, each with `"recommendation": "flag-for-review"` and `"status": "pending"`. **No score change is applied by this study.**

**No proposal was filed on the band-crossing clause alone.** Every one of the ten cleared the 5.0-point composite threshold as well, the smallest delta being Universal Robots at -20.9. The new clause was not exercised here.

**It should nonetheless be retained.** The 20.3 study found Djibouti and Mauritania both crossing into Critical on a 2.8-point delta with no route to file. That defect is real and is not disproved by this cluster failing to reproduce it. A seed sitting 0.9 points above a band edge will generate sub-threshold band crossings whenever an assessed entity lands between 55.9 and 60.0 — a five-point window this cluster happened to skip entirely, since the highest assessed value was 40.0.

Each proposal records its `band_change_evidence_test` result truthfully, including the four FAIL and three PARTIAL outcomes set out in section 4. **Recording a failed evidence test rather than suppressing the filing is deliberate.** The test failing is diagnostic information: it tells a coordinator that the entity cannot be verified at tier 4 in either direction, which is the strongest possible argument that a tier-4-level placeholder should never have been published for it.

## 7. Entity-record-currency defects found

Two, and both are more consequential than the scores attached to them.

### Rethink Robotics is not an operating company

The original Rethink Robotics closed in 2018 and HAHN Group bought its technology. United Robotics Group relaunched the brand in September 2024 with a white-labelled product line. On **16 September 2025** The Robot Report reported that it had shut down again: United Robotics Group's investors withdrew funding, the parent filed for bankruptcy, and Rethink Robotics was discontinued in the United States. The fate of the Hebron, Kentucky company's intellectual property and latest models had not been announced. A customer asked publicly, "I have a new arm from Rethink robotics, what can I do?" No answer is recorded.

The index carries this entity at rank 27 with the band **"Established"** — the benchmark's second-highest label, applied to a company that no longer exists.

**The recommended remedy is delisting or a successor record with accurate status, not a re-ranking.** The 13.1 composite is included because the methodology has no not-applicable state and a carried entity must be scorable. It states that no compassion infrastructure exists. It is not a moral judgment about a company that ran out of money, and it should not be published as one. This is the sixth open entity-currency case in the backlog alongside Hercules Offshore, dissolved in 2016.

### Aethon has the wrong parent attached to it

The index lists the entity as **"Aethon (Teradyne)"**. Aethon has never been a Teradyne company. ST Engineering agreed to acquire Aethon on 17 July 2017 for $36 million and completed the acquisition on 14 August 2017, per the Singapore Exchange announcement, placing Aethon in its Land Systems sector under VT Systems. Teradyne owns Universal Robots and Mobile Industrial Robots; the likely origin of the error is confusion with Mobile Industrial Robots.

**The parenthetical should be corrected to "Aethon (ST Engineering)" or removed, regardless of any score decision.** This defect also means the index contains two entities labelled as Teradyne subsidiaries when only one is.

## 8. Labs too thin to score confidently

Named plainly, three of the ten carry materially weaker evidence than the rest and their scores should be treated as provisional.

- **Clone Robotics** is the thinnest entity in the study by a wide margin, and its -38.4 is the least secure figure in it. Fifty employees, $7.1 million raised, no shipped product, no published policy of any kind, no independent assessment. **Thirty-six of its forty subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure.** The two facts carrying its four floor-level scores — no unit shipped and a pre-order page unchanged since December 2024 — both come from a single tier-2 consumer technology review and were not independently corroborated. A separate question is raised and not answered here: **whether a pre-revenue 50-person company with no shipped product belongs in a published ranking at all.** That is a scope question for the index, not a scoring question.

- **Neura Robotics** is the second thinnest. **Thirty-four of forty subdimensions rest on absence of disclosure.** The only third-party verification located is an ISO 9001 quality certification, which speaks to manufacturing consistency rather than to any compassion dimension, and the certificate itself was not obtained. Five of its eight dimensions land at exactly 2.0, which reflects how little exists to differentiate them.

- **Engineered Arts** has the weakest source quality of any lab assessed. **Its entire evidence base sits at tier 2**, including two Wikipedia articles. Critically, the founder's twenty-year non-labour positioning — which drives Boundaries, Systemic Thinking and Integrity, three of eight dimensions, and produces the cluster's highest Integrity score — is characterised in a secondary source rather than quoted from a primary company statement. It should be verified against a primary source before any future cycle relies on it.

Two further limitations affect the whole study. The Universal Robots and Teradyne corporate responsibility PDFs could not be parsed directly and their content was obtained through search-engine extracts of those documents, so figures for health and safety, gender diversity and whistleblower use could not be verified line by line. And this is a desk-based assessment throughout: **no lab here has community testimony or independent audit evidence of a working compassion practice**, which is why nothing in the study scores 5 on any subdimension and only nine of 400 subdimension scores reach 4.

## 9. Did "absence of disclosure versus evidence of harm" materially drive the result?

**Yes. It drove almost all of it, and the distinction is what makes the study defensible.**

For seven of the ten labs — AnyBotics, Fourier Intelligence, Engineered Arts, Agility Robotics, Neura Robotics, Clone Robotics and Universal Robots — **no adverse conduct of any kind was located.** No lawsuits, no safety incidents, no labour findings, no regulatory action. Their movement is entirely the distance between a placeholder asserting formal pathways, published data and independent verification, and companies that disclose none of those things.

The rule was applied strictly and in the direction that costs the assessor. Absence of disclosure was scored at the low anchor of 2 and no adverse inference was imported. The clearest test was Clone Robotics at A5 Anticipatory Awareness: it would have been easy to read a viral video of a suspended twitching humanoid, which drew reactions "from disdain to outright hostility", as evidence that no harm assessment preceded the decision. That reading was rejected and A5 scored 2, because absence of a published assessment is not evidence that none occurred.

Three labs produced located adverse conduct, and in two of the three it moved scores in **both** directions:

- **Aethon.** The April 2022 JekyllBot:5 disclosure lowers Awareness to 2.2 and Boundaries to 2.0, because five zero-days including one rated 9.8 out of 10 existed in hospital robots and were found by a customer's auditor rather than by the company. It simultaneously *raises* Action to 3.0 and Accountability to 2.6, because the response was dated, fleet-wide, proportional to the exposure rather than to the complaint, and followed through past the patch to firewall assistance for hospitals.
- **Naver Labs.** Parent-level tier-4 governance disclosure raises Systemic Thinking to 3.2 and Awareness to 3.0, while regulator-verified labour findings at the same parent push Empathy and Integrity to 2.0 with five subdimensions at the floor. It is the only lab in the cluster where genuine positive tier-4 disclosure and genuine adverse tier-4 findings both exist, and it has the most internally uneven vector as a result.
- **Rethink Robotics.** Sixteen of its forty subdimensions sit at the floor of 1, and every one of them rests on positive documented evidence of a specific failure, not on silence.

**Practical consequence for how these results should be communicated.** Seven of these ten movements are calibration corrections, not conduct findings. Publishing them as downgrades would convert a measurement problem into an accusation, which is the error the Cerebras Systems filing was written to avoid. Each proposal carries that framing explicitly in its notes field.

## 10. Attribution discipline applied

Every lab was scored as the entity listed. Parent-level policy was credited only where it demonstrably governs the subsidiary, and where it was credited it was named.

- **Universal Robots.** Parent policy is credited, and this is the clearest case for doing so. Teradyne's group corporate responsibility programme states that it covers Universal Robots A/S and its global subsidiaries, and Universal Robots files its own statutory responsibility statement under the Danish Financial Statements Act. Teradyne's wider conduct is not credited wholesale. The two 2025 layoff rounds *are* scored, because Teradyne Robotics executed them on the Danish workforce Universal Robots employs, including 6% of Danish staff.
- **Aethon.** Parent disclosure is credited narrowly. ST Engineering's Sustainability Report is genuine tier-4 institutional data and supports two subdimensions as governance context, but nothing was found confirming it reports on Aethon specifically, so it is not treated as evidence of Aethon's own practice. Aethon sits in ST Engineering's Land Systems sector, the group's defence arm; that adjacency is recorded as context and **is not charged against Aethon**, whose products are civilian hospital logistics robots.
- **Naver Labs.** Parent evidence is credited symmetrically in both directions, and this ruling is stated in the assessment. NAVER Corp.'s environmental, social and governance framework, AI Ethics Principles and AI Safety Framework are group-level instruments covering the subsidiary, so they are credited. The Korean labour ministry findings and the two employee deaths are also parent-level, and they are scored. **It would be indefensible to credit Naver Labs for the parent's reporting while excusing it from the parent's regulator-documented workplace failure.** No Naver Labs-specific labour incident was found, and that is stated in the assessment.

Two further disciplines from the brief were applied throughout.

**A funding round is not a compassion signal.** Robotics coverage is dominated by financing news. Neura Robotics' Series C of up to $1.4 billion is scored at exactly one subdimension, AC4 Resource Mobilization, where the anchor genuinely concerns bringing resources to bear, and nowhere else. The same treatment was applied to AnyBotics at $150 million, Fourier Intelligence at $210 million cumulative and Agility Robotics at a $2.5 billion merger valuation.

**Publication dates were separated from event dates.** The most consequential instance is Aethon: the JekyllBot:5 coverage is dated April 2022, and although several 2026 aggregator pages resurface it, the event is four years old, sits outside the preferred evidence window, and is recorded as such with confidence set to low.

## 11. Reconciliation with pre-existing assessments

Two of the ten already carried assessment files. Both were checked before work began.

**Agility Robotics is the significant case, and it is a methodological finding in its own right.** A file dated 2026-07-22 returned a composite of 61.9 and recommended CONFIRM against the published 60.9. It is superseded rather than reconciled, because **all forty of its subdimension rows cite the same single TechCrunch article of 17 July 2026, rotating four quotations from it**, and several of those quotations are used to justify scores in dimensions they have no bearing on. A single article cannot evidence forty distinct behavioural anchors.

The consequence is worth stating plainly. **That file's apparent agreement with the published 60.9 was not a confirmation of the seed. It was a restatement of it.** A one-source assessment that lands within one point of a placeholder has tested nothing. The present assessment uses six independent sources and reaches 30.6, a 31.3-point difference from the July file.

An earlier Agility proposal dated 2026-05-21 had dropped the company roughly 17 points on the basis of a Toyota commercial partnership. That is not revived: a commercial partnership is not a compassion harm, and the July file was right to reject it.

**Rethink Robotics** carried no prior assessment. The remaining eight had none either. `rotation-state.json` was not modified.

## 12. Recommended pipeline fixes

These extend the fixes recommended by the Sahel and 20.3 studies rather than replacing them.

1. **Add seed-position asymmetry to the prioritisation rule.** The 20.3 study recommended ranking clusters by distance to a band boundary. This study adds a second factor: **where on the scale the seed sits.** A seed above a dimension mean of about 3.0 is asymmetrically exposed to downward correction, because the upper anchors require published data, independent audit and community testimony that most private entities cannot supply. A seed below about 2.0 is asymmetrically exposed to upward correction. Distance to a boundary tells you how urgent a cluster is; position on the scale tells you which way it will move and how far.

2. **Run a full seed audit of the robotics-labs index, not just this cluster.** Almost the entire index is seeded, including the eleven entities at ranks 3 to 13 carrying a flat 4.0 vector and published in the **Exemplary** band. Exemplary is the benchmark's highest claim. Eleven entities holding it on a placeholder is a larger exposure than the ten this study corrected, and it is the same defect one band higher.

3. **Record failed band-change evidence tests rather than suppressing the filing.** Seven of ten proposals here carry a FAIL or PARTIAL result on the tier-4 requirement. That is diagnostic, not disqualifying: it tells a coordinator the entity cannot be verified at tier 4 in *either* direction, which is the strongest available argument that a tier-4-level placeholder was never publishable for it.

4. **Add an entity-status check to index validation.** Two of ten entities in this cluster carry a status or ownership error — one dissolved company published as "Established", one company attributed to the wrong parent for nine years. At that rate the index family will contain a substantial number of similar defects. Six entity-currency cases are now open in the backlog.

5. **Set a minimum-substance threshold for index inclusion.** Clone Robotics has 50 employees, no shipped product and no published policy of any kind. There may be no honest score for such an entity, and publishing any number for it — 60.9 or 22.5 — asserts more than the evidence supports.

6. **Retain the band-crossing filing clause even though it was not exercised here.** It was not needed because every delta in this cluster exceeded 5.0 points. The defect it was written to fix is real and will recur in any cluster whose members land within five points of the seed.
