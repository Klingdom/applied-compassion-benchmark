## 2. Patterns to adopt

1. **Rank spread, not rank.** LMArena defines ties by overlapping rank spreads, each an interval from best to worst possible rank given every model's CI (F: https://arena.ai/blog/ranking-method/). For us: show "rank 1 to 3" for Fable, Opus and Sonnet.
2. **Grades or bands instead of ordinal ranks.** AILuminate reports five coarse tiers with evaluator-error bounds (F: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05731). Our existing bands (Established, Functional) already do this; keep them and drop within-band ordering.
3. **Name which uncertainty source the interval covers.** METR separates the bootstrap CI from external-validity uncertainty it says dominates (F: https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/). Our pilot interval resamples items only; state that it excludes judge, rubric and bank-authorship uncertainty.
4. **Paired comparisons and clustered SEs.** Miller's paper recommends standard errors, clustering and paired comparisons between models, and power analysis for planning (F: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640). Our paired-difference intervals already follow this; add a power calculation for the next wave (how many items to separate a 2-point gap).
5. **Control for length in the model, not only in the instructions.** LMArena style control and length-controlled AlpacaEval regress out length (F: https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-08-28-style-control/; F: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475). The latter raised Spearman correlation with Arena from 0.94 to 0.98. EQ-Bench truncates to equal length in pairwise mode (F: https://eqbench.com/about.html). Telling judges "length is not a criterion" is weaker than any of these.
6. **List biases as controlled vs uncontrolled.** EQ-Bench's about page does this in two plain lists (F: https://eqbench.com/about.html). Copy the structure.
7. **Judge ensemble across families.** AILuminate uses a jury to avoid favouring one developer (S: https://mlcommons.org/ailuminate/methodology/). Our same-family judge pool is the gap this answers.
10. **Disclose when the tested artefact differs from the released one**, as Anthropic does (S, Opus 4.5 card). Equivalent for us: model version, access tier, date, and the post-hoc judge exclusion already disclosed in the pilot.

## 3. Patterns to avoid, with evidence of harm

- **A single ordinal list with no uncertainty.** Open LLM Leaderboard v1 was archived in June 2024 and replaced (F: https://huggingface.co/docs/leaderboards/en/open_llm_leaderboard/archive). The v2 task description says MMLU suffered noisy data, falling difficulty and contamination (F: https://huggingface.co/docs/leaderboards/en/open_llm_leaderboard/about). The successor leaderboard was itself retired in March 2025 because it was "slowly becoming obsolete" and "could encourage people to hill climb irrelevant directions" (F: https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard/discussions/1135). A claim of saturation as the cause of v1 retirement comes from a search summary only (UNVERIFIED). Lesson: a fixed public item set gets optimised against; our bank is likewise public (the pilot lists "an unpublished item pool" as a publication requirement).
- **Unequal access and selective disclosure.** The Leaderboard Illusion paper found 27 private variants from one provider, retractable scores, and "up to 112%" relative gains from extra arena data (F: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879). Our independence policy already forbids the paid version; add a rule that every run is published, including failed ones.
- **Raw preference scores without style control.** Ranks moved by up to 12 places when length and markdown were controlled (F: https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-08-28-style-control/).
- **One judge, one family.** EQ-Bench discloses judge self-preference of possibly 0 to 10% (F: https://eqbench.com/about.html). If effect sizes between models are 1 to 2 composite points, a bias of that size is the same order as the differences we are reporting.

## 4. Best practice: reporting statistical ties

Synthesis from sources above; the combination is my recommendation.

1. Use paired differences on shared items and report the interval; call two models tied when the interval includes zero (Miller, F: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640; same principle as Arena's overlap rule, F: https://arena.ai/blog/ranking-method/).
2. Present a rank range or a band, never a bare position. Order within a tie by alphabet or by a neutral rule, and say so.
3. State the unit of resampling (items, judges, trials) and what the interval leaves out (METR, F).
4. Report the number of items and a minimum detectable difference, so that "no difference found" is not read as "no difference" (power analysis, Miller).
5. Never imply an order by sorting. A sorted table reads as a ranking; sort within a tie group alphabetically and draw a bracket around it.

## 5. Best practice: disclosing length and verbosity bias in LLM-judged evals

1. **Measure and show it.** Report the length-score relationship pooled and within-model, as the pilot does (pooled r = 0.60; within-model slopes mixed). Arena found length the dominant style coefficient (F: https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-08-28-style-control/).
2. **Give a length-adjusted score beside the raw one**, and label the adjusted one a bound, not an estimate when the adjustment would remove all between-model difference (the pilot's Haiku 57.5). Length-controlled AlpacaEval shows the adjustment can improve agreement with human preference (F: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475), but regression adjustment cannot resolve a confound where length is a genuine part of quality; say so.
3. **Design for it next wave.** Add length-matched response pairs, or ask subjects for fixed-length replies on a subset, which gives a causal test that observational slopes cannot (EQ-Bench truncation is the cheap version, F: https://eqbench.com/about.html).
6. **Do not rely on the instruction** "length is not a criterion" as evidence of absence; the pilot already says this, and MT-Bench treats the bias as one needing a mitigation beyond prompting (F: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685).
