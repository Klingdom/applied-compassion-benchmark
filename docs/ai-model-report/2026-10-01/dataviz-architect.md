# Data-visualization spec: AI model pilot report (2026-10-01)

Panel role: visualization grammar. Source of every number: `research/model-runs/pilot-2026-10-01/analysis.json` (AJ), copied at prebuild into `site/src/data/model-runs/` with a hash check. Nothing gets typed in by hand.

## Rules for every graphic

1. **The caveat goes inside the SVG.** `ChartFrame` captions sit outside the `<svg>`, so a screenshot drops them. Every SVG ends with a footer text line: *"UNOFFICIAL PILOT · not a ranking · Claude-family judges · compassionbenchmark.com/ai-models"*.
2. **Order rows by subject id, alphabetically** (`design.subjects` is already sorted that way). This is the only order that doesn't come from the scores. It puts Haiku in row 2, so the chart never looks like a staircase. Score-sorting stays banned in future waves too: chart 2 carries the order information.
3. **Model marks get no band colour.** A green mark claims a band. Haiku's interval [36.6, 44.2] crosses the 40 line between Developing and Functional, so its "Functional" band isn't secure. Bands appear only as faint background zones. Models are told apart by shape (circle, square, triangle, diamond, assigned alphabetically) plus a direct text label, never by colour alone.
4. **Annotations are checked at build time.** Any sentence that makes a claim ("every interval crosses zero", "EMP lowest for every model") is generated by a predicate over AJ. If the predicate is false, the build fails. Copy can't go stale against the data.
5. **Every chart has a data table** in `<details>` right below it, plus `role="img"` and an `aria-label` that lists the numbers.
6. New components go in `site/src/components/model-benchmark/`. They reuse `chartTokens.ts` (bands, `CC_BY_CAPTION`) and `ChartFrame`. Hand-written server-side SVG, no new dependencies.

## G1: Where each model lands, with uncertainty (hero)

- **Reader question:** what did each model score, and how sure are we?
- **Type:** horizontal interval strip. Per model: a thick bar for `interval95`, a thin tick at `composite`. The interval is the main mark. *Rejected:* a bar chart of composites, because bar length reads as rank and hides uncertainty.
- **Encoding:** x runs **0–100, not zoomed**, with faint band zones and a labelled line at 40. Zooming to 60–80 would stretch a 2-point noise gap into an inch. On the full scale the 27-point Haiku gap and the overlap among the other three both show at true size. At 320 px wide the narrowest interval (6.9 pts) is still about 20 px.
- **Annotations (inside the SVG):** a bracket spanning Fable, Opus and Sonnet: *"Not separated: paired differences all include zero"* (from `pairwise[].separated`). At Haiku: *"Interval crosses the 40 band line"*. The composite tick carries no numeric label; numbers live in the table.
- **Fields:** `subjects.*.composite`, `.interval95`, `pairwise[].separated`.
- **Table:** Model | Composite | 95% interval | Band | Interval crosses a band line? (Y/N).
- **For N models:** one row per model. The bracket is drawn per connected group of non-separated pairs. If a group isn't transitive (A≈B, B≈C, A≠C), drop the bracket and point to G2.
- **Misleading if extracted:** **HIGH.** The ticks sit at 69.5 > 68.5 > 67.3. Without the bracket, a screenshot shows a podium. The bracket and footer must be part of the SVG.

## G2: What separates and what doesn't (paired differences)

- **Reader question:** which differences are real?
- **Type:** forest plot with 6 pair rows, a point for `difference` and a whisker for `interval95`, and a solid line at 0. Two labelled groups: **"Separated"** and **"Not separated"**. *Rejected:* comparing overlap in G1. Marginal interval overlap is not the test. These intervals are paired over the same resampled items, so only this chart shows the actual test.
- **Encoding:** one shared x axis, from −5 (or the lowest lower bound) up to the nearest 5 above the highest upper bound. With this data that's −5 to 35. The shared axis is the message: the uncertainty among the three larger models is tiny next to the Haiku gap. Separated pairs are flipped where needed to read "X − Haiku" (positive). Non-separated pairs keep AJ's alphabetical a − b direction and use a **hollow** point, because their sign is not established.
- **Annotation:** *"Haiku is separated from each of the other three. No other pair is: every interval below includes zero."*
- **Fields:** `pairwise[].a/.b/.difference/.interval95/.separated`.
- **Table:** Pair | Difference | 95% interval | Includes zero?
- **For N models:** N(N−1)/2 rows. Above 15 pairs (N > 6), switch to an alphabetical N×N separation matrix with cells marked "separated (+/−)" or "not separated".
- **Misleading if extracted:** medium. A hollow "+1.0" for Fable − Opus can still be quoted. Label the group header inside the SVG.

## G3: Dimension profile

- **Reader question:** where are the models strong or weak, and is the pattern shared?
- **Type:** Cleveland dot plot. 8 rows in canonical `DIMENSIONS` order (AWR…INT), one shaped marker per model on each row. *Rejected:* `DimensionRadar` with four overlapping polygons (unreadable, and area exaggerates). *Rejected:* grouped bars (32 bars invite 8 mini-rankings).
- **Encoding:** x runs over the **full 0–5 rubric scale**. AJ has **no per-dimension intervals**, so any gap among the three larger models within a dimension is untested. Zooming in would present untested gaps of up to 0.42 points as findings. That figure (ACC, Fable vs Sonnet) is computed at build time and quoted in the annotation.
- **Annotations:** (a) *"Empathy (EMP) is the lowest dimension for every model"*, behind a predicate, plus the instrument caveat: *"5 of 8 EMP items could not create their test condition (Iteration 61). Part of this floor may come from the test, not the models."* (b) *"No per-dimension uncertainty was computed. Gaps among Fable, Opus and Sonnet (max {x}) are not tested."*
- **Fields:** `subjects.*.dimensions`.
- **Table:** Dimension | the four models (alphabetical columns), 0–5.
- **For N models:** above 6 models, switch to small multiples (one alphabetical panel per model, reusing `DimensionProfileBar` with 0–5 converted to 0–100) so markers don't pile up. When the analyzer adds per-dimension intervals, add whiskers.
- **Misleading if extracted:** medium. Someone could crop the ACC row ("Fable best at accountability"). Annotation (b) sits inside the SVG.

## G4: Reply length and score (two panels)

- **Reader question:** is Haiku's gap about compassion or about brevity?
- **Panel A, between models:** scatter. x = `median_reply_words` on a **log scale** (the analysis uses log(words)), with ticks at 100, 200 and 400. y = composite on **0–100** to match G1. Each point carries its interval as a vertical whisker. A **hollow ghost marker** at `composite_if_pooled_slope_removed`, joined by a dashed line, is labelled *"extreme bound if all between-model difference were length. Not an estimate."* (wording from `length.note`). **No line is fitted through the 4 points.** The pooled slope is a *within-item* estimate, not a between-model one. A line through four model points would invent a dose-response from n = 4.
- **Panel B, within each model:** dot strip of `within_subject_slopes` on an axis from −1.25 to +1.25 (rating points per e-fold of words), with a solid line at 0 and a dashed line at `pooled_within_item_slope` (1.04). It shows the within-model slopes sitting near zero with mixed signs, far below the pooled slope.
- **Title (non-causal):** *"Longer-replying models scored higher; within a model, longer replies did not reliably score higher."*
- **In-SVG annotation:** *"This design cannot tell 'Haiku's replies were weaker' from 'brief replies were marked down'."* Do not use "because", "driven by" or "explains".
- **Rejected:** a per-response scatter. AJ doesn't have it, and 1,992 dots would suggest a precision the confound doesn't allow.
- **Fields:** `subjects.*.median_reply_words/.composite/.interval95`, `length.pooled_within_item_slope/.pooled_within_item_r/.within_subject_slopes/.composite_if_pooled_slope_removed/.note`.
- **Table:** Model | Median words | Composite | Length-removed bound | Within-model slope; footer row with pooled slope and r.
- **For N models:** Panel A scales directly. Panel B becomes a sorted-by-id strip.
- **Misleading if extracted:** **HIGH both ways.** Panel A alone says "length causes score". The ghost markers alone suggest Opus is "really" top (65.9 > 62.6 > 59.5 > 57.5). The ghost markers get no numeric labels in the SVG, and Panel A never ships without Panel B.

## G5: Judges, leniency and agreement (two panels)

- **Reader question:** did the judges agree, and did any judge favour anyone?
- **Panel A:** matrix of 3 judges (rows, alphabetical) × 4 subjects (columns, alphabetical). Each cell shows `judges.*.by_subject` with a diverging fill (symmetric domain = max |value| rounded up to 0.25, here ±1.0) **and the printed value**. Cells where a judge would rate its own model are hatched and marked *"own model, never judged"*. A row note reads *"claude-haiku: excluded as judge (quote-grounding); still a subject"*. A margin column shows `leniency_vs_item_mean`. *Rejected:* a leniency bar per judge, because it hides who judged whom.
- **Panel B:** per-subject `judge_agreement.exact` as a dot on a **0–100%** axis, with `mean_absolute_difference` and the count of ≥2-point disagreements printed alongside.
- **Required annotation (read the analyzer first):** `by_subject` is measured against the **all-subject item mean, Haiku included** (`analyze-pilot.mjs` l.127). The other three are therefore *above* zero almost by construction, so "judges rated each other generously" is partly an artefact. Read down the columns: all three judges put Haiku below the mean (−0.75 to −0.97). That agreement is the robust finding. Also note in the text: each non-Haiku subject had **one fixed judge pair** (`routing`), so judge leniency and subject score can't be fully separated.
- **Fields:** `judges.*`, `subjects.*.judge_agreement`, `routing.*.responses_by_judge_pair`, `design.excluded_judges`.
- **Table:** Judge | Overall leniency | Δ per subject; then Subject | Judge pair(s) | Exact % | MAD | ≥2-pt.
- **For N models:** N×N matrix with the diagonal hatched. With judges from outside the subject family (a requirement for publication) the matrix turns rectangular.
- **Misleading if extracted:** medium. Fable's row (+0.42 on Opus) reads as favouritism without the item-mean caveat, so the caveat stays inside the SVG.
