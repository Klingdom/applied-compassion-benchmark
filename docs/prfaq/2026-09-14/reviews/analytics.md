## 3. Findings

**F-1 (High).** The published research indexes do not disclose that 61.6% of scored entities (820/1,331) have never received an individual assessment — they carry a seed/placeholder composite only. Evidence: `research/rotation-state.json` (`last_assessed: null` count = 820, computed by grep in this session); corroborated narratively by `research/digests/2026-09-14.md`'s own description of France and Slovenia as members of "a 14-country placeholder cluster... never individually assessed." System: institutional research/rotation pipeline. This is a measurement-transparency gap, not a scoring-accuracy claim — the numbers may well be defensible placeholders, but a benchmark that ranks 447 companies and 193 countries without disclosing what fraction of those rankings rest on an assessed-versus-placeholder basis is exposed to exactly the credibility risk the PR/FAQ needs to pre-empt.

**F-2 (High).** Research cadence collapsed by roughly 6x after 2026-07-31 (from near-daily to 7 cycles in 44 days) with no automated alert — confirmed independently in this review via file counts (`research/scans/*.json`) matching the coordinator's stated fact exactly, and via `INCIDENTS.md` INC-001 (deploy pipeline never succeeded) and INC-008 (WebSearch cap exhaustion). System: nightly research pipeline + CI. `OBSERVABILITY.md` itself identifies "no scheduled-run alert for the nightly research pipeline itself failing outright" as an inferred, not-yet-incident-confirmed blind spot — this review's cadence computation is arguably that missing alert's manual substitute, and it should not have to be manual.

**F-3 (Medium).** Research-quality self-correction is real and already narrated per-cycle (26.3% of assessed entities required a factual correction on 09-14 alone: two year-errors, one arithmetic error, one date error, one currency error) but is not aggregated into a trend anywhere. System: digest stage. Without a rolling rate, a real regression (e.g., the digest's own flagged "scanner year-error defect... a pipeline pattern") would only be visible by reading each day's prose, which does not scale as cadence recovers.

**F-4 (Medium).** Three "public trust/usage" metrics explicitly requested for this review — feed subscribers, badge requests, alert opens — are **unmeasured from the repository** despite being instrumented in code (Worker `/admin/status`, badge endpoint, Umami events). All three live in external, live-only systems (Cloudflare KV, Listmonk, Umami) with no periodic committed snapshot. System: Worker + analytics stack. This is the single largest gap between "instrumented" and "measurable" in this review.

**F-5 (Low, structural).** Two of the three metric-count discrepancies documented in `OBSERVABILITY.md` (INC-005 queue undercount, INC-007 manifest-pointer disagreement) share one root cause with a third this review independently found (79 vs. 81 daily-briefing file counts, §2 above): counts are self-reported by whichever pipeline stage last touched them, with nothing that cross-checks a count against an independent listing. System: repo-wide (digest, lint tooling, manifest writers). Recommend a single generalized fix rather than three separate patches — see Recommendation 3.

## 5. PR/FAQ inputs

### "How will we measure success?" (FAQ answer)

We will not claim success from vibes or page views alone. Three families of numbers, computed from the same committed data we publish rankings from, are proposed as our accountability set:

1. **Research integrity, not just research volume.** We track and will publish: what fraction of ranked entities have actually received an individual, evidence-based assessment versus carrying a placeholder score (today: 38.4% assessed at least once, 10.1% assessed within the last 30 days); how fresh that assessment is (today: a median of roughly 60 days among ever-assessed entities); and how often our own pipeline catches and corrects its own mistakes before publication (today: roughly 1 in 4 assessed entities required a factual correction — a year error, a currency error, an arithmetic error — in our most recent completed cycle, all caught and fixed pre-publication). A rising correction rate is a warning sign we will act on, not hide.
2. **Cadence, honestly reported.** Continuous research only means something if it is continuous. We track the percentage of days with a completed research cycle (recent months: roughly 6 in 10, with a documented, disclosed 12-day gap in September 2026 from an infrastructure limit) and will report our recovery toward a daily cadence rather than let a quiet dashboard hide a loud gap.
